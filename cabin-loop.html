KAYAK CAMPOUT · FRI SEPT 11 – SUN SEPT 13, 2026

The Cabin Loop
Guemes → Cypress Head

Two nights at one camp — Cypress Head, the marine-trail site on the tombolo directly across Bellingham Channel from the Guemes launch. Short crossings timed to slack, and the best coho rips of the year right off camp. The two clock times that matter all weekend are launch by 2:00 pm Friday and be across the channel by 9:00 am Sunday.

RouteGuemes W shore ↔ Cypress Head
Crossing~1.3 nm, at slack only
CampCypress Head, both nights
FishingCoho, non-selective (MA 7)

Day by day

Friday 9/11 — Cross & make camp

LAUNCH BY 2:00 PM

Ferry to Guemes, drive across to the west shore, pack boats, and make the short (~1.3 nm) crossing straight to Cypress Head. Slack is 1:20 pm; after slack the building flood sets north, and Friday afternoon’s max flood (3.9 kn) is the strongest current of the whole weekend — miss the window and we wait until evening. Bonus of camping at the Head: the evening tide change happens right off our beach.

  • by 12:00Catch the Guemes ferry (7-min crossing)This is the commitment that makes everything else easy.

Saturday 9/12 — Fish both changes, boats stay light

NO CAMP MOVE · FLEX DAY

Camp stays put, so the whole day is ours. The two tide changes — 7:06 am and 7:49 pm — are the fishing windows, and both happen in the rips a few hundred yards off camp. In between: laze on the tombolo, hike the Head’s trails, or take the empty boats up the east shore. Mid-channel is serious water at max ebb (10:24 am, 3.8 kn) — stay near shore and out of the rips outside the change windows.

  • 6:00–8:30Fish the morning change (slack 7:06) — troll the kelp edges and seams off the Head
  • 8:30–10:00Big breakfast in camp — no packing, no deadline

Sunday 9/13 — Fish, cross at slack, home by 10

ACROSS BY 9:00 AM — HARD

The tight day: Matt cleans the cabin 11 am–2 pm, so the return crossing must ride the 8:10 am slack. The good news is the tide change is the bite — we pack first, fish the rips off the Head as the flood dies, then troll the channel seam on the crossing itself. After the window closes, the ebb builds to 3.3 kn by 11:15 and the channel is effectively shut for a loaded kayak until mid-afternoon.

  • 6:00–7:00Up at first light (sunrise 6:44) — break camp, load boats, rig rods
  • 7:00–8:00Fish the rips off the Head as the flood dies into the changeBest water of the morning — gear’s already aboard.
  • 8:00–8:45Cross on the slack, trolling the channel seam on the way
  • 9:00HARD DEADLINE: be across — ebb is already 1.4 kn and building fast
  • 9:00–9:45Land at the Edens Rd beach, carry boats (many hands), load cars
  • 10:00At the cabin — clean fish, hour of buffer before the 11:00 clean

Current turns lag the tide here by about an hour (high water 7:02, slack 8:10). Our ~9:00 takeout lands just past high tide — shortest beach carry of the day.

Why 9:00 is hard, not soft

The flood dies gently but the ebb builds fast: 1.4 kn by 9:00, 2.6 by 10:00, 3.0 by 10:30. A loaded kayak in a 3-knot spring ebb with the rips lit up is not a plan — and the next slack (2:45 pm) is after the cabin window ends.

Fishing & fine print

Regs (Marine Area 7, September): non-selective coho Sept 1–30 — any coho may be kept, daily limit 2 salmon, release Chinook. Single-point barbless hooks. WA combo licence + catch record card required. Bleed fish immediately; insulated fish bag with a frozen bottle keeps a September coho perfect until the cabin.

Weather call: current windows above assume paddleable wind. Check the Rosario Strait marine forecast Saturday night — a fresh southerly against Sunday’s ebb steepens the rips and moves the go/no-go call more than the tide table does.
