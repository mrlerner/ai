Shared data pipeline — where every number comes from

  • Open-Meteo Marine API (ECMWF-WAM / NOAA WaveWatch III): hourly swell height, period and direction at a fixed offshore point per spot, 8-day horizon. This is the early-warning backbone: global wave models are forced by forecast winds, so a Gulf-of-Alaska storm appears in the swell forecast days before the storm exists. The swell transit itself buys little notice — deep-water group speed ≈ 1.5 × period in knots, so a 13s swell covers the Gulf-to-entrance distance in ~1.5–2 days. Nearly all the lead time is the weather model predicting the storm, not the swell crossing the ocean.
  • Open-Meteo wind (10-day) at each beach: dawn wind speed/direction and overnight wind — the glass and morning-sickness inputs.
  • Surfline kbyg API (premium, 10-day, 3-hourly): the spot rating, surf min/max, and the per-train swell decomposition including each train's power — LOTUS's spot-specific propagated energy, which already accounts for refraction and shadowing at the break. Power is period-weighted, so it's the energy signal; it deliberately under-weights tall short-period seas (handled separately as texture, below).
  • Verification only (never inputs): NOAA buoys 46211 (Grays Harbor) and 46267 (Angeles Point); a daily 9am automated cam census at Westport (frame-differencing for surfers in the lineup); and the surf diary.
  • Visibility row: NOAA tide predictions (Seattle, 9447130) and the UW ORCA profiling buoy at Point Wells (measured chlorophyll & turbidity).

The Strait — custom entrance rideable-bar model (score 0–10)

📷 Strait cam  ·  📊 Angeles Pt buoy 46267
Each morning's most-active frame (9/10/11am) from the Sekiu/Hoko cam — burst-sampled over ~5 min so it shows a set, not a lull. W entrance ~40 mi W of the spot: a read on swell & wind arriving, not the break itself.
🔗 All Strait cam shots  ·  📊 Angeles Pt buoy 46267 — the in-strait source of truth.
  • Why custom: Surfline has no cam or forecaster in the strait; its rating there is a generic height+wind fallback that can't see the swell-angle keyhole, so we model it ourselves and show Surfline's nearest-spot rating only as a footnote.
  • Forecast point: 48.5°N, 125.0°W — open water outside the entrance, deliberately before the strait bends the swell. A 2.5-year buoy hindcast showed direction measured inside the strait is non-discriminative (refraction aligns everything to the channel); at the entrance it's the strongest single predictor — P(rideable) rises from ~3% for southerly-angle swell to ~70%+ near 290°.
  • Direction tiers (recalibrated Jul 2026): GO band 250–310°, MAYBE band 240–250° (scored marginal only), dead below 240°. From a 2.5-yr hindcast at the gate point vs the in-strait buoy: P(good day | size+period present) runs ~75% at 280°, ~70% at 260°, ~55% at 250°, ~36% in the 240s, ≤21% below — a ramp, not a cliff, because oblique swell sheds twice the energy running down the strait (transfer ~0.42 at 280–290° vs ~0.20 at 230–245°). Both bands test total-wave or swell-partition direction: a southerly local sea can drag the total reading out of the keyhole while a westerly swell runs underneath (75% of sub-245° rideable hours had an in-window partition). Long period does not rescue southerly angles — Cape Flattery geometry blocks them regardless. The upper bound is moot: model direction at this point never reads above ~290°.
  • Rideable bar (Jun 2026): uses total wave (sea+swell), not just the swell partition — the partition misses mid-period W pulses that actually break here (it missed the Jun 27–28 2026 swell). The Strait is "on" whenever total wave clears ≥1.8 m and ≥8 s from the W for ≥3 daytime hours. A lower bar than before by design: the Strait is genuinely nice even when small, so we favor recall — better an extra heads-up than a missed day.
  • Verdicts: GO when the rideable bar is met and wind isn't blown out (≥20 kt); MAYBE on a short window or wind up. Calibrated against the 46267 Angeles Pt in-strait buoy (recall ~0.80 of buoy-confirmed rideable days over the past year). Every GO says to confirm on the Angeles Pt buoy before committing — that buoy is ground truth. Days beyond the wave model's 8-day range fall back to the Surfline nearest-spot pill.

Westport & Short Sands — Surfline-primary + a period/glass overlay

📷 Westport cam
  • Premise: at cam-covered, forecaster-corrected spots, Surfline's rating is already the best "how big / how clean" signal — we don't compete with it. The gap it leaves is "is this Fair day worth a 5–8 hour round trip from Seattle?" The overlay answers only that, with the two things that actually predict Matt's good days: a long-period swell or a glassy morning.
  • No power gate (changed Jun 2026): we used to require organized swell "power," but power ≈ height²·period is mostly a size proxy — and Matt caps his size. It wrongly SKIP'd his Sep 2025 Westport standout (power just under the old floor). Period drives quality, not power; power is now shown in the mix bar but doesn't gate. See the methodology's research basis (research_forecasting.md in the repo).
  • Long-period GO (worth the drive): Surfline ≥ Fair and a real long-period groundswell — strongest ≥10 s train is ≥13 s and ≥1.2 ft — and not chopped out and wind not blown out and Fair holds ≥4 daytime hours (no cameo days). Go for the swell even when it isn't dead-glass.
  • Glass GO (the dawn-patrol pattern): Surfline ≥ Fair and a particularly glassy dawn — sustained ≤4 kt Westport / ≤3 kt Short Sands, morning peak gust ≤12 kt — and at least knee-high and no texture veto. Any period — wind is the master variable: all three of Matt's logged good days were glassy at 8–11 s.
  • Your size ceiling: surf max ≥ 7 ft → "bigger than your range" MAYBE at both spots (Matt doesn't surf 6 ft+). This personal cap replaces the old cove-closeout rule — his ceiling already sits below the size where long-period swell closes a beachbreak out.
  • Texture veto — judged by chop height, not power (power's period-weighting hides a tall 5 s sea that still wrecks the surface): a ≥5 s wind sea ≥2.5 ft that stands ≥1.2× the groundswell's height demotes the day; if it crosses the main train by >30° the bar drops to 0.9× — crossed ("sidewinder") seas corkscrew every face, aligned chop just rides as bumps. Short Sands is exempt (protected cove). A 14 s+ Westport train with a taller wind sea between sets gets a long-lull demotion; ≥12 kt overnight wind voids a glass call (morning sickness).
  • Override: a human-forecaster Good/Epic rating is an automatic GO — rare in the PNW, so alert-worthy by itself.
  • Thresholds are provisional (13 s / 1.2 ft / 4 glass-kt / 12 gust-kt / 4 Fair-hours): set from research + Matt's diary, refined as graded cam/session days accumulate.

Reading the swell-mix bar — the per-train display under each day

  • The bar shows Surfline's per-train decomposition (data they compute but don't display): dark blue = organized groundswell (10s+, the "engine"), light blue = windswell (8–9s, the wave-count workhorse), gray = short-period chop aligned with the engine, yellow = chop crossing it. One segment per train — gaps separate them, so two groundswells show as two dark-blue segments.
  • The bar is power-weighted, so tall chop shows as a sliver on purpose: a 3ft @ 5s sea carries almost no wave-making energy — its threat is texture and a tough paddle, which is what the ⚠ labels track.
  • Why alignment matters: all the trains share the same water. Chop moving the same direction as the main swell rides along with it — bumps on otherwise organized waves, annoying but surfable. Chop crossing at 30°+ runs diagonally across the faces — every wave corkscrews, sections shift sideways, and the paddle-out is relentless because there's no rhythm between trains. Same chop height, completely different day. A 2.5ft+ short-period sea also means a tough paddle-out regardless of alignment.

Puget Sound visibility — freediving model (score 0–10)

  • Multiplicative: score = 10 × wind × rain × tide × plankton — any one bad factor sinks the day, which matches how visibility actually fails.
  • Wind history (not just the day-of): local diver rule of thumb — under 10 kt fine, 15 kt marginal, 20 kt stay home. Recent windy days keep the water stirred.
  • Rain (72 h): runoff and turbidity from the rivers and storm drains.
  • Tide: fraction of daylight on a flood — incoming water is clearer ocean water; a double-digit (≥10 ft) exchange amplifies the effect both ways, so a big-exchange ebb-only day is the worst case. Puget Sound is mixed-semidiurnal (≈two floods/day), so we list the full clock times of both floods (low→high) — summer daylight here is long and early, so you pick which flood has the light you want.
  • Plankton: a monthly climatology (Sep–Oct clearest, May bloom worst) overridden in the near term by measured chlorophyll from the UW ORCA buoy at Point Wells (<3 µg/L clear → >15 heavy bloom), blended back toward climatology over 6 days.

Verification loop & known limitations

  • Every morning run logs each spot-day's scores append-only; verdicts are graded against buoy actuals (46211 Grays Harbor, 46267 Angeles Point) weekly, logged to data/accuracy_notes.md. Threshold changes only happen through review, never automatically.
  • Honest limitations: the power thresholds rest on one summer's backtest; the cam surfer-counter is still an uncalibrated index; the strait model is calibrated on only three confirmed sessions; wave-model skill degrades past ~day 5 (mitigated by re-running daily — far-out GOs re-alert when they hold inside 6 days); and solid 8–9 s windswell days at Westport are a known blind spot under review.
