This week in Seattle
Today at 1pm they start giving away rides on the wooden fleet — and then not again until September 27
TODAY, SUN AUG 30 — SIGN-UP 1PM, BOATS 1:15–5PM  ·  FREE  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The working maritime museum at the south end of Lake Union puts volunteer skippers and its historic boats at the disposal of whoever shows up: about forty-five minutes under sail, free, no experience needed. Sign-ups are in person in front of the Wagner Education Center at 1pm sharp — first come, first served, every person in your party present, no advance booking, no holding a place in line. You get a wristband with a departure time and boats run out until 5pm; between eighty and two hundred seats go out depending on how many crews turn up. Lifejackets provided, no age limit, digital waiver at sign-up, donations welcome and not required.
This is the fifth of six public-sail days in all of 2026 — April 12, May 10, June 14, July 26, today, and then the last one on September 27. Conditions are as good as the scarcity: 69°, partly sunny, 5% rain, south-southwest around 5 mph, which is enough air to move a boat and not enough to be a problem. Treat 1pm as the start of the line, not the target. If it is already hopeless when you get there, the boathouse floats and livery dock next door cost nothing to wander, and rowboat rentals and the Public Peapod program are the same water by other means.
Tuesday is the last of the weekly Tuesday-night sailboat races — the summer season closes on Lake Union
TUE SEP 1 — FIRST START 7PM, RAFT-UP ~8PM  ·  FREE  ·  LAKE UNION  ·  WATCH FROM GAS WORKS PARK
The Duck Dodge is the 52-year-old Tuesday-night race on Lake Union, and it is deliberately unserious: no entry fee, no club, no protests, four heats sent off by rating — Fast, Half Fast, Cruising and Dinghies — and the prize is a duck sticker for your hull. Costumes are the point as much as the sailing, and each week carries a theme; this one is "Committee Re-Appreciation," a thank-you night for the volunteers who have run the line since April. After the finish, boats raft up together in the middle of the lake around 8pm and grill until they drift back to shore. It is the closest thing the city has to a weekly water party you can watch for free from a lawn.
The reason it matters this week: the published 2026 schedule runs April 28 through September 1, and then jumps to a single September 26 "Rum Run" with an end-of-season party still marked to be announced. So Tuesday is the last ordinary Tuesday of the year for this. Weather cooperates about as far as you need: 73° and dry through the afternoon, rain not until after 5pm and a south wind picking up to 12 mph in the evening, which is more breeze than the fleet usually gets. Watch from the Gas Works Park shoreline at the north end, or paddle into the spectator fleet — rentals at the Northwest Outdoor Center on the west side of the lake or Agua Verde at Portage Bay. Rain plan: the same lawn, a jacket, and a shorter stay.
A ten-piece jazz orchestra plays new music for nothing, and lets you watch the rehearsal first
MON AUG 31 — DOORS 6:30PM, OPEN REHEARSAL 7PM, MUSIC 7:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Wayne Horvitz has been writing for Seattle rooms for three decades and co-founded this one; the ensemble is new, formed early this year — ten players, trumpet, trombone, saxophones, contrabass and drums — built specifically around new compositions of his that hand a lot of the piece over to group improvisation and live conducting. Which is why the open rehearsal at 7pm is the interesting half: you get to see the thing being assembled before it is played. Admission is free; donations to the Seattle Historic Music Project are encouraged.
Free with a catch worth knowing: the online reservation does not hold you a chair — seating is first come, first served, so the 6:30 door is the real call time. The Royal Room is the neighborhood jazz-and-everything room in Columbia City, food served, sight lines from anywhere in the house. A 7:30 downbeat on a Monday is a short, contained evening, and it pairs cleanly with an early hour at the locks before it.
Nine and a half thousand chinook have already climbed the ladder, and Monday is the last dry evening to stand over it
FISH LADDER OPEN DAILY 7AM–9PM  ·  FREE  ·  FREE GUIDED TOUR 2PM WED–SUN  ·  HIRAM M. CHITTENDEN LOCKS, 3015 NW 54TH ST, BALLARD
State fish counts put the Lake Washington chinook run at 9,566 fish through August 25 — the number that matters, because the last two weeks of August are the chinook peak and the fish are physically in the ladder right now. The underwater viewing windows are on the south side of the Lake Washington Ship Canal locks, free, and the room is open 7am to 9pm every day of the year. Coho start arriving through September and peak at the end of it, so the next few days are the short overlap when the windows can show you the tail of one run and the front of the next. The lock chambers themselves cycling boats up and down between salt and fresh water are free theatre on the walk in, and the Carl S. English Jr. Botanical Garden around them is a genuine garden, not a lawn.
Take it Monday evening: 72°, mostly sunny, 0% rain — the only rainless day in the seven-day forecast — and the ladder room is open until nine, so an after-dinner hour there costs nothing and closes out the good weather. Honest caveat from the operators: even at peak you may see nothing through the glass on a given hour, depending on tide and temperature. If you want a person explaining it, the free daily tour leaves at 2pm, Wednesday through Sunday, through September 30. The viewing room and visitor center are the built-in rain plan for everything else on site.
Free night at the natural history museum lands on the day the sky falls apart
THU SEP 3, 10AM–8PM  ·  FREE, RESERVE ONLINE  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, 4303 MEMORIAL WAY NE, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON
Washington's museum of natural history and culture waives admission on the first Thursday of every month and holds the doors open until eight in the evening instead of five. Two things are running on top of the free day this time: an artist working in the open studio from 10am to 5pm, and STEAM Spotlight: Queers in STEAM from 4 to 7pm, a free evening programme of science and art in the galleries. The building is designed inside out — the fossil preparation lab, the collections and the herbarium are visible through glass while staff work — and the Experience Alcoves: Water installation is up only through September 13.
Thursday is the worst weather day of the week to be outside and the best to be here: 65°, a chance of rain before 11am, then showers and thunderstorms between 11am and 5pm. Free but reserve online — first Thursdays run heavy — and light rail to University of Washington station removes the campus parking problem entirely. Separately: the museum closes at 2pm on Wednesday September 2 for maintenance, so do not try it a day early.
The legislative end, the courtroom end and the organizing end of one argument, in one room
FRI SEP 4 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM, ~75 MIN  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Congressman Jared Huffman on No Prophets, his book on the separation of church and state, and Skye Perryman — the litigator who runs the legal group Democracy Forward — on Ordinary People, Extraordinary Times, moderated by Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal. The pitch is the combination rather than either book: someone who writes the laws, someone who sues over them and someone who organizes around them, taking the same question from three directions. Downstairs in the Wyncote Northwest Forum, entrance off Seneca Street.
The sliding scale is unpoliced — $10 buys the same seat as $35 — and seventy-five minutes from a 7:30 start puts you back on the street before nine. Friday is 67° and a 29% chance of rain, so indoors is the right instinct anyway. Straight conflict, same evening: the Seattle Esperanto Society's one-night screening of Incubus at The Beacon in Columbia City, 7pm, 74 minutes — the 1966 horror film shot entirely in Esperanto with William Shatner, a year before Star Trek.
The Labor Day festival is next weekend and the weather has turned back in its favour
SAT SEP 5 & SUN SEP 6  ·  FROM $70.50 A DAY, $125.50 THE WEEKEND, UNDER-12s FREE  ·  SEATTLE CENTER
Fifty-three years in and back on the Seattle Center grounds for the full holiday weekend: music across several stages plus the comedy, film, visual art and speaker programming that makes it more of a fair than a concert. This year's names include Turnstile, Japanese Breakfast, Death Cab for Cutie, Bikini Kill, Lucy Dacus, Blood Orange, Orville Peck, De La Soul and Noname. All ages; there is a re-entry ticket tier if you want to leave for dinner and come back, and a deluxe tier above it.
The honest read for an early-evening person: this is an outdoor festival that runs late, so the value is in the daytime hours and the non-music programming, not the headliners. Saturday is 69° and partly sunny at 13%, the best day since Monday. A single day at $70.50 is the sane entry point, and the price tiers climb rather than fall as the weekend approaches. If you would rather not: the same Saturday evening has the free star party in Shoreline, below.
A Venezuelan kitchen on Holman Road just made the short list of new places worth the drive
ADDED TO THE HIT LIST AUG 27  ·  WACA MAYA, 10410 HOLMAN RD N, GREENWOOD
The restaurant guide The Infatuation added Waca Maya to its running list of the best new Seattle restaurants three days ago. It is a small, casual Venezuelan operation on Holman Road doing empanadas — fried masa pockets of chicken or cheese, with a herby green sauce — arepas, tequeños and a three-meat burger that the review describes, approvingly, as a beautiful mess. Counter-service, good for takeout, and a real gluten-free menu since so much of the cooking is corn-based to begin with.
Geography is the argument for it: Holman Road is the road you are already on coming back from Ballard, so it slots in after an evening at the locks without a detour. Check the hours before you go — a kitchen this size keeps its own schedule and does not publish them consistently.
Tide and aurora feeds are quiet. Nothing in the next eight days comes near the −2 ft that would justify a tidepooling trip — the water turns negative only pre-dawn, from −0.4 ft at 3:51am on September 3 down to −0.9 ft at 8:12am on September 7, which is the lowest and the only one at a civilised hour. Geomagnetic activity peaks at Kp 3.67 today and settles from there; the space weather forecast's own verdict for this latitude is no aurora. The Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalist season ended with July, so there are no guided low-tide walks to pair with any of it.
