This week in Seattle
They put strangers on wooden sailboats for free six days a year, and Sunday is one of them
SUN AUG 30 — SIGN-UP OPENS 1PM, BOATS 1:15–5PM  ·  FREE  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The Center for Wooden Boats is the working maritime museum at the south end of Lake Union, and its Public Sail is the part that is not a museum at all: volunteer skippers take walk-up passengers out on the historic fleet for roughly forty-five minutes on the water, at no charge. This is the scarce part — they only run it six days in all of 2026, and after Sunday the next one is September 27. Sign-ups are in person in front of the Wagner Education Center starting at 1pm, first come first served, everyone in your party present, no advance booking and no holding a place in line. Between eighty and two hundred seats go out depending on how many boats and crews turn up. Lifejackets provided, no age limit, a digital waiver at sign-up.
The forecast is the reason this leads: Sunday is 69°, partly sunny, 0% chance of rain — the only zero in the seven-day file, and a light southerly at 5 mph, which is enough air to sail on and not enough to be unpleasant. The catch is that a free thing on the one clean day of a wet week draws a crowd, so treat 1pm as the real start and not the target. If the line is hopeless, the fallback is a hundred yards away and also free: the boathouse floats and the livery dock, plus the paddle rentals at the Northwest Outdoor Center on the west side of the lake if you would rather be in your own boat anyway.

A band on the lawn above the fish ladder, and the last weekend of the free-concert season
SUN AUG 30, 2–4PM  ·  FREE, DONATIONS ASKED  ·  CARL S. ENGLISH JR. BOTANICAL GARDEN AT THE BALLARD LOCKS
The concerts happen on the grass of the seven-acre garden on the Ballard side of the locks, and they are genuinely free — the donation bucket pays the performers, the gardeners and the education programs. Sunday is the Lynnwood Community Band, a volunteer wind band; Saturday's slot, which the rain will decide, is Música Molida playing Cuban street organ and percussion. Downstairs and included: the fish-ladder viewing windows, open 7am to 9pm, where chinook are still running through in late-summer numbers, and the lock chambers themselves cycling pleasure boats up and down all afternoon.
Straight scheduling note: this overlaps the free sail exactly — Ballard 2–4pm against Lake Union 1:15–5pm, and they are twenty minutes apart. Pick one. The concert is the better call if you would rather not gamble on a walk-up line, and the garden holds up fine even under Saturday's 80% if you want to try that day instead; the visitor center and the fish-ladder room are both under a roof.

Sixty vendors and two hundred dishes, inside a building, on the day it rains
TONIGHT FROM 4PM · SAT 10AM–10PM · SUN 10AM–8PM  ·  FROM $14 ($2 MORE SATURDAY), UNDER 6 FREE  ·  FISHER PAVILION, SEATTLE CENTER
Second year for this one, and it has grown into one of the larger Asian street-food festivals in the country: more than sixty food vendors and over two hundred dishes across Fisher Pavilion and the lawn around it, plus a market of craft and merchandise sellers, live performances and cultural demonstrations. Tickets are timed and tiered — general admission starts at $14, Saturday runs two dollars dearer at every tier, and children under six are free. Expect a real crowd; the organizers added extra security this year on the back of the turnout.
This is the answer to Saturday. Fisher Pavilion is an enclosed hall dug into the hillside below the fountain, so the 80% chance of rain and the 11am–4pm thunderstorm window hit the overflow tables and not the food. Saturday also runs latest, to 10pm, which does not matter much here — go at opening, eat, and be out by mid-afternoon. If you would rather not pay to eat, the free alternative is next door and outdoors: the Ukraine independence commemoration takes the Mural Amphitheatre all day Saturday with performances and food, no ticket.

The free half of a sold-out arena week — music from 4:30, out the door by eight
TUE SEP 1 AND WED SEP 2, 4:30–8PM  ·  FREE, RESERVE A SPOT  ·  21+  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, 472 1ST AVE N, SEATTLE CENTER
The radio station throws these in its own street-level room on the nights a big act is in town, and the point of this one is that you do not need the arena ticket — both evenings are free and open, with station disc jockeys playing across the two nights while the Tame Impala audience filters past outside. The Gathering Space is the glass-fronted hall on the north edge of the Seattle Center campus, the same room the free in-studio sessions happen in, with a coffee bar and the live studio visible through the window.
Shape is the appeal here: a 4:30pm start and an 8pm finish is about as friendly as this city's music calendar gets, and it costs nothing. Register on the reservation link rather than turning up cold — the room fills. One restriction to know before driving over: it is 21 and over both nights. Tuesday reads 69° with a 20% chance of rain, and this is indoors regardless.

The natural history museum drops its admission and stays open till eight
THU SEP 3, 10AM–8PM  ·  FREE, RESERVE ONLINE  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, 4303 MEMORIAL WAY NE, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
The Burke is Washington's state museum of natural history and culture, and the building is designed so you can see the researchers working — the fossil preparation lab, the collections rooms and the herbarium are all behind glass rather than behind doors. On the first Thursday of each month admission is free and the doors stay open to 8pm instead of the usual 5, which is the only version of this that works on a weekday. Currently up: Woven in Wool, on the revival of Coast Salish weaving, alongside the permanent fossil, culture and Pacific Northwest natural-history halls.
Reserve the free ticket online first — the museum says first Thursdays run heavy and the campus parking around it is thin, so the light rail to University of Washington station and the walk up is the cleaner approach. Thursday's forecast is 64° with a 42% chance of rain, which makes an indoor evening the right shape for that day anyway. Arriving after work still leaves a real two hours.

Two new books on church, state and what ordinary people actually do about it — ten dollars if you want it to be
FRI SEP 4 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE
Congressman Jared Huffman of California, who founded the Congressional Freethought Caucus, comes with No Prophets, on the separation of church and state. He is joined by Skye Perryman, the lawyer who runs the litigation group Democracy Forward, whose Ordinary People, Extraordinary Times argues the case from the courtroom side. Seattle's own Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal moderates — which is the reason to go rather than read: three people who work the same problem from the legislative, legal and organizing ends, in one room, arguing in front of you.
Town Hall talks run about seventy-five minutes, so a 7:30 start is home before ten with time to spare. The sliding scale is real: $10 buys the same seat as $35, and nobody checks which you picked. Entrance is off Seneca Street on First Hill, and the Forum is the smaller downstairs room rather than the big Great Hall, so it will feel close.

A Seattle singer's first record, with most of the band that made it, at seven o'clock on a Sunday
SUN AUG 30, 7PM  ·  TICKETED  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
NicoleYvette is releasing Say Yes, a jazz record produced by the three-time Grammy-winning vocalist Sara Gazarek, with guests across it including Kurt Elling, the drummer and bandleader Terri Lyne Carrington, the vibraphonist Joel Ross and the trumpeter Keyon Harrold. She is bringing several of the musicians who played on the album to the stage for this. The Royal Room is the low-ceilinged Columbia City room that functions as the city's neighborhood jazz club — food served, all ages until late, sight lines from everywhere.
The reason it makes the list over the eight o'clock rock shows in Ballard this week: 7pm on a Sunday is the hour that actually fits, and a first album with the studio players in the room is a night that does not come back. It also lands after the sail and after the Locks concert — the whole of Sunday can be one line if you want it to be, ending indoors.

An hour-long Orson Welles nobody screens, on the wettest evening of next week
WED SEP 2, 7:30PM  ·  ALSO THU SEP 3, 4:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY THEATER BUILDING
The Beacon's run of European puzzle films rolls into The Immortal Story, the 1968 Welles adaptation of an Isak Dinesen tale — a rich merchant in old Macao pays to make a sailor's legend literally happen. It runs about an hour, which is the whole point of putting it on a school night: a 7:30 start is finished before nine. Around it the week holds The Saragossa Manuscript, the three-hour Polish nesting doll of ghost stories, at 7:30 Tuesday and Thursday and a 4:30pm Wednesday matinee for anyone who wants it at a sane hour.
Wednesday is the one day next week the forecast calls rain likely — 63°, 55%, and the coolest of the seven, so an indoor evening is the honest use of it. The Beacon is a single small room inside the Columbia City Theater building, a few doors from the Royal Room; the two make an obvious pair of nights in the same three blocks.
