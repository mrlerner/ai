SUNDAY — THE CLEAREST AFTERNOON AND EVENING OF THE WEEK
The last free boat ride on Lake Union until spring, then moonrise over Lake Washington
SUN SEP 27  ·  FREE  ·  SIGN-UP FROM 1PM, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION  ·  MOONRISE 7:05PM
The afternoon. The Center for Wooden Boats (the South Lake Union boat museum) still lists September 27 as its last public sail of 2026; checked again this morning. Volunteer skippers take people out for about 45 minutes, boats leaving 1:15 to 5. Sign-up is in person only, first come, from 1pm, and they say they can't always take everyone, so be there before one. It's also Steamship Sunday: a separate line for the 1922 steamship Virginia V, last ride at 3. Forecast: 63°, 0% rain, 15% cloud from two to five. Wind 2 to 6 mph.
The evening. Sunset is 6:58. The moon clears the horizon at about 7:05, due east-northeast (azimuth 71°), 97.5% lit, one day past full (computed this morning from the Jet Propulsion Laboratory ephemeris). Evening cloud is forecast at 14%. So it rises into the last of the twilight, which is the best light for it. Saturday is the actual full moon (99.8%, rising 6:45, before the 7:00 sunset), but its evening is forecast at 45–54% cloud. Pick Sunday. Watch from an east-facing Lake Washington shore: Seward Park, Magnuson, Madrona. Be in place by 6:50; you'll be home well before nine.
If the forecast turns: the Local Sightings closing night (the Northwest Film Forum's festival of Northwest films), The Joyless Economy, 7pm on Capitol Hill.
TONIGHT — NEW TO THIS BRIEF, AND HOME BEFORE THE RAIN GETS GOING
Emma Holten on why economics can't see care work
THU SEP 24, 6:30–8PM  ·  $15, $10 MEMBERS  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
Holten is a Danish feminist economist; her book Deficit: The Hidden Value of Care is out in 12 languages. The argument: the economic models governments use to make decisions were built centuries ago to leave out unpaid caregiving, so they count monetary wealth well and miss the social damage. She asks what caregiving is really worth, including in the Nordic countries, the "happiest countries on earth". Talk plus audience questions. It's part of the museum's Nordic Innovation Series. Kate Raworth (Doughnut Economics) blurbed it. It ends at eight, in Ballard, the best-shaped evening of the week. Rain goes from 35% at seven to 76% at eight, so bring a jacket for the walk out.
The bigger ticket, same night: Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor, with Mayor Katie Wilson moderating, 7:30 at Town Hall on First Hill, $10–50 sliding, out by about 8:45. It's their book End Times Fascism. Free instead: Christopher W. Smith on the Tsimshian carver Samuel Elwitt, Burke Museum (the University of Washington's natural history museum), 7pm, register ahead.
SATURDAY AFTERNOON — MUSIC, $5, NEW TO THIS BRIEF
International Women in Jazz, with Kobe's jazz queen making her Seattle debut
SAT SEP 26, 2–4PM (DOORS 1)  ·  $5, RESERVATION REQUIRED  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
The fifth year of this concert, put on by the Seattle-Kobe Sister City Association. The headliner is Kumiko Hisano, winner of Japan's Kobe Jazz Queen Competition, in her first Seattle show, alongside local women jazz players. The Royal Room is the cheap, all-ages jazz and experimental room in Columbia City. Tickets are online only (the room doesn't sell advance tickets at the door) and seats are first come, so arrive near one. Two to four on a dry Saturday leaves the evening free.
FRIDAY — STORMS, 94%: AN INDOOR NIGHT
Jason Dove Mark on how each generation forgets what nature used to look like
FRI SEP 25, 7:30PM (OUT BY ~8:45)  ·  $10–35 SLIDING  ·  WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
Yesterday's new find, still the Friday pick. The former Sierra magazine editor's The Earth Said Remember Me is about "shifting baseline syndrome": if you only know the salmon run you grew up with, you can't tell how much has gone. The fix he offers is record-keeping by ordinary people (fishermen and birders are his examples). With Lynda Mapes. Music option, same hour upstairs: Ganesh Rajagopalan and Marina Albero, South Indian classical violin and piano, $25–50, a full-length concert.
NEXT TUESDAY — NOW THREE GOOD TALKS AT ONCE; ONE IS FREE
Against Tech Oligarchy: JS Tan and Clarissa Redwine, with Kshama Sawant
TUE SEP 29, 7–8:30PM  ·  FREE WITH RSVP  ·  ELLIOTT BAY BOOK COMPANY, CAPITOL HILL
New this morning. The book is an inside history of the tech-worker movement that grew after 2016 (protests against military contracts, walkouts over sexism, union drives) and the executive backlash that followed: firings, layoffs, and a turn to the right. Tan is an ex-tech worker doing a doctorate at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Redwine helped organize the Kickstarter union. Kshama Sawant led the Seattle campaigns for the $15 minimum wage and the Amazon Tax that funds affordable housing.
The clash: Two Cities, One Industry at the National Nordic Museum in Ballard, 6–8pm, $20 ($15 members): Margaret O'Mara, GeekWire's John Cook and Jonathan Weber on how tech reshaped housing and politics in Seattle and San Francisco. That one is the closer fit to your work and gets you home earliest. Also 7:30 that night: Town Hall's salmon-run talk. Tuesday is 38% rain in the day, 24% at night.
