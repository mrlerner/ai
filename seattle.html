TUE AUG 18  ·  79° AND DRY, SUNSET ~8:08PM
6pm, downtown. The Freeway Park fiftieth-anniversary forum at Town Hall Seattle — the five concrete acres poured over Interstate 5 in 1976, argued over by the people trying to build the next lid.
7pm, Lake Union. The Duck Dodge, the costumed Tuesday sailboat race, free from the Gas Works Park hill and better from a rented kayak. This is the best water evening in the seven-day forecast.

The moon rises already bitten into on the 27th — 96 percent of it goes dark, and Seattle sees the deep half
THU AUG 27, MOONRISE JUST BEFORE 8PM / DEEPEST 9:12PM  ·  FREE  ·  ANYWHERE WITH AN OPEN EASTERN HORIZON
A deep partial lunar eclipse — 96.2 percent of the moon's disk inside the Earth's dark umbral shadow, which is about as close to a total as a partial gets. The shadow starts crossing at 7:33pm Pacific, but in Seattle the moon is still below the horizon then: it clears the skyline just before 8pm with the bite already taken out of it, hits maximum at 9:12pm, and slides clear by 10:52pm. A low, deeply eclipsed moon coming up over water is the good version of this, so the play is an eastern view across Lake Washington — Seward Park, Magnuson, the Mount Baker shoreline — rather than anywhere with hills or trees to the east. No equipment needed, and nine days is enough notice to keep the evening clear.
The rest of this morning's nature pull is quiet: no minus tides inside seven days — today's lowest low was +1.4 ft at 3:40am and the week bottoms out at +0.2 ft next Monday — and the space-weather forecast peaks at a planetary index of 4.7 overnight, which is no aurora at this latitude. The tides do go negative the same week as the eclipse: −0.1 ft on Aug 25, −0.3 ft on Aug 26, deepening after that.

The Breeders close out the zoo-lawn season on the one properly sunny day of the week
THU AUG 20, EVENING  ·  TICKETED, ONLINE ONLY  ·  WOODLAND PARK ZOO, PHINNEY RIDGE
The Breeders with Team Dresch — two bands out of the same early-nineties Pacific Northwest and Ohio underground, on a lawn, and it is the final date of the zoo's 2026 summer concert series. Thursday is the only day the National Weather Service calls flatly sunny (79°, no rain, falling to 59° after dark), so this is the outdoor evening of the week whether or not you buy a ticket. Low chairs and blankets only, and the lawn fills early — the reason regulars queue before gates.
Free version of the same weather: the Ballard Locks fish ladder is inside its chinook window, viewing until 8:45pm daily, and a Thursday-evening walk out to the salt side is the cheapest way to spend that forecast.

The world's first park built over a freeway throws itself a free fiftieth birthday party
SAT AUG 22  ·  FREE, INCLUDING THE FOOD  ·  FREEWAY PARK, DOWNTOWN AT 8TH & SENECA
Tonight's forum is the talking; Saturday is the party. Seattle Parks and the Freeway Park Association are programming the whole brutalist canyon — live music from the Garfield High School jazz band and Seattle buskers, guided architecture and landscape tours, temporary artworks and site-specific audio installed through the concrete, family activities, and free locally catered food and drink. For anyone who builds civic software, this is the rare chance to walk the 1976 lid with the people who maintain it and the ones planning the next one, on the day they are actually answering questions.
Weather call: 70° and mostly cloudy, 8 percent rain — fine for this one, and the park's overhangs and water-wall alcoves cover you if it spits. Indoor fallback three blocks away: the Central Library.

Three miles of lakefront boulevard go car-free from Saturday morning to Sunday evening
SAT 10AM – SUN 6PM  ·  FREE  ·  LAKE WASHINGTON BOULEVARD, MOUNT BAKER PARK TO SEWARD PARK
Lake Washington Boulevard between Mount Baker Park Beach and Seward Park closes to motor traffic for a straight thirty-two hours — walking, riding and rolling only, with local access kept open by cross street. It runs every weekend through Labor Day, so this is not scarcity; it is just the best flat waterfront hours in the city, and Sunday is the better of the two days at 72° and partly sunny against Saturday's cloud. Pairs neatly with the eclipse scouting: the Seward Park end is the eastern-horizon viewpoint you would want on the 27th.

A field guide to the Northwest year, launched by its illustrator — the next lecture worth holding a night for
WED AUG 26, 7:30PM (DOORS 6:30)  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Emily Poole launches “Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest” — the region's plants and animals organized by the season you would actually meet them in, illustrations plus essays plus things to go do. She is joined by Kate Rogers, editor-in-chief at Mountaineers Books, and Katie Campbell of the public radio station KUOW, and the evening doubles as a preview of the press's fall list. Seventy-five minutes, sliding scale down to $10, and it is the natural-history talk of this stretch of the calendar.

The last film by the man who made animation strange gets a four-day run in Columbia City
SUN AUG 23 5PM, MON 7:30PM, TUE 4:30PM, WED 4:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
“Gandahar” (1987, 83 minutes) is René Laloux's final feature — a warrior investigates an army of automata turning his civilization to stone, and ends up facing a disembodied cosmic brain. The Beacon bills it as a cult classic of mind-bending science fiction from a pioneer of alternative animation. Four screenings, single screen, forty seats; the Sunday 5pm is the one that leaves the evening free for the Tractor.
Black Arts Fest takes over Seattle Center for free, both weekend days
SAT AUG 22 & SUN AUG 23  ·  FREE  ·  SEATTLE CENTER
Festival Sundiata's Black Arts Fest, one of the city's long-running Festál cultural weekends: hip hop, dance, gospel, visual art, soul food and a marketplace across the campus grounds. Free, outdoors but with the Armory as built-in shelter, and it is a ten-minute walk from the same lot you would use for anything else at the Center that weekend.
