This week in Seattle
Tomorrow the moon clears the horizon already 96% eaten, and there is a free telescope field waiting for it
THU AUG 27 — MOONRISE ~7:50PM, MAXIMUM 9:13PM  ·  FREE  ·  SEWARD PARK, 5900 LAKE WASHINGTON BLVD S
This is a deep partial lunar eclipse — at maximum, 96% of the moon's disk sits inside the Earth's dark umbral shadow, which is close enough to total that the visible part will likely go copper. The partial phase starts at 7:34pm, before the moon is even up here, so the moon rises already in eclipse over south Lake Washington at roughly 7:50pm, climbs to maximum at 9:13pm, and does not finish the partial phase until 10:52pm. A moon that comes up wrong is a rarer sight than an eclipse overhead, and the low eastern angle across the water at Seward Park is the whole reason to be there rather than in your own yard.
The Seattle Astronomical Society runs a free public star party at the Lakewood Playground end of Seward Park, 7:30 to 10pm, with member telescopes set up and pointed — they ask for a reply on their site so they know numbers. Thursday night is forecast partly cloudy, 57°, 5% chance of rain, the cleanest night in the seven-day file, and the good part is over by around 9:30 if you leave after maximum. If cloud rolls in, any east-facing shoreline works — Magnuson, Madrona, Leschi — and the eclipse is naked-eye, so no gear is required anywhere.

Last call: the Northwest's outdoors publisher shows its whole fall list tonight, plus the artist who painted a year of it
TONIGHT, WED AUG 26, 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE
Mountaineers Books is the nonprofit Seattle publishing house behind most of the region's trail and natural-history writing; Skipstone is its illustrated imprint. Tonight they launch Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest, by author and illustrator Emily Poole, with editor-in-chief Kate Rogers — and then keep going into a preview of every art and natural-history title they have coming this fall. It is the one evening a year you get the publisher's own read on what is worth reading about this place.
This has been sitting in the brief for five days and tonight is where it stops. Sliding scale means $10 is a real price, not a concession. The entrance is off Seneca Street. A 7:30 start at Town Hall usually runs about seventy-five minutes with signing after, so the talk itself lands around 8:45 — leave at the applause and you are home on time. Today is 79° and mostly sunny if the evening would rather be spent outside; this is indoors either way.

Seven free hours on the new piers, the first year the whole twenty-acre waterfront is actually finished
SAT AUG 29, 1–8PM  ·  FREE  ·  PIER 62, PIER 58 AND THE SALISH STEPS, CENTRAL WATERFRONT
Fifth year of this, and the first one that doubles as the completion party for the twenty-acre Waterfront Park. Four live sets on the Pier 62 stage over the afternoon — among them Clinton Fearon & the Boogie Brown Band, the Jamaican bassist and singer who has been a Seattle fixture for decades — with cultural performances and disc jockeys running separately on Pier 58 and dance workshops on the Salish Steps, the wide stair that drops from Pike Place down to the water. Free food-truck-and-family sprawl in between, and it is all outdoors on the edge of Elliott Bay.
Weather call: Saturday is the soggier half of the weekend — 71° with a 35% chance of light rain — so treat the early afternoon as the better bet and carry a shell. Two indoor fallbacks a few minutes away if it turns: the Seattle Aquarium is running free public programming about whales on its Ocean Pavilion rooftop from 11:30am to 3:30pm the same day, and Tidelands, a free gallery of Native art on the walk down, is open 11am to 5pm. Nothing here costs anything, and it wraps at eight.

The weekend's one clean sunny day is also a free ride on Lake Union in somebody's wooden boat
SUN AUG 30 — SIGN-UPS OPEN 1PM, BOATS DEPART 1:15–5PM  ·  FREE  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, SOUTH LAKE UNION
Volunteer skippers take the public out on Lake Union at no charge — sprit-rigged boats, steamboats, electric launches, schooners, ketches, yawls, whatever the maintenance schedule and the volunteer roster allow that day. It runs six times a year and this is the second-to-last one of 2026; the only date left after it is September 27. Sign-ups are in person at 1pm in front of the Wagner Education Center, first come, first served, everyone in your party present, wristbands on the spot — between 80 and 200 seats depending on the day.
Weather call: Sunday is 70° and mostly sunny with a 4% chance of rain — the only day between now and Tuesday under ten percent, and a full thirty points drier than Saturday. That will bring people, so arriving at 1pm sharp is the difference between a boat and a wait. If the line beats you, the rowboat livery on the docks rents by the hour and the two Peapod rowboats go out on the same first-come basis; failing that, walking the docks and the Jubilee wooden-boat exhibition costs nothing and is indoors-adjacent if the sky changes its mind.

Two hundred amateur jazz players take the room in shifts, at six o'clock, for whatever you put in the bucket
FRI AUG 28 — DOORS 5PM, MUSIC 6PM  ·  $20 SUGGESTED DONATION, PAY AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Jazz Night School is the Seattle nonprofit that teaches adults to play jazz in ensembles rather than alone in a practice room, and twice each summer it empties the entire program onto the Royal Room stage: student combos back to back, the Performance Big Band, the gospel choir, solo singers. Over two hundred students and faculty across the two nights, of which this is the second. There is no version of this that is polished, and that is the appeal — short sets, real stakes, a room pulling hard for people it knows.
A 6pm downbeat is the rarest thing on any music calendar in this city, and the room is all ages until 10pm. Seating is first come, first served with no reserved tables, so the 5pm doors are worth using. Friday is the day the dry stretch ends — 73° falling into a 54% chance of light rain overnight — which makes an indoor, early, cheap Friday the correct shape for that evening anyway. Thursday's Night #1 is the same bill at the same hour, but it collides head-on with the eclipse.

The man who sang the gallows song in a Coen brothers film, in a two-hundred-seat Ballard bar
MON AUG 31, 8PM  ·  $25.08  ·  21+  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, 5213 BALLARD AVE NW
Willie Watson founded Old Crow Medicine Show and left it to sing old American folk and prison songs by himself with a guitar and a banjo — the voice at the end of The Ballad of Buster Scruggs. He is touring with Erin Rae, a Nashville songwriter whose records run quiet and close-miked. Two singers, almost no band, in the Tractor — the Ballard Avenue roots room with the wagon wheels on the wall, and the right size for material this bare.
Straight talk on the hour: 8pm start, and a Tractor headliner is rarely offstage before 10:30. This is a late one by any normal standard, and it is on a Monday. It earns the mention because Watson does not come through often and this room is much smaller than what he can fill — but if that clock does not work, Ballard Avenue is a five-minute walk from the locks and the same money goes further on a Sunday afternoon there. Monday is 70° and partly sunny with a 21% chance of rain, so nothing outdoors is being given up.
