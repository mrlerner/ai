This week in Seattle
Seven Seattle singers, one thunderstorm evening, done by nine
TONIGHT THU SEP 3 — 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30  ·  $20 ADVANCE / $25 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Royal Room is the low-ceilinged, cheap, food-serving music room in Columbia City that books more interesting bills than anywhere its size in the city. Tonight is Great Women of Folk & Country: GenX Radio — a rotating cast of Seattle singers working through the songbook of the women who owned the radio between roughly 1975 and 1999. Seven voices trading a stage is a different night than one songwriter with a guitar, and it fits the room.
It is also the right shape for today's forecast: showers and thunderstorms are possible between 11am and 5pm, and the evening is the first dry stretch in three days. Light rail drops you three blocks away, which matters more than usual on a wet afternoon.
Two other things happen today and both end tonight: the Burke Museum is free until 8pm for first Thursday (no ticket needed at the door, and the Water alcove closes Sep 13), and The Beacon runs The Saragossa Manuscript, the three-hour Polish nesting-doll epic, at 7:30 — that one goes past eleven.
Vintage tugboats you can walk aboard, then race, and none of it costs anything
FRI SEP 4 5–8PM  ·  SAT SEP 5 10AM–7PM  ·  SUN SEP 6 10AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  PERCIVAL LANDING PARK, OLYMPIA WATERFRONT (ABOUT AN HOUR SOUTH ON I-5)
Olympia Harbor Days is the Labor Day maritime festival at the very bottom of Puget Sound, and it bills the largest vintage tugboat races in the world. The working boats tie up along Percival Landing all weekend for a walk-aboard show — you go on the decks, into the wheelhouses, and talk to the people who run them — and then on Sunday they run the course out in the deep channel of Budd Inlet, which you watch from the boardwalk for free.
Admission is free, the whole thing is outdoors on a boardwalk, and the weather cooperates completely: Friday 68°, Saturday 69°, Sunday 72° and mostly sunny at 2% rain. Free parking at Market Street and Marine Drive. Sunday is both the race day and the best-weather day, so that is the trip.
If an hour each way is too much of the weekend: the same water interest is walkable at the Ballard Locks, where chinook stand at 10,785 through the end of August and the coho run is just opening at 413 — early days, the real coho show is late September.
Nature & sky
The lowest daylight tides of the next eight days land on the two mornings you are not working
MON SEP 7, 8:12AM  ·  TUE SEP 8, 9:12AM  ·  FREE  ·  ANY PUGET SOUND BEACH — CONSTELLATION PARK (WEST SEATTLE) OR GOLDEN GARDENS
The water. The tide table bottoms out over the long weekend and just after: −0.9 ft at 8:12am on Labor Day Monday, then −1.0 ft at 9:12am on Tuesday — the only low in the eight-day file the script flags as notable. Both are in full daylight, both fall on 70°-plus mostly-sunny mornings, and a foot below zero uncovers a wide walkable flat: sea stars, anemones, moon snails, the sand-dollar beds. Tuesday's is the lower and the later, which makes it the civilised one.
The sky. Same Tuesday, the other end of the morning: a thin waning crescent Moon sits beside Jupiter in the pre-dawn eastern sky, with earthshine on the Moon's dark limb — naked eye, no equipment needed, any east-facing view. It is the opening act of a run of Moon–Jupiter meetings that turns into an actual occultation in October. Sunday pre-dawn has the smaller version with Mars.
This is a do-it-yourself tidepool — the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalists finished their season over the summer and are not stationed on these dates. Aurora is off the table: the space-weather forecast peaks at Kp 1.7, nowhere near Seattle's latitude.
The Apple Cup on a Sunday, in September, in daylight — none of which has ever happened before
SUN SEP 6 — 1PM KICKOFF  ·  TICKETED (GOHUSKIES.COM)  ·  HUSKY STADIUM, MONTLAKE  ·  ALSO ON NBC
The University of Washington and Washington State University have played this game 117 times and never once like this. The 118th Apple Cup is the season opener for both programmes, it is on a Sunday for the first time in the history of the series, and it kicks at 1pm rather than after dark. Husky Stadium on a 72° cloudless afternoon, with Lake Washington behind the open end, is the best-looking stadium in the country and this is the day for it.
Practical version: it is a rivalry sellout crowd on the same weekend as Bumbershoot, so take the light rail to the U District station or the boat — the Montlake approach will be a mess by noon. Out by about 4:30, with the whole evening left.
Free alternative on the same water at the same hour: walk the Montlake Cut and the Arboretum instead and watch the boat traffic. And if the crowd is the objection entirely, Sunday is also the driest day to be anywhere outdoors at all.
The last chairlift of the summer runs on Labor Day and then stops until snow
SAT SEP 5, SUN SEP 6 AND MON SEP 7 — 9:30AM TO 5:30PM  ·  TICKETED, RIDE AS MANY TIMES AS YOU LIKE  ·  SUMMIT WEST, SNOQUALMIE PASS, ABOUT AN HOUR EAST ON I-90
Summit West runs its scenic lift on weekends from early July to Labor Day — and Labor Day Monday is the final day of the 2026 season. A ticket buys the whole day and unlimited round trips: over thirty minutes of ride time above the pass, out over the meadows, with the option of walking down instead of riding. It is the least strenuous way in Washington to be a thousand feet above where you parked.
Weather is as good as the pass gets in September — Sunday 72° and Monday 71°, both mostly sunny — and an hour of I-90 gets you there. Go Monday if you want the last-day-of-the-season version; Sunday if you would rather not compete with the holiday drive home. The lift runs 9:30 to 5:30 either way, so this is a morning trip that still leaves the evening free.
Indoor fallback is not needed this weekend, but the mountain version if you would rather earn it: the same pass exits reach Snow Lake and Franklin Falls, and the Washington Trails Association has current conditions for both. Trailhead lots at the pass fill by 8am on a holiday weekend.
The man who made the harmonica a jazz instrument, in a hundred-seat room for twenty dollars
MON SEP 7 (LABOR DAY) — DOORS 6:30PM, MUSIC 7:30PM  ·  $20 ADVANCE ONLINE / $25 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Howard Levy is the harmonica player who worked out how to get every chromatic note out of a ten-hole diatonic instrument — the technique is genuinely his — and then spent a career proving it in Bela Fleck's Flecktones and about two hundred records since. He plays piano in the same set. Chris Siebold, his long-time Chicago partner, is on guitars. The venue's own description is jazz, blues, pop, classical and originals, which is a fair warning that the set will not sit still.
Advance tickets are online only, and buying one lets you then reserve a table if you want dinner; non-dining seats are first come, first served, so early doors are worth it in a room this small. Music at 7:30 on the last night of the long weekend, and light rail to Columbia City means no parking problem.
Same night in Ballard, if a called dance beats a listening room: the Tractor Tavern square dance with Three Forks of Candy starts at 6:30 for $12.42, caller included, no partner needed.
Four free live sessions at the radio station next week, and one of them is Turkish psychedelic folk
MON SEP 7 11AM  ·  TUE SEP 8 3PM  ·  WED SEP 9 11AM  ·  THU SEP 10 3PM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  KEXP, 472 1ST AVE N AT SEATTLE CENTER
All four are listed by the station as open to the public in the studio proper, the NW Rooms behind the lobby — you walk in, you stand in the room, it costs nothing and it lasts half an hour.
Wednesday 11am is the interesting one: Altın Gün, the Amsterdam-based band that rebuilds 1970s Anatolian rock — saz, farfisa organ, Turkish folk songs played like a psych band — and who have already made one of the more-watched sessions in the station's archive. Monday 11am is Death Cab for Cutie, the morning after they close Bumbershoot, which is a different band on no sleep. Tuesday 3pm is Haute & Freddy; Thursday 3pm is Shabazz Palaces, the Seattle group who have been the city's strangest and best hip-hop act for fifteen years.
No capacity is published for studio sessions, so treat all four as an early-arrival situation. The 11am ones leave the entire afternoon intact.
A National Book Award novelist and a two-time Pulitzer cartoonist, on working in more than one medium
THU SEP 10 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM, ABOUT 75 MINUTES  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE (SENECA ST ENTRANCE)
New on the calendar. Charles Johnson won the National Book Award for Middle Passage and taught writing at the University of Washington for three decades — and started out as a cartoonist, which is the part nobody mentions; he has just published a collection of cartoons about the Black Power era. David Horsey won two Pulitzer Prizes for editorial cartooning in this city and has just published his first novel. So it is two people who each went the other way, talking about why the career advice to pick one thing is wrong.
Sliding scale from ten dollars, seventy-five minutes, out around a quarter to nine. Town Hall is on First Hill, walkable from downtown light rail.
The night before, same building, same price: Casey Michel on United States of Oligarchy with Steve Scher, Wed Sep 9 at 7:30 upstairs in the small reading room — how a handful of very rich men built political power and found allies in authoritarian governments abroad. And earlier that same evening, Known Wishes, on the facts and myths of medical aid in dying, at 6pm.
Also running, already covered: Bumbershoot takes Seattle Center Saturday and Sunday (from $70.50 a day, under-12s free, re-entry now a paid add-on) with Turnstile closing Saturday and Death Cab closing Sunday — and it lands on the two best-weather days of the week.
