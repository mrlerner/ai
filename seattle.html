Sunset at Olympic Sculpture Park with Ai Weiwei’s twelve zodiac heads, then a reverb-folk show or a grunge-era talk at 7:30
FRI OCT 16  ·  OLYMPIC SCULPTURE PARK, BELLTOWN WATERFRONT, FREE  ·  SOMESURPRISES 7:30PM, $15, ALL AGES, GOOD SHEPHERD CENTER, WALLINGFORD  ·  OR TOWN HALL 7:30, $10–35
Seattle Art Museum’s waterfront sculpture park, facing west over Elliott Bay toward the Olympics, now holds Ai Weiwei’s Circle of Animals/Zodiac Heads: twelve bronze heads, each over 1,500 pounds, recast from the ones looted from a Qing emperor’s fountain in the Second Opium War. The hourly grid has the sky clearing to 12% at 6 and 71° at 5, so be there by 5:45 for the sun going down behind the mountains. Then pick your 7:30: somesurprises and Ilyas Ahmed with Morgan Henderson, hushed folk under heavy reverb and shoegaze fuzz (EverOut’s comparison: Mazzy Star, Slowdive, Broadcast) in the Good Shepherd Center’s old chapel, $15, all ages, next to Low and Songs: Ohia; or Town Hall’s As Many Weirdos As Possible, scene veterans Kelly Canary, Kerri Harrop and Danny Bland telling stories of the 1985–95 Northwest underground, $10–35, a talk, so about 75 minutes, out by 8:45.
Earlier and free: Joe Casalini Trio, happy-hour jazz, Royal Room, Columbia City, 4pm. If you’d rather paddle: a sunset loop off Golden Gardens works too; wind is 5 mph. Indoor fallback isn’t needed today; the rain holds off until after 2am.
SATURDAY · URBANISM, FREE, ALL DAY · 30% CHANCE OF LIGHT SHOWERS BEFORE 5
The Ave for All: one block of University Way closed to cars, last day of the pilot
SAT OCT 17  ·  11AM–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  UNIVERSITY WAY NE BETWEEN NE 42ND AND 43RD, U DISTRICT
New on the brief, and squarely your field: a neighborhood pilot that closes the block of the Ave between 42nd and 43rd to cars and fills it with cafe seating, live music, games and a giant chessboard, a block from the University District Farmers Market. The organizers frame it as a test of whether a people-first street helps the Ave’s businesses (their line: “If it can happen at Pike Place Market, it can happen on The Ave”), and they’re collecting a petition and visitor feedback. October 10 was day one; this is the final day. Worth seeing how the block actually behaves with the cars gone, and who’s running it.
Weather: mostly cloudy, 59° at noon, a 30% chance of light rain before 5 (under a tenth of an inch); it’s a drop-in, so go when it’s dry. Indoor evening: Chris Morgan’s Life in the Wild, the bear ecologist and host of the podcast The Wild, with Home Range Wildlife Research on North Cascades carnivores, REI Seattle, doors 5:30, talk 6–8, $10–100, register first. Star party: the Seattle Astronomical Society’s free Lakewood Playground night (Moon and Saturn), 5:30–11, Seward Park area, but the grid has 45–52% cloud, so it’s a gamble.
SUNDAY · BRANCH-OUT OF THE WEEK · A 2PM START · DRY, MOSTLY SUNNY, 57°
Grupo Falso Baiano, Brazilian choro, at the Royal Room
SUN OCT 18  ·  2–4PM  ·  $20 ADVANCE / $25 AT THE DOOR, ALL AGES  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
A West Coast group twenty years into playing choro, Rio de Janeiro’s turn-of-the-century instrumental music, in which acoustic strings and winds trade fast, intricate melody lines. They’ve worked with clarinetist Anat Cohen and Seattle’s own Jovino Santos Neto. Sounds like an acoustic string band playing chamber jazz; sits next to Hermanos Gutiérrez and the fingerpicked side of Steve Gunn, with the melodic restlessness of bluegrass. Over by 4, home before dark. Tickets through the page’s Stranger box-office link.

WEDNESDAY BEFORE DAWN · NATURE & SKY · ORIONID METEOR PEAK UNDER A 10%-CLOUD SKY
Orionid meteors: set the alarm for 4:30, the moon is down and the sky is clear
WED OCT 21, ABOUT 2:15–5:50AM  ·  UP TO ~20 AN HOUR UNDER DARK SKIES  ·  FREE, ANY DARK SPOT WITH A WIDE SOUTH-EAST VIEW
The Orionids, debris from Halley’s Comet, peak the nights of Oct 21–22 (Star Walk: about 20 an hour at best, fewer from the city). The catch is a 74%-lit waxing moon, which by JPL Horizons sets at about 2:15am Wednesday; astronomical dawn starts about 5:53. Between those, the National Weather Service grid has the overnight Tuesday-into-Wednesday block at 10% cloud. Thursday morning is 78% cloud, so this is the one shot. You’re up early anyway: 4:30 to 5:45, lie back and look high in the south and east, where Orion stands before dawn. A dark park beats the backyard: Magnuson, Discovery or the Golden Gardens beach.
If you’d rather sleep: the Epsilon Geminids peak Sunday at about 3 an hour; not worth an alarm.
