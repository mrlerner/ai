This week in Seattle
The last Lake Union race of the year is tomorrow, and it got the last clear evening of the week
TUE SEP 1 — CHECK-IN BY 6:55PM, FIRST GUN 7:00PM  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION; SHORE VIEWS FROM GAS WORKS PARK
Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night sailing race that has run on Lake Union for fifty-two years — costumed crews, a theme every week, no entry fee and no qualifications beyond owning or crewing a boat. Tomorrow's theme is Committee Re-Appreciation, and per the organisers' own schedule it is the last event on the lake this season: they finish on the first Tuesday of September, and the only thing after it is the Rum Run on September 26, which leaves the lake entirely and sails from Shilshole across the sound to Port Madison.
The starts are staggered by speed — 7:00 for the fast boats, then 7:05, 7:10, 7:15, and 7:20 for dinghies — so the whole fleet is on the water inside twenty minutes and the racing is over well before dark. Afterwards the boats raft up together in the middle of the lake; their permit does not let them stay anchored past 10pm.
Two ways in. From shore, Gas Works Park puts you on the north end looking straight down the course, and it costs nothing. On the water, the spectator fleet is open to anyone who paddles out — kayak rentals are a few hundred metres away at the Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde, and you want to be off the start line, not on it. Conditions cooperate: 74°, mostly sunny, 11% rain, south wind 2–10 mph, and the rain does not arrive until after 11pm. Indoor fallback is not needed tomorrow; it is needed Wednesday, and there are three cards below for that.
Tomorrow the coho column opens at the Ballard Locks — the one number on the board that is still zero
FROM TUE SEP 1  ·  FREE  ·  HIRAM M. CHITTENDEN LOCKS FISH LADDER, 3015 NW 54TH ST, BALLARD
The state counts every salmon that climbs the ladder at the locks, and has done since 1972. The season so far, through the most recent published day of August 25: 31,302 sockeye and 9,566 chinook, with chinook still coming hard — 797 fish on August 24 alone, 388 the next day. Coho are logged at zero, because the count for them does not begin until September 1, and the run builds from early September into October. So tomorrow the third column starts filling, and the underwater viewing windows switch over from the tail of the chinook run to the front of the coho one.
Free, walkable, and it works in any weather — the viewing room is below ground and glassed in, so it is one of the few good things to do here on Wednesday. The other half of the site is the Carl S. English Jr. Botanical Garden, seven acres of lawn above the locks, and the free volunteer concert series there has three dates left: Saturday, Sunday and Labor Day Monday, all at 2pm, symphonic bands and jazz ensembles, no seating provided, bring a chair. Saturday is 69° and 11% rain.
The free night at the natural history museum lands on the one day this week with thunderstorms in the forecast
THU SEP 3, 10AM–8PM, LAST ENTRY 7:30PM  ·  FREE, RESERVE ONLINE  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, 4303 MEMORIAL WAY NE, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
The Burke is Washington's natural history and culture museum, and the building it moved into in 2019 was designed to be turned inside out: the labs, the workrooms and the collections storage are behind glass and visible from the galleries, so on a normal day you are watching paleontologists and archaeologists actually working. Free First Thursday runs the whole day and stays open to 8pm, which is the point — it is the only evening in the month you can do this without paying.
Thursday reads 66° with showers and thunderstorms likely between 11am and 5pm and rain likely before that — a genuinely bad day to be outside and a good one to be under a roof looking at fossils. Reserve the free ticket online rather than turning up; the museum says First Thursdays run heavy. Go late: arriving at 6 gets you a quiet building and still leaves an hour and a half.
A congressman, a constitutional litigator and Seattle's own representative on church, state, and what a citizen can actually do
FRI SEP 4 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall is the city's civic lecture room, a restored 1916 building on First Hill that runs several hundred talks a year on science, politics and books, and prices nearly all of them on a sliding scale so cost is never the reason to skip one. Jared Huffman is a California congressman; Skye Perryman runs a litigation group that sues the federal government; each has written a book on how far Christian nationalism now reaches into the machinery of government. Pramila Jayapal, who represents Seattle, joins them, and the stated back half of the conversation is the practical one — what participation looks like at the level of a person rather than an institution.
Tickets were still open when this was checked this morning. Note the collision: the same evening at 7pm, the Beacon in Columbia City is showing Incubus, the 1966 horror film shot entirely in Esperanto with William Shatner in it, presented by the Seattle Esperanto Society and 74 minutes long. Both are one night only, they overlap, and only one of them will ever happen again.
Bumbershoot takes the whole of Seattle Center for the weekend, and the forecast finally cooperates
SAT–SUN SEP 5–6  ·  DAY PASSES FROM ABOUT $70, WEEKEND FROM ABOUT $125; UNDER-12S FREE WITH AN ADULT  ·  SEATTLE CENTER, LOWER QUEEN ANNE
The city's Labor Day arts and music festival, in its 53rd year, spread across the Seattle Center grounds in themed districts — music on several stages plus comedy, visual art, fashion and food, rather than a single main-stage event. This year's passes allow re-entry and the festival has dropped ticketing fees. Turnstile headlines Saturday; Death Cab for Cutie, from Bellingham, closes Sunday night, with Japanese Breakfast, Blood Orange, Orville Peck and Bikini Kill also on the bill.
This is an all-day outdoor festival and the headliners are late, which is the honest catch — but re-entry means an afternoon shift is a real option, and Saturday is 69°, mostly sunny, 11% rain after a week that was none of those things. Indoor fallback is built into the site itself: the Armory, the Fisher Pavilion and the museums are all inside the grounds. If the price is the objection, the card below is the same band for nothing.
The band that closes the festival on Sunday night plays a free public session at 11 the next morning
MON SEP 7 (LABOR DAY), 11:00–11:30AM  ·  FREE, LISTED AS OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP STUDIO, NORTHWEST ROOMS, SEATTLE CENTER
KEXP is the listener-supported public radio station at Seattle Center, and most of its in-studio sessions are broadcast-only — you hear them on the radio and see the video on the internet weeks later. This one is marked open to the public, which is unusual, and it is Death Cab for Cutie playing a half-hour live set the morning after headlining Bumbershoot. Thirty minutes, free, on a holiday Monday, in a room that holds a fraction of what the festival stage does.
The station does not publish a capacity or an entry procedure for this one, so treat it as first-come and turn up early — the Gathering Space at 472 1st Ave N is the front door for public KEXP events. Worth a phone call to the station on Friday if you want certainty. Same site as Bumbershoot, so it also works as the free coda if you skip the weekend passes.
A three-hour French fever dream, programmed at 5pm so it still ends before bedtime
SUN SEP 6, 5:00PM  ·  THE BEACON, 4405 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon is the small repertory cinema above the Ark Lodge in Columbia City, and it programmes the difficult stuff on purpose. Celine and Julie Go Boating is Jacques Rivette's 1974 film about two women who keep wandering into the same haunted house and eating a sweet that makes them remember it — a comedy, not a horror film, and one of the genuinely strange things in the canon. It runs well over three hours, which is exactly why the 5pm slot is the one to take: it puts you out around 8:15 rather than past midnight.
The rest of the week there is stacked and every screening is a repertory one-off: tonight Last Year at Marienbad at 7:30, Tuesday The Saragossa Manuscript at 7:30, Wednesday Orson Welles's The Immortal Story at 7:30 — sixty minutes, and the correct answer to an 88%-rain Wednesday — and Saturday Tarkovsky's Mirror at 7pm. Sunday also opens with Dodsworth at 2pm if you want the double.
Two Hammond organs, a Rhodes, a Wurlitzer and five players in a room that seats a couple of hundred
SAT SEP 5, 8:00PM — RUNS LATE  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Royal Room is the cheap, serious jazz-and-everything-else room in Columbia City, and its three-night Staycation Music Festival runs Friday to Sunday. Night two is the keyboard night: two Hammond B3 organs, piano, Fender Rhodes, Wurlitzer electric piano and assorted others, played by Joe Doria, Wayne Horvitz, Tim Kennedy, Ryan Burns and drummer D'Vonne Lewis. Friday is a hip-hop night with a Ghetto Children reunion, Sunday is the Ancient Robotz.
Flagging the obvious: an 8pm downbeat means this one runs past 10, so it is a deliberate late night rather than an accidental one. The early alternative at the same venue is the Joe Casalini Trio at 4pm on Friday, jazz, same room, done by dinner. Tickets through Stranger Tickets.
