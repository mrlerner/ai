This Week in Seattle
TONIGHT — THE DRY EVENING BEFORE THE RAIN
George Saunders on Vigil: a ghost story at an oil man’s deathbed
MON SEP 28, 7:30PM  ·  $10–123  ·  BENAROYA HALL, TAPER AUDITORIUM, DOWNTOWN  ·  SEATTLE ARTS & LECTURES
Tonight. In the new novel from the Lincoln in the Bardo author, a spirit who has guided hundreds of people into death arrives at the bedside of an oil executive who regrets nothing. Jeff Martin moderates a question-and-answer session afterward. Seattle Arts & Lectures still lists it this morning at $10–123, and the $10 tier is a real seat. They publish no run time. The drive home stays dry, since rain arrives around 1am.
Closer to home by 8:45: Sonali Kolhatkar with Josefina Mora-Cheung, Breaking ICE, Town Hall 7:30, $10–35 sliding scale. It’s a standard 75-minute Town Hall talk.
TUESDAY — THE ONE WET NIGHT (57% AT 6PM, 94% CLOUD)
Two Cities, One Industry: Big Tech, Power, and Politics in Seattle and San Francisco
TUE SEP 29, 6–8PM  ·  $20, $15 MEMBERS  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
Tomorrow. This is the question you think about in your own work, turned around: not how the cities built tech, but how tech rebuilt their housing, politics and civic life. It’s a live taping of the Blue City Blues and GeekWire podcasts with historian Margaret O’Mara (University of Washington, The Code), GeekWire co-founder John Cook, and Jonathan Weber (City on the Edge). The Nordic Museum still lists it this morning. It starts at 6, so you’re home by 8:15 on the rainiest evening of the week.
Music instead: Lizzie Steiner, a solo harp album release, 7–8:30pm in the Good Shepherd Center chapel, Wallingford. Correction: earlier briefs called this free. Eventbrite shows tickets at $11–28, still on sale. Or salmon: From Awe to Action: The Salmon Run, Town Hall 7:30, $10–35.
WEDNESDAY — THE NICEST WEEKNIGHT (2% RAIN, 25% CLOUD)
Gyetk at the Burke: the Ts’msyen harvest year, recipe by recipe
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, FREE FOR MEMBERS  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON
Spencer and Rachel Greening’s North Coast cookbook follows the harvest calendar: salmon, shellfish and seaweed, with the harvesting law behind each one. It sits where water, nature and food overlap, and the natural-history museum’s early slot gets you out before eight. The page still shows September 30 and $10. Tech-policy alternative: Francine Berman with Sabina Nawaz, Better Tech, Town Hall 7:30, $10–35. It argues that social media, chatbots and search should be treated as critical infrastructure.

SATURDAY, DAYTIME — FREE, ON THE WATER, 0% RAIN
Fishermen’s Fall Festival: the North Pacific fleet comes home
SAT OCT 3, 11AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  FISHERMEN’S TERMINAL, 1900 W NICKERSON ST, INTERBAY
The Seattle Fishermen’s Memorial page still says “October 3, 2026” this morning. The festival marks the Alaska fleet’s return, a mile from Ballard, with seafood, live music, Ship Canal boat tours (listed at 11:45, 1:30 and 4) and the thing worth planning around: survival-suit races at Dock 9, 3pm. Money raised goes to families of fishers lost at sea. Forecast: 0% rain, 48% cloud in the afternoon. Fallback: none needed, and it’s one day only.
