TOMORROW — THE CLEAREST EVENING OF THE WEEK: 2% CLOUD AT MOONRISE HOUR
The last free boat ride on Lake Union until spring, then moonrise over Lake Washington
SUN SEP 27  ·  FREE  ·  SIGN-UP FROM 1PM, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION  ·  MOONRISE ~7:05PM
The afternoon. The Center for Wooden Boats (the South Lake Union boat museum) still lists September 27 as its last public sail date (checked this morning). Volunteer skippers take you out for about 45 minutes, with boats leaving between 1:15 and 5. Sign-up is in person, first come, from 1pm outside the Wagner Education Center, and your whole party has to be there. They take 80 to 200 people. Arrive by 12:45 on a day this nice. Cloud is 39% at 1pm and 5% from 2pm, so the later boats get the best of it.
The evening. Sunset is 6:58. The moon, 97.5% lit and a day past full, rises at about 7:05 in the east-northeast (azimuth 71°). Those figures are from Thursday's Jet Propulsion Laboratory ephemeris run; the moon's position doesn't change with the forecast. The hourly forecast is 2% cloud from 5 to 7pm and 11% at 8, the best sky of the week. Watch from an east-facing Lake Washington shore (Seward Park, Magnuson, Madrona), get there by 6:50, and you'll be home well before nine. If plans change: Monday evening is 52% cloud, so there's no good second chance.
TODAY — SMALL-ROOM MUSIC, $5, OUT BY FOUR
International Women in Jazz: Kobe's jazz queen makes her Seattle debut
SAT SEP 26, 2–4PM (DOORS 1)  ·  $5, RESERVATION REQUIRED  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
An afternoon set at the Royal Room, Columbia City's cheap jazz-and-experimental club, presented by the Seattle-Kobe Sister City Association. The headliner is pianist Kumiko Hisano, winner of Kobe's Jazz Queen competition, playing in Seattle for the first time. It's still on the Royal Room calendar this morning. Reserve on the event page. You'll be out at four, with daylight left and the festivals still open.
Or, today's food pick: the Northwest Tea Festival (Seattle Center Exhibition Hall, 10–6, $25 two-day pass with a porcelain cup, more than 50 vendors), with the free Festa Italiana next door (10–7, with cooking demonstrations and a grape stomp). With the afternoon now sunnier, the Seattle Center lawns are worth the time between the two.
MONDAY — NEW TO THIS BRIEF; SEATTLE ARTS & LECTURES' SEASON
George Saunders on Vigil, his new novel
MON SEP 28, 7:30PM (DOORS ~6:30)  ·  $10–123  ·  BENAROYA HALL, TAPER AUDITORIUM, DOWNTOWN
The Booker winner (Lincoln in the Bardo) on his new novel, set at the deathbed of an oil company CEO. His guide into the afterlife is a spirit who has comforted 343 dying people, and he's the first one with no regrets. It's a book about climate and conscience told as a ghost story, followed by a question-and-answer session with Jeff Martin. Seattle Arts & Lectures publishes no run time; a 7:30 conversation plus questions usually ends near nine, but that's a guess, not a listed time. Tickets with the book have it shipped by Third Place Books. Streaming tickets are also sold. Monday is dry (1%).
Same hour, Town Hall: Sonali Kolhatkar, Breaking ICE, on community resistance to immigration raids, $10–35 sliding, out by about 8:45.
TUESDAY — THE WET NIGHT (64%); PICK ONE OF THREE INDOOR EVENINGS
Two Cities, One Industry: what tech money did to Seattle's and San Francisco's civic life
TUE SEP 29, 6–8PM  ·  $20, $15 MEMBERS  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
Still the week's closest match to your civic-tech work. It's a live taping of two podcasts, Blue City Blues and GeekWire's, in your own neighborhood. University of Washington historian Margaret O'Mara (The Code), GeekWire co-founder John Cook and Jonathan Weber (City on the Edge) discuss housing, politics and who the cities now work for. You're home by 8:15.
Free music instead: Lizzie Steiner, a solo harp album release (Reluctant Princess, arrangements that lean toward R&B and soul), 7–8:30pm, Good Shepherd Center chapel in Wallingford. Reserve free on Eventbrite. Or salmon: Town Hall's From Awe to Action: The Salmon Run, 7:30, $10–35, with conservation photographer Amy Gulick's Alaska stories alongside Save Our Wild Salmon and the Columbia–Snake River campaign.
WEDNESDAY — NATURE AND FOOD, $10
Gyetk: the harvest year of the Ts'msyen coast, told through its kitchen
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, FREE FOR MEMBERS  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
Spencer Greening (Gitga'at anthropologist of Indigenous harvesting law) and Rachel Greening (a dietitian working on Indigenous food sovereignty) wrote 60 recipes arranged around the seasonal round on British Columbia's North Coast: salmon, shellfish, seaweed, and the ecological knowledge behind each one. It's an early evening at the Burke, and Wednesday's rain chance is only 15%. Later, if you'd rather talk tech policy: Francine Berman, Better Tech, Town Hall 7:30, $10–35, on regulating social media, chatbots and search like roads and power grids.

Two Town Hall housing nights: the policy, then the neighbors
THU OCT 1, 7:30PM, FREE ($0–35 SLIDING)  ·  SAT OCT 3, 7:30PM, $10–35  ·  WYNCOTE FORUM, TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
