Aurora watch, now Sunday: the clearest night of the week
SUN OCT 11  ·  SUNSET 6:30, FULL DARK 8:11  ·  KP 5.33 5–8PM, 5.67 (MODERATE) 8–11PM, 5.33 11PM–2AM  ·  1–2% CLOUD
NOAA’s Space Weather Prediction Center rewrote its forecast at 5:30 this morning. The several solar eruptions it is tracking now arrive later: Saturday evening drops to Kp 2.3–3 (nothing to see), and the storm lands on Sunday evening, Pacific time: Kp 5.33 from 5 to 8pm, 5.67 from 8 to 11pm, 5.33 until 2am. At Seattle’s latitude that means aurora is possible low on the northern horizon from a dark spot. And Sunday is the sky to do it under: the National Weather Service hourly grid has 2% cloud from 8 to 10pm, and the moon is a 2%-lit sliver that sets about 6:25, right behind the sun. The honest caveat: this is the third time this week the arrival has moved (Thursday’s never came; Saturday’s slid a day), and the past 24 hours peaked at Kp 3.
The plan: at sunset (6:30) look low in the west-southwest for the day-old crescent moon, a nice sight on its own. Then check the 30-minute aurora forecast around 8. If it is lit, Carkeek Park or the north end of Golden Gardens facing north-northwest, 3-second phone exposures every few minutes until 9; the camera sees it first. 52° at 8pm, then 50°. A school night, but the 8–9pm hour is the strongest stretch you can catch before bed. Tonight instead: plain stars at the new moon, 7–11% cloud. The Seattle Astronomical Society’s free public star party at Snoqualmie Point Park, North Bend, is still listed for 6 to 11:30pm.
