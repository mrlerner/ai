NEW · TONIGHT · DOWNTOWN DOUBLE · FREE · CLEAR, 6–10% CLOUD
Día de Muertos procession at Pike Place Market
FRI OCT 23  ·  MARKET EVENT NOON–9, PROCESSION 6:30–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  PIKE PLACE MARKET HISTORIC DISTRICT
The finale of the Market’s after-hours Night Shift series: community altars (you can add a photo of someone you’ve lost, pets included), mariachi, folklórico dancers, and at 6:30 a procession of dozens of costumed Catrinas, dancing devils and caballos bailadores (dancing-horse costumes) looping the historic district and coming down Pike Place. Lucha libre wrestling in Pike Plaza 3–5pm if you’re downtown early. The route differs between listings; the Market says its own notice wins.
Start at 5, ten minutes’ walk uphill: Lantern Fest in Freeway Park, 5–7:30pm, free — the park built over Interstate 5 closes its 50th season with lanterns, live music and free food (Upper Lawns, 6th and Seneca). Lanterns at dusk (sunset about 6:08), walk down Seneca to the Market for the 6:30 procession, home by 8. Cold after dark, down toward 42°; bring a layer. Look east as you leave: the nearly full moon with Saturn just below it.
NEW · SATURDAY · NATURE · 10AM–2PM · FREE · LAST WEEKEND
Cedar River Salmon Journey: spawning salmon with Aquarium naturalists
SAT OCT 24  ·  10AM–2PM  ·  FREE, DROP IN  ·  RIVERVIEW PARK AND BELMONDO REACH, CEDAR RIVER, RENTON (SUN: CEDAR GRANGE, 11–2)
The Seattle Aquarium posts naturalists along the Cedar River each October weekend to point out spawning salmon in the shallows and explain the late-run changes — hooked jaws, color shifts, redds being dug. This is the series’ last Saturday. Weather: grey but dry, 56°, 11% rain chance until late night — good river-watching light, no glare on the water. Polarized glasses help. Indoor fallback: the National Nordic Museum’s A Ballard Story: Pacific Fishermen, the history of the Salmon Bay shipyard, $20.
SATURDAY · IN YOUR LANE · 7:30–10PM · $28–31
Bedouine at Ballard Homestead
SAT OCT 24  ·  7:30–10PM  ·  $28–31  ·  ALL AGES  ·  BALLARD HOMESTEAD, 6541 JONES AVE NW, BALLARD
Tomorrow night: the Syrian American songwriter’s quiet voice over fingerpicked nylon-string guitar and soft strings, in a converted church with good acoustics. If you like Aldous Harding or Iron & Wine, this is that room. A late one (runs to 10); the first hour is the opener, Other Delights, so arriving at 8:30 still gets you her whole set.
The early alternative: Myra Melford’s Splash, an exploratory jazz piano trio and one of the last Earshot Jazz Festival shows, 5pm at the Royal Room, Columbia City, $44 — home by 7. The festival closes Sunday.
SUNDAY · BRANCH-OUT OF THE WEEK · 7:30PM · $25 ADVANCE
Newberry & Verch, old-time banjo and fiddle
SUN OCT 25  ·  DOORS 6:30, SHOW 7:30  ·  $25 ADVANCE ONLINE / $30 DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Joe Newberry (Missouri Ozarks banjo and guitar) and April Verch (Ontario fiddle and step dancing) playing ballads and dance tunes in two-part harmony, co-presented by the Seattle Folklore Society. Five straight nights of minus tides
MON OCT 26 11:50PM −2.2 FT  ·  WED 12:31AM −2.8  ·  THU 1:16AM −3.0  ·  FRI 2:05AM −2.7  ·  SAT OCT 31 2:59AM −2.0  ·  NOAA SEATTLE STATION
This morning’s run extends the series to a fifth night: −2.0 ft before dawn on Halloween. The deepest is −3.0 ft early Thursday. All of them land around or after midnight, so they’re headlamp outings, not evening ones. Monday’s 11:50pm low is the earliest, and Sunday’s −1.1 at 11:13pm is a milder warm-up. Monday night is about half cloud, with the moon near full lighting the beach. Where: Constellation Park on Alki or the Lincoln Park beach; boots, a friend, and watch where you step for sea stars and anemones.
MONDAY · IDEAS · 6:30PM · $10
Pamela Ronald: what feeds us in a hotter world
MON OCT 26  ·  6:30PM, OUT ABOUT 7:45  ·  $10  ·  TOWN HALL, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL (ALSO LIVESTREAMED)  ·  UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON PUBLIC LECTURES
The plant geneticist from the University of California, Davis isolated the gene behind rice that survives two weeks underwater. Millions of farmers now grow it. Her talk covers what plant science can and can’t do as floods and heat hit crops. It’s the main university lecture of the week, and you’ll be home well before 9.
