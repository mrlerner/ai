This week in Seattle
Four nights out, and the forecast still says you get to see the eclipse
THU AUG 27, FROM 7:30PM  ·  FREE, NO REGISTRATION  ·  LAKEWOOD PLAYGROUND, SEWARD PARK, LAKE WASHINGTON SHORE
Thursday brings a deep partial lunar eclipse, and Seattle's problem with it is not clouds but altitude — the moon comes up around 7:50pm already inside the Earth's shadow and stays only a few degrees above the horizon through the best of it, so any treeline or ridge to the east eats the whole show. The Seattle Astronomical Society, the local amateur-astronomy club, has solved that for you with a free public star party on the Seward Park shoreline, where there is open water under an eastern horizon and nothing in the way of the moonrise. Members bring their own telescopes and pass them around. Maximum is 9:12pm, with about 96 percent of the disk covered — short of total, but deep enough that most of the face should go copper with one bright rind left along the edge.
The weather call has now held for three straight mornings: partly cloudy, low 55°, after a 72° day at a 4 percent rain chance. Bedtime works out for once — because the moon rises already eclipsed, the deepest part is early and it is well into shadow before nine. Nothing to buy and nothing to wear on your eyes; a lunar eclipse is safe to look at directly. Seward Park is a twenty-minute drive from Ballard; leave by seven to be set up before moonrise.

Last day of the free one, and it got the good weather half of the weekend
TODAY, 1–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  SEATTLE CENTER — ARMORY, MURAL AMPHITHEATRE, FISHER PAVILION
Festival Sundiata presents Black Arts Fest, one of the free cultural festivals Seattle Center runs all summer under the Festál banner, closes today across the Armory food hall, the outdoor Mural Amphitheatre and Fisher Pavilion. The bill runs from Morris Day and The Time — the Minneapolis funk band out of the same early-eighties scene as Prince — through Zimbabwean-American rapper Draze, the Pacific Northwest Community Gospel Choir and the Double Dutch Divas, with a Taste of Sundiata food hall, market vendors and an art sale alongside. New this year is Skate Sundiata, which turns Fisher Pavilion into a roller rink with a live DJ, open to any level.
Weather: 74° and mostly sunny at a 7 percent rain chance — the cleaner of the festival's two days, after yesterday's showers. It costs nothing, it happens in daylight, and it is done by eight, so none of it argues with a nine o'clock bedtime. Indoor fallback is built in: the Armory hall and Fisher Pavilion are both under a roof. If you would rather stay north, the Ballard Farmers Market runs Sunday on Ballard Avenue.

Tuesday just went up to eighty-four — and it is still the night Lake Union fills with costumed sailboats
TUE AUG 25, FIRST START 7PM  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION — GAS WORKS PARK, OR ON THE WATER
The Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night sailboat race that has gone around Lake Union every summer for fifty-two years: staggered starts by boat speed beginning at 7pm, costumed crews, and a fleet that is obviously out there for the evening rather than the trophy. The season runs May to the end of September, so there are five Tuesdays left. Tuesday's forecast has now climbed to 84° and sunny — four degrees hotter than it read yesterday, the highest number anywhere in the file, and the single best outdoor evening of the next week by a wide margin.
Two free ways in: the hill on the north shore at Gas Works Park looks straight down the course, or put a boat in and watch from inside the spectator fleet — the Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde both rent on the lake, a short paddle from the start. Racing is done well before dark. On an 84° evening the water is the correct place to be; the fallback if you would rather not drive is Wednesday at 76°, which is the same idea minus the sailboats. Note Monday's patchy smoke after 3pm — check air quality before committing to a long paddle that afternoon.
An illustrated field guide to the Northwest year, launched by the artist who drew it
WED AUG 26, 7:30PM (DOORS 6:30)  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall Seattle — the First Hill talks venue that carries most of the city's science and civic programming — opens its season with the launch of Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest. Emily Poole wrote and illustrated it as a walk through the region's wildlife season by season, essays paired with her own drawings. She is joined by Kate Rogers, editor-in-chief of Mountaineers Books, the Seattle outdoors publisher behind it, and Katie Campbell, digital editor and reporter at the public radio station KUOW. The evening doubles as Town Hall's preview of what it has booked for the coming season.
It runs about seventy-five minutes, so 7:30pm means out the door by a quarter to nine — workable. Sliding scale from $10 means you set your own price, and doors at 6:30 give you time to eat first on First Hill. This is the week's clean overlap of the two things worth showing up for: a lecture and a nature book, in the same room. Entrance is off Seneca Street.

A six o'clock jazz show in Columbia City, which almost never happens
FRI AUG 28, 6PM (AND THU AUG 27, SAME TIME)  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Jazz Night School is the Seattle nonprofit that teaches adults to play in combos and big bands, and twice a year it moves its students onto a real stage for end-of-term concerts — big band, small ensembles, Latin groups and vocalists across the evening. The Royal Room is the Columbia City room built for exactly this: low ceiling, low cover, no pretension, a piano in the corner. Two nights this week, Thursday and Friday, both starting at 6pm — which is the whole argument for it. Almost nothing else in this city puts live jazz in front of you early enough to be home by nine.
Take the Friday one: Thursday at six collides head-on with the eclipse, and the eclipse does not reschedule. The Royal Room does not post a cover for these — the school's term concerts are typically free or a small donation at the door — so treat it as the cheap option and check at the door. It is a full kitchen, so dinner and the music are the same trip. Rainier Avenue, two blocks from the Columbia City light rail station.

Next Sunday is the one Sunday a month they take strangers out on Lake Union for nothing
SUN AUG 30  ·  SIGN-UPS IN PERSON FROM 1PM, BOATS OUT 10:30AM–3PM  ·  FREE  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The Center for Wooden Boats is the working maritime museum at the south end of Lake Union — a dock full of restored wooden hulls you are allowed to touch. On the last Sunday of each month from April through October it runs a free public sail: volunteers crew, you ride, about an hour on the water, no money and no membership. What is tied up that day decides what you get — sprit boats, a steamboat, electric launches, schooners, ketches and yawls all rotate through. Next Sunday, August 30, is this month's.
Two things to know: it is first-come, in person, day-of — there is no reservation, sign-ups start at 1pm in front of the Wagner Education Center, and arriving early is how you get the boat you want. And they sail rain or shine, but high wind cancels. The seven-day forecast does not reach next Sunday yet; this morning's file ends dry and sunny on Saturday at 71°, which is at least a decent lean. If it blows out, the docks, the boatshop and the museum are open anyway and still free.

The repertory house down the street from the jazz has a French horror classic and the most famous puzzle in cinema, back to back
FRI AUG 28, 4PM & 10PM; SAT AUG 29, 7PM  ·  THE BEACON, 4405 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon is the small repertory cinema above the Columbia City bakery, which programs old films the way a record store recommends records. Friday is Georges Franju's Eyes Without a Face from 1960 — the quiet, beautiful French horror film about a surgeon stealing faces for his disfigured daughter, and the one everybody since has borrowed from. Saturday at 7pm is Alain Resnais's Last Year at Marienbad from 1961, the celebrated argument-starter in which a man insists to a woman that they met last year and the film refuses, for ninety-four minutes, to say who is right.
Saturday's 7pm is the one that fits the bedtime — ninety-four minutes puts you out around 8:45. Friday's options are a 4pm and a 10pm, so the early show means leaving work early. The Beacon sells single tickets and runs a membership; the calendar page carries the current price. Both are indoor, which makes them the standing fallback for any night this week that turns.
