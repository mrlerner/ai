Nature & water
The coho switched on Thursday: 463 fish in one day, after 13 on Tuesday
ANY DAY — FISH LADDER VIEWING ROOM OPEN 7AM–8:45PM DAILY  ·  FREE  ·  HIRAM M. CHITTENDEN LOCKS, 3015 NW 54TH ST, BALLARD
This is the number the brief has been waiting for. The Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife counts every salmon that enters fresh water at the locks, and the coho line had been essentially flat all summer — 420 fish across the whole of June, July and August, then 13 on Tuesday Sep 1 and 76 on Wednesday. On Thursday Sep 3 it jumped to 463 in a single day, nearly half the season's running total of 972 in one twenty-four-hour period. That is the run arriving, and it is the reason to walk down the stairs into the underground viewing room this week rather than next month.
The viewing room is a concrete gallery under the fish ladder with glass windows straight into the rushing water — the fish are a foot from your face, holding against the current. It costs nothing, it is open 7am to 8:45pm every day, and it is a twenty-minute walk from the Ballard farmers market on Sunday morning. Sunday is 71° and mostly sunny; Wednesday is 74° and cloudless, and the grounds and the botanical garden alongside are worth the trip on either. There is no bad-weather problem here at all — the viewing room is indoors and underground, so a wet Thursday or Friday works exactly as well.
One honest caveat: state counters still put the true peak in the last two weeks of September, and a daily count is fish passing the counting window, not fish visible in the ladder at the moment you arrive — low tides and warm afternoons both thin out what you see. Go in the morning or the evening rather than mid-afternoon. Counts update daily at the Fish and Wildlife page if you want to check before driving over.
Nature & sky
A foot below zero on Tuesday morning, and the darkest sky of the month on Friday
TUE SEP 8, 9:12AM  ·  WED–THU SEP 9–10 AFTER MIDNIGHT  ·  FRI SEP 11  ·  FREE  ·  PUGET SOUND BEACHES; DARK SKY EAST OF THE CITY
The tide. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration gauge at Seattle drops to −1.0 ft at 9:12am on Tuesday Sep 8 — the only low in the eight-day table flagged as notable, and the bottom of this run. Monday morning is close behind at −0.9 ft at 8:12am, and Wednesday holds −0.9 ft at 10:04am. Tuesday is the pick on all three counts: lowest water, a civilised hour, and 71° and mostly sunny overhead. A foot under zero uncovers a wide walkable flat — sea stars, anemones, moon snails, the sand-dollar beds off Constellation Park in West Seattle, or the same idea at Golden Gardens in Ballard. You are back before ten.
The sky. Friday Sep 11 is the new moon, which makes this the darkest week of the month, and the timing is unusually kind: the September epsilon Perseids peak the nights of Sep 9 and 10, two days before the moon is out of the way entirely. Set expectations honestly — this is a modest shower at roughly eight meteors an hour at its best, from a radiant that only climbs properly after midnight. It is a reason to look up if you are already outside, not a reason to drive to a mountain. The reason to drive is Friday and Saturday.
If you want a telescope pointed for you, the Seattle Astronomical Society runs free public stargazes with member scopes: tonight at 8pm at Paramount Park in Shoreline (close in, and light-polluted — planets rather than deep sky), then the good ones on the new moon: Duvall, Friday Sep 11 at 7:30pm and Snoqualmie Point Park, Saturday Sep 12 at 7:30pm. Two other notes: the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalists finished the 2026 season in mid-July, so the Tuesday tidepool is a do-it-yourself trip; and aurora is off the table, with the space-weather forecast peaking at Kp 3.3, far short of this latitude.
The reporter who follows the money on kleptocracy, and where the United States sits in it
WED SEP 9 — 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  THE MEHDI READING ROOM AT TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall Seattle is the century-old civic hall on First Hill that runs most of the city's serious talks. Casey Michel is the investigative journalist who spent a decade tracking how foreign money is laundered through American shell companies, real estate and law firms; his new book United States of Oligarchy argues the machinery is now domestic. He is in conversation with Steve Scher, for thirty years the interviewer on public radio here.
Town Hall's format is the right length for an early bedtime: doors 6:30, on stage 7:30, about 75 minutes, so you are out around a quarter to nine. Sliding-scale tickets start at $10 and you choose your own number. The Mehdi Reading Room is the smaller upstairs room — a hundred-odd seats, closer to a seminar than a lecture.
Note that Wednesday is also the one cloudless 74° day of the week — if that belongs outdoors, the same room and price on Thursday has Charles Johnson and David Horsey at 7:30, the novelist who won the National Book Award for Middle Passage and taught at the University of Washington for thirty years, with the two-time Pulitzer-winning editorial cartoonist. Thursday is forecast at 15% rain, which settles it neatly.

A two o'clock rock show with chairs: Eilen Jewell plays a Saturday matinee at the Tractor
SAT SEP 12 — 2PM, PARTIALLY SEATED  ·  $44.65  ·  21+  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, 5213 BALLARD AVE NW
The Tractor Tavern is the century-old roots room on Ballard Avenue, and its shows almost always start at eight or half past — which is why this one is worth flagging. Eilen Jewell, the Boise songwriter whose band plays a spare, reverby mix of country, blues and early rock, has sold the Friday night so well that the promoter added a second show at two o'clock on Saturday afternoon, partially seated. That is a genuinely unusual thing in this town: a real gig, in the good room, that is over by late afternoon.
$44.65 all in, 21 and over, presented by the community radio station KBCS out of Bellevue. Buy through the Get Tickets button on the Tractor's calendar. The Friday Sep 11 show at 8:30pm is the same band at the usual hour if the afternoon does not suit — but that one runs past eleven, and Friday is the wet day anyway.
Cheaper and earlier this week in the same building: the Tractor square dance on Labor Day Monday, 6:30pm, $12.42, with the band Three Forks of Candy and Alex MacLeod calling the figures — no experience assumed, the caller teaches each dance. It is the earliest downbeat on the Tractor's September calendar.
Four free in-studio sessions, Monday through Thursday, twenty feet from the band
MON 11AM  ·  TUE 3PM  ·  WED 11AM  ·  THU 3PM  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP STUDIO, 472 1ST AVE N, SEATTLE CENTER
The public radio station KEXP records bands in a glass-walled studio at Seattle Center and lets anyone stand in the room while it happens, at no charge and with no ticket. This week, all four sessions are marked open to the public: Death Cab for Cutie on Labor Day Monday at 11am, the Bellingham band that has been the definitive Northwest indie group for twenty-five years; Haute & Freddy Tuesday at 3pm; Altın Gün Wednesday at 11am, the Amsterdam group that plays Turkish Anatolian folk as heavy psychedelic rock; and Shabazz Palaces Thursday at 3pm, the Seattle experimental hip-hop project on Sub Pop.
Each session is thirty minutes, listed as broadcast-only with video posted later. The catch is the hour — these are working-day slots. Monday is the holiday, which makes the Death Cab session the one that costs nothing to attend in any sense. No capacity figure is published and there is no list, so arriving early is the entire strategy.
Three hundred wooden boats you can climb aboard, six days out — buy the ticket now
FRI–SUN SEP 11–13 — FRI/SAT 9AM–6PM, SUN 9AM–4PM  ·  $30 ONE DAY / $55 ALL THREE  ·  NORTHWEST MARITIME AND POINT HUDSON MARINA, PORT TOWNSEND
This is the forty-ninth Port Townsend Wooden Boat Festival, the largest event of its kind in North America and the single best on-the-water day of the Northwest year: about 300 boats on land and in the water, most of which you can walk onto and talk to the owner about, plus more than 100 presentations — boatbuilding, navigation, rigging, small-craft seamanship — running all three days on the docks at Point Hudson.
$30 for one day or $55 for all three; $25 and $50 for seniors 65 and over, students and active military; children 12 and under free with a paying adult. It is entirely outdoors on docks and gravel, so weather matters, and the forecast only reaches the Friday: 69° with a 25% chance of light rain, the wettest day in the file. Saturday and Sunday are past the seven-day horizon — check again mid-week, and if you can only do one day, hold out for the weekend.
Getting there is the part to plan: Port Townsend is either the Bainbridge or Edmonds ferry and then an hour's drive, or the long way round through Tacoma. Festival parking fills early on the Saturday. Live music runs on the main stage until 11:30pm Friday and Saturday, which is well past bedtime and easy to skip — the boats are a daylight event.

TUE SEP 8 7:30PM  ·  WED 4:30PM  ·  THU 7:30PM  ·  FRI 4PM & 7PM  ·  TICKETED  ·  THE BEACON, 4405 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon is the small repertory cinema above a bakery in Columbia City, and it is the reliable answer whenever the forecast turns. Two programmes overlap this week. Andrei Tarkovsky's Mirror (1975, 107 minutes) — the film assembled from a childhood, poems read aloud by the director's father, and archive newsreel, in no fixed order — plays Tuesday 7:30, Wednesday 4:30 and Thursday 7:30. Then Friday, the wet day, opens a run under the title Movies Without a Face: Hiroshi Teshigahara's The Face of Another (1966), in which a disfigured engineer is given a lifelike mask and finds it comes with a different person attached, at 4pm (and again at 10pm), with the science-fiction film Invasion at 7pm between them. The 4pm show puts you home before dinner; the 7pm is out by around nine.
Same room, same week: Bertolucci's The Spider's Stratagem Wednesday 7:30 and Thursday 4:30, and Five Minutes to Live!, the 1961 bank-robbery picture starring Johnny Cash, on Labor Day Monday at 7:30. Full listings and showtimes on the Beacon's calendar.
