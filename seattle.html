This week in Seattle
Today: the block party is open on the Lake Union lawn until 7pm
TODAY & SUN AUG 16, 10AM–7PM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  LAKE UNION PARK, SOUTH LAKE UNION
This is the one thing on the list with a deadline attached — it ends tomorrow at 7pm and doesn't come back for a year. A street fair on the Lake Union shoreline run by the Seattle chapter of the American Institute of Architects, built around walk-in installations from design teams responding to this year's theme, IN[FLUX]. Practical advice for a same-day decision: the installations reward an unhurried lap and are the reason to come with no plan at all; the mainstage of short talks is the closest thing the city has to a free urbanism conference and rewards checking the running order first; the hands-on workshops need you standing in a specific tent at a specific hour, so they're the one part worth scheduling. Today is the better of the two days on paper — partly sunny, 79° — while Sunday goes mostly cloudy at the same temperature, which for a shadeless lawn full of tents is arguably the more comfortable version. Lake Union Park is the north end of the streetcar line and a walk from Westlake; the Center for Wooden Boats shares the same lawn if you want to end on the water.

The crescent moon meets Venus tonight — but the clear sky arrives a night late
TONIGHT AT DUSK, LOW IN THE WEST  ·  FREE  ·  MOSTLY CLOUDY TONIGHT, CLEARING SUN & MON
Tonight is the date the almanacs give: a thin crescent moon, three days past new, sitting beside Venus low in the west after sunset. The catch is local — the National Weather Service has tonight mostly cloudy with a low of 60°, while Sunday and Monday nights both go partly cloudy. The pairing is worth chasing anyway, because it degrades gracefully: Venus is the brightest object in the evening sky all month and the moon climbs a little higher and fattens slightly each night, so Sunday and Monday give you the same two objects, further apart, under a better sky. No equipment, no dark-sky drive and no alarm clock — just a flat western horizon, which means Golden Gardens, Alki, or the Ballard bank of the ship canal. Worth saying plainly what is not happening, so you don't go looking: the tide table's deepest low for the next eight days is only +0.4 feet, nowhere near tidepooling range, and the space weather forecast peaks at a planetary index of 3, which means no aurora at this latitude. This is the sky event this week.

Freeway Park at fifty — and where the case for lidding the interstate goes next
TUE AUG 18, DOORS 5PM, PANEL 6–8PM  ·  FREE, REGISTRATION REQUIRED  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, FIRST HILL
The week's strongest weekday-evening pick, and the one where the people on stage can actually move the thing they're discussing: Rebecca Bear of the Seattle Parks Foundation moderating, Lara Rose — the landscape architect running the current improvement project inside the park — Riisa Conklin of the Freeway Park Association, and state senator Jamie Pedersen, majority leader in the Washington State Senate. Subject is the half-century-old park built on a concrete deck over Interstate 5: how the brutalist original has aged, what its landmark designation permits and forbids, what the capital work now underway is repairing, and how all of that feeds the far bigger Lid Interstate 5 proposal. If the appeal of urbanism for you is legislative sessions and capital budgets rather than renderings, this is the rare public event that operates at that altitude. Free with registration, doors an hour early for a social hour with light food and a cash bar, and recorded for later if Tuesday doesn't work.

Elaine May's Mikey and Nicky alternates with a Korean cult favorite, Monday through Thursday
MON AUG 17 – THU AUG 20, 4:30PM & 7:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon — the small repertory house above the Ark Lodge block in Columbia City — spends four straight nights swapping between two films, which is the whole argument for going on a weeknight rather than saving it. Mikey and Nicky is Elaine May's 1976 film about two low-level mob friends across one long paranoid night, shot with the actors improvising and the camera struggling to keep up; it was buried on release and is now the one people cite when they argue May was the best American director of that decade. Against it, Take Care of My Cat, Jeong Jae-eun's 2001 debut about five young women in Incheon drifting apart after school ends — quiet, unsentimental, and long hard to see outside a festival. May's film plays Monday at 7:30 and Wednesday at 7:30; the Korean film gets Tuesday and Thursday at 7:30, with matinees at 4:30 on the alternate days. Monday and Tuesday evenings are dry and in the high 50s, so nothing outdoors is being sacrificed after dark.

Dale Watson brings the Lone Stars to Ballard on a Wednesday
WED AUG 19, 7:30PM  ·  $31  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD AVENUE
The Texas honky-tonk singer who has spent thirty years refusing to make a Nashville record — a baritone, a pompadour and a band that plays shuffles and truck-driving songs straight, without the wink that usually comes attached when a young crowd shows up. He calls it Ameripolitan rather than country, which started as a joke about radio formats and turned into an awards show he now runs. The Tractor is the right size for it: a long room on Ballard Avenue with a wooden floor and enough space that people actually dance to this, which is most of the point. Doors are early enough to eat on the same block first. Disc jockey Marvelette spins records between sets. $31 in advance and Wednesday's forecast is dry and 75°, so the walk from anywhere in Ballard is pleasant.

Jessica Lurie and friends — the cheap jazz night in Columbia City
THU AUG 20, 7:30PM  ·  LOW COVER, PAY AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
The saxophonist and composer Jessica Lurie came out of the Seattle scene that produced the Billy Tipton Memorial Saxophone Quartet, went to New York, and has spent the years since somewhere between free jazz, Balkan brass and rock — the sort of player whose "and friends" billing is a promise rather than filler, because the lineup is assembled for the night. The Royal Room is the reason to trust an unnamed bill: a restaurant and music room on Rainier Avenue co-founded by the pianist Wayne Horvitz, where the booking runs from straight-ahead jazz to genuinely strange, the cover stays in single or low double digits, and you can eat at a table while it happens. Worth knowing for the rest of the week: the room is closed Tuesday for maintenance and closed to the public Saturday and Sunday for a private event, so Thursday is the pick. The Beacon is four blocks away if you want a double feature of a night.

Silkworm with Nina Nastasia — the Friday show that will sell out first
FRI AUG 21, 8PM  ·  $25  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD AVENUE
Silkworm are a Missoula-by-way-of-Seattle band from the early nineties whose records — dry, wordy, guitar-driven, recorded mostly with Steve Albini — have the kind of devoted following that turns any appearance into an event; they stopped playing after their drummer Michael Dahlquist was killed in 2005, which is why a billed date carries the weight it does. Nina Nastasia opens: a New York songwriter with a stark, unadorned voice, also an Albini regular, who returned to touring after more than a decade away. This is the sort of bill where the room matters more than the ticket price — the Tractor holds a few hundred and both acts have audiences who will travel. $25, and it's the pick to buy now rather than to think about, since Friday is the last night of the week and the only one competing with a Mariners home game.

Tuesday on the water: Duck Dodge, on the best evening of the week
TUE AUG 18, EVENING  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION  ·  MOSTLY SUNNY 77°, WIND 3–7 MPH
The Tuesday-night sailboat race on Lake Union has run every summer week since 1974 — a costumed, deliberately unserious handicap race in four fleets, from fast boats down to dinghies, and the only Seattle sporting event where the spectator fleet is part of the course. Two ways in: watch from the Gas Works Park hill, which is free and needs no planning, or put a kayak in and sit at the edge of the action, which is the better version of the same evening. Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde both rent on the lake and both want you off the water before dark, so an early launch is the move. Tuesday reads as the week's best evening on the water — mostly sunny, 77°, and 3 to 7 mph of southwest wind, which is enough to move a fleet without making a rental kayak hard work. It's the same evening as the Freeway Park panel and both start early, so this is a choose-one. Check the club's schedule page before heading over.
