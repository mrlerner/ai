This week in Seattle
The costumed sailboat race is tonight, and tonight is the last dry evening on the board
TUE SEP 1 — FIRST GUN 7:00PM, FLEET AWAY BY 7:20, RACING DONE AROUND 8  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION; SHORE VIEWS FROM GAS WORKS PARK
Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night race that has run on Lake Union every summer for fifty-two years — four heats sorted by boat speed, a costume theme every week, gold, silver and bronze duck stickers for the winners and an honorary black duck for the best-dressed boat. No entry fee, no qualification beyond being on a boat. Afterwards the fleet rafts up in the middle of the lake to grill and swap crews, and their permit ends that at 10pm.
One correction to last week's brief: it called tonight the season finale, and that came from a race schedule the organisers have not actually updated since 2023. The Washington Yacht Club, which supplies a good share of the boats, states the series simply runs Tuesdays May through September — so there may well be more. What is not in doubt is the weather: tonight is 73°, partly sunny, 3% rain, south-southwest wind 3 to 10 mph, and the next Tuesday evening on the calendar comes after two days of rain.
Two ways in, both free. From shore, Gas Works Park sits at the top of the course and you can see the whole lake from the hill. On the water, the spectator fleet takes anyone who paddles out — kayak rentals at the Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde are minutes from the start — and the etiquette is to stay off the line. Home well before dark; sunset is 7:50.
A radio host plays a live set inside the laser dome on the wettest night of the week, and you are out by 8:30
WED SEP 2, 7:30–8:30PM  ·  TICKETED, PRICE VARIES  ·  LASER DOME, PACIFIC SCIENCE CENTER, 200 SUE BIRD COURT N, SEATTLE CENTER
The Laser Dome is the Pacific Science Center's domed theatre — the one that has been running laser shows under an arch of white arches since the 1970s. The science centre and the public radio station KEXP have paired it with a resident disc jockey: Eva Walker, a Seattle musician who hosts the station's early-morning show Early, curates a playlist each month and then plays a live set to the lasers once a month. September 2 is her last of three.
This is the answer to Wednesday. The forecast is rain from 7am, 90% chance, a quarter to half an inch — and this is an hour long, indoors, under a dome, finished at 8:30. Buy ahead; the dome is not large and the price moves with the show. Same campus as the free day party in the next card, if you want to make an evening of it.
The free party the radio station throws when an arena act is in town — today and tomorrow, no arena ticket involved
TUE SEP 1 + WED SEP 2, 4:30–8:00PM  ·  FREE, RSVP REQUESTED  ·  21+  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, 472 1ST AVE N, SEATTLE CENTER
KEXP, the listener-funded public radio station at Seattle Center, opens its own room to the street when a big act plays across the plaza — here, the Australian band Tame Impala at Climate Pledge Arena. The band is not at the day party; the station's disc jockeys are, playing records, with the café open and the doors unlocked to anyone. It is the same room that hosts the free in-studio sessions, and it costs nothing.
Three and a half hours long, so the useful move is to drop in for one — and note that a 4:30 start makes it a genuinely early evening. Today it competes with Duck Dodge; tomorrow it fills the gap before the 7:30 laser show two hundred metres away. Twenty-one and over. RSVP through the station's page so they can count heads.
The free evening at the natural history museum falls on the one day the forecast says thunderstorms
THU SEP 3, 10AM–8PM  ·  FREE; ADVANCE TIMED TICKETS RECOMMENDED  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, 4303 MEMORIAL WAY NE, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
The Burke is Washington's natural history and culture museum, and the building it opened in 2019 was designed to be turned inside out — the fossil labs, the workrooms and the collections storage sit behind glass and are visible from the galleries, so on a working day you watch the science happen rather than only its results. Admission is $24 every other day of the month; on the first Thursday it is free and the doors stay open until 8pm.
Thursday reads 65°, rain likely before 11am, then showers and thunderstorms likely between 11am and 5pm — a sentence the National Weather Service rarely writes here, and a strong argument for a roof. Reserve the free timed ticket online rather than turning up cold. Go late: arriving at six gets a quiet building and two hours. Parking on campus is the hard part; the N1 lot next door takes cards at the machine.
A congressman, a litigator who sues the federal government, and Seattle's own representative, on church, state, and what one person can do
FRI SEP 4, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall is the city's civic lecture room — a restored 1916 building on First Hill that runs several hundred talks a year and prices almost all of them on a sliding scale, so money is never the reason to skip one. Jared Huffman is a California congressman; Skye Perryman runs a litigation outfit that takes the federal government to court; each has a new book on how far Christian nationalism now reaches into government. Pramila Jayapal, who represents Seattle, moderates, and the back half is the practical one — what civic action looks like for a person rather than an institution.
It is the only lecture on the board this week — Town Hall's next ones are September 9 (a talk on oligarchy) and September 11 (Angela Duckworth), and the University of Washington's public series does not resume until October. Friday's other two, both one-night-only and both colliding: the free OSEES session at KEXP at 3pm, all-ages, first come first served, in the card above's room; and Incubus at the Beacon in Columbia City at 7pm, the 1966 horror film shot entirely in Esperanto with William Shatner in it, 74 minutes, presented by the Seattle Esperanto Society.
Saturday night is the clearest sky of the week and there are free telescopes set up in a Shoreline park under it
SAT SEP 5, 8:00–11:50PM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  PARAMOUNT PARK, 15300 8TH AVE NE, SHORELINE
The Seattle Astronomical Society is the local amateur club — four hundred-odd members, sixty loaner telescopes — and it runs public star parties where members bring their own instruments and let anyone look through them. This one is authorised by Shoreline's parks department, free, and explicitly open to families. Saturday night is forecast at 55°, partly cloudy, 10% rain, north wind 2 to 6 mph — the only single-digit precipitation number in the whole seven-day file.
The honest catch is the hour: 8pm is well past sunset but the good viewing is later, and this runs to midnight. Treat it as a one-hour visit rather than a night out. Note also that Paramount Park is a heavily light-polluted site — the club rates it near the bottom of the dark-sky scale — so this is planets and the moon, not the Milky Way. The club posts a cancellation call by 4pm on the day. The same club runs a free beginners' night-sky and telescope class in Woodinville on Friday at 7pm, no charge, if the how-to interests you more than the looking.
Labor Day, free at eleven and twelve dollars at half six: a band that headlined the festival, then a square dance in Ballard
MON SEP 7 — 11:00AM FREE, KEXP STUDIO, NORTHWEST ROOMS, SEATTLE CENTER  ·  AND 6:30PM, $12.42, TRACTOR TAVERN, 5213 BALLARD AVE NW
Most of KEXP's in-studio sessions are broadcast-only — you hear them live on the radio and see the video months later. This one is marked open to the public: Death Cab for Cutie, the Bellingham band, playing a half-hour live session at 11am on the holiday Monday, the morning after they close Bumbershoot across the plaza. Free, thirty minutes, in a room a fraction the size of the festival stage.
Then the evening: the Tractor Tavern's square dance, the Ballard Avenue roots room's recurring participatory night — a live string band (Three Forks of Candy) and a caller (Alex MacLeod) who teaches the figures from the floor, so no experience or partner is needed. 6:30pm, $12.42, twenty-one and over. It is the earliest downbeat on the Tractor's whole September calendar; everything else there starts at 8.
KEXP publishes no capacity or entry procedure for the morning session, so treat it as first come and arrive early; the Gathering Space at 472 1st Ave N is the public front door. Labor Day is 69° and partly sunny with a 28% chance of rain, which makes the walk between them pleasant enough. Buy the square dance ticket ahead — the Tractor's cheap nights go.
The tide runs a foot below zero on Labor Day morning and again Tuesday — the lowest daylight water of the next eight days
MON SEP 7, LOW 8:12AM AT −0.9 FT  ·  TUE SEP 8, LOW 9:12AM AT −1.0 FT  ·  FREE  ·  GOLDEN GARDENS, CARKEEK, CONSTELLATION PARK IN WEST SEATTLE
The tide table for the Seattle gauge does something useful over the long weekend. The lows walk later and lower each morning — −0.6 ft at 4:49am Friday, −0.7 at 5:55 Saturday, −0.8 at 7:05 Sunday, −0.9 at 8:12 on Labor Day, and −1.0 ft at 9:12am on Tuesday the 8th, which is the only day in the file flagged notable. By Monday and Tuesday the water is out at a civilised hour and in full daylight, which is what actually makes a tide walkable.
This is a do-it-yourself trip: the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalist programme, which staffs these beaches with volunteers who name what you find, finished its season in August. Go with the tide, not after it — arrive an hour before the low and walk the edge out. −1.0 ft is good, not exceptional; the flag for a genuinely rare tidepooling day is −2.0, and nothing in the next eight days approaches it. Monday is 69° and partly sunny.
Quiet elsewhere. The aurora feed says no — the planetary index peaks at Kp 2.0 today and sits in the 1s through Thursday, well under the 5 that would put anything on the northern horizon at this latitude. The Ballard Locks fish counters opened the coho column yesterday but have published nothing in it, and the chinook count has not been updated past August 25 and its 9,566 fish; the ladder and its underwater windows are open regardless, free, 7am to 9pm. Bumbershoot takes Seattle Center Saturday and Sunday — day passes from about $70, weekend from about $125, under-12s free — and the Mariners are away all week.
