Aurora chance plus the Draconid meteor peak, under a moonless sky
THU OCT 8  ·  SUNSET 6:36, FULL DARK 8:17  ·  STORM FORECAST KP 5 (G1) 8–11PM, KP 5.67 (G2) 2–5AM FRI  ·  FREE, ANY NORTH-FACING DARK SPOT
What changed. NOAA’s Space Weather Prediction Center issued its 3-day forecast at 5:30 this morning, and it says minor-to-moderate geomagnetic storms are likely because a solar eruption launched Monday is expected to arrive. In Seattle time, the forecast Kp index jumps from under 2 to 5 (minor storm) between 8 and 11pm Thursday, eases to 4.3, then peaks at 5.67 (moderate storm) between 2 and 5am Friday. At our latitude, Kp 5 means aurora is possible low on the northern horizon from a dark spot: a grey-green or pink glow that the phone camera often sees better than your eyes. Eruption arrival times can miss by half a day either way, so treat this as a watch, not a promise.
The sky. Clear until 7pm (10% cloud), then the National Weather Service hourly grid has 45% cloud from 8 to 10pm, 49% to 1am and 60% by 2am, with no rain until after 5am. So there will be gaps, not a clean sky. The moon is a 3% sliver that set at 5:35pm, so it costs nothing. The same evening is the Draconid meteor peak, about 5 an hour, slow meteors out of Draco in the north. It is the one shower that is best in the evening, so both things are in the same patch of sky at the same hour.
The plan. 8:30–9:30pm on a beach facing north-northwest: Carkeek Park past the railroad bridge, or the north end of Golden Gardens past the lot. 61° at 8pm, 59° at 9. Look at the horizon between Whidbey and Kingston and take a 3-second phone photo every few minutes; if it shows green, look again. In bed by 10. The 2–5am peak is the stronger window but 60% cloud; check the Space Weather Prediction Center’s 30-minute aurora forecast at 9pm before deciding about an alarm.

This is a talk and a concert on one ticket, and it is about the city itself. Jackson Street After Hours is the standard history of Seattle’s jazz scene, which grew up in the clubs around Jackson Street and the Central District where Ray Charles and Quincy Jones started out. He talks first. Then the festival’s resident artist, Sidney Hauser, debuts the nine-piece Big Little Band, playing Hauser’s own pieces and tunes by the Jackson Street players.
