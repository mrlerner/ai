TODAY · MUSIC · NOON–12:30 · FREE · IN YOUR LANE
Greg Freeman plays a free lunchtime set in the KEXP studio
MON NOV 2, 12–12:30PM  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP, SEATTLE CENTER
The studio-power scare from the weekend is over: KEXP’s page lists the session again, open to the public, 12 to 12:30. Freeman is the Vermont songwriter whose shaggy, slow-burning guitar records drew a Stereogum Album of the Week and comparisons to Songs: Ohia; the in-studio is the free way to hear him before his full show tonight at the Tractor Tavern in Ballard (8pm).
Same day, ideas: Iran, the US and the world in crisis, Oman’s former United Nations ambassador in conversation, University of Washington, Kane Hall Walker-Ames Room, 5–6:30pm, free with registration. Or Allison Aubrey on building a midlife you love, Town Hall, 7:30, $5.
WED · IDEAS · 7:30PM · $10–124 · RAINY NIGHT, INDOOR CALL
Claudia Rankine, on a rain-soaked Wednesday
WED NOV 4, DOORS 6:30, TALK 7:30, OUT ~8:45  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
Wednesday is the week’s wettest evening (86%), which makes it the right night for a talk. Rankine, the poet behind Citizen, reads from her first book after that trilogy, a memoir of a long friendship. Town Hall talks run about 75 minutes, so you’re home by nine.
Same night, later: Big Star Quintet with R.E.M.’s Mike Mills playing the Big Star songbook, Neptune Theatre, University District, 8pm.
THU · WATER · 10AM–8PM · FREE (+$5 EXHIBIT)
Free Thursday at the Nordic Museum, for the working-waterfront show
THU NOV 5, 10AM–8PM  ·  FREE ADMISSION, $5 SPECIAL EXHIBIT  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
A Ballard Story: Pacific Fishermen tells the story of one of the last working shipyards on Salmon Bay, the stretch you paddle past: shipbuilding, multigenerational fishing families, oral histories and the real dangers of the work. It runs to February, but first Thursday is the cheap day (free entry, $5 at the door for the exhibit; reserve online or walk in). Daytime chance of rain is only 26%, so walk the Ship Canal afterward before it turns wet at night.
Thursday evening, two options: The Tallest Man on Earth, the Swedish fingerpicker in the early-Dylan mold, the Crocodile, Belltown, doors 7, show 8, $55.30 all-in, 21+. Or free and earlier: Valeria Luiselli on her new novel, Town Hall, 7:30.
FRI · MUSIC · 8PM · $62–134 · THE MINIMALISM LANE
The Philip Glass Ensemble plays Powaqqatsi live under the film
FRI NOV 6, 8PM  ·  $62.20–133.50  ·  ALL AGES  ·  BENAROYA HALL, DOWNTOWN
You stream Steve Reich; this is the other pillar of that sound, played live. Godfrey Reggio’s wordless 1988 film (the sequel to Koyaanisqatsi) on labor and industrialization across the globe, with Glass’s own ensemble performing the score in sync. No end time posted; the film itself is about an hour and forty minutes, so figure out near 10. Rain likely Friday (59%), which only helps.
Also Friday, if guitar wins: John Scofield’s Electrospective, the jazz guitarist with Oteil Burbridge (bassist in Dead & Company and the Allman Brothers), Larry Goldings and Adam Deitch, Neptune Theatre, University District, doors 7, show 8, tickets on Ticketmaster. Earliest of the three: Corey Harris and Cedric Watson, acoustic blues and Creole fiddle, Town Hall, 7:30, $25–50 sliding.
SAT · MUSIC · 8:30PM · $27 · LATE, BUT IN LANE
Squirrel Flower at the Tractor, for Big Thief people
SAT NOV 7, 8:30PM  ·  $27.14  ·  21+  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD
Ella O’Connor Williams sings in an airy voice over twangy, crunchy guitar; EverOut’s pitch is “fans of Big Thief, Courtney Barnett,” and both are on your list. KEXP is presenting it, Sour Widows open. The 8:30 start means the headliner lands past your bedtime, so it’s a once-in-a-while night, not a default.
Saturday morning, nature: Forest bathing at Discovery Park, a slow guided walk from the Nordic Museum, 10am–noon, $30 ($25 members); Saturday is the drier weekend day (28%). Chum salmon stewards are back on Piper’s Creek at Carkeek, 11–3, free.
SUN · BRANCH-OUT · 2PM · $0–20
A symphony about Mount St. Helens, on a wet Sunday afternoon
SUN NOV 8, PRE-CONCERT TALK 1:30, CONCERT 2PM  ·  $0–20  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
The Seattle Festival Orchestra opens its 50th season with Alan Hovhaness’s Symphony No. 50, “Mount St. Helens,” written after the 1980 eruption, plus Khachaturian’s “Sabre Dance” and Spartacus. Sounds like slow, modal, chant-like brass building to a volcanic finale; sits next to the Glass and Reich you already like more than it does to Beethoven. Afternoon, nearly free, rain likely (59%).
NEXT MON · CITIES · 7PM · $10–60
Washington’s best new buildings, announced live on stage
