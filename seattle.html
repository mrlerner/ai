This week in Seattle
The hottest evening of the month lands on the one night Lake Union fills with costumed sailboats
TONIGHT, FIRST START 7PM  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION — GAS WORKS PARK, OR ON THE WATER
The Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night sailboat race that has gone around Lake Union every summer for fifty-two years — staggered starts by boat speed from 7pm, a costume theme every week, and a fleet that is plainly out there for the evening rather than the trophy. It has been climbing in this brief for five days as the Tuesday forecast kept getting warmer: 77°, then 80°, then 84°. It finished at 86° and sunny. That is the warmest reading anywhere in the seven-day file and it is gone by Wednesday, which drops eight degrees. Rain is in the file by Friday. There is no better outdoor evening between now and next week.
Two free ways in: the hill on the north shore at Gas Works Park looks straight down the course, or put a boat in and watch from inside the spectator fleet — the Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde both rent on the lake, a short paddle from the start line. Racing is finished well before dark and well before nine. Four Tuesdays left in the season after this one, but none of them will be 86°. If the drive south is a hassle, the same evening works from any west-facing Ballard beach — you just lose the sailboats.
Two days out, and the eclipse forecast is still holding
THU AUG 27, 7:30–10PM  ·  FREE, NO REGISTRATION  ·  SEWARD PARK, LAKE WASHINGTON SHORE
Thursday brings a deep partial lunar eclipse, and Seattle's problem with it is not cloud but altitude. The umbral phase starts at 7:34pm, before the moon is up; the moon then clears the horizon around 7:50pm already inside the Earth's shadow and stays low in the east through the best of it, so any treeline or ridge eats the show. The Seattle Astronomical Society, the local amateur-astronomy club, has fixed that with a free public star party on the Seward Park shoreline — open water under an eastern horizon, nothing in the way of the moonrise, members' telescopes passed around. Maximum is 9:13pm with about 96% of the disk covered: short of total, deep enough that most of the face should go copper with one bright rind left along the edge. It ends at 10:52pm, but the good part is over well before that.
Fifth straight morning the call has held: partly cloudy, low 57°, after a 74° day at a 4% rain chance. Bedtime works out for once — because the moon comes up already eclipsed, the deepest part is early and it is well into shadow before nine. Nothing to buy and nothing to wear on your eyes; a lunar eclipse is safe to look at directly. Seward Park is about twenty minutes from Ballard; leave by seven to be set up before moonrise. This one does not reschedule — anything else on Thursday evening loses.

An illustrated field guide to the Northwest year, launched by the artist who drew it
TOMORROW, WED AUG 26, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall Seattle — the First Hill hall that carries most of the city's science and civic programming — opens its season with the launch of Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest. Emily Poole wrote and illustrated it as a walk through the region's wildlife month by month, essays paired with her own drawings. She is joined by Kate Rogers, editor-in-chief of Mountaineers Books, the Seattle outdoors publisher behind it, and Katie Campbell, digital editor at the public radio station KUOW. The evening doubles as Town Hall's preview of what it has booked for the coming season — useful if you want to know what is worth holding a date for between now and spring.
Wednesday is the second-best evening of the week at 78° and mostly sunny, so this is an indoor night that costs you a good one — but it runs about seventy-five minutes, which means out the door by a quarter to nine, and the sliding scale from $10 means you set your own price. Entrance is off Seneca Street. It is the week's clean overlap of the two things worth leaving the house for: a lecture and a nature book, in the same room.

If the lake is not the answer tonight, there is a free author night in Lake Forest Park
TONIGHT, 7PM  ·  FREE, RSVP REQUESTED  ·  THIRD PLACE BOOKS, LAKE FOREST PARK
Ed Park — the novelist whose Same Bed Different Dreams was a Pulitzer Prize finalist — brings his new book Three Tenses to the Lake Forest Park store, in conversation with the Seattle writer Thomas Kohnstamm. Third Place Books runs these in the open commons at the top of the mall, so it is a real room rather than a corner of a shop, and the events are free with a request that you register so they know how many chairs. Tonight is the busier of the two author nights they have this week.
Honest conflict: this is the same night as the 86° Duck Dodge, and the weather does not come back this week. Take the lake tonight if you can and note that the same store has Amanda Holden and Emma Pattee on Thursday at 7pm — except Thursday is the eclipse. This one is indoors, free, twenty-five minutes up Bothell Way from Ballard, and done by half past eight.

The rain arrives Friday at five, and there is a six o'clock jazz show waiting for it
FRI AUG 28, 6PM  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Jazz Night School is the Seattle nonprofit that teaches adults to play in combos and big bands, and twice a year it moves its students onto a real stage for end-of-term concerts — big band, small ensembles, Latin groups and vocalists across one evening. The Royal Room is the Columbia City room built for exactly that: low ceiling, low cover, a piano in the corner, a full kitchen. Two nights, Thursday and Friday, both at 6pm, and six o'clock is the entire argument. Almost nothing else in this city puts live jazz in front of you early enough to be home by nine.
Take Friday, not Thursday — Thursday at six collides with the eclipse. It also happens to be the night the dry stretch breaks: rain moves in after 5pm Friday and the chance climbs to 41% overnight, which makes a warm indoor room with dinner attached the right call anyway. The Royal Room does not post a cover for these; the school's term concerts are typically free or a small donation at the door, so treat it as the cheap option and check when you arrive. Two blocks from the Columbia City light rail station.

Saturday is the thunderstorm day — here is the film to spend it on
SAT AUG 29, 7PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY  ·  ALSO MON AUG 31, 7:30PM
The Beacon is the small repertory cinema above the Columbia City bakery, a single room that programs old and strange films on 35mm and digital rather than anything currently in multiplexes. Saturday it shows Alain Resnais's Last Year at Marienbad from 1961 — the French film about a man insisting to a woman in a vast baroque hotel that they met a year ago, which she may or may not remember, and which the film never resolves. It is the canonical argument that a movie does not have to tell you what happened. If you miss Saturday it plays again Monday at 7:30pm.
Saturday's forecast is the worst of the week — 68° with a 41% chance of showers and thunderstorms between 11am and 5pm — so the outdoor plan is the one that needs the fallback, not this. A 7pm start puts you out around nine; if that is too late, Sunday recovers to 69° and partly sunny, and the Beacon has Charlie Chaplin's A King in New York at 2pm that afternoon instead.

A ten-piece chamber jazz orchestra, on a donation, with the rehearsal open first
MON AUG 31 — DOORS 6:30, OPEN REHEARSAL 7PM, SHOW 7:30  ·  DONATION  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Wayne Horvitz is the composer and keyboard player who co-founded the Royal Room and has been writing for Seattle players for decades. His Ensemble is a ten-piece chamber jazz orchestra built around new pieces of his that lean on group improvisation and conduction — the technique where the leader shapes the improvising in real time with hand signals rather than a written part. The unusual thing about this booking is that the 7pm rehearsal is open to the audience, so you can watch him assemble the thing before he performs it. Admission is a donation.
Get there for the 6:30 doors and you get a full hour of music-in-progress before the 7:30 downbeat, which is a better deal than most rooms give you at any price. It will run past nine if you stay for all of it — the rehearsal is the part worth showing up early for if the end has to be an early exit. Monday's forecast is 69° with a 31% chance of light rain, so nothing outdoors is competing.
