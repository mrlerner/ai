This week in Seattle
Two members of Congress and a constitutional lawyer on church and state — 75 minutes, and you are home by nine
TONIGHT FRI SEP 4 — 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  THE WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM AT TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE (SENECA ST ENTRANCE), FIRST HILL
Town Hall Seattle is the century-old civic hall on First Hill that runs most of the city's serious talks. Tonight, Congressman Jared Huffman of California and Skye Perryman, the lawyer who runs Democracy Forward, present their new books on how far Christian nationalism now reaches into federal government — and, more usefully, what a citizen actually does about it. Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal joins them on stage.
This is the only lecture on the board before Wednesday, and the format is the right length: doors 6:30, on stage 7:30, roughly 75 minutes, so you are out around a quarter to nine. Sliding-scale tickets start at $10 and you pick your own number, which is Town Hall's standing arrangement.
Earlier today and free: OSEES — the long-running San Francisco garage-psych band — tape a session in the KEXP Gathering Space at Seattle Center at 3pm, all ages, first come first served, no ticket. It is a taping, not a broadcast; it airs later.
Nature & sky
A foot below zero on Tuesday morning, and Jupiter sitting next to the Moon on the way there
MON SEP 7, 8:12AM  ·  TUE SEP 8, 9:12AM  ·  FREE  ·  ANY PUGET SOUND BEACH — CONSTELLATION PARK IN WEST SEATTLE, OR GOLDEN GARDENS IN BALLARD
The water. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's Seattle gauge drops to −0.9 ft at 8:12am on Labor Day Monday and then −1.0 ft at 9:12am on Tuesday — the only low in the eight-day table flagged as notable, and the deepest of the run. Both fall in full daylight on mornings forecast at 73° and 70°. A foot under zero uncovers a wide walkable flat: sea stars, anemones, moon snails, the sand-dollar beds off Constellation Park. Tuesday is both the lower tide and the later hour, which makes it the civilised one — you are back before ten.
The sky. The same Tuesday, at the other end of the morning: a thin waning crescent Moon sits right beside Jupiter low in the eastern sky before dawn, close enough to hold in one glance, with earthshine on the Moon's dark side. Naked eye, no equipment, any view east. Sunday pre-dawn has the quieter version of the same trick with Mars in Gemini.
Two notes. This is a do-it-yourself tidepool — the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalists finished the 2026 season in mid-July and are not stationed on these dates. And aurora is off the table: the space-weather forecast peaks at Kp 3.3, far short of Seattle's latitude. If you want a telescope pointed for you, the Seattle Astronomical Society holds a free public stargaze at Paramount Park in Shoreline, Saturday at 8pm.
The tugboats parade out at eleven and race at noon, and Sunday is the day for it
SUN SEP 6 — FESTIVAL 10AM–6PM, PARADE 11AM, RACES 12PM  ·  FREE  ·  PERCIVAL LANDING, OLYMPIA WATERFRONT — ABOUT AN HOUR SOUTH ON INTERSTATE 5
Olympia Harbor Days is the free maritime festival at the very bottom of Puget Sound, and it claims the largest vintage tugboat races in the world. The working boats tie up along the Percival Landing boardwalk all weekend — you walk the decks, climb into the wheelhouses, talk to the crews. Then on Sunday they cast off: the organisers ask you to be down there by 11am to watch the parade out from the Port Plaza or the Harbor House, and the race starts at noon in the deep channel of Budd Inlet, watched free from the boardwalk.
The forecast makes Sunday the obvious pick over the other two days: 71° and mostly sunny at 2% rain, the lowest number in the seven-day file, and the race is Sunday-only anyway. Admission is free; it is entirely outdoors on a flat boardwalk. Festival runs Friday 5–8pm and Saturday 10am–7pm too, but those are the walk-aboard days without the racing.
If two hours in the car is not the plan: the same water interest is walkable at the Ballard Locks fish ladder, where the coho run is just beginning — state counters put the real show in the last two weeks of September, so this is early.
Monday is the last chairlift of 2026 — after that it does not turn again until there is snow under it
SAT SEP 5, SUN SEP 6, MON SEP 7 — 9:30AM TO 5:30PM  ·  $24 ADULTS, $17 AGES 6–12, UNDER 6 FREE, RIDE AS OFTEN AS YOU LIKE ON THE DAY  ·  SUMMIT WEST, SNOQUALMIE PASS — ABOUT AN HOUR EAST ON INTERSTATE 90
The scenic lift at Summit West runs weekends only from early July to Labor Day, which means Monday September 7 is the final day of the season. It carries you up over the pass without the four hours of switchbacks the same view normally costs, and the ticket is good for unlimited rides that day — ride up, walk a ridge, ride down, go again. Ninety minutes from the city, and now the price is posted: $24 adults, $17 for ages six to twelve.
Weather is the reason to go on Monday specifically: 73° and mostly sunny at 3% rain in town, the warmest day of the week, and pass-level air will be cooler and clearer than that. Interstate 90 westbound will be a Labor Day parking lot from mid-afternoon, so go early and come back early — which suits a nine-thirty opening.
Rain fallback is not needed this week, but the low-effort city version of the same Monday is the −0.9 ft tide at 8:12am in the card above — both are early-morning outdoor plans that leave the whole day after them.
Eight Seattle players, one record, one night — a jazz octet is a rare thing to get into a small room
TUE SEP 8 — 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30  ·  $20 ADVANCE (ONLINE ONLY) / $25 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Pianist Dylan Hayes puts out Vol. 1, the debut record by his eight-piece band, on the local Pinch Records label — and this is the release show. Trumpet, alto and tenor saxophone, trombone, piano, guitar, bass, drums: a small big band, which is a size almost nobody can afford to keep together, played once in a room that seats a couple of hundred. The Royal Room is the low-ceilinged Columbia City music room that serves food and books better bills than anywhere its size in Seattle.
Practical: advance tickets are online only at $20 and go to $25 at the door, doors at 6:30 and music at 7:30. Light rail drops you three blocks away. A first set runs about an hour, so you can take the 7:30 and still leave in good time.
Monday has two earlier-starting alternatives if Tuesday is out: harmonica player Howard Levy — the one from Béla Fleck's Flecktones — with guitarist Chris Siebold at the same room, 7:30 and doors 6:30, $20 advance; or the Tractor Tavern square dance in Ballard with a caller and a live string band, doors 6:30, $12.42, the earliest downbeat of the month.
Four free in-studio sessions in four days, and you can just walk in
MON SEP 7 11AM  ·  TUE SEP 8 3PM  ·  WED SEP 9 11AM  ·  THU SEP 10 3PM  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP, 472 1ST AVE N AT SEATTLE CENTER
The listener-funded radio station at Seattle Center records bands in a glass-walled studio and marks a good share of those sessions open to the public — no ticket, no charge, thirty to forty minutes, twenty feet from the band. This week has four: Death Cab for Cutie on Monday at 11am, the morning after they close Bumbershoot; Haute & Freddy on Tuesday at 3pm; Altın Gün on Wednesday at 11am, the Amsterdam group that plays Anatolian folk songs as psychedelic rock; and Shabazz Palaces on Thursday at 3pm, the Seattle experimental hip-hop duo.
The catch is that these are working hours, not evenings — sessions are scheduled around the broadcast day. But Monday is a holiday, and the Death Cab session is the one people will queue for. No capacity is published and there is no list, so early is the whole strategy.

WED SEP 9 & THU SEP 10 — BOTH 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30, ABOUT 75 MINUTES  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
