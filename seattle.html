This week in Seattle
Tonight the moon clears the horizon 96% swallowed, and there is a free telescope field waiting for it
TONIGHT, THU AUG 27 — MOONRISE ~7:50PM, MAXIMUM 9:13PM  ·  FREE  ·  SEWARD PARK, 5900 LAKE WASHINGTON BLVD S
This is the last big lunar eclipse anywhere until the end of 2028, and it is a deep one: NASA's own number for maximum is 96.3% of the moon's disk inside the Earth's dark umbral shadow, close enough to total that the covered part should go copper. The partial phase opens at 7:34pm, which is before the moon is even up over Seattle — so the moon rises already in eclipse across south Lake Washington at roughly 7:50pm, climbs to maximum at 9:13pm, and does not finish the partial phase until about 10:52pm. A moon that comes up wrong is rarer than an eclipse overhead, and it is the low eastern sightline over the water that makes Seward Park worth the drive rather than your own yard.
The Seattle Astronomical Society has a free public star party at the Lakewood Playground end of Seward Park, 7:30 to 10pm, member telescopes set up and pointed; they ask for a reply on their site so they know numbers. Tonight reads partly cloudy, 58°, 13% chance of rain — a few points worse than yesterday's forecast but still the best night in the seven-day file, and the good part is done by about 9:30 if you leave after maximum. Naked-eye, so no gear required; if cloud parks over the lake, any east-facing shore does — Magnuson, Madrona, Leschi.
One long-shot bonus: the Space Weather Prediction Center has a G1 minor geomagnetic storm watch out for today, from a high-speed stream off a coronal hole, running into Saturday. Straight talk on the odds — the predicted peak of Kp 5.67 lands around 5pm our time, in full daylight, and the overnight numbers settle to about Kp 4, which is under the threshold for anything visible here. But an eclipsed moon is a dark full moon, so if you are already standing in a park facing east, it costs nothing to turn around and check the northern horizon.

The woman who ran Starbucks' global message for eleven years on how she got into the room
TONIGHT, THU AUG 27 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, 1119 8TH AVE
Wanda Herndon was Starbucks' first senior vice president of global communications, 1995 to 2006 — the decade the company went from a regional roaster to a thing on every corner on earth — and she now runs her own firm, W Communications. Her memoir is Working Class to Breaking Glass: Flint, Michigan, a working-class family, first in that family to go to college, journalism degrees from Michigan State, and then the corporate ladder. Local television journalist Angela Poe Russell asks the questions. It is a career-mechanics conversation more than a business one.
The collision is real and worth naming: this starts at 7:30, the same half hour the moon comes up in shadow. But Town Hall runs about seventy-five minutes, so the talk lands near 8:45 — and maximum eclipse is 9:13 with the partial phase going to 10:52. First Hill to an east-facing shoreline is fifteen minutes. You can have both if you skip the signing line. Sliding scale means $10 is a real price, not a concession, and the entrance is off Seneca Street.

Two hundred amateur jazz players take the room in shifts, at six o'clock, for whatever you put in the bucket
TOMORROW, FRI AUG 28 — DOORS 5PM, MUSIC 6PM  ·  $20 SUGGESTED DONATION AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Jazz Night School is the Seattle nonprofit that teaches adults to play jazz in ensembles instead of alone in a practice room, and twice each summer it empties the whole program onto the Royal Room stage — student combos back to back, the Performance Big Band, the gospel choir, solo singers. Over two hundred students and faculty across the two nights. Nothing about it is polished, which is the appeal: short sets, real stakes, a room pulling hard for people it knows.
Night #1 is tonight at the same hour, and tonight belongs to the eclipse — so Friday is the one to take. A 6pm downbeat is the rarest thing on this city's music calendar, all ages until 10pm, no reserved tables, which makes the 5pm doors worth using. Friday is also when the dry stretch ends: 71°, rain moving in after 5pm and a 67% chance overnight. Indoors, early and cheap is the right shape for that evening regardless.

Seven free hours on the piers — but the forecast under it has gone from damp to wet
SAT AUG 29, 1–8PM  ·  FREE  ·  PIER 62, PIER 58 AND THE SALISH STEPS, CENTRAL WATERFRONT
Fifth year of this, and the first one that doubles as the completion party for the twenty-acre Waterfront Park. Four live sets across the afternoon on the Pier 62 stage — among them Clinton Fearon & the Boogie Brown Band, the Jamaican bassist and singer who has been a Seattle fixture for decades — with cultural performances and disc jockeys running separately on Pier 58, and dance workshops on the Salish Steps, the wide stair dropping from Pike Place down to the water.
Honest weather revision: Saturday was carrying a 35% chance yesterday and it now reads 69° with rain likely, 70% — the wettest day of the next seven. It is free and it is outdoors, so this is a go only if you are content to be rained on for a few hours, and the early afternoon is the better half. The indoor pivot is ninety seconds away by design: the Seattle Aquarium's Ocean Pavilion sits directly on the same block of the waterfront. If the whole day is a wash, Saturday night has a film waiting for it further down this page.

The week's one clean sunny day is also a free ride on Lake Union in somebody else's wooden boat
SUN AUG 30 — SIGN-UPS OPEN 1PM, BOATS DEPART 1:15–5PM  ·  FREE  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, SOUTH LAKE UNION
Volunteer skippers take the public out on Lake Union at no charge — sprit-rigged boats, steamboats, electric launches, schooners, ketches, yawls, whatever the maintenance board and the volunteer roster allow that day. It happens six times a year and this is the second-to-last of 2026; the only date after it is September 27. Sign-ups are in person at 1pm in front of the Wagner Education Center, first come first served, everyone in your party present, wristbands handed out on the spot — somewhere between 80 and 200 seats depending on the boats.
Weather call: Sunday is 70°, partly sunny, 1% chance of rain — the only day in the whole seven-day file under ten percent, and sixty-nine points drier than the day before it. That will bring a crowd, so 1pm sharp is the difference between a boat and a wait. If the line beats you, the next card is the fallback and it starts at two.

A free band in the garden at two, and a glass wall of chinook forty steps away
SAT AUG 29 & SUN AUG 30, 2–4PM  ·  FREE, DONATIONS SUGGESTED  ·  CARL S. ENGLISH JR. BOTANICAL GARDEN, BALLARD LOCKS, 3015 NW 54TH ST
The Friends of the Ballard Locks put free bands on the lawn of the seven-acre garden above the locks most summer weekend afternoons, and the series only runs another week and a bit. Saturday is Música Molida, a traditional Cuban street organ with percussion — a hand-cranked instrument you will otherwise never hear outdoors in this city. Sunday is the Lynnwood Community Band, which is exactly the municipal-brass-in-a-garden thing it sounds like, and is the better of the two weather bets by a mile. No seating is provided; bring a chair or a blanket.
The actual reason to go is on the other side of the locks: the fish ladder viewing room is open 7am to 9pm and chinook peak in August. Fair warning, the state's count has not been updated past its August 16 reading — 4,934 chinook for the season — and the coho column does not open until September, so you are going on the season, not on a fresh number. Weather: Sunday 70° and 1% against Saturday 69° and 70%, so take Sunday. It also pairs with the wooden boats — a 1pm sign-up on Lake Union that does not land leaves you fifteen minutes from a 2pm downbeat in Ballard.

What to do with the wettest Saturday night of the month: the most beautiful film ever made about not remembering
SAT AUG 29, 7PM  ·  ALSO MON AUG 31, 7:30PM AND TUE SEP 1, 4:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon is the small repertory cinema in the Columbia City Theater building, and it is running a stretch of European puzzle films this week. Last Year at Marienbad (Alain Resnais, 1961) is the anchor: a man insists to a woman at a baroque hotel that they met a year ago, she insists they did not, and the film refuses to settle it — hedge mazes, frozen guests, one of the most imitated looks in cinema. It plays three times. The Saragossa Manuscript (Wojciech Has, 1965), a three-hour Polish nesting-doll of ghost stories, follows it Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.
Clock check: Marienbad runs about ninety-four minutes, so the 7pm Saturday show is out by around 8:40 — well inside the rules. The Monday 7:30 works too. The Saragossa one is where to be careful: the full Polish cut is 182 minutes and even the trimmed prints run well over two hours, so a 7:30 start is a very late finish and its 4:30pm Tuesday and Wednesday matinees are the civilized way in. Saturday's 70% chance of rain makes any of this a better use of the evening than standing on a pier.
