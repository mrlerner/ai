TONIGHT — FREE, OUTDOORS, AND IT ONLY HAPPENS FOUR TIMES A YEAR
A band at six in the U District square, then Grease on a screen outdoors
FRI SEP 18  ·  BAND 6PM, FILM AT DUSK  ·  FREE  ·  THE SQUARE AT U DISTRICT STATION
Outdoors On The Ave is a music-and-movie series that the U District Advocates run on the four Fridays after Labor Day in the new plaza next to the light rail station — step off the train and you are there. This is its sixth year and tonight is the second of the four, so there are only two left after it. Everything is free: no ticket, no RSVP, no bar tab. The whole shape of the evening is the thing this brief keeps looking for — live music at six o'clock, outdoors, on a 73° sunny day.
Tonight’s band, 6pm: Hockey Teeth, whom the series describes as a fresh take on classic power pop and compares to Phoenix, Elvis Costello and Wilco. That is the set worth turning up for, and it is done well before dark.
Then the film, and here is the honest arithmetic. This year’s movie theme is “Back to School” and tonight’s is Grease (1978), billed as a sing-along. The series starts its films “at dusk.” Sunset tonight is 7:16 and the light is properly gone by 7:46, so a start around a quarter to eight, and Grease runs about 110 minutes — which puts the end credits somewhere near 9:35. Past your bedtime. The clean version of tonight is the band at six, the first half hour of the film, and home; the sing-along crowd is the draw either way, and Scarecrow Video in the U District supplies the prints.
Weather call: sunny, 73°, no rain, going down to 54° overnight — so it will be genuinely cold in that square by nine. Bring a layer and something to sit on. Rain answer, not needed tonight but worth having: the indoor Friday is Priya Parker at Town Hall, 7:30 in the Great Hall, $10–35 — she wrote The Art of Gathering and is here on conflict; doors 6:30, about 75 minutes, so out by a quarter to nine.

WATER — THE ONE WHERE YOU ARRIVE BY KAYAK
The floating concert off Gas Works plays its last show of the year on Sunday
SUN SEP 20, 3–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  ON THE WATER, WEST SIDE OF GAS WORKS PARK, LAKE UNION
Lake Union Concerts put a stage on a boat, anchor it off the west shore of Gas Works Park, and everybody paddles out and floats there. Eight of these ran this summer; Sunday is the last one of the season, and there is no version of it that comes back before June. Free, and the sign-up page reads “tickets from $0.00” — you reserve a free spot and that is the whole transaction. About 820 people are signed up as of this morning.
Why this one is bigger than the others: they have partnered with Seafair, who are bringing the Trek Ferry — a larger stage, bigger speakers and a video screen instead of the usual small barge. Three Seattle DJs: Fame Sounds, N8VBOY and Average Citizens. It is electronic music, played loud, over water, in the afternoon.
Two details that make it yours. First, 3 to 6pm — you are off the water and dry before dinner. Second, the organisers’ own terms require every attendee to bring and wear a Coast Guard-approved life jacket and state plainly that no alcohol or intoxicants are permitted, on the reasonable grounds that this is a lake. It is a sober daytime event on the water by design, not by accident.
Getting on the water: you cannot put in at Gas Works this year — construction has closed that shoreline access — so the organisers point you at the Sunnyside Boat Launch, the Fremont Canal, or the Lake Washington Rowing Club dock. From any of those it is a short, flat paddle across to the anchorage. If you would rather rent than haul, the Northwest Outdoor Center on the west side of Lake Union and Agua Verde at Portage Bay both hire boats by the hour.
Weather call: Sunday is mostly sunny, 71°, zero percent rain, north wind about 6 mph — that is a flat lake and an easy crossing, and it is the last forecast like it attached to this event until next summer. If it turns: there is no indoor version of a paddle-out; the fallback that keeps the day on the water is the boat show two cards down, which is on dry piers a mile south and open until five on Sunday.

MUSIC — SMALL ROOM, THREE LOCAL BANDS, DONE BY NINE
KEXP puts its Northwest music show on a stage on Saturday and broadcasts it live
SAT SEP 19, 5–9PM  ·  $18 ADULT, $12 UNDER-18  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, 472 FIRST AVE N, SEATTLE CENTER
Audioasis is KEXP’s long-running Pacific Northwest music show — the one that exists to play bands from here. A few times a year they move it out of the studio and into the Gathering Space, the glass room off the Seattle Center campus where the station does its public sessions, and put the whole thing out over the air live. That is Saturday.
The bill: TV Star, Plash and glass egg, all Northwest acts, hosted on air by DJ Kennady, with an hour-long opening DJ set from Phoenix, an alumnus of the station’s 90.TEEN youth programme. The station bills it as a shared listening session rather than a gig — the room seats and stands a couple of hundred, and everyone is there to actually listen.
The timing is the argument. Doors at five, off the air by nine — a full four-band evening that starts before dinner and ends when a normal club night is still soundchecking. All ages; the bar is there for people who want it and irrelevant if you don’t.
How to get in: $18 adult, $12 for under-18s, sold through the station’s ticket page and still available this morning. 472 First Avenue North, the north edge of Seattle Center — street parking around the building plus paid lots, and no coat check, which matters on a 54° night. Indoors, so the forecast is irrelevant. No event image is published on the station’s page, so this card has none.

WATER — ENDS SUNDAY
A hundred and thirty boats in the water at the Lake Union piers, for three more days
THRU SUN SEP 20  ·  FRI 11AM–6PM, SAT 10–6, SUN 10–5  ·  $21 ONLINE / $24 AT THE GATE  ·  901 FAIRVIEW AVE N, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The Boats Afloat Show is the Northwest Yacht Brokers Association’s autumn show and it has run since 1978. What makes it worth an hour is that the boats are in the water, tied up along the South Lake Union piers rather than sitting on trailers in a hall — you walk the docks and go aboard. More than 130 vessels, and the interesting end for anyone who fishes or paddles is not the motor yachts: it is the sailboats, the fishing boats and the recreational tugs, which is a category of small working boat you rarely get to stand on.
On the pier, not on the boats: marine electronics and gear exhibitors, free seminars included with admission, and live music across the four days. Kids’ boat-building on the weekend.
The cost, said plainly: $21 for an adult single day bought online, $24 at the gate; $5 online for a teenager; under-13s free; a multi-day adult pass is $36 online. Some listings show this as “$0–$24,” which is the children’s price at one end — it is not a free event.
Weather call: dry and 71–73° through Sunday, which is the whole point of a show where you spend the visit standing on floating docks. The published hours differ between the show’s own site and the listings sites — the times above are the show’s, which open an hour earlier on Saturday and close an hour earlier on Sunday than the aggregator says. Rain answer: it is open-air on the docks; if it turned, the seminars are the sheltered part.

NATURE & IDEAS — NEW ON THE CALENDAR THIS MORNING
Marina Richie on the birds that live in the old growth, and what they tell you about the forest
TUE SEP 22, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35  ·  WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
This was not on the calendar yesterday. Marina Richie is a nature writer whose new book is Feathered Forest: Aloft with Birds in Ancient Trees, and the argument is a structural one: an old-growth stand in Cascadia is a vertical world with distinct layers, and the birds are the way in — which species lives at which height, and what that tells you about the age and health of the stand. She works from her home in central Oregon across both sides of the Cascades, so this is our forests, not a general nature talk. In conversation with Maria Mudd Ruth.
Why it lands this week: this is the closest thing on the calendar to the thing the tide and aurora feeds have not produced in eight days — nature learning about the ground you actually walk on, a week before larch season starts pulling people east over the passes.
How to get in: $10 to $35, sliding, so it is a $10 evening if you want it to be. It is in the Wyncote Northwest Forum, the smaller downstairs room at Town Hall, 1119 Eighth Avenue — a couple of hundred seats rather than the big hall upstairs. Town Hall’s evening talks run about 75 minutes from 7:30, so out around a quarter to nine. Indoors; Tuesday is 67° and back down to a 5% rain chance.

IDEAS — ALSO NEW, AND THE SAME NIGHT AS THE ONE ABOVE
Emily St. John Mandel, upstairs at Town Hall, on the same Tuesday
TUE SEP 22, 7:30PM  ·  $10–128  ·  THE GREAT HALL, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Town Hall has booked two things into the same 7:30 slot on Tuesday, in its two rooms, so this is a genuine choice rather than a double bill. Emily St. John Mandel wrote Station Eleven — the post-pandemic novel about a travelling Shakespeare company, largely set in the Great Lakes — and Sea of Tranquility. The new one is Exit Party: Los Angeles in 2031, the first spring after the collapse of the United States, a man vanishing from a party and a woman trying to work out how. Doubles, shadow worlds, fractured timelines.
How to get in: $10 at the bottom of the range, rising to $128 for the tiers that bundle books and better seats. This one is in the Great Hall, the big upstairs room, which is the tell that they expect it to sell — if you want it, this is the week to buy rather than the day.
Choosing between the two: the birds-and-old-growth talk downstairs is the smaller, quieter, more local evening; this is the full-hall literary event. Same building, same start, same $10 floor.

CIVIC — FREE, AND IT STARTS AT HALF PAST SIX
Who catches an older adult when the mental health system doesn’t
MON SEP 21, 6:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Sound Generations is the King County non-profit behind Meals on Wheels and the Hyde Shuttles, and this is the first of a series of public discussions it is running on how older adults get lost between the aging-services system and the behavioral health system. The panel is people who run the actual programmes rather than commentators: Traci Adair, who oversees investments and partnerships in equity-driven aging and disability services; Dr. Someireh Amirfaiz, a mental health professional who has spent 25 years in the non-profit sector and trains clinicians in culturally responsive care for refugees; and Hazel G. Boado of Asian Counseling and Referral Service, on care for Asian and Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander communities.
Why it is in this brief: it is a free, early, service-delivery conversation about a system that is failing quietly — closer to civic infrastructure than to a lecture, which is the register you build software in. And it is the only free thing at Town Hall this week.
Practicalities: free, in the downstairs Wyncote Northwest Forum, 1119 Eighth Avenue on First Hill. 6:30 start — the earliest weeknight start on the calendar this week, and a panel of three plus questions will not run past eight. Monday is 70° and dry, so it costs you a good evening; the trade is that it costs nothing else.
