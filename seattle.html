The design festival block party is tomorrow — free, outdoors, and on the best weekend of the month
SAT AUG 15 & SUN AUG 16, 10AM–7PM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  LAKE UNION PARK, SOUTH LAKE UNION
Two days of street fair on the Lake Union shoreline, run by the Seattle chapter of the American Institute of Architects: walk-in installations built by design teams around this year's theme, IN[FLUX] — a city in transition and what design owes it — plus a mainstage of short talks from design leads and pop-up workshops that run anywhere from thirty minutes to two hours. The three parts are genuinely different experiences, so pick one: the installations reward a slow lap, the mainstage is the closest thing Seattle has to a free urbanism conference, and the pop-ups need you to show up at a specific hour. Weather is the whole argument for going now — partly sunny and 80° Saturday, 78° Sunday, zero rain chance both days, and it is entirely outdoors with no wet-weather plan needed. Lake Union Park is the streetcar's north end and a short walk from Westlake; the Center for Wooden Boats sits on the same lawn if you want boats before tents. Indoors alternative if you'd rather do it small: the same festival's evening mixers Tuesday and Wednesday.

Saturday: the crescent moon parks next to Venus — and Jupiter and Mercury nearly touch before dawn
SAT AUG 15, DUSK IN THE WEST & PRE-DAWN IN THE EAST  ·  FREE  ·  PARTLY CLOUDY, LOW 59°
Two easy naked-eye pairings on the same date, at opposite ends of the night. After sunset, look low in the west: Venus is the brightest thing in the evening sky all month, and Saturday a thin crescent moon — three days past new — sits right beside it. That one takes no equipment, no dark sky and no alarm clock; a west-facing waterfront does it, so Golden Gardens, Alki or the Ballard side of the ship canal. The harder half is before sunrise, when Jupiter and Mercury close to about half a degree apart — a moon's width — very low in the east. Both are bright, but they sit in the dawn glow, so it wants a flat eastern horizon and binoculars; the pre-dawn sky also still has Mars and Saturn strung along the ecliptic behind them. No tide or aurora window this week to compete with it: the deepest daytime low is −0.9 feet at 12:35 today and it only gets shallower from there, and the geomagnetic forecast peaks at a planetary index of 4, which is nothing at this latitude.

The chinook run at the locks enters its two best weeks
ANY DAY, VIEWING ROOM 7AM–9PM  ·  FREE  ·  HIRAM M. CHITTENDEN LOCKS, BALLARD
The locks' own guidance puts peak chinook viewing in August, with the last two weeks of the month the best of it — roughly fifteen thousand king salmon a year come up this ladder, and you watch them through glass from an underwater room rather than from a bank. Sockeye are largely done (their window is mid-June to mid-July) and coho don't stack up until September, so this is the chinook fortnight. Free, open 7am to 9pm daily, and a walkable loop from Ballard Avenue: gardens, the lock chambers, the ladder, back over the spillway. Fair warning from the operators themselves — even at peak you may see nothing on a given hour, and hot weather and low tides are the usual culprits, which argues for a morning visit rather than the middle of an 80° afternoon. If the windows are empty, the lock chambers themselves are the backup show, and the Sunday farmers market is four blocks up.

Freeway Park at fifty — and the argument for lidding the interstate
TUE AUG 18, DOORS 5PM, PROGRAM 6–8PM  ·  FREE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, FIRST HILL
The panel is better than the billing suggests, and this is the week's one civic-design event with actual decision-makers in the room: Rebecca Bear of the Seattle Parks Foundation moderating, Lara Rose of the landscape architecture firm Walker Macy, who is running the current improvements to the park itself, Riisa Conklin of the Freeway Park Association, and state senator Jamie Pedersen, the Senate majority leader for the 43rd district. The subject is the half-century-old park built on a deck over Interstate 5 — how it has aged, what the landmark designation constrains, what the capital work is fixing, and where the much larger Lid I-5 proposal goes next. If you want the version of urbanism that is budgets and legislative sessions rather than renderings, this is it. Free, doors an hour early with a social hour, light food and a cash bar, and it's recorded if you miss it. It competes with two other Tuesday picks below.

A repertory double bill all week at the Beacon: Elaine May's “Mikey and Nicky” and “Take Care of My Cat”
MON AUG 17 – THU AUG 20, ALTERNATING 4:30 & 7:30PM  ·  COLUMBIA CITY
The single-screen room above the Columbia City bakery runs two films in rotation for four straight days, which is unusual for them and means you can see both without clearing a weekend. Elaine May's 1976 film follows two low-level mobsters through one night, one of whom has been marked — famously shot with the cameras left running until the actors forgot them. “Take Care of My Cat” is the 2001 Korean film about five friends drifting apart in the year after high school in Incheon, a quiet film that has aged into a favorite. Check the calendar for which title is in which slot; they swap the 4:30 and 7:30 daily.
