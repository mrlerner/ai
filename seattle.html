This week in Seattle
Tomorrow is the best evening the week has, and the zoo lawn's last one of the summer lands on it
THU AUG 20, GATES 4:30PM  ·  FROM $47, ONLINE ONLY  ·  WOODLAND PARK ZOO NORTH MEADOW, PHINNEY RIDGE
The Breeders with Team Dresch closes the zoo's 2026 summer concert series — two bands out of the same early-nineties underground, on grass, in front of about five thousand people on blankets. The National Weather Service now has Thursday at 80° with zero percent rain, the single warmest and driest day in the seven-day file, dropping to 60° and mostly clear overnight. Tickets are sold online only and never at the gate, so this is a today decision, not a tomorrow one.
Free version of the same forecast, no ticket required: the Ballard Locks fish ladder is still inside its chinook window with viewing until 8:45pm, and the salt-side walk is the cheapest way to spend an 80° evening. Indoor fallback is moot — nothing is going to rain on Thursday.

The wooden-boat yard on Lake Union turns fifty and opens the bar for fifteen dollars
SAT AUG 22, 5–8PM  ·  $15, 21+  ·  CENTER FOR WOODEN BOATS, LAKE UNION PARK, SOUTH LAKE UNION
A fiftieth-anniversary reunion for the boathouse and livery at the south end of Lake Union — the place that keeps a fleet of wooden sailboats and rowboats on the water and hands them to the public. Heavy appetizers, open bar, live music, three hours, fifteen dollars, on the dock. This is the paddling and small-boat crowd in one room on one night, which is the part that does not repeat; the boats are there any weekend.
Weather call: Saturday's rain chance is 37 percent but it is all before 5pm — this starts exactly as the forecast clears, at 71° falling to 58°. Bring a layer for the dock. Fully sheltered fallback: the Museum of History and Industry is fifty yards across the same park.

The other fiftieth on Saturday: the world's first park built over a freeway, free, food included
SAT AUG 22, DAYTIME  ·  FREE, INCLUDING THE FOOD AND DRINK  ·  FREEWAY PARK, 700 SENECA ST, DOWNTOWN
Last night's anniversary forum was the argument — a room of planners making the case for a much bigger lid over Interstate 5. Saturday is the party, and Seattle Parks and the Freeway Park Association are programming the whole brutalist canyon: the Garfield High School jazz band and street musicians, guided architecture tours, temporary artworks from Kate Clark, Julie Reinke Carrier and Amity Preston, a site-specific audio piece routed through the concrete, history programming with the city archives and the Lid I-5 campaign, and free catered food. For someone who builds civic software, the draw is the day the people who maintain a fifty-year-old piece of urban infrastructure stand in it and take questions.
Weather call: the rain risk sits over Saturday morning and early afternoon, so go late in the program rather than early. The park's overhangs and water-wall alcoves are real shelter; the Central Library is three blocks downhill if it turns serious. Start time was not posted as of this morning — check the association page before you leave.

Last free outdoor movie of the summer, on a forty-foot screen under the Space Needle
FRI AUG 21, DUSK — ABOUT 9PM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  MURAL AMPHITHEATRE, SEATTLE CENTER
The Friday outdoor film series that has run since late July ends here, with the 1987 sword-and-spaceship oddity “Masters of the Universe” on the grass bowl below the Space Needle. Free, no ticket, blankets and low chairs; people arrive well before dark for the lawn spots down front. It is the last night of the series and also the last dry Friday evening in the file — rain arrives before dawn on Saturday.
Weather call: 79° Friday falling to 60°, mostly cloudy after dark and dry until roughly 5am — fine, but bring the layer, the bowl gets cold once the sun is off it. Indoor fallback in the same neighborhood: The Beacon in Columbia City has “Mysterious Skin” at 7pm, or the Tractor is a twelve-minute drive north.
Silkworm and Nina Nastasia, two hundred people, wooden floor, ten minutes from home
FRI AUG 21, 8PM  ·  $25  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD
The Missoula-by-way-of-Seattle trio whose records defined a particular dry, unsentimental strain of nineties Northwest rock, with the New York songwriter Nina Nastasia opening — a pairing that only makes sense in a small room, which is exactly what this is. Twenty-five dollars in Ballard on a Friday. If the Seattle Center lawn is the free version of Friday night, this is the version worth paying for.
Same room, rest of the week: a Willy Braun matinee at 2pm Saturday and the music blog Guerrilla Candy's twenty-fifth anniversary with SHIM and Smokey Brights at 8pm, then Horse Feathers playing “Words Are Dead” end to end on Sunday at 7:30pm, $27.

Morris Day and The Time play Seattle Center for free, and Fisher Pavilion becomes a roller rink
SAT AUG 22 & SUN AUG 23, 1–8PM  ·  FREE  ·  SEATTLE CENTER: ARMORY, MURAL AMPHITHEATRE, FISHER PAVILION
Festival Sundiata's Black Arts Fest, the longest-running African American festival in the Northwest, first held in 1980 — two stages, seven hours a day, free. The headline is the Minneapolis funk band Morris Day and The Time; also on the bill are the Seattle hip hop artist Draze, Michael Hepburn of the Portland group Pleasure, the Pacific Northwest Community Gospel Choir and the Double Dutch Divas. New this year: Skate Sundiata turns Fisher Pavilion into a roller rink with a live DJ, all skill levels.
Weather call: this is the rain-proof weekend pick — the Armory food hall and Fisher Pavilion are both indoors, so Saturday's 37 percent morning rain costs you nothing. Sunday at 72° and partly sunny is the better day for the outdoor amphitheatre stage.
The car-free boulevard is back this weekend — and this time Sunday is clearly the day
SAT 10AM – SUN 6PM  ·  FREE  ·  LAKE WASHINGTON BOULEVARD, MOUNT BAKER BEACH TO SEWARD PARK
Lake Washington Boulevard closes to motor traffic for thirty-two straight hours between Mount Baker Beach and Seward Park — walking, riding and rolling only, with residents and deliveries entering from the nearest cross street and the park lots still open. It runs every weekend through Labor Day, so the pick here is the day, not the event: Saturday carries a 37 percent rain chance and a 10 mph south-southwest wind off the lake, Sunday is 72°, partly sunny, one percent rain, six mile-an-hour breeze. Ride Sunday.
Worth doing while you are down there: the Seward Park end is the open eastern-horizon viewpoint you want for the eclipse a week Thursday, and it is worth walking the last stretch once in daylight to pick a spot.
The one lecture in the next seven days worth holding an evening for — and it is a field guide launch
WED AUG 26, 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Emily Poole launches “Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest” with Kate Rogers, editor-in-chief at Mountaineers Books — the region's plants and animals arranged by the season you would actually run into them, illustration plus essay plus things to go do. Sliding scale down to ten dollars. It is a week out, which is the right amount of notice for the only ideas night on the calendar with any pull.
Quiet on the lecture front otherwise: Town Hall's only other August dates are a Solstice Symphony Orchestra program Saturday and a memoir night on the 27th, and the University of Washington public lecture series is dark until fall — registration for the season opens September 9. Nature feeds are quiet too: no minus tide inside seven days (the week bottoms at +0.5 ft) and the planetary index peaks at 4, which is no aurora at this latitude.
