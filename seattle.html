NEW — FREE, ON THE WATER, AND THE LAST ONE OF THE YEAR
Paddle out to a concert on Lake Union — from a ferry deck, on Sunday afternoon
SUN SEP 20, 3–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  LAKE UNION, OFF GAS WORKS PARK
This is the one to take the kayak to. Seattle Paddle Rave is a floating concert: the music plays from a boat anchored off the west side of Gas Works Park and the audience is whatever paddles up to it — kayaks, paddleboards, canoes, inflatables. It began last summer, when a few hundred paddleboarders turned up off Gas Works to listen to house music, and this year's series ran to eight shows. Sunday is the finale, and it will not happen again until next summer.
Bigger than the usual one: the organisers have partnered with Seafair, the group behind the August hydroplane weekend, which is bringing a retired Alaskan ferry to act as the stage — more speakers, a proper stage and a screen, instead of the small flamingo-decorated boat the earlier shows used. Three local disc jockeys play house music: Fame Sounds, N8VBOY and Average Citizens. The costume theme is witches, which you are free to ignore.
How to get in: free — the ticket page lists it at $0.00, and about 700 people have claimed one. Bring your own boat and a Coast Guard-approved life jacket, which the organisers require you to wear. Worth knowing: the terms also say no alcohol on the water, which is unusual for this sort of thing and makes it a better afternoon than it sounds.
Where to put in: not Gas Works — construction has closed the shoreline access there. The organisers point people at the Sunnyside Avenue North boat launch, the Fremont canal, or the Lake Washington Rowing Club dock; the Northwest Outdoor Center on Westlake rents boats if you would rather not car-top your own.
The weather call: Sunday is 71°, sunny, zero percent rain, and 3–6pm puts you off the water long before dark and long before bedtime. Monday brings the rain back, so this is the last clean afternoon of the run. Fallback if it turns: the show is weather-dependent in a way an indoor event is not — check the organiser's page the morning of.

NEW — FREE, AND TWENTY BLOCKS LONG
Two hundred artists take over the downtown sidewalks on Saturday
SAT SEP 19, 11AM–7:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  WESTLAKE PARK TO OCCIDENTAL SQUARE
WALKDONTRUN calls itself an art walk marathon, and the format is the interesting part: twenty-plus blocks of downtown sidewalk, plaza and park — Westlake Park down through Pioneer Square to Occidental Square — turned into a continuous route of stages. You walk the course at your own pace and take what you hit. Dance, painting, brass bands, funk, textile work, live theatre, roaming performers, participatory sculpture, a few secret gardens.
Second year. Last year it drew 300 artists and thousands of walkers over the same stretch of pavement, which is a genuinely unusual thing to do to a downtown — the point is as much about what a street is for as about the art on it, which is why it is in your brief rather than an arts newsletter's. It opens Arts and Culture Week, Sep 19–26.
How to get in: free. The attendee ticket on the organiser's page is $0.00 and exists only for a headcount; the paid tiers above it ($52 and up) are straight donations to the nonprofit behind it. No ticket is needed to walk up.
The weather call: 71° and sunny with no rain in the file — the whole thing is outdoors and eight and a half hours long, so pick your hour rather than the day. It runs until 7:30, which collides with everything else on Saturday night (see two cards down); the honest advice is to walk the route in the afternoon and keep the evening for one of the indoor things. Wet-weather fallback: not needed, and the route passes the art museum and Pioneer Square's covered arcades anyway.

A hundred and twenty boats in the water, and now all four days are dry
THU–SUN SEP 17–20  ·  $24 DAY / $40 ALL FOUR  ·  SOUTH LAKE UNION
Re-verified against the show's own page this morning: Thursday and Friday 11am–6pm, Saturday 10am–6pm, Sunday 10am–5pm, at the Lake Union piers, 901 Fairview Avenue North. More than 120 power and sailing craft, tied up and in the water so you walk aboard them — the floating show rather than the hulls-on-trailers one. Free seminars and live music on the docks; the yacht brokers' association has run it since 1978.
$24 for a single day at the gate, $40 for a multi-day pass, teens 13–17 $7, under-12s free, $15 on a military ID. Buying online is discounted through Sunday.
What changed since yesterday: the weekend dried out, so the advice softens. Thursday or Friday still gets you emptier docks at 73° and no rain, and Friday closes at six. But if the weekend suits you better, Sunday now works too — and it is the same afternoon as the paddle concert half a mile up the same lake, which is either a conflict or a very good day on the water, depending on how you want to sequence it. The show closes at 5, the concert starts at 3.

NEW ON THE CALENDAR — FREE, AND THIRTY MINUTES LONG
Chelsea Wolfe plays the radio station's studio on Friday morning, for nothing
FRI SEP 18, 11–11:30AM  ·  FREE  ·  SEATTLE CENTER
This appeared on the station's calendar overnight and is the best small-room music on the week's list, because the room is about as small as it gets. KEXP's in-studio sessions are open to the public and free, held in the studio in the Northwest Rooms at Seattle Center, and the audience is a few dozen people standing a short distance from the band.
Chelsea Wolfe is the Californian songwriter who has spent fifteen years moving between doom metal, gothic folk and electronics — heavy, slow, largely acoustic when stripped back, which is what these sessions do to a band. She normally plays ticketed rooms several hundred times this size.
The catch is the clock: it is 11am on a Friday and it lasts half an hour, broadcast live. That is a working-day hole rather than an evening, so it only works if Friday morning is yours — but it costs nothing, it is thirty minutes, and it is on your side of the water. Sessions are broadcast-only, so there is no filming; the video goes up later. Attendance works through the station's in-studio sign-up, linked from the event page.

How to change someone's mind without lying to them — free, and over by six
THU SEP 17, 5–6PM  ·  FREE WITH REGISTRATION  ·  UNIVERSITY DISTRICT
A book launch for Just Hear Me Out: How to Change Minds Without Lies, Bullshit, or Moral Compromise by Colin Marshall, a philosopher at the University of Washington, in conversation with his colleague José Jorge Mendoza. The argument is the practical one — that persuasion which does not cheat is both possible and better — which is a more useful hour than the title suggests if you spend your working life trying to get people to agree about cities.
Barnes & Noble on University Way Northeast, 4324 University Way NE, in the U District. Free, registration through the bookshop's ticket page.
Why it keeps appearing here: it is still the only University of Washington public lecture on the calendar before Oct 8 — the lectures office has withdrawn invitations to six speakers in its signature series pending a review of how they were chosen, which has thinned the autumn considerably. Five to six on a Thursday is the friendliest slot on this week's list, and it leaves the evening free.

SAT SEP 19  ·  $15–18, OR WHATEVER THE SYMPHONY COSTS
Audioasis Live, 5–9pm, $18 adult and $12 under-18 — the radio station's local-music programme played live in its Gathering Space, 472 First Avenue North, with TV Star, Plash and glass egg, an hour of records first, all ages. A six o'clock concert almost never happens and this is the orchestra's biggest soloist of the year. The price is still not published on the event page and the box office refused an automated read again this morning — so treat it as unknown and ring them rather than trusting a number from anywhere else.

The 4pm screenings are the ones that fit: Sugarfly Saturday at 4, Into the Unknown: My Cancer Story Sunday at 4. Singles $7–15, three-film bundles $17–35.

NATURE & SKY — A DATE THAT CANNOT MOVE
The equinox lands at 5:06 on Tuesday — and at 7:30 a book about the birds of old-growth forest
TUE SEP 22  ·  FREE / $10–35 SLIDING  ·  FIRST HILL
The autumn equinox is next Tuesday at 5:06pm our time — computed again this morning from the solar ephemeris, which puts the sun's apparent ecliptic longitude across 180° between 5:05 and 5:10, interpolating to 5:06. Worth knowing the folk version is slightly wrong: Tuesday still has 12 hours 13 minutes of daylight (sunrise 6:55, sunset 7:08), because the sun is a disc and the air bends its light. The genuinely equal day here falls a few days later.
Tides and aurora are both quiet, fifth morning running — no low below zero anywhere in the eight-day file, every flag null, and the lows that do go low all fall between 2 and 4 in the morning. Nothing to pounce on.
So the nature pick is a talk instead. Marina Richie on Feathered Forest: Aloft with Birds in Ancient Trees — the old-growth forests of this region read upward, floor to canopy, with the resident birds as the guides to each layer. She wrote Halcyon Journey, the belted kingfisher book. In conversation with Maria Mudd Ruth, who wrote Rare Bird about the marbled murrelet, the seabird that nests in these same old trees and took ornithologists until 1974 to catch at it.
Wyncote Northwest Forum at Town Hall, 1119 Eighth Avenue. Doors 6:30, talk 7:30, about 75 minutes, so out by a quarter to nine. Sliding scale from $10. Part of the venue's environmental series. If you would rather have the novelist: Emily St. John Mandel is in the Great Hall the same night at 7:30 with a new book, $10–128 — the bigger name, the more expensive room, and the one that will sell out.
