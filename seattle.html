This week in Seattle
Ideas & lectures · opens today · $10
University of Washington autumn lecture registration opens this morning
REGISTRATION OPENS TODAY, WED SEP 9  ·  $10 SUGGESTED  ·  EVENTS AT TOWN HALL, DOWNTOWN
The Office of Public Lectures runs the university's free-to-the-city talk series, and its autumn slate opens for registration today. Two of the three are the ones to take: Paola Ramos, the Emmy-winning journalist, in conversation with the university's Sophia Jordán Wallace on Latino voter engagement and the demographic shifts reshaping American elections — Wednesday October 14, 6:30pm at Town Hall, and Pamela Ronald, the University of California Davis plant geneticist behind the flood-tolerant rice work, on science, climate and the future of food — Monday October 26, 6:30pm, also Town Hall. Both are $10 suggested rather than free, and both livestream if the night goes sideways.
New on the page since yesterday, and the one closest to your own interests: the Doug Walker Reception and Lecture on Wednesday October 21, 5:30 to 7:45pm in the Town Hall Forum, given by Phil Levin, who directs the university's School of Marine and Environmental Affairs — Puget Sound ecology from the person who runs the program. That one is a reception-plus-lecture format and does not list the September 9 registration date, so it may be on a separate ticket; worth a look at the same time.
These sell through quickly when the speaker is a name, and a 6:30 start at Town Hall puts you out the door by about eight. Do this one with coffee this morning, not tonight.
Nature & water · free · this morning · last of the series
The last usable low tide of this series, on the last warm day of this stretch
TODAY, 10:04AM  ·  −0.9 FT  ·  FREE  ·  ANY PUGET SOUND BEACH
The tide table has been sliding all week and today is where it stops being worth the trip. Low water at 10:04 this morning, −0.9 ft below mean lower low water, at the Seattle gauge — a mid-morning hour rather than the 9am-ish scramble earlier in the week, and it lands on 75° and mostly sunny, the warmest, brightest day in the seven-day file. Tomorrow's low is only −0.3 ft, Friday's is +0.5 ft, and the series does not get back below zero in daylight for the rest of the file.
This is self-guided. The Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalist program — the volunteers who stand on the sand and name things for you — finished for the season in July, so nobody is meeting you there. Constellation Park in West Seattle, Golden Gardens in Ballard and Carkeek Park all expose good rock and cobble at this level. Go about an hour before the low and let the water walk out ahead of you.
Indoor fallback is not needed today — it is 3% — but if this morning gets away from you, the same sunny hours are just as good spent on the water: the Ballard Locks fish ladder is free and walkable, and coho are in their run.
Ideas & lectures · tonight · $10–35 sliding
Casey Michel on how oligarchy actually gets built, tonight at 7:30
TONIGHT, WED SEP 9, 7:30PM (DOORS 6:30, OUT BY ~8:45)  ·  $10–35 SLIDING  ·  MEHDI READING ROOM, TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
Michel is the reporter who has spent a decade on dark money and cross-border corruption — the plumbing of shell companies, anonymous trusts and the American states that sell them. His new book, United States of Oligarchy, traces how the country's wealthiest have moved from buying influence to holding office, and how that lines up with dictators abroad. He is in conversation with Steve Scher, the longtime local public-radio interviewer, which usually means a real interrogation rather than a book reading.
Sliding scale $10 to $35, doors at 6:30, and the format runs about 75 minutes, so you are on the sidewalk by a quarter to nine. Two more the same week, same time, same price: Thursday, novelist Charles Johnson (Middle Passage) with the cartoonist David Horsey on how drawing and writing feed each other; Friday, Angela Duckworth in the Great Hall on her new book Situated, which partly walks back Grit — her argument now is that context beats grinding. Friday is the night rain arrives, so that is the one to buy if you only pick one. (The Timothy White Eagle show listed for Thursday at 7pm is still canceled.)
Nature & sky · free · the window moved
The new moon is Thursday and the meteors peak tonight — but the clear sky does not show up until Sunday
DARK-SKY WINDOW THU–MON  ·  FREE  ·  BEST NIGHTS: SUN SEP 13 AND MON SEP 14
Two natural clocks are running and they do not line up with the weather. New moon is Thursday September 10 at 8:29pm, which normally makes Thursday, Friday and Saturday the darkest nights of the month. And the September epsilon Perseids peaked this morning around 11am our time — a modest shower, roughly eight meteors an hour at best from a radiant in Perseus, active through September 21, with calculations suggesting Earth may clip an older debris trail early Thursday for a brief bump.
The problem is cloud. Tonight is mostly cloudy, Thursday night mostly cloudy, Friday night brings rain after 11pm, Saturday night 27%. The two free Seattle Astronomical Society public star parties fall exactly in that hole — Duvall, Big Rock Ball Fields, Friday 7:30pm and Snoqualmie Point Park, Friday's sibling on Saturday at 7:30pm — and for the third morning running this brief has to say the same thing: do not drive out for either.
What to do instead: the file clears late. Sunday night is 5% and partly cloudy; Monday night is 3%. The moon will be a three- and four-day crescent by then, setting early and leaving the sky dark by ten — still deep in the shower's active window, and one of the last months this year with the Milky Way core up. Point at Sunday or Monday night, from anywhere with an open horizon and no streetlight.
Aurora is over: after Tuesday night's storm the planetary K-index is back down to 2.7 to 3.0 through Friday — nothing to see.
Music · free · the wet-Saturday answer
Three days of free drum and dance in Columbia City, mostly under a roof
FRI–SUN SEP 11–13  ·  FREE, DONATIONS WELCOME  ·  COLUMBIA CITY, THREE VENUES
Saturday has now degraded three mornings in a row in this brief — 13%, then 36%, now 51% and light rain, high 68° — and this is the pick that does not care. The Seattle World Percussion Society's annual festival is a hundred-plus workshops and performances across African, Middle Eastern, Latin, Indian, Brazilian and North American traditions, pitched at everyone from complete beginner to working player. Admission is free all three days; donations welcome.
Three venues, all within a few blocks: Columbia Park at 4721 Rainier Avenue South (the outdoor one), Rainier Arts Center at 3515 South Alaska Street and Southside Commons at 3518 South Edmunds Street — the last two indoors, which is the whole point this weekend. The Columbia City light rail station is blocks away, so no parking problem and no driving in the rain.
Take a workshop rather than watch one. This is a participation festival first and a concert second, and the beginner sessions are genuinely for beginners.
Ideas · $10 · saturday, indoors
A Hugo-winning novelist and a working astronaut, on what comes after the dystopias — ten dollars
SAT SEP 12, 7PM  ·  $10 IN PERSON, $5 STREAM  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
New detail since yesterday: this one is $10, which for the pairing is close to free. Mary Robinette Kowal, who wrote The Calculating Stars, and Dr Sian Proctor, the geoscientist who flew as pilot on the first all-civilian orbital mission, talk about where the world's space programs actually stand and what futures are plausible from here — moderated by K Wilson. It is part of the Clarion West writing workshop's conversation series pairing science-fiction authors with the scientists whose work they borrow. There is a $150 reception ticket; ignore it, the $10 seat is the same conversation.
The earlier alternative, if 7pm on a Saturday is already late: the Ray Skjelbred Trio at the Royal Room in Columbia City — hot Chicago piano, 1920s and '30s material played straight, with Jacob Zimmerman on clarinet and Matt Weiner on bass. Doors 5pm, show 5:30 to 7:30, and that end time is printed on the listing rather than hoped for. $20 in advance online only, $25 at the door; the ticket does not hold you a seat, so turn up at doors. Same neighborhood as the drum festival — the two make one dry evening.
Music & food · free · sunday is the weekend's good day
Live Aloha Hawaiian Cultural Festival — two stages of music and hula, free, and the weather turns for it
SUN SEP 13, 11AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  SEATTLE CENTER (ARMORY, MURAL AMPHITHEATRE, FISHER ROOFTOP)
One of the free Seattle Center Festál cultural festivals, and this year's theme is Mālama i ka ʻike o nā kūpuna — care for the knowledge of the elders. Two stages run Hawaiian music and hula all day, headed by Hoʻokena with guests including the singer and actor Ocean Kaowili and kumu hula Nani Dudoit. There is a food hall's worth of Hawaiian cooking in the Armory, a marketplace, and hands-on lei-making with fresh flowers.
Weather call: Sunday is 69°, partly sunny, 18% with a slight rain chance between 11am and 5pm — the best outdoor window of the weekend by a wide margin. Indoor fallback is built in: the Armory Food and Event Hall and the Fisher Rooftop are both covered, so a shower moves the day rather than ending it.
And for the same evening, if you want the early version: the Columbia City Beatwalk runs 4pm to midnight on Ferdinand Festival Street — free, family-friendly, live music spilling out of the venues and onto the street, themed “Anything Goes.” This is the last Beatwalk of the summer, and the first hours are the good ones for a nine o'clock bedtime.
Music · free · thirty minutes · daytime
Altın Gün play the KEXP studio today, free and open to walk-ins
TODAY AND THURSDAY  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, SEATTLE CENTER
The listener-supported Seattle radio station opens its live in-studio sessions to walk-in audiences at no charge, and they run about half an hour — a whole musical outing that costs nothing and fits in a lunch break. Today it is Altın Gün, the Amsterdam band that plays 1970s Turkish Anatolian rock with fuzz guitar and saz, and Thursday is Shabazz Palaces — Ishmael Butler's project, and the most interesting hip-hop this city has produced in twenty years. Next up after that: anaiis on Tuesday September 15.
Same two caveats as last week, still true: the station publishes no capacity and no guest list, so it is a turn-up-early proposition, and the events page no longer prints start times — check the listing the morning of. The Gathering Space is on the Second Avenue North side of Seattle Center, five minutes from the Live Aloha festival grounds.
Still quiet, sixth morning: the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife has posted no new daily coho count at the Ballard Locks since the 463-fish day on September 3. The season total holds at 972. Peak is still the last two weeks of September.
