NEW · TONIGHT · NATURE TALK · 6:30PM · $18
For the Love of Bats, with naturalist Niki Desautels
THU OCT 22  ·  DOORS 6, PROGRAM 6:30–8:30  ·  $18 ($15 MEMBERS)  ·  MOUNTAINEERS SEATTLE PROGRAM CENTER, 7700 SAND POINT WAY NE, MAGNUSON PARK
The Mountaineers’ BeWild speaker series, launching Desautels’ new field guide to the West’s bats (all 32 species found west of the Rockies). She is a nocturnal-education specialist on the board of Bats Northwest, the regional bat research and conservation group, and the evening is about what bats actually do in a healthy ecosystem — timed for Halloween, but a real natural-history talk. 136 of 300 seats were open this morning. Advance ticket sales close at noon today; after that it’s at the door, space permitting. Out by 8:30, ten minutes from Wedgwood.
Same night, in Wedgwood: Fiddler’s Inn, the 1933 neighborhood tavern, has its Thursday live music (Igor Abuladze’s Road to Home; no time published, 206-525-0752).
FRIDAY · CITY & PARKS · 5–7:30PM · FREE · MOSTLY SUNNY, 56°
Lantern Fest in Freeway Park
FRI OCT 23  ·  5–7:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  UPPER LAWNS, 6TH AVE AND SENECA ST, DOWNTOWN
The park built over Interstate 5 — Lawrence Halprin’s 1976 concrete canyon — closes its 50th-anniversary season with lanterns, live music and free food; still on the park association’s events page this morning. Friday is the best weather of the week (1% rain chance, partly cloudy evening, chilly toward 41° overnight, so bring a layer). The nearly full moon rises with Saturn just below it in the east-southeast as the event wraps up.
Indoor fallback: the free Freddy Fuego Orchestra open rehearsal, 5–7pm, Seattle JazzED, 380 Boren Ave N, South Lake Union.
NEW · SATURDAY · IN YOUR LANE · 7:30–10PM · $28–31
Bedouine, with Other Delights, at Ballard Homestead
SAT OCT 24  ·  7:30–10PM  ·  $28–31  ·  ALL AGES  ·  BALLARD HOMESTEAD, 6541 JONES AVE NW, BALLARD
The Syrian American singer-songwriter: soft, jazz-tinged voice over fingerpicked guitar, string swells and a little bossa nova — the hushed-folk side of your lane, near Aldous Harding and Iron & Wine, with a 70s Joni Mitchell warmth. She has opened for Fleet Foxes and Father John Misty; her new record is Neon Summer Skin. The Homestead is a converted church with good acoustics, a rainy-night room. Runs to 10, so it’s a late one; the opener, Other Delights (a self-described “lounge/exotica” band that doubles as her backing group), is the first hour.
Earlier Saturday, 5pm: Myra Melford’s Splash, exploratory jazz piano trio, Royal Room, Columbia City, $44 — home by 7. The Earshot Jazz Festival ends Sunday.
SUNDAY · BRANCH-OUT OF THE WEEK · 7:30PM · $25 ADVANCE
Newberry & Verch: Ozarks banjo meets Canadian fiddle
SUN OCT 25  ·  DOORS 6:30, SHOW 7:30  ·  $25 ADVANCE / $30 DOOR, $10 UNDER 18  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
