Minus 1.9 feet at 11:52 this morning — then the beach closes for the month
TODAY 11:52AM (−1.9 FT)  ·  FREE  ·  MOSTLY SUNNY, 80°
This is the last one. Tomorrow's midday low is −0.9, Saturday's is +0.4, and from Sunday on the daytime low never goes below two feet again this month — the tide table rolls the deep lows into the middle of the night and keeps them there. So the mid-morning −1.9 today, on an 80° mostly sunny Thursday, is the closing hour of the summer's tidepooling season. Golden Gardens and Carkeek are the walkable ones from Ballard; Lincoln Park in West Seattle exposes the widest bench of rock. Show up around 11 and follow the water out rather than arriving at the bottom of the tide. Self-guided — the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalist season closed out earlier in August — so the etiquette is on you: step on bare rock, lift nothing you can't put back exactly as you found it.

Seattle Design Festival Block Party — two days, free, on the water
SAT AUG 15 & SUN AUG 16, 10AM–7PM  ·  FREE, NO REGISTRATION  ·  LAKE UNION PARK, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The open, unticketed heart of the festival run by the local chapter of the American Institute of Architects: a two-day street fair of walk-in installations, pop-up experiences that run half an hour to two hours, and a mainstage of short talks from design leads. This year's theme is IN[FLUX] — the city changing under you and what design does about it, which is close enough to the civic-software problem to be worth two hours of your Saturday. The weather is exactly what this event wants: mostly sunny and 78° Saturday, 79° Sunday, and it's all outdoors on the Lake Union shoreline, with the Center for Wooden Boats on the same waterfront if you want a free Sunday sail before the tents. Wet-weather fallback isn't needed this weekend; if you'd rather do it indoors and small, the same festival's evening mixers are Tuesday and Wednesday.

Two nights left on the Perseids — and a pre-dawn line of planets to go with them
TONIGHT & FRI, AFTER MIDNIGHT TO FIRST LIGHT  ·  FREE  ·  MOSTLY CLEAR, LOW 60°
The peak was Wednesday morning, but the Perseids hold above half their peak rate through August 14 and then fall off sharply — so tonight and Friday night are the last two worth setting an alarm for, and the American Meteor Society's own guidance is that after the 15th it isn't worth a special trip. The moon turned new on Wednesday, so there's still no skyglow to fight, and the National Weather Service has both nights mostly clear with lows near 60°. Same rules as always: after midnight only, twenty minutes of dark adaptation, no phone screen, lie back instead of craning. Stay out to the very end and you get the second half of it — the pre-dawn sky this month has Mercury, Jupiter and Saturn strung out along the ecliptic with Mars and the two ice giants filling the gaps, best in the half hour to hour before sunrise. Discovery Park's bluff and the north end of Magnuson are the darkest in-city options; Rattlesnake Ridge or anything east of the foothills is a different sky.

Chinook peak at the Ballard Locks fish ladder — the best two weeks of the year, now
DAILY, VIEWING ROOM 7AM–9PM  ·  FREE  ·  HIRAM M. CHITTENDEN LOCKS, BALLARD
The sockeye run is over for the year, but the last two weeks of August are the peak of the chinook return, and a good day pushes better than 400 fish up the ladder — full-size kings holding in the current a foot from the glass in the underwater viewing room. It's free, it's open 7am to 9pm, and it's a fifteen-minute errand from Ballard Avenue, which makes it the highest ratio of spectacle to effort available in the city right now. Two practical notes: the fish move poorly during the deepest low tides, so today's late-morning minus tide is the wrong window and the late afternoon is better; and the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife posts the ladder counts daily, so you can check whether the fish are actually running before you drive over. Warm evening, botanical garden on site, no ticket.

Freeway Park at 50 — the lid-over-the-highway argument, in public
TUE AUG 18, 6PM  ·  FREE, REGISTRATION  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Fifty years of the brutalist park poured on top of Interstate 5 downtown — the first highway lid anywhere, and the direct ancestor of every lid proposal Seattle has argued about since.
