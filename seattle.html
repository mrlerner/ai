Nature & sky · free · tonight · will not repeat
The storm watch held overnight — tonight is the night, and the sky is clear for it
TONIGHT, TUE SEP 8, 8PM ONWARD  ·  FREE  ·  ANYWHERE WITH A DARK, OPEN NORTHERN HORIZON
This is the payoff of the watch that went up over the weekend, and the reason to read this brief before tonight rather than after. The 12:30 UTC issuance this morning from the Space Weather Prediction Center still says G1 (minor) geomagnetic storms are likely through 09 September, driven by a coronal hole high-speed stream plus the two coronal mass ejections that left the sun on the 5th and 6th. The numbers did not soften overnight — if anything the peak sharpened.
This morning's table, converted from UTC to our clock:
Tonight, 8–11pm — K-index 4.67, flagged G1. This is the window that fits a nine o'clock bedtime, and it is fully dark by about 8:15.
Tonight, 11pm–2am — K-index 5.00, the forecast peak.
Wednesday night — back to 3.00 and falling. By Thursday the file is down at 2.00. There is no second chance this week.
Set expectations honestly, because a G1 is a small storm and Seattle sits at 47.6° north. At K-index 5 the realistic result here is a faint green or grey glow low on the northern horizon — often something a phone on a three-second exposure registers before your eyes do. It is not an overhead display. What makes it worth twenty minutes is that everything else lines up for once: the sky is forecast mostly clear at 0% with a low of 55°, and the moon is two days from new, so it does not rise until the small hours and the evening sky is genuinely dark. Clear skies plus a dark moon plus a G1 is a rare three-way, and it is why tonight beats a stronger storm on a cloudy night.
What you need is an unobstructed view north with the city lights behind you, which in practice means getting to water on the north side of town. Golden Gardens in Ballard and the north bluff at Discovery Park both open onto Puget Sound; Magnuson Park works looking straight up Lake Washington. Give your eyes a full fifteen minutes to adjust and keep off your phone screen while they do. The live check before you drive anywhere is the agency's 30-minute aurora forecast, which updates through the evening.
If it busts, it cost you a walk on a 55° beach. Timing and magnitude on coronal-mass-ejection arrivals are the least reliable part of this forecast — the storm can simply show up late.
Nature & sky · water · free · one day only
Wednesday is 75° and the water pulls back to −0.9 ft at ten in the morning — take the morning off
WED SEP 9, LOW AT 10:04AM  ·  FREE  ·  ANY PUGET SOUND BEACH
Two things land on the same morning and neither repeats soon. Wednesday is the warmest and clearest day in the seven-day file — 75°, mostly sunny, 0% rain — and it is also the last comfortable minus tide of this run. The low is −0.9 ft at 10:04am. After that the series collapses: Thursday only reaches −0.3 ft, and by Friday the daily low is above zero at +0.5. Today's low was deeper on paper (−1.0 ft) but it came at 9:12 this morning, which for most people has already gone. Wednesday is an hour later, on a much better day.
Worth saying plainly: at −0.9 ft this is a good tidepooling morning, not a spectacular one — the −2.0 ft extremes that empty the flats out are a summer thing and they are gone for the season. Golden Gardens, Carkeek and Constellation Park in West Seattle all open up usefully at this level. This is self-guided; the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalists ended their season in July, so there is nobody out there to name things for you.
A correction to yesterday's stargazing advice, because the forecast moved. New moon arrives Thursday at 8:29pm, making Thursday through Saturday the darkest nights of the month, and the Seattle Astronomical Society has free public stargazes on Friday at Duvall, 7:30 and Snoqualmie Point Park, Saturday 7:30 — telescopes set up, no ticket. Yesterday this brief recommended taking the Saturday one. Saturday has since gone from 13% to 36% with afternoon thunderstorms, and Friday night now carries a 36% chance of rain too. Neither night is a good bet any more. If you want dark sky this week, tonight's clear 0% is the sky you are getting — which is another argument for the card above.
Quiet note on the salmon, unchanged for a fifth day: the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife has still posted no coho count at the Ballard Locks since Thursday the 3rd's 463-fish day. Season total holds at 972. The viewing room is free, 7am to 8:45pm, and the state still puts the peak in the last two weeks of the month.
Ideas & lectures
Three nights at Town Hall, and Friday's is the one to buy — Angela Duckworth walking back Grit
WED, THU AND FRI  ·  ALL 7:30PM, DOORS 6:30  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Friday, in the Great Hall — Angela Duckworth with Bill Hite. The University of Pennsylvania psychologist who wrote Grit is back with Situated, a book that partly argues against the first one: that personality is less fixed than it looks, that it moves with context, and that choosing your rooms and your people beats grinding harder at yourself. Her phrase for it is the path of reduced effort, and she is candid about her own burnout. Friday is also the 22% rain evening with rain arriving after 5pm — exactly the night to have already bought a ticket to a warm room on First Hill. $10–35 sliding scale.
Ahead of it: Wednesday, Casey Michel on United States of Oligarchy with the local interviewer Steve Scher, on how foreign money moves through American shell companies and lobbying — in the Mehdi Reading Room, the small downstairs space, doors 6:30, up at 7:30, 75 minutes and out by about 8:45. Thursday, Charles Johnson and David Horsey: a National Book Award novelist who also draws, and a two-time Pulitzer-winning editorial cartoonist who also writes, on working in more than one form. Thursday's separately-listed Timothy White Eagle show remains cancelled.
On scheduling: Wednesday is the 75° cloudless day, so spend it outside and skip Michel without guilt. All three follow the house pattern and are over by a quarter to nine.

Music · free · indoors · the wet-weekend answer
A hundred free drum and dance workshops, indoors, on the weekend the weather turns
FRI–SUN SEP 11–13  ·  FREE, DONATIONS WELCOME  ·  COLUMBIA CITY & RAINIER VALLEY
The Seattle World Percussion Society's annual festival, and the answer to a weekend that just went wet. More than a hundred multicultural drum and dance workshops and performances, beginner through advanced, and all of it free — donations welcome, no ticket. Most of it happens under a roof, which is the point this particular weekend.
It runs across three neighbourhood venues, all within a few blocks of each other and walkable from the Columbia City light rail station: the Rainier Arts Center at 3515 S Alaska Street, Southside Commons at 3518 S Edmunds, and outdoor sets at Columbia Park on Rainier Avenue South. The beginner workshops are genuinely for beginners — you sit down at an instrument you have never touched and someone teaches you a pattern.
The weather call: aim Saturday. Saturday is the 36% showers-and-thunderstorms day, which is exactly when a free indoor festival beats anything outdoors. The Columbia Park sets are the part that is weather-dependent; the Rainier Arts Center and Southside Commons programs are not.
Music · small rooms · one of them ends at 7:30
Saturday is a jazz day in the south end — twenty acts on Jackson Street, and a trio in Columbia City with a stated 7:30 finish
SAT SEP 12, FROM 4:30PM  ·  TWO OUTDOOR STAGES FREE / $35 ADV, $40 DOOR INDOORS  ·  CENTRAL DISTRICT & COLUMBIA CITY
The 13th annual Jackson Street Jazz Festival takes over South Jackson Street between the 18th and 30th blocks in the Central District from 4:30pm — twenty acts across twelve stages, spanning jazz, Afro-Latin, funk, blues and soul, with the D'Vonne Lewis Quartet, the Alex Dugdale FADE-Tet, Eugenie Jones and the Joe Brazil Legacy Tribute Band among them. The two outdoor street stages are free. The ten indoor rooms and the rooftop sets need a wristband: $35 in advance, $40 at the door. Weather caveat, and it is a real one this year: Saturday carries a 36% chance of showers and thunderstorms between 11am and 5pm. The free part of this festival is the outdoor part, so check the radar before committing the afternoon.
The rain-proof version of the same evening, twenty minutes south: the Ray Skjelbred Trio at the Royal Room in Columbia City, with Jacob Zimmerman on alto and clarinet and Matt Weiner on bass. Skjelbred plays hot Chicago piano — 1920s and '30s repertoire, New Orleans and early swing, played straight rather than as a museum piece. Doors 5pm, show 5:30 to 7:30, and that end time is printed rather than hoped for, which is rare enough to be the reason to go. $20 in advance, online only — the venue does not sell advance tickets at the door — or $25 on the night. All ages until 10pm, and the ticket does not reserve you a seat, so arrive at doors if you want one.
Music · free · thirty minutes · daytime
Three free in-studio sessions this week, and Thursday's is Shabazz Palaces
TUE, WED AND THU  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, SEATTLE CENTER
The radio station's live in-studio sessions are open to walk-in audiences at no charge, and they run about half an hour — a full musical outing that costs nothing and fits inside a lunch break. This week: Haute & Freddy today, Altın Gün on Wednesday — the Amsterdam band that plays Turkish Anatolian rock from the 1970s with fuzz guitar and a saz — and Shabazz Palaces on Thursday, Ishmael Butler's Seattle project and the most interesting hip-hop to come out of this city in twenty years.
Two practical notes: the station does not publish a capacity or a guest list, so this is a turn-up-early proposition, and it does not publish start times on the events page either — call ahead or check the listing the morning of. The Gathering Space is at Seattle Center, off the Second Avenue North side.
