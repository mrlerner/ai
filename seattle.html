Nature & sky · free · new this morning · won't repeat
A geomagnetic storm watch just went up for Tuesday night — and Tuesday night is the clearest night of the week
TUE SEP 8, DARK ONWARD  ·  FREE  ·  ANYWHERE WITH A LOW, DARK NORTHERN HORIZON
This is the one genuinely new thing in the file today. Every run since Thursday has reported the same dull number — planetary K-index peaking around 3, meaning no aurora at this latitude, don't bother. This morning's 12:30 UTC forecast from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's Space Weather Prediction Center reads differently: G1 (minor) storm conditions are likely, with a slight chance of reaching G2 (moderate), because two coronal mass ejections that left the sun on Friday and Saturday are forecast to arrive. The agency's own note is honest about the catch — “confidence is low on the timing and magnitude of the arrivals.”
The numbers, converted to our clock:
Tonight, 11pm–2am — K-index 4.67, G1. Tonight is partly cloudy, 4% rain.
Tuesday, 8–11pm — K-index 4.67, G1. This is the window that fits a nine o'clock bedtime.
Tuesday, 11pm–2am — K-index 5.00, the forecast peak. Mostly clear, low 56°.
Set expectations correctly, because a G1 is a small storm and Seattle sits at 47.6° north. At K-index 5 the realistic result here is a faint green or grey glow low on the northern horizon — often the kind of thing a phone on a three-second exposure picks up before your eyes do. It is not the 2024 overhead display. What makes it worth the twenty minutes is that everything else lines up: the sky is forecast mostly clear at 0%, and the moon is a thin waning crescent three days from new, so it does not rise until the small hours and the evening sky is genuinely dark.
What you need is an unobstructed view north with no city behind you — which in practice means getting to the water on the north side of town. Golden Gardens and the north bluff at Discovery Park both open onto the Sound with the city lights at your back; Magnuson Park works looking up Lake Washington. Anywhere you can see Whidbey's direction is the right idea. Give your eyes fifteen minutes to adjust and don't look at your phone screen while you wait. The live check before you drive anywhere is the agency's tonight-and-tomorrow view line, which updates through the evening.
Indoor fallback if it busts: it costs you a walk to the beach and nothing else. The storm may simply not arrive on time — that is what the low-confidence note means.
Music · free · outdoors · last date on the calendar
The last free dance in a Seattle park is tomorrow at six
TUE SEP 8, 6–9PM  ·  FREE, NO REGISTRATION  ·  OCCIDENTAL PARK, PIONEER SQUARE
Dancing 'til Dusk is the Seattle Parks and Recreation series that puts a live band and a wooden floor in a different park each summer evening. The published June schedule ended August 30; the organisers then added three September dates, and Tuesday is the last one on the calendar. The closer is Jo Miller and Her Burly Roughnecks, a long-running local country and honky-tonk band.
The format is worth knowing if you have never gone: the first hour is a beginner lesson in that night's style, partners rotate through it, and you do not need to bring one. So a full, complete evening here starts at 6 and is over by 7:30 — you are home well before nine without leaving early. It is free, and it stays free.
Tuesday is 71° and mostly sunny at 0%, which is as good as this gets in September. Occidental Park is the brick-and-plane-tree square in Pioneer Square, a block from the light rail. If it turns, the rain hotline is 206-264-5646. The indoor twin that same night is the Dylan Hayes Octet at the Royal Room in Columbia City, 7:30 (doors 6:30), $20 in advance or $25 at the door — the pianist's eight-piece band releasing Vol. 1, its first record, on a New York label, with Mark Taylor and Xavier Lecouturier in the horn and drum chairs. Or do both: dance at six, drive south at 7:15.
Nature & sky · water · free
Tuesday morning is the lowest water of the week, and Thursday the moon goes out
TUE SEP 8, 9:12AM  ·  THU SEP 10, 8:29PM  ·  FREE
Tuesday's low is −1.0 ft at 9:12am — the only tide in the eight-day window that clears the notable threshold, and the lowest number in the file. It is not an extreme minus tide (that would be −2.0 or below) but it is a genuine one, it lands mid-morning in daylight, and it comes on a 71° mostly sunny day. Around it: Monday was −0.9 at 8:12am, Wednesday is −0.9 at 10:04am on the 75° cloudless day — the second-best of the three, and an hour later, which matters if you would rather not be on a beach at nine. By Thursday it is only −0.3 and by Friday the low is above zero. This is a self-guided walk: the Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalists finished their season in July, so nobody is out there to name things for you.
New moon arrives Thursday at 8:29pm our time, which makes Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights the darkest of the month. That is what makes Tuesday's aurora window above unusually favourable too — a nearly-dead crescent that does not rise until the small hours. The Seattle Astronomical Society has free public stargazes on the two nights after: Duvall Friday 7:30 and Snoqualmie Point Park Saturday 7:30, telescopes set up and no ticket needed.
The weather has moved against Friday since yesterday and it is worth re-planning around: Friday was 18% chance of rain in yesterday's file and is 38% with rain after 5pm in this one. Take the Saturday stargaze — 68°, 21% by day and only 11% overnight, and Snoqualmie Point sits above the valley haze looking east. Useful hour is 8:30 to 9:30.
Quiet note on the salmon, unchanged: the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife's daily coho count at the Ballard Locks has still not posted a number since Thursday the 3rd's 463 fish. Running total 972. The viewing room is free, 7am to 8:45pm, and state counters still put the peak in the last two weeks of the month.
Ideas & lectures
Three nights at Town Hall, and the new one is Angela Duckworth arguing against self-improvement
WED, THU AND FRI  ·  ALL 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Friday is the pick. Angela Duckworth, with Bill Hite, in the Great Hall — the University of Pennsylvania psychologist who wrote Grit, back with a book called Situated that partly walks the first one back. Her argument now is that grit is necessary and often insufficient, that personality is less fixed than it looks and shifts with context, and that choosing your rooms and your people beats grinding harder at yourself. Her line is that the path of reduced effort is frequently the effective one, and she is candid in the book about her own burnout. For someone who spends his week deciding which problems to sit down in front of, this is the more interesting talk of the three. $10–35 sliding scale.
Before it: Wednesday, Casey Michel on United States of Oligarchy with the local interviewer Steve Scher, on how foreign money moves through American shell companies and lobbying. Thursday, Charles Johnson and David Horsey — a National Book Award novelist who also draws, and a two-time Pulitzer-winning editorial cartoonist who also writes, on working in more than one form. (Thursday's Timothy White Eagle show, listed earlier in the week, has been cancelled.)
All three run the house pattern — doors 6:30, up at 7:30, out around 8:45, which is a 75-minute program that respects a nine o'clock bedtime. On scheduling: Wednesday is the 75° cloudless day, so spend that afternoon outside and don't feel bad about skipping Michel. Friday is the 38% rain evening, which is exactly when you want a ticket to a warm room on First Hill.
Music · free · all weekend · new find
A hundred drum and dance sessions in Columbia City, and every one of them is free
FRI–SUN SEP 11–13  ·  FREE, DONATIONS WELCOME  ·  FIVE VENUES AROUND RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
The Seattle World Percussion Society's World Rhythm Festival is one of the better-kept free things in the city and it has not appeared in this brief before. Over three days it puts more than a hundred drum and dance workshops and performances into five buildings within a few blocks of each other, explicitly graded beginner through advanced — meaning you can walk into a West African drumming or Brazilian percussion session having never held a stick, sit down, and be handed one.
The venues are the Rainier Arts Center (3515 S Alaska St, Friday night and Saturday), the Royal Room (Friday night), Columbia Library and New Freeway Hall on Rainier Avenue South (Saturday and Sunday), and Southside Commons (Sunday). Admission is free at all of them; donations are welcome and that is the whole ask.
This is almost entirely indoors, which makes it the right answer to a weekend that starts wet — Friday is 67° with rain after 5pm at 38%, Saturday 68° and 21%, Sunday 71° and mostly sunny at 7%. Take Saturday or Sunday during the day and it costs you nothing but parking; the Columbia City light rail station is a short walk from all five. Sunday's session at Southside Commons is the one to pair with the sunny afternoon.
Music · partly free · outdoors
Twelve stages down Jackson Street on Saturday, and the two outdoor ones cost nothing
SAT SEP 12, FROM 4:30PM  ·  OUTDOOR STAGES FREE / $35 ADV, $40 DOOR FOR INDOORS  ·  S JACKSON ST, 18TH–30TH, CENTRAL DISTRICT
Thirteenth year of this, on the stretch of Jackson Street that was Seattle's Black music district from the 1940s into the 1960s — the clubs that Quincy Jones and Ray Charles came up through. Twenty acts across twelve stages, jazz alongside Afro-Latin, funk, blues and soul: the Alex Dugdale FADE-Tet, the D'Vonne Lewis Quartet, Eugenie Jones, the Joe Brazil Legacy Tribute Band, SERICA.
The two outdoor street stages are free and unticketed. The $35 advance wristband ($40 at the door) is what opens the ten indoor rooms and the rooftop sets. Given a 4:30 start, the free version is a genuinely good evening on its own — arrive at 4:30, three hours of music on the street, home by eight.
Saturday is 68°, partly sunny, 21% before 5pm — a light risk right at the start, and the indoor rooms are the built-in fallback if it opens up. Centred on the Pratt Fine Arts Center at 1902 S Main St. If you would rather sit down, the same evening has the Ray Skjelbred Trio at the Royal Room — doors 5, and the show is billed to run 5:30 to 7:30 with a stated end time, which is a rare and civilised thing for a jazz gig. $20 advance, $25 door.
Music · free · daytime
Three free in-studio sessions this week, and Altın Gün is the one to catch
TUE 3PM, WED 11AM, THU 3PM  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, NORTHWEST ROOMS, SEATTLE CENTER
The radio station's live sessions are broadcast from a room with public seating and no ticket. Roughly thirty minutes each, and the station publishes no capacity and keeps no list — you turn up. This week: Haute & Freddy, Tuesday 3pm; Altın Gün, Wednesday 11am, the Amsterdam band that plays Turkish Anatolian rock from the 1970s on electric instruments and is a much bigger touring proposition than a free morning session would suggest; Shabazz Palaces, Thursday 3pm, Ishmael Butler's long-running Seattle experimental hip-hop project. (Death Cab for Cutie played this morning at 11.)
A word on the trade: Wednesday is the 75° cloudless day, and an 11am session costs you the middle of it. Altın Gün is worth the half hour, and the Northwest Rooms are a two-minute walk from the Seattle Center grounds — so make it the pivot of an outdoor day rather than the alternative to one. There is a −0.9 ft low at 10:04 that same morning if you would rather be on a beach.
