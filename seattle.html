TONIGHT · IDEAS · 6:30PM · $10 · HOME BY 8
Pamela Ronald: the rice that survives a flood
MON OCT 26  ·  6:30PM  ·  $10  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL (ALSO LIVESTREAMED)
Tonight’s University of Washington public lecture. Ronald, a University of California, Davis plant geneticist, found the gene that lets rice sit underwater for two weeks and live; the variety now feeds millions of small farmers in flood-prone Asia. Her subject is how crops get bred for drought, flood and disease, and what it takes to move a discovery from the lab bench to a field. The Stranger’s editors made it their Monday talk pick this morning. Evening is mostly dry (20–28% rain until 9).
Later, if you’re up: The Tubs, a London band playing jangly guitar pop with fiddle on their first US tour (think Teenage Fanclub with a Celtic streak), 8pm at Baba Yaga in Pioneer Square. No official ticket page found; resale only.
NEW · CITY HISTORY · CLOSES FRIDAY · FREE · WEEKDAYS 8:30–4
The Rise and Fall of the Kingdome
MON–FRI THROUGH OCT 30  ·  8:30AM–4PM  ·  FREE  ·  KING STREET CENTER, 201 S JACKSON ST, PIONEER SQUARE
A pop-up photo exhibit from the King County Archives, in the lobby of the county office building next to the old stadium site: historic photographs of the dome going up in the 1970s, the games and concerts it held, and its implosion in March 2000. A small piece of how Seattle builds and unbuilds its big civic projects, and the kind of public-records show a civic-software builder tends to enjoy. Office hours only, and the last day is Friday, so it fits a lunch break on one of the rainy weekdays. Five minutes from King Street Station.
