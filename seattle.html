This Week in Seattle
TONIGHT, THE RAINY ONE — THREE TECH-AND-CITIES TALKS AT ONCE
Two Cities, One Industry: how Big Tech rebuilt Seattle and San Francisco
TUE SEP 29, 6–8PM  ·  $20 ($15 MEMBERS)  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
Last call: this is the night. A live taping of two podcasts with Margaret O’Mara (the University of Washington historian who wrote The Code), GeekWire co-founder John Cook and Jonathan Weber (City on the Edge) on what the industry did to both cities’ housing, politics and civic life. Still on the museum’s calendar this morning at $20. Six o’clock start on your side of town, home by 8:15. The free alternative, same hour: JS Tan and Clarissa Redwine with Kshama Sawant, Elliott Bay Book Company, Capitol Hill, 7–8:30pm, no ticket needed. Their book Against Tech Oligarchy is the inside story of the tech-worker movement (union drives, walkouts over military contracts) and the owner backlash that followed; Redwine helped organize the Kickstarter union. And the water-life option: From Awe to Action: The Salmon Run, Town Hall, 7:30pm, $10–35 sliding. Photographer Amy Gulick (Salmon in the Trees, The Salmon Way; she lives on an island in Puget Sound) with Save Our Wild Salmon and the Columbia Snake River Campaign on restoring runs. Seventy-five minutes, out by 8:45. (The Lizzie Steiner solo-harp release at the Good Shepherd Center chapel is also tonight, 7pm, $11–28, still on sale.)

Gyetk: a Ts’msyen cookbook built on the salmon, shellfish and seaweed year
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, MEMBERS FREE  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
Spencer and Rachel Greening on their North Coast cookbook, organized around the harvest calendar. Still listed for Wednesday on the Burke calendar (the page shows its usual unreliable “in the past” banner). Do the outdoors bit first: Wednesday is 1% rain and 21% cloud from 5 to 7pm, and EverOut lists the Wallingford Farmers Market (Meridian Park, 3–7pm) as running through September, which makes this its last Wednesday of the season. Sunset 6:52. Same-hour alternatives: Francine Berman at Town Hall, 7:30pm, $10–35, on running social media, chatbots and search as critical infrastructure rather than products (civic tech, 75 minutes); Helen Whybrow at Elliott Bay, 7pm, free, reading from The Salt Stones, a shepherd’s year on a Vermont hill farm, longlisted for the National Book Award; and Claudia Castro Luna, Seattle’s first Civic Poet, launching her memoir at the Central Library, 7pm, free.
THURSDAY — FILM, AND IT’S ABOUT CITIES: JOHN WILSON ON CONCRETE
The History of Concrete
THU OCT 1, 7PM  ·  $15 ($10 STUDENT/SENIOR, $7 MEMBERS)  ·  NORTHWEST FILM FORUM, CAPITOL HILL  ·  101 MIN, OUT ABOUT 8:45
The new feature from John Wilson, the How To with John Wilson documentarian: he sets out to make the definitive film about concrete, the world’s most-used building material and the bedrock of his New York, then decides to tell it with the Hallmark-movie formula he learned at a workshop, and the foundation crumbles. Thursday’s 7pm is the “Truth To Fiction” screening, co-presented with the Seattle Film Society and the Seattle Documentary Association, with a giveaway of a piece of Film Forum history. It then runs daily through Monday (Fri 5pm, Sat and Sun 4 and 7pm) if Thursday is full. Thursday is dry but grey (62% cloud), so indoors costs you nothing. Free the same hour: The Future of Affordable Housing, Town Hall, 7:30pm, $0–35 sliding, Seattle Times housing reporter Heidi Groover with Habitat for Humanity leadership. The big-name one: Kyle MacLachlan with Carrie Brownstein on his memoir Fictional Selves, Seattle Arts & Lectures, 7:30pm at the Edmonds Center for the Arts, $10–128, no run time published. Daytime: Free First Thursday at the Nordic Museum (10am–8pm) and at the Burke, where the 4–7pm STEAM Spotlight has Latine scientists at tables.

SAT OCT 3, 11AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  FISHERMEN’S TERMINAL, 1900 W NICKERSON ST, INTERBAY
