This week in Seattle
Orson Welles's last finished feature, on a 90% rain night, done well before nine
TONIGHT WED SEP 2 — 7:30PM  ·  $15 GENERAL ADMISSION  ·  THE BEACON, 4405 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY (LIGHT RAIL TO COLUMBIA CITY)
The Beacon is the 60-seat repertory cinema above a Columbia City storefront that programs almost nothing you can stream. Tonight is The Immortal Story — Welles's first film in colour and the last fiction feature he actually completed, a short, hallucinatory adaptation of an Isak Dinesen tale, scored throughout with Erik Satie. It is one of the shortest features anyone screens; the house lights are up long before Matt's bedtime.
This is the correct answer to today's weather. Rain arrives after 10am and stays — 90% chance, a tenth to a quarter of an inch, south wind gusting to 21 mph, and the evening stays wet at 60%. There is no outdoor version of tonight worth chasing.
If you would rather go long: the same night has The Saragossa Manuscript, the sprawling Polish nesting-doll epic, at 4pm. And the pairing repeats tomorrow with the times swapped — Immortal Story 4:30pm, Saragossa 7:30pm — so a rainy Thursday has a second chance at it.
The natural history museum is free tomorrow and stays open until eight
THU SEP 3 — 10AM TO 8PM  ·  FREE ADMISSION, NO TICKET NEEDED AT THE DOOR  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS AT 17TH AVE NE & NE 45TH
The Burke — Washington's state museum of natural history and culture, the one with the fossil-prep lab and the herbarium visible behind glass — drops admission on the first Thursday of every month and holds the doors open four hours later than usual. Tomorrow is that day, and it lands on 67° with rain likely at 60%, which is exactly when an all-afternoon indoor building earns its keep.
Two things are on beyond the permanent galleries. STEAM Spotlight: Queers in STEAM runs 4 to 7pm — working scientists, engineers and artists at tables you can actually talk across — and Amber Stephens is in the artist studio 10am to 5pm. The temporary alcove is on Water: Washington waterways back through ancient oceans, and it closes Sep 13.
Free Thursday is the museum's busiest day of the month; late afternoon is calmer than lunchtime, and the 4pm STEAM tables are the reason to time it that way anyway.
Two nights of civics at Town Hall, both sliding-scale, both out by a quarter to nine
FRI SEP 4 AND WED SEP 9 — DOORS 6:30PM, TALK 7:30PM, ABOUT 75 MINUTES  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE ON FIRST HILL
Friday: Congressman Jared Huffman and Skye Perryman, with Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal moderating — two new books on Christian nationalism and the separation of church and state, argued by people who litigate and legislate it rather than write about it from a distance. In the Wyncote Forum, the big downstairs room, entrance off Seneca Street.
New on the calendar since yesterday — Wednesday Sep 9: journalist Casey Michel on United States of Oligarchy, with broadcaster Steve Scher. It is the reporting on how a handful of very rich men accumulated political power and made common cause with authoritarian governments abroad — the structural version of a story usually told as personality. Upstairs in the Mehdi Reading Room, which is small and will go first.
The same Wednesday has a second, earlier one: Known Wishes, on the facts and myths of medical aid in dying, at 6pm. Different room, different subject, and it ends in time to still make the 7:30.
Two free live sessions at the radio station, one Friday afternoon, one Labor Day morning
FRI SEP 4 3PM AND MON SEP 7 11AM  ·  FREE, NO TICKET  ·  KEXP, 472 1ST AVE N AT SEATTLE CENTER
Friday 3pm — OSEES live in the Gathering Space, the glass-walled room off KEXP's lobby that anyone can walk into. Free, all ages, first come first served, no RSVP. John Dwyer's band plays about as loud as a room that size can take, and it is over inside an hour. The same night they headline a winery amphitheatre out in Woodinville for real money; this one costs nothing.
Labor Day 11am — Death Cab for Cutie live on KEXP, in the studio proper (the NW Rooms), listed as open to the public, 11:00 to 11:30. This is the morning after they close Bumbershoot on the Sunday night, which is a different band on four hours' sleep and worth being in the room for. No capacity is published, so treat it as an early-arrival situation.
Both are indoors, both are free, and the Monday one leaves the whole 70° afternoon still ahead of you.
The big one lands on the two sunniest days on the board
SAT SEP 5 AND SUN SEP 6  ·  FROM $70.50 A DAY, $125.50 THE WEEKEND, UNDER-12S FREE WITH AN ADULT  ·  SEATTLE CENTER
Seattle's Labor Day arts festival, spread across the Seattle Center grounds, indoor halls included. Saturday is 69° and mostly sunny at 13% — the lowest rain number anywhere in the seven-day file — and Sunday is 71° and mostly sunny. After two soaked days, that is the argument.
Turnstile takes Saturday's top slot and Death Cab for Cutie closes Sunday; underneath them, the bill runs Japanese Breakfast, Blood Orange, Bikini Kill, Orville Peck, De La Soul, Noname, ATARASHII GAKKO!, Sudan Archives and Silvana Estrada. Headliners land late, but the middle of the afternoon is the good part anyway and nothing stops you leaving at seven.
One catch worth knowing before you buy: re-entry is a paid add-on this year — $20 on a day ticket, $30 on the weekend pass — so the old trick of ducking out for a proper dinner and coming back now has a price.
A called square dance in Ballard that starts at half past six — the earliest downbeat in the room all month
MON SEP 7 (LABOR DAY) — 6:30PM  ·  $12.42  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, 5213 BALLARD AVE NW
The Tractor is the roots-and-Americana room on Ballard Avenue, and almost everything it books starts at eight. This one starts at 6:30: a live square dance with Three Forks of Candy playing and a caller walking the whole room through every figure, which is the point — no partner and no prior idea what a do-si-do is required. Twelve dollars and change.
It is the last night of the long weekend, on a 70° partly sunny Labor Day, twenty minutes from the water. Pair it with a late-afternoon walk at Golden Gardens or Shilshole and be home at a civilised hour.
Rest of the Ballard week if the square dance is not it: Southern Culture on the Skids Friday is sold out; Saturday is a Soundgarden tribute, Sunday is KELS, both 8pm.
Nature & sky
A crescent Moon next to Jupiter before dawn Tuesday, and the week's lowest tide an hour after it
MON SEP 7 AND TUE SEP 8, EARLY MORNING  ·  FREE  ·  ANY EAST-FACING VIEW, THEN ANY PUGET SOUND BEACH
The sky. Before sunrise on Tuesday Sep 8, a thin waning crescent sits about 50 arcminutes from Jupiter — a bit under two Moon-widths — low in the east in Cancer, with Jupiter at magnitude −1.8 and earthshine on the Moon's dark side. Naked eye or binoculars; the pair is too far apart for a telescope to hold both. It is the dress rehearsal for October, when the Moon actually passes in front of Jupiter. Sunday morning has the smaller version: the crescent near Mars, same eastern sky.
The water. The tide table turns over the long weekend. Monday Sep 7: −0.9 ft at 8:12am. Tuesday Sep 8: −1.0 ft at 9:12am — the only tide in the next eight days the script flags as notable, and both fall in full daylight on days forecast at 70°. That is a wide, walkable flat: sea stars, anemones, moon snails, the sand-dollar bed. Constellation Park in West Seattle and Golden Gardens are the two easiest from Ballard.
Do this one yourself — the Seattle Aquarium's beach naturalists finished for the season in the summer and are not out on these dates. Same weekend, at the Ballard Locks: chinook are through 10,785 as of Aug 30 and still moving, while coho stand at only 413 through Aug 31 with the September rows not yet posted. Coho peak late in the month, so this is the build, not the show.
Mark your calendar
Fri Sep 11 – Sun Sep 13 — Wooden Boat Festival, Port Townsend. North America's largest, at the Northwest Maritime Center and Point Hudson Marina: 300 boats in the water and on land, 100-plus talks and demonstrations. 9am–6pm Friday and Saturday, 9am–4pm Sunday; under-12s free with a paying adult. A ferry-and-a-drive day trip — decide this week.

Fri Sep 11 — new Moon, the darkest sky of the month. The Seattle Astronomical Society has free public stargazing at Duvall that night at 7:30pm and at Snoqualmie Point Park on Sat Sep 12 at 7:30pm — both far enough east to actually beat the city glow, unlike the in-town parks.

Wed Sep 16, 6:30pm — David B. Williams, In the Range of Fire and Ice, at the Burke Museum, in conversation with former Seattle Times book critic Mary Ann Gwinn. A human and natural history of the Washington Cascades, by the local geology writer. Ticketed; not a free-Thursday night.

Sun Sep 27, sign-up 1pm — free public sail, Center for Wooden Boats, Lake Union. Volunteer skippers take anyone out on the historic fleet for nothing, 1–5pm, first come first served at the Wagner Education Center. This is the last one of 2026.

Late September — coho peak at the Ballard Locks ladder. The count is barely started; the viewing window is the back half of the month.
