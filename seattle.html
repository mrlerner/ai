THURSDAY — THE WET DAY MOVED FORWARD, AND IT LANDED ON THE BEST TALK OF THE WEEK
Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor on the people betting on collapse — with the Mayor of Seattle asking the questions
THU SEP 24, 7:30PM (OUT BY ~8:45)  ·  $10–50 SLIDING  ·  TOWN HALL GREAT HALL, FIRST HILL
Yesterday Thursday was a 19% afternoon. This morning it is rain likely after eleven, 59% by day, 66% overnight, and 87% cloud cover from five o’clock on. That is a whole day’s worth of movement in one direction, and it settles the question of what to do with the evening: be indoors.
The big one. Klein and Taylor are out on End Times Fascism and the Fight for the Living World — their argument that religious fundamentalists, Silicon Valley technologists and ethno-nationalists have converged on the same conviction, that a cleansing catastrophe is coming and they will be on the right side of it, and that this belief makes them welcome the fire rather than fight it. The angle for you is who is holding the microphone: Mayor Katie Wilson is moderating — a sitting mayor running a public argument about collapse and cities. $10–50 sliding, optional book add-on, doors 6:30.
The timing discovery, which applies to everything at Town Hall this week. Their event pages carry a line I had not read closely before: “Town Hall events are approximately 75 minutes long.” Doors 6:30, start 7:30, finish about 8:45. A 7:30 talk there is not a late night — you are out the door on First Hill before nine. That is true of Klein on Thursday, Richie on Tuesday, and the two on the calendar list at the bottom.
The free alternative, same night: Christopher W. Smith at the Burke Museum, 7pm, doors 6:30, free with registration, on the University of Washington campus. Eighteen years of work reconstructing Samuel Elwitt (1834–1919), a Tsimshian master carver of the Kitselas First Nation whose bowls, spoons and headdresses sit in the British Museum and the American Museum of Natural History under nobody’s name. Their page is still showing a “this event is in the past” banner on a future date — a site fault, but register ahead and confirm rather than turning up.

NATURE & SKY — THE HARVEST MOON, AND A CORRECTION TO WHAT I TOLD YOU SATURDAY
The full moon clears the horizon ten minutes before the sun sets on Saturday — but Sunday’s sky is the clearer one
SAT SEP 26, MOONRISE 6:50PM  ·  SUNSET 7:00:30PM  ·  FREE, FROM ANY EAST-FACING SHORE
Recomputed this morning from the Jet Propulsion Laboratory’s ephemeris, refracted, at five-minute steps for downtown Seattle. The moon is fullest on Saturday — 99.76% lit at midnight Saturday, against 99.52% on Sunday — which makes it the Harvest Moon, the full moon nearest the equinox. Moonrise Saturday is 6:50pm at azimuth 81°, very slightly north of due east. Sunset the same evening is 7:00:30pm. The full moon is therefore up and clear of the horizon ten minutes before the sun goes down, which is the whole reason to bother: you get it large and orange against a sky that is still lit.
The Harvest Moon effect itself, in numbers. Moonrise runs Thursday 6:15pm, Friday 6:30, Saturday 6:50, Sunday 7:05 — gaps of fifteen, twenty and fifteen minutes, against the usual fifty. That near-standstill is the whole phenomenon, and it means four consecutive evenings all have a nearly full moon arriving at a civilised hour, not just Saturday.
Now the correction. On Saturday and again yesterday I told you the 26th was the clear one, on the strength of the daily rain figure — 8%, then 5% this morning. Rain probability is not sky cover, and I should have pulled the sky numbers before calling it. Here they are, for five to nine in the evening: Thursday 87% cloud with 59% rain — write it off. Friday 53% cloud, 30% rain. Saturday 56% cloud, 5% rain. Sunday 48% cloud, 16% rain. Saturday is still the pick, because it is the actual full moon and it is nearly certain to stay dry — but it is a better-than-even chance of broken cloud, not a clear night, and Sunday evening is forecast marginally clearer than Saturday’s with a moon still 99.5% lit rising at 7:05. If Saturday is socked in, Sunday is not a consolation prize.
Where to stand. You want an unobstructed eastern horizon across water, which means the Lake Washington shore: Seward Park, Magnuson, Madrona Beach, Mount Baker Beach. Be in place by 6:30 — the ten minutes either side of moonrise are the whole show, and a low bank of cloud on the eastern horizon will eat it even on an otherwise fine evening. Free, and it wants nothing but a coat.

SUNDAY — THE LAST FREE BOAT RIDE OF THE YEAR
Volunteers give away rides on Lake Union in wooden boats, and this is the final one until April
SUN SEP 27  ·  FREE  ·  SIGN-UP IN PERSON FROM 1PM, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION
The Center for Wooden Boats runs free public sails on Lake Union, and it is monthly rather than weekly — there have been six all year and Sunday is the last of them. April 12, May 10, June 14, July 26, August 30, September 27, and then nothing until spring. Volunteer skippers take passengers out in sprit boats, schooners, ketches, steam and electric launches; rides run about 45 minutes, boats depart between 1:15 and 5pm, four to six boats, 80 to 200 seats.
The catch, in their own words: sign-ups are in person only, from 1pm, in front of the Wagner Education Center, first come first served, and they say plainly they cannot always take everyone who queues. Arrive before one.
The bonus that only applies to this date. Steamship Sunday is the last Sunday of each month, so it falls on the same day: a second queue, also from 1pm, for the Virginia V, the 1922 steamship, last ride at 3pm. Two lines, one for the Center’s own boats and one for the steamer. Weather: 63°, 16% rain after eleven — fine for an afternoon on the water, and the same evening is the clearer of the two moon nights.
Correcting yesterday: I said the Local Sightings film festival ended Saturday. It does not — the Film Forum’s calendar now shows closing night on Sunday the 27th, The Joyless Economy, 7pm. The twenty-ninth edition runs a full ten days.

Benaroya is at 200 University Street, across Second Avenue from the art museum, four minutes from the light rail. 7:30 is a genuinely late start for you, and unlike Town Hall there is no published run time, so plan on being home after ten. It is the one pick this week where that is worth it.
