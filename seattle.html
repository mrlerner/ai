Cedar River Salmon Journey: spawning salmon with Aquarium naturalists
SAT OCT 24  ·  10AM–2PM  ·  FREE, DROP IN  ·  RIVERVIEW PARK AND BELMONDO REACH, CEDAR RIVER, RENTON (SUN: CEDAR GRANGE, 11–2)
Seattle Aquarium naturalists stand along the Cedar River and point out the salmon in the shallows: fish digging nests in the gravel, males with hooked jaws and deep color. This morning’s schedule still lists both sites today; Sunday shrinks to one site, Cedar Grange, and that’s the end of the season. Today is the day to go: 55°, almost no wind, flat grey light that cuts glare on the water (polarized glasses help). Sunday is 63% rain. Indoor fallback: the National Nordic Museum in Ballard, whose exhibit on the Salmon Bay fishing fleet is $20.
TONIGHT · IN YOUR LANE · 7:30–10PM · $28–31
Bedouine at Ballard Homestead
SAT OCT 24  ·  7:30–10PM  ·  $28–31  ·  ALL AGES, MOSTLY SEATED  ·  BALLARD HOMESTEAD, 6541 JONES AVE NW, BALLARD
Azniv Korkejian’s songs are soft-voiced, played on fingerpicked guitar with strings behind them. The new album, Neon Summer Skin, came out of a family visit to Saudi Arabia. The venue is a converted church, mostly seated, and it serves no alcohol. It sits next to Aldous Harding and Iron & Wine. It runs until 10, but Other Delights opens, so you can arrive around 8:30 and still hear her whole set.
The early alternative: Myra Melford’s Splash, a piano, bass and drums trio that improvises freely, 5pm at the Royal Room in Columbia City, $44. It’s one of the last Earshot Jazz Festival shows, and you’d be home by 7.
SUNDAY · BRANCH-OUT OF THE WEEK · 7:30PM · $25 ADVANCE · INDOORS FOR THE RAIN
Newberry & Verch, old-time banjo and fiddle
SUN OCT 25  ·  DOORS 6:30, SHOW 7:30  ·  $25 ADVANCE ONLINE / $30 DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Two players, two-part harmony: Joe Newberry on banjo and guitar, from the Missouri Ozarks, and April Verch on fiddle, an Ontario player who also step-dances. They play ballads and square-dance tunes, and the Seattle Folklore Society is co-presenting. It sounds like a kitchen session and sits next to The Band at their most rural. It’s also the Fiddler’s Inn sound, in a seated room. Sunday is the rainy day, so it fits. No end time is published.

MONDAY · IDEAS · 6:30PM · $10
Pamela Ronald: what feeds us in a hotter world
MON OCT 26  ·  6:30PM, OUT ABOUT 7:45  ·  $10  ·  TOWN HALL, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL (ALSO LIVESTREAMED)  ·  UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON PUBLIC LECTURES
This is the University of Washington’s public lecture of the week, held at Town Hall. Ronald is a plant geneticist at the University of California, Davis. She found the gene behind a rice that survives two weeks underwater, and millions of farmers now plant it. Her talk is about what crop science can do as floods and heat get worse, and what it can’t. $10, still listed this morning; home well before 9.
