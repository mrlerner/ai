Festival of Fruit at Piper’s Orchard, Carkeek Park
SUN OCT 4, 11AM–2PM  ·  FREE  ·  CARKEEK PARK, HALF-MILE WALK IN  ·  FRIENDS OF PIPER’S ORCHARD, 17TH YEAR
Re-read this morning, the orchard’s page still says “Sunday, October 4, 2026 from 11 AM to 2 PM” and promises “song & dance, fresh cider, tasting local apples, and celebration of community,” plus an apple-identification table if you have a mystery tree. It is the oldest public orchard in Seattle, planted by the Piper family in the 1890s and rediscovered under blackberry in the ravine; you cannot drive to it. Walk up along Piper’s Creek from the main park entrance, or take the steep short trail down from Viewlands Elementary. The forecast is the reason to go today rather than file it: 71°, 0% rain, 11–14% cloud through the afternoon, and the week turns wet Friday.

MONDAY · IDEAS · CITIES · FREE
Michael Pollack, Sidewalk Nation: a Week Without Driving book talk
MON OCT 5, 6:30–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  HUSKY UNION BUILDING, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
Second day on the list, and still the civic pick of the week. Pollack teaches property and land-use law at Cardozo; the organizer’s own text calls the book “a vivid travelogue spanning more than a dozen cities and towns” on who owns, pays for and polices the sidewalk: disability access, whose job it is to clear snow, the First and Fourth Amendment fights that happen on concrete, and the property, tax and zoning law underneath. His argument is that local governments should carry both the obligation and the funding. Pegged to the Oct 1–8 Week Without Driving, the Disability Rights Washington campaign that asks drivers to spend a week as non-drivers. Out by 8; the light rail stops a block away.

Same night, both 7:30: Blue Monday #4 at the Royal Room, doors 6:30, $20 advance / $25 door, with Del Rey’s resonator-guitar ragtime and prewar blues and the Kings of Mongrel Folk (Mark Graham and Orville Johnson), the fingerpicking root system under your Steve Gunn and Nick Drake shelf; and Act Now: Saving Our Orcas at Town Hall, $10–35, Lynda Mapes moderating a panel of the researchers and Lummi leaders working on the 74 remaining Southern Residents, 75 minutes, out by 8:45.
