This Week in Seattle
TODAY — LAST ONE UNTIL SPRING, ON THE CLEAREST AFTERNOON OF THE WEEK
Free wooden-boat ride on Lake Union, then the moon over Lake Washington
SUN SEP 27  ·  FREE  ·  SIGN-UP FROM 1PM, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION  ·  MOONRISE 7:05PM
1pm, Lake Union. The Center for Wooden Boats (the South Lake Union boat museum) still lists September 27 as the last 2026 public sail date, checked this morning. Volunteer skippers take you out for about 45 minutes on anything from sprit boats to schooners. Sign-up is in person, first come, from 1pm in front of the Wagner Education Center, and the museum says to arrive early. Boats go out between 1:15 and 5. Cloud falls from 25% at noon to 11% at 2 and 6% by 4, so a later slot is the sunnier one.
7pm, Lake Washington. Sunset is 6:58. The moon, 97.5% lit and a day past full, rises at 7:05pm in the east-northeast (azimuth 71.5°). That's from this morning's Jet Propulsion Laboratory ephemeris run and agrees with Thursday's. The hourly forecast is 2% cloud at 6pm and 5% at 7. Go to an east-facing shore (Seward Park, Magnuson or Madrona) by 6:50 and you'll be home well before nine. Fallback: none needed today. Monday evening is 50–65% cloud, so today is the chance.
MONDAY — SEATTLE ARTS & LECTURES OPENS ITS SEASON FOR THIS BRIEF
George Saunders: a climate ghost story at a CEO's deathbed
MON SEP 28, 7:30PM  ·  $10–123  ·  BENAROYA HALL, TAPER AUDITORIUM, DOWNTOWN
Tomorrow night. In Vigil, the Lincoln in the Bardo author sends a spirit who has eased 343 people into death to an oil executive's bedside, and he's the first one who regrets nothing. After the talk, Jeff Martin leads a question-and-answer session. The $10 tier gets you a real seat. Seattle Arts & Lectures publishes no run time, so plan on roughly nine as an estimate, not a listed time. Monday stays dry until after midnight. Shorter option at the same hour: Sonali Kolhatkar, Breaking ICE at Town Hall, $10–35 sliding scale, out by about 8:45.
TUESDAY — THE RAINY NIGHT (68%), SO GO INDOORS
Two Cities, One Industry: Big Tech, Power, and Politics in Seattle and San Francisco
TUE SEP 29, 6–8PM  ·  $20, $15 MEMBERS  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
Two days out, and still the week's best match for the civic software you build. It's a joint live taping of Blue City Blues and GeekWire's podcast. Historian Margaret O'Mara (University of Washington, The Code), GeekWire co-founder John Cook and Jonathan Weber (City on the Edge) talk about how the industry reshaped housing and politics in both cities. It's a short drive in the rain and over by eight.
Free music instead: Lizzie Steiner, a solo harp album release, 7–8:30pm in the Good Shepherd Center chapel, Wallingford. It's free with an Eventbrite reservation. Or salmon: From Awe to Action: The Salmon Run, Town Hall 7:30, $10–35, with photographer Amy Gulick and the Columbia–Snake River campaigners.
WEDNESDAY — COAST FOOD KNOWLEDGE, OR TECH AS INFRASTRUCTURE
Gyetk at the Burke: the Ts'msyen harvest year, recipe by recipe
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, FREE FOR MEMBERS  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON
Spencer and Rachel Greening organize their North Coast cookbook around the seasons: salmon, shellfish and seaweed, and the harvesting law that governs each one. SATURDAY, DAYTIME — FREE, ON THE WATER, 0% RAIN
Fishermen's Fall Festival: the North Pacific fleet comes home to Interbay
SAT OCT 3, 11AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  FISHERMEN'S TERMINAL, 1900 W NICKERSON ST
The Seattle Fishermen's Memorial Foundation page confirms October 3, and admission is free. The festival marks the return of the Alaska fishing fleet to Fishermen's Terminal, a mile from Ballard, with seafood, live music and Ship Canal boat tours (listed at 11:45, 1:30 and 4). The best thing to watch is the survival-suit races at Dock 9, 3pm. Money from the activities goes to the families of fishers lost at sea. The forecast is 0% rain and 36% cloud all afternoon. Fallback: none needed. If plans change, it's Saturday only, with no second day.

SAT OCT 3, 7–11PM  ·  FREE  ·  PARAMOUNT PARK, 15300 8TH AVE NE, SHORELINE  ·  SEATTLE ASTRONOMICAL SOCIETY
