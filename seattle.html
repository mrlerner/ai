This week in Seattle
Tonight the zoo lawn closes for the year, and the forecast spent its one perfect day on it
TONIGHT, THU AUG 20  ·  GATES 4:30PM, MUSIC 6PM  ·  FROM $47, ONLINE ONLY  ·  WOODLAND PARK ZOO NORTH MEADOW, PHINNEY RIDGE
The Breeders with Team Dresch is the last night of the zoo's forty-second summer concert season — two bands out of the same early-nineties underground, on grass, doors at 4:30 and music at 6. The National Weather Service has today at 80° and sunny with zero percent rain and a 60° overnight low: the only day in the whole seven-day file with no cloud qualifier on it. This is the 6pm start Matt actually wants, and it is the last one until next June.
Tickets are online only and are not sold at the gate — if it goes, it goes before you leave the house. Free alternative on the same 80°: the Ballard Locks fish ladder, still inside its late-summer chinook window, open until 8:45pm and costing nothing. No indoor fallback needed today.

A week from tonight the moon rises already eclipsed — and you will need a clean eastern horizon to catch it
THU AUG 27  ·  MOONRISE 7:51PM, DEEPEST 9:12PM, OVER 10:52PM  ·  FREE, NO EQUIPMENT  ·  ANY EAST-FACING SHORE
A deep partial lunar eclipse, and the geometry over Seattle is unusual: the partial phase starts at 7:33pm, eighteen minutes before the moon clears the horizon here at 7:51pm, so it comes up already bitten. Sunset is 7:59pm and civil twilight ends at 8:31pm, which means the sky darkens around it exactly as the shadow deepens. Maximum is 9:12pm with 96.2 percent of the disk inside the umbra — almost totality, so nearly the whole face should go copper with one bright sliver left. It leaves the shadow at 10:52pm.
The catch is altitude: at the good part the moon is only a few degrees up in the east-southeast, so trees and ridgelines will eat it. That argues for the west shore of Lake Washington — Madrona, Leschi, Seward Park — or Magnuson looking back across the water, not Golden Gardens or Alki, which face the wrong way. It is naked-eye; binoculars help and nothing else is needed. Realistic bedtime version: it is already deeply eclipsed by 8:45pm, so the good half of this fits before 9.
The radio station's backyard party is the one Saturday thing that starts at two in the afternoon
SAT AUG 22, 2–9PM  ·  $70 GENERAL, $50 AGES 13–17, UNDER 12 FREE WITH AN ADULT  ·  KEXP AND THE NORTHWEST COURTYARD, SEATTLE CENTER
The annual all-ages barbecue thrown by the listener-funded radio station at Seattle Center: Ty Segall, Arlo Parks, Los Mirlos, Angine de Poitrine, Packaging and Sea Lemon across seven hours, with local food vendors, the station's disc jockeys on the ground and the studios open. Seven hours for seventy dollars is roughly one Tractor Tavern ticket per band, and the shape of it — afternoon into early evening, done at nine — beats the usual doors-at-eight math.
Weather call: the courtyard is open air and Saturday carries a 47 percent chance of rain before 5pm, so the front half is the exposed half — arriving at four or five buys the drier, later, better-billed hours. The studio spaces and the Armory food hall next door are both indoors if it turns. It runs to 9pm, which is the outer edge; leaving after the second-to-last set still gets you home on time.

The Firebird and a Rachmaninoff symphony for twelve dollars, indoors, on the wettest night of the week
SAT AUG 22, DOORS 6:30PM, 7:30PM  ·  $12–$18  ·  THE GREAT HALL, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Solstice Symphony Orchestra — a volunteer-run community orchestra now in its fourth season — takes the big room on Eighth Avenue for an all-Russian program: the overture to Glinka's “Ruslan and Ludmila,” the 1919 suite from Stravinsky's “The Firebird,” and after intermission the whole of Rachmaninoff's Second Symphony. That is a genuinely large piece of orchestral repertoire in a five-hundred-seat hall at a price below a movie ticket, which is a trade the Seattle Symphony downtown cannot offer.
Honest timing: the Rachmaninoff alone runs about an hour, so with the intermission this is a 9:45 or 10pm finish, not a 9. Worth knowing before committing on a Saturday. The upside is that it is completely rain-proof on the one evening this week that may need it, and Town Hall is a four-block walk from the Central Library and the downtown bus spine.

Next week's best outdoor evening is a Tuesday, and Lake Union already has something on it
TUE AUG 25, FIRST START 7PM  ·  FREE TO WATCH  ·  LAKE UNION — GAS WORKS PARK, OR ON THE WATER
The Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night sailboat race that has been going around Lake Union for fifty-two summers — costumed crews, a different theme every week, four staggered heats by boat speed, and a fleet that is out there for the party more than the result. Tuesday is forecast at 75° and mostly sunny with a 6 percent rain chance, the warmest day of the coming stretch, and the season only has a handful of Tuesdays left before it winds down in September.
Two ways in and both are free: the north-shore hill at Gas Works Park looks straight down the course, or put a boat in and watch from inside the spectator fleet — the Northwest Outdoor Center and Agua Verde both rent on the lake and both are a short paddle from the start line. Racing is over well before dark. If Tuesday sours, Monday is the drier day at 73° and zero percent.
A field guide organized by season, not by species — and it is out by a quarter to nine
WED AUG 26, DOORS 6:30PM, 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  THE WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Artist and naturalist Emily Poole launches “Cascade of Life: An Artist's Seasonal Guide to the Wild Northwest” with Kate Rogers, editor-in-chief of Mountaineers Books — the nonprofit Seattle publisher — and the evening doubles as a preview of the whole fall natural-history list. The book runs shoreline to alpine to shrub-steppe by season, pairing illustration with essay, and gives as much room to the pickleweed and the silver-haired bat as to the gray whales.
Town Hall runs these at roughly 75 minutes, so a 7:30 start is an 8:45 finish — the rare lecture that fits the bedtime without a compromise. Sliding scale starts at ten dollars, no questions asked. Wednesday is 72° and mostly sunny, so there is no weather reason to skip it.

A seven-o'clock Monday show in Columbia City, and a farewell fifteen years in the making
MON AUG 24, DOORS 6PM, SHOW 7PM  ·  $22 ADVANCE, $28 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
The Victor Janusz Band — six pieces led by a Seattle pianist who also works as a music therapist — plays the Columbia City room on a Monday, and this one is Arwen Dewey's last: she has shared lead vocals for fifteen years and is moving to Colombia. Saxophonist Medearis Dixson leads the reunited band. A 7pm downbeat in a room that is all ages until 10 is about as compatible with an early bedtime as live music in this city gets.
Advance tickets are online only; the room takes table reservations for diners once you hold a ticket, and everyone else is first come, first served. Monday is 73° and mostly sunny, so there is daylight on either side of it — Seward Park and the lake are ten minutes away if you want the walk first.

The second ideas night of the week is on the same evening as the eclipse — pick one
THU AUG 27, DOORS 6:30PM, 7:30PM  ·  $10–$35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  THE WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Wanda Herndon, who ran global communications at Starbucks through its expansion years, talks with broadcaster Angela Poe Russell about her memoir “Working Class to Breaking Glass” — Flint, Michigan and a father on the General Motors line, to Michigan State journalism, to a seat at the table in a Seattle headquarters. It is a corporate-power-and-who-gets-in story told from the inside, in the smaller of Town Hall's two rooms.
The conflict is real and worth naming: this ends around 8:45, which is the exact hour the eclipse is at its best low in the east. First Hill has no eastern horizon to speak of. If the sky is clear on the 27th, take the moon; the book will still be there.
