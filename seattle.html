This week in Seattle
The park built on top of a freeway turns fifty — and this is the week it gets both the argument and the party
FORUM TUE AUG 18, 6PM  ·  PARTY SAT AUG 22, IN THE PARK  ·  BOTH FREE OR NEAR IT  ·  FIRST HILL / DOWNTOWN
Freeway Park is five acres of concrete canyons poured over Interstate 5 in 1976 — the first park in the world built as a lid over a highway, and the argument that every lid proposal since has had to answer for. Its fiftieth lands this week in two halves. Tuesday is the talk: a public forum at Town Hall Seattle on the park's past, present and future, convened by Lid I-5, the Freeway Park Association and the Seattle Parks Foundation — which is to say the people actively trying to build the next lid, using the first one as evidence. Saturday is the payoff, in the park itself: Garfield High School's jazz band and Seattle buskers playing live, guided architectural and landscape tours, temporary artworks and audio installations, free locally catered food and drink, and a volunteer sign-up table. Free, no ticket.
Saturday is the softest weather day of the week — 72°, a 20% rain chance — which is close to ideal for a park that is mostly shade and water walls anyway. If it does come down, Freeway Park's concrete overhangs are the shelter; the Seattle Public Library's central branch is four blocks away.

Tuesday is the best water evening left in the forecast — 79°, no rain, and the sailboats on Lake Union
TUE AUG 18, BOATS OFF AT 7PM  ·  FREE  ·  LAKE UNION — GAS WORKS PARK OR ON THE WATER
The Duck Dodge is the Tuesday-night sailboat race that has run on Lake Union every summer since 1974: costumes, four heats sorted roughly by how fast the boat is, and a fleet that treats the rules as a suggestion. It runs weekly through September 2, so the case for this Tuesday specifically is the sky — 79° and mostly sunny is the warmest, driest evening in the seven-day forecast, with a 3 to 7 mph southwest breeze, which is enough to move boats without making the paddle out miserable. Two ways in: the hill at Gas Works Park is the free grandstand and the racing runs right below it, or you rent from the Northwest Outdoor Center or Agua Verde on the south and west shores and sit in the spectator fleet, which is the version worth doing. Sunset is around 8:15pm; be off the water before the light goes. If the wind dies or plans change, the same evening works from a bench on the Burke-Gilman Trail with no equipment at all.

547 chinook came up the fish ladder in a single day — the run is peaking now, with a free concert on the lawn above it
FISH LADDER DAILY 7AM–9PM  ·  CONCERTS SAT & SUN 2–4:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  BALLARD LOCKS
The state's count at the Ballard Locks put 547 chinook through the ladder on August 13 alone, against a running season total of 3,440 — meaning roughly one fish in six arrived that day. That is what a peak looks like in the data, and the last two weeks of August are when it holds. The underwater viewing room is free, open 7am to 9pm, and puts you eye-level with fish holding against the current through glass.
Pair it with the free concert series the Friends of the Ballard Locks run every Saturday and Sunday afternoon through Labor Day, 2 to 4:30pm in the Carl S. English Jr. Botanical Garden beside the locks — this weekend it's a Woodinville swing band Saturday and a saxophone ensemble Sunday. No seating provided, so bring a blanket; donations suggested, nothing required. Rain plan is simple: the fish don't care and the viewing room is indoors.
Quiet elsewhere in the natural calendar: no minus tides this week (the lowest low is +0.9 ft Friday at 6:20am), the space weather forecast peaks at a planetary index of 4 — no aurora at this latitude — and the Perseid meteor shower peaked four nights ago.

Lake Washington Boulevard closes to cars again next weekend — two of the last four all summer
SAT AUG 22 & SUN AUG 23, ALL DAY  ·  FREE  ·  SEWARD PARK TO MOUNT BAKER BEACH
Seattle Parks and Recreation shuts the lakefront stretch of Lake Washington Boulevard between Seward and Mount Baker parks to motor traffic, and the road becomes flat, shoreline-hugging, car-free pavement for anyone walking, running, biking or rolling. It has run most weekends since Memorial Day and there are only two left after this one — Aug 29–30 and the Labor Day weekend — so the season is genuinely closing.
Saturday brings a 20% rain chance and 72°, Sunday is the drier-looking of the two on current guidance, and either way it's a road you can bail off at any point. Worth noting for the day-planning: this is the same Saturday as the Freeway Park party downtown, and both are free and drop-in, so the morning on the boulevard and the afternoon in the park is a real itinerary rather than a conflict.

Courtney Barnett and Built to Spill on the zoo lawn — the same Tuesday as everything else
TUE AUG 18, DOORS 4:30PM / MUSIC 5PM  ·  $80–$185  ·  WOODLAND PARK ZOO, PHINNEY RIDGE
ZooTunes is the summer series on the north meadow at Woodland Park Zoo — lawn seating, blankets, gates at 4:30pm. This one is a genuinely good triple bill: Courtney Barnett, the Melbourne songwriter whose deadpan is doing more work than it lets on; Built to Spill, Boise's guitar band and one of the load-bearing records of Pacific Northwest indie rock; and Zoh Amba, a young free-jazz saxophonist who is a strange and welcome thing to put on a zoo lawn.
The honest problem is the calendar: this, the Freeway Park forum and the Duck Dodge are all Tuesday evening, and the tickets are the expensive option of the three at $80 and up, online only — nothing is sold at the zoo gates. Weather is not the risk here (79°, mostly sunny, dropping to 58° overnight, so bring a layer for the back half). If you're pricing it against a free race on the lake, the deciding factor is Built to Spill.

Silkworm with Nina Nastasia at the Tractor — a band that stopped playing twenty years ago
FRI AUG 21, 8PM  ·  $25  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD
Silkworm formed in Missoula, spent its best years in Seattle and Chicago, and made a run of dry, unsentimental, Steve Albini-recorded rock records before the band ended abruptly in 2005 with the death of its drummer. Shows are rare and are not a nostalgia circuit. Nina Nastasia opens — spare, unnervingly direct songwriting, and a real bill rather than a warm-up. The Tractor is the roots-and-Americana room on Ballard Avenue with a low stage and no bad sightlines, and $25 for this pairing is a Ballard bar price for a show that would sell out a bigger room. Friday is also when the dry stretch breaks: a slight chance of rain after 5pm, which matters only for what you wear on the walk to Ballard Avenue.

Jessica Lurie & Friends — the cheap Thursday night that's actually good
THU AUG 20, 7:30PM  ·  LOW COVER, PAID AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Jessica Lurie is a saxophonist and composer with roots in the Seattle scene that produced the Billy Tipton Memorial Saxophone Quartet, and her "& Friends" nights are loose, improvisation-forward and different every time. The Royal Room is Wayne Horvitz's Columbia City bar — food, a real piano, tables close to the band, and covers that run a fraction of a jazz-club ticket. It's a fifteen-minute walk from the Columbia City light rail station, which makes it the easiest weeknight show in this brief to get home from. Note if you're planning further out: the room is closed for maintenance on Tuesday.

Two nights of Mysterious Skin at the Beacon, on 35mm-era repertory terms
FRI AUG 21 & SAT AUG 22, 7PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
Gregg Araki's 2004 film, adapted from Scott Heim's novel, is the one where his provocateur streak resolved into something genuinely devastating — two Kansas boys, one who remembers, one who doesn't. It is not a light Friday, and it's better in a small room with strangers than at home. The Beacon is the tiny second-floor repertory house above Columbia City with a bar and roughly fifty seats, which means these two showings are the entire run.
The rest of the Beacon's week if that one isn't the mood: Elaine May's Mikey and Nicky and the Korean drama Take Care of My Cat alternate Monday through Thursday at 4:30 and 7:30pm, and Sunday brings Chaplin's Limelight at 2pm and the French animated oddity Gandahar at 5pm. This is the indoor fallback for the whole week — the one venue here that improves when it rains.
