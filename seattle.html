This Week in Seattle
TONIGHT — A $10 FOOD-AND-SALMON TALK, OR OZU IN COLOR
Gyetk: Spencer and Rachel Greening on their Ts’msyen cookbook
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, MEMBERS FREE  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
Still on the Burke calendar for tonight: a North Coast cookbook organized around the harvest year (salmon, shellfish, seaweed), presented by its authors, with the museum open around it. Home by 8. The film option, new this week: the Northwest Film Forum has Yasujiro Ozu’s Equinox Flower at 7pm ($15, $10 student/senior, $7 members), his first color film, about an old-fashioned father and his newfangled daughter; it plays again Thursday at 7:15. Same-hour talks: Francine Berman at Town Hall, 7:30, $10–35, on treating social media, chatbots and search as critical infrastructure (75 minutes, out by 8:45); Helen Whybrow at Elliott Bay Book Company, 7pm, free, reading from her National Book Award-longlisted shepherding memoir The Salt Stones; and Claudia Castro Luna, Seattle’s first Civic Poet, launching her memoir at the Central Library, 7pm, free. Before any of them: the Wallingford Farmers Market is at Meridian Park 3–7pm; the market page gives no season end date, but EverOut listed it as running through September, so treat tonight as possibly the last one. Cloud is 25% at 6.
THURSDAY — JOHN WILSON’S CONCRETE MOVIE, WITH A FREE HOUSING TALK NEXT DOOR
The History of Concrete
THU OCT 1, 7PM  ·  $15 ($10 STUDENT/SENIOR, $7 MEMBERS)  ·  NORTHWEST FILM FORUM, CAPITOL HILL  ·  101 MIN, OUT ABOUT 8:45
Thursday’s 7pm is the one-night “Truth To Fiction” screening with the Seattle Film Society and the Seattle Documentary Association: the How To with John Wilson documentarian tries to make the definitive film about concrete, the world’s most-used building material, using the Hallmark-movie formula he learned at a workshop. If Thursday doesn’t work, the calendar now shows it running every day through Tuesday (Fri 5pm; Sat and Sun 4 and 7pm; Mon and Tue 7pm). Free, same hour: The Future of Affordable Housing, Town Hall, 7:30pm, $0–35 sliding, Seattle Times housing reporter Heidi Groover with Habitat for Humanity leadership. Also free at 7: historian Douglas Smith at Elliott Bay on The City Without Jews, Nazi Vienna through the diaries of a nurse who stayed all seven years, with Vivian Phillips. The big-name one: Kyle MacLachlan with Carrie Brownstein on his memoir Fictional Selves, Seattle Arts & Lectures at the Edmonds Center for the Arts, 7:30, $10–128 (Elliott Bay lists it as ending at 9). Daytime: Free First Thursday at the Nordic Museum (10am–8pm) and the Burke, where the 4–7pm STEAM Spotlight puts Latine scientists at tables. Thursday is dry, 40% cloud until 7, so the evening costs nothing indoors.
FRIDAY — TWO 6PM STARTS: THE FREE OUTDOOR FINALE, OR SON LUX DONE BY 9
Outdoors on the Ave season finale: REPOSADO at 6, Ferris Bueller’s Day Off at dusk
FRI OCT 2  ·  ROBERT LESLIE 4:30PM, REPOSADO 6PM, MOVIE AT DUSK (~7:15)  ·  FREE  ·  THE SQUARE AT U DISTRICT STATION
The forecast got better overnight: 0% rain, 23% cloud from 5pm, 21% after 8, 65° at the 6pm set and 58° by the end of the film. Folk soloist Robert Leslie at 4:30, then the nine-piece “TequilaFunk” band REPOSADO at 6 (the site’s own words), then the movie at dusk (sunset 6:48, civil twilight ends 7:17). Step off the light rail and you’re there; the 6pm set is your shape, leave the film when you like. The rival, and it’s a real one: Son Lux at the Crocodile, Belltown, with mmeadows opening: doors 5, show 6 to 9pm, $30 plus fees ($43.59 all-in), 21-plus. A touring act in a 500-cap room that ends before your bedtime is rare enough to say out loud. Early and free before either: the Joe Casalini Trio at the Royal Room, Columbia City, 4pm, no cover, swinging jazz standards. Food, outdoors, from noon: the Seattle Greek Festival opens Friday noon–9pm at St. Demetrios, 2100 Boyer Ave E in Montlake: cooking under a big tent, pastries, dance groups, church tours; the site prints no admission price. Indoor fallback: novelist Peter Rock at Elliott Bay, 7pm, free, with Makeshift, three sisters alone on a rock-bound island off the Pacific Northwest coast, in conversation with Seattle’s Thomas Kohnstamm.
SATURDAY, DAYTIME — FREE, ON THE WATER, 0% RAIN AND 16% CLOUD
Fishermen’s Fall Festival at Fishermen’s Terminal
SAT OCT 3, 11AM–6PM  ·  FREE  ·  1900 W NICKERSON ST, INTERBAY
The one Saturday a year the working fleet throws a party: seafood, live music, Ship Canal boat tours, a wooden-boat table for kids, and survival-suit races off Dock 9 in the afternoon. The memorial’s page still says “October 3, 2026” and sends you to its Facebook page for the day-of schedule (earlier briefs had tours at 11:45, 1:30 and 4, races at 3). Proceeds go to families of fishers lost at sea. Best weather of the festival’s three checks so far: 67°, 16% cloud. Two food festivals to pair, both indoors or under a tent: the Northwest Chocolate Festival, Seattle Center Expo Hall, Sat 9–6 and Sun 9–4, 100-plus makers (tickets via the site, price not printed); and the Greek Festival, Sat 10am–9pm in Montlake. The two-day Diwa Filipino Film Festival is at the Wing Luke Museum Sat and Sun.

Star party at Paramount Park, Shoreline
SAT OCT 3, 7–11PM  ·  FREE  ·  PARAMOUNT PARK, 15300 8TH AVE NE, SHORELINE  ·  SEATTLE ASTRONOMICAL SOCIETY
Fourth morning in a row it’s held, and the sky keeps getting better: 12% cloud from 5 to 10pm, 0% rain (18% yesterday). Sky wins on a night like this.

CroatiaFest: tambura orchestras, kolo dancing, cooking demos

MONDAY — WATER LIFE, THE SCIENCE PANEL: 74 ORCAS LEFT
Act Now: Saving Our Orcas
MON OCT 5, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING  ·  TOWN HALL, WYNCOTE FORUM, FIRST HILL  ·  75 MINUTES, OUT BY 8:45
Town Hall and Oceans Initiative on the Southern Resident killer whales, “now numbering just 74 individuals, and perilously close to extinction” (their page). Seattle Times environment writer Lynda Mapes moderates Erin Ashe and Rob Williams (Oceans Initiative co-founders), wildlife vet Joe Gaydos (SeaDoc Society), Lummi Nation fisherman and former chairman Jay Julius, and Misty MacDuffee, wild-salmon director at the Raincoast Conservation Foundation. Whale ecology, salmon and Indigenous stewardship in one room, and it’s the whales your kayak shares the Sound with. Same night, bigger room: fantasy novelist V.E. Schwab in the Great Hall, 7:30, $10–90. Monday clouds up to 60% in the afternoon but stays dry.
TUESDAY — A PULITZER NOVELIST ON A CITY AFTER THE STATE FALLS, OR A MINI BIG BAND
Hernan Diaz with Nick Hilden: Ply
TUE OCT 6, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35  ·  TOWN HALL, WYNCOTE FORUM  ·  75 MINUTES
The Trust author’s new novel, per Town Hall’s page: centuries from now, an orphan in a city being remade after the state is dismantled becomes a “pincher,” stealing electricity for the black market, and invents something that could “change the fabric of reality.” The conversation is about empire, what the state does to our connection to each other, and what happens when it falls. Cities, infrastructure, power: your beat, in fiction. The music option, same hour: Nelda Swiggett’s Megabopolis at the Royal Room, Columbia City, doors 6:30, show 7:30, $20 advance / $25 door: the venue describes tight ensemble arrangements producing a “mini-big band” sound. Diners can reserve a table; everyone else is first come.
