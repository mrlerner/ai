TONIGHT · IDEAS · CITIES · FREE
Michael Pollack, Sidewalk Nation: a Week Without Driving book talk
MON OCT 5, 6:30–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  HUSKY UNION BUILDING, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
The one night this week that is both free and over by 8. Pollack, a land-use law professor at Cardozo, has written the first full book about the sidewalk as a legal object: who owns the strip in front of a house, who has to clear it, who gets to sleep, sell, protest or park on it, and how disability law, zoning, tax policy and the First and Fourth Amendments collide on it in a dozen American cities. That is the layer under every civic app you have built. Pegged to the Week Without Driving (Oct 1–8); the page re-read this morning still says Monday, October 5, 6:30–8 pm, Free, All Ages. The Husky Union Building is a block from the U District light-rail station.
Also tonight, 7:30 at Town Hall: Act Now: Saving Our Orcas, Lynda Mapes of the Seattle Times moderating Oceans Initiative scientists on the 74 remaining Southern Resident killer whales, $10–35 sliding scale, out by 8:45. TUE OCT 6, 7:30PM  ·  $39–$109  ·  TOWN HALL GREAT HALL, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
New in the Town Hall feed this morning. Whittingham is the English classical guitarist The Guardian called a young 21st-century virtuoso; she went to Chetham’s School of Music at eleven and built a large YouTube following on Spanish and Romantic repertoire played with a very plain, singing tone. The other show you asked for, and the one with the right shape: a Sunday matinee, home by 4:30. Verma grew up as an Indian classical musician in the West and trained under the sarod master Ali Akbar Khan; Hess is a Danish pianist in New York whose sound runs from Nordic jazz to the West African side he picked up working with Fela and Femi Kuti. Sitar and piano is not a standard pairing, which is why it is the branch-out: the sitar’s long drone-and-bend lines land where your Steve Gunn and Ryley Walker open-tuning records live, and the piano does the Steve Reich pulse underneath.
