This week in Seattle
Tonight is the night — Perseid peak, new moon, clear forecast
TONIGHT AFTER MIDNIGHT INTO THU PRE-DAWN  ·  FREE  ·  FORECAST: MOSTLY CLEAR, LOW 59°
The three things that have to line up for a good Perseid year all landed on the same date this time: the shower peaks the night of the 12th into the morning of the 13th, the moon turns new today (zero illumination, no skyglow at all), and the National Weather Service has tonight mostly clear with a low near 59°. Rates run 50 to 75 an hour at a properly dark site. Don't bother in the early evening — the radiant in Perseus doesn't clear the horizon until late, and the shower gets better every hour until dawn, so the productive window is roughly 1am to first light. Twenty minutes of dark adaptation, no phone screen, and lie back rather than crane. In town, the Discovery Park bluff and the north shore at Magnuson Park are the least-lit options; anything east of the Cascade foothills is a different sky altogether. If tonight clouds over, Thursday and Friday nights are also forecast clear and rates hold up for a few nights after the peak.

Minus 2.5 feet at 11:07 this morning — then the window shuts
WED 11:07AM (−2.5 FT)  ·  THU 11:52AM (−1.9 FT)  ·  FREE  ·  MOSTLY SUNNY, 76°
Today's low at the Seattle tide station is the last one this summer deep enough to count as a real minus tide, and it falls at a civilized hour on a mostly sunny 76° morning. Tomorrow gives back half a foot at −1.9, Friday is only −0.9, and from Saturday on the daytime low never drops below zero again this month. Below about −2 feet you're walking on ground that stays underwater nearly all year — sunflower stars, nudibranchs, moon snail egg collars. Golden Gardens and Carkeek are the walkable ones from town; Lincoln Park in West Seattle has the widest exposed bench. Arrive an hour early and follow the water out. The Seattle Aquarium's free beach naturalist season ended in July, so this is self-guided: step on bare rock, and put anything you lift back exactly as you found it.

Elaine May's "Mikey and Nicky" — a four-day repertory run
MON AUG 17 7:30PM  ·  TUE 4:30PM  ·  WED 7:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
May's 1976 film, made with John Cassavetes and Peter Falk playing small-time mobsters across one very long night — Nicky has stolen from his boss, holes up in a hotel, and calls the one friend who might help him, which is where the trouble actually starts. It was buried on release and has spent fifty years being reclaimed; the two leads improvising at each other is still the best argument for it. Multiple showings at the Columbia City repertory house, so the pressure that killed last week's one-offs isn't a factor — but the room is small and Monday evening is the good slot. Monday is also the week's first rain chance, which makes it the right night to be indoors.

TUE AUG 18, 6PM (DOORS 5)  ·  FREE, REGISTRATION REQUIRED  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
The half-century mark for the concrete park built on top of Interstate 5 downtown — the first of its kind anywhere, and the direct ancestor of every lid proposal since.
