This Week in Seattle
TODAY · NATURE · 11AM–3PM · FREE · SUNNIEST DAY OF THE WEEK
Chum salmon in Piper’s Creek, in the sun, after yesterday’s rain
SAT & SUN 11AM–3PM, WED 2–4PM, THROUGH DEC 6  ·  FREE  ·  CARKEEK PARK, NORTHWEST SEATTLE
The stewards said they were hoping for rain to bring the fish in; they got a full day of it yesterday, and today is the clear one. Volunteer Salmon Stewards stand on the creek bank 11–3 to point out fish and explain the run; look for the beavers’ work in the wetland too. Mostly sunny, 54, 1% rain. Sunday is a fine backup (dry, but grey and foggy early). Stay on the trails, out of the creek bed. Peak spawning usually comes around Thanksgiving.
Indoor alternative, also free: Seattle Catrinas Festival, Day of the Dead altars, artisan market and folk dance at Town Hall, First Hill, today 1pm through Sunday, $0–150.

Halloween, early edition: The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari with a live score
SAT OCT 31  ·  5–6:30PM  ·  BY DONATION  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
The 1920 German silent film, all crooked painted sets and a sleepwalking murderer, with a live score from Wayne Horvitz’s band, presented with the jazz station KNKX. Done by 6:30, which makes it the one Halloween event in town that fits your evening. Home before the trick-or-treaters peak.
SUN 2PM · NATURE & SCIENCE TALK · FROM $33 · NEW
Battling Bloodsuckers: a National Geographic explorer on nature’s vampires
SUN NOV 1, 2PM (ALSO MON & TUE 7:30PM)  ·  FROM $33 PLUS FEES  ·  BENAROYA HALL, DOWNTOWN
Ryan Carney, an epidemiologist and National Geographic Explorer, opens this season’s National Geographic Live speaker series on why bats, mosquitoes and other animals evolved to live on blood, and what studying them teaches about infectious disease, including how artificial intelligence and citizen-science photo apps now track mosquitoes. Big-screen National Geographic photography throughout. The Sunday 2pm show is the one for you: a grey, foggy afternoon indoors, out well before dark.
