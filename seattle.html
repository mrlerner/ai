This week in Seattle
The university opens its hundred-year-old telescope to the public tomorrow night — free, but you have to register
TUE AUG 18, DOORS 8:45PM / 9–11PM  ·  FREE, REGISTRATION REQUIRED  ·  THEODOR JACOBSEN OBSERVATORY, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
The Theodor Jacobsen Observatory is the little stone building on the north end of the University of Washington campus, and its open houses run only twice a month in summer — this is the August one, and the next is not until September 1. The format: a 45-to-50-minute talk by a University of Washington student or someone from the local astronomy community, then Q&A, then small-group tours up into the dome, and science demonstrations with spectral lamps and smaller telescopes set up on the front lawn. If the sky cooperates, the dome opens and the century-old refractor gets pointed at something. Seating and dome capacity are both small, which is why the free ticket still has to be claimed in advance.
What's actually up there this week: the moon reaches first quarter on Thursday, and on Thursday and Friday evenings it sits next to Antares, the red star at the heart of Scorpius, low in the south. That same waxing moon is a decent pointer toward the center of the Milky Way, which is as high as it gets on August evenings. Tuesday night runs clear-ish and 59° — bring a jacket, the dome is unheated. If you'd rather have a telescope handed to you outdoors, the Seattle Astronomical Society runs a free public astronomy class and stargazing night at Covington City Park on Friday from 6pm, about 30 minutes southeast of town.
Quiet in the rest of the natural calendar, per this morning's data pulls: no minus tides in the next seven days — the lowest low is +0.8 ft Saturday at 7:22am — and the space-weather forecast peaks at a planetary index of 4, which means no aurora at this latitude. The tides do get interesting the following week: −0.1 ft on Aug 25 and deepening from there.

Tuesday is the whole week in one evening — a park's fiftieth, the sailboat race, and a zoo-lawn triple bill, all at once
TUE AUG 18, 5PM ONWARD  ·  TWO OF THE THREE ARE FREE  ·  DOWNTOWN, LAKE UNION, PHINNEY RIDGE
Everything landed on the same night. 6pm, downtown: the free public forum at Town Hall Seattle on Freeway Park's fiftieth — the five acres of concrete poured over Interstate 5 in 1976, the first park in the world built as a lid over a highway, discussed by the people currently trying to build the next lid. 7pm, Lake Union: the Duck Dodge, the costumed Tuesday-night sailboat race that has run since 1974, free from the hill at Gas Works Park or from a rented kayak in the spectator fleet — and at 78° and dry with a light southwest breeze, this is the best water evening the forecast offers. 5pm, Woodland Park Zoo: Courtney Barnett with Built to Spill and the saxophonist Zoh Amba on the lawn, $65 and up, online only.
The workable version if you don't want to pay: the forum lets out around 7:30, Gas Works is fifteen minutes north of it, sunset is about 8:10pm, and the observatory open house above starts at 9pm ten minutes further east. That is a genuinely good free Tuesday — talk, boats, telescope — and it costs bus fare. The zoo show is the one that forces a choice, and the deciding factor is whether Built to Spill is worth $65 to you.

Thursday is the city-nerd night: the urbanist meetup and the design festival's last party, both at 6pm
THU AUG 20, 6–8PM AND 6–9PM  ·  FREE / PAY-WHAT-YOU-CAN  ·  SOUTH LAKE UNION
Two rooms full of the people who argue about zoning and sidewalks for a living, ten minutes apart. The Urbanist — the local publication that covers land use, transit and housing policy — holds its monthly social at TeKu Tavern on Denny Way, back section reserved, bike racks out front, free to show up and buy your own beer. And the Seattle Design Festival, the region's biggest design event, closes its sixteenth year at The Shop, the architecture firm LMN's own space, with music and light food; tickets are free-to-donation, and the donation is what keeps the festival's public programming free.
This year's festival theme was IN[FLUX] — movement and instability — and it has been running installations and mixers since the block party at Lake Union Park last weekend, so Thursday is the end of it rather than the start. Both events are indoors, which matters not at all on a 79° day — Thursday is the warmest of the week. If you only do one: the Urbanist social is free and the conversation is the point; the festival party is the better room.

A trio that started playing together in mid-1980s Seattle books one night back — forty years on
WED AUG 19, DOORS 6:30PM / MUSIC 7:30PM  ·  $20 ADVANCE, $25 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Brad Shepik on guitar, Phil Sparks on bass, Michael Sarin on drums — a band that formed in the Seattle jazz scene of the mid-1980s and then scattered, with Shepik and Sarin building careers in New York while Sparks stayed and became a fixture of the Northwest scene. They still play together when the geography allows, and this is billed as forty years of it. The Royal Room is the pianist Wayne Horvitz's Columbia City bar: real food, a real piano, tables close enough to the band that a quiet trio works, and a fifteen-minute walk from the Columbia City light rail station.
The room's week, if Wednesday doesn't work: it's closed Tuesday for maintenance, Jessica Lurie's loose improvisation-forward "& Friends" night is Thursday at 7:30, the guitarist Scott Andrew plays a free happy hour Friday at 4, and the house band The Royal We takes Friday night. Then it's closed all weekend for private events.

Two reunion nights in a row on Ballard Avenue — a band that ended in 2005, and a blog that turns 25
FRI AUG 21 8PM & SAT AUG 22 8PM  ·  $25 EACH  ·  TRACTOR TAVERN, BALLARD
Friday: Silkworm, the dry, unsentimental rock band that formed in Missoula, spent its best years between Seattle and Chicago, and stopped abruptly in 2005 when its drummer was killed. The shows are rare and are not a nostalgia circuit; Nina Nastasia opens with her spare, unnervingly direct songwriting. Saturday: Guerrilla Candy, the Seattle music blog that has been photographing and writing up this city's shows for twenty-five years, throws itself a party — SHIM playing their first show in more than a decade, Smoker Dad previewing an album, and Smokey Brights headlining. Twenty-five dollars each, same low stage, same no-bad-sightlines room.
Friday night is also when the dry stretch finally breaks — an 18% chance of rain after 5am Saturday — which affects nothing except the walk down Ballard Avenue. The rest of the Tractor's week is deep too: honky-tonk with Dale Watson and His Lone Stars on Wednesday at 7:30, a rare 2pm matinee from Willy Braun of Reckless Kelly on Saturday, and Horse Feathers playing Words Are Dead in full for its twentieth anniversary on Sunday.

A cooler, softer weekend — a car-free lakefront road, a free concert on the Locks lawn, and the Cubs in town
SAT AUG 22 & SUN AUG 23  ·  72°, LIGHT RAIN CHANCE  ·  SEWARD PARK, BALLARD, SODO
Seattle Parks and Recreation closes the lakefront stretch of Lake Washington Boulevard between Seward Park and Mount Baker Beach to cars both days, leaving a flat shoreline road for walking, running and riding — and after this there are only two Bicycle Weekends left all summer, Aug 29–30 and Labor Day weekend. At the other end of town, the Friends of the Ballard Locks run their free concert in the Carl S. English Jr. Botanical Garden at 2pm both afternoons, on the lawn directly above a chinook run that is in its peak weeks; the underwater viewing room at the fish ladder is free and open 7am to 9pm.
The forecast wants a plan: Saturday carries a slight rain chance before 11am and clears to partly sunny at 72°, so the boulevard is an afternoon rather than a morning; Sunday is the steadier of the two. Bring a blanket for the Locks — no seating is provided, donations suggested, nothing required. The indoor version of the same weekend is baseball: the Mariners open a home series against the Chicago Cubs Friday at 7:10pm, Saturday at 4:15pm and Sunday at 1:10pm.

Chaplin's last great performance, on a Sunday afternoon, in a fifty-seat room
SUN AUG 23, 2PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
The Beacon — the tiny second-floor repertory house above Columbia City, with a bar and roughly fifty seats — runs Chaplin's Limelight at 2pm Sunday, his 1952 film about an aging music-hall comedian who has stopped being funny, and the only time he and Buster Keaton appeared on screen together. At 5pm it's Gandahar, René Laloux's strange 1987 French science-fiction animation. Both repeat Monday afternoon and evening.
This is the week's indoor fallback, and it's the one venue here that gets better when the weather turns. The rest of the run: Elaine May's Mikey and Nicky and the Korean drama Take Care of My Cat alternate at 4:30 and 7:30 tonight through Thursday, and Gregg Araki's Mysterious Skin plays Friday and Saturday at 7pm.
