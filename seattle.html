MON AUG 10, 3–3:30PM  ·  FREE  ·  KEXP STUDIO, SEATTLE CENTER
A half-hour soul set from the granddaughters of Sam Cooke and nieces of Bobby Womack, played live on the air at the listener-supported radio station's Seattle Center studio — one of the few in-studio sessions this month that's open to the public rather than broadcast-only. Seattle Jazz Fellowship on Pier 58 — free, on the water
THU AUG 13, 6–8PM  ·  PIER 58, DOWNTOWN WATERFRONT  ·  FREE  ·  SUNNY, 79°
The nonprofit that runs Seattle's serious straight-ahead jazz room takes it outdoors, as part of the free Thursday performance series on the rebuilt downtown waterfront. Sunset over Elliott Bay behind the band, no ticket, no cover. Weather call: clear and 79°, ideal — but the series has been pulled once this summer for wildfire smoke, so glance at the air quality Thursday afternoon. Indoor fallback: the Fellowship's regular nights in Pioneer Square.

"Urgh! A Music War" with the Professors of Urgh — one night only
FRI AUG 14, 6:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
The 1982 concert documentary — 34 bands in 122 minutes, The Police and Dead Kennedys and The Cramps and Gang of Four, all shot live in 1980 — presented by Chaki and Chris Slusarenko, who have made 55-plus podcast episodes tracking down the people in it. They're promising to crack the case on one of the film's most obscure bands. Exactly the sort of thing the Columbia City repertory house exists for, and it isn't coming back around.
