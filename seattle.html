The Perseids peak tomorrow night — clear sky, new moon, no compromise
WED AUG 12 LATE INTO THU PRE-DAWN  ·  FREE  ·  DARK SKY REQUIRED  ·  FORECAST: MOSTLY CLEAR, LOW 59°
The best meteor shower of the year peaks the night of August 12 into the morning of the 13th, and this is the good version: the moon is new, so the sky is genuinely dark, and the National Weather Service has Wednesday night mostly clear with a low near 59°. Rates run up to about 60 an hour in real darkness, and the hours after midnight are the productive ones. In-city viewing is thin — Discovery Park's bluff or Magnuson's north shore beat a lit street, but the difference between Seattle and an actual dark site is enormous. If you're willing to drive, the Seattle Astronomical Society has free public stargazing at Duvall on Friday at 8pm and Snoqualmie Point Park on Saturday at 8pm, both an hour out and both well past the streetlights; rates stay respectable for several nights after the peak. Cloud fallback: Thursday and Friday nights are also forecast clear.

Outside chance of northern lights — tonight, around 11pm
TUE AUG 11, ~11PM  ·  FREE  ·  NORTHERN HORIZON  ·  MOSTLY CLEAR, 59°
The Space Weather Prediction Center's three-day forecast puts geomagnetic activity at 5 on the 9-point planetary scale for the window centered on 11pm tonight — the threshold where Seattle gets a maybe rather than a yes. The honest read: possible, low on the northern horizon, only from a genuinely dark spot with an unobstructed north view. Golden Gardens looking north up the Sound, or the Edmonds and Mukilteo waterfronts, are the realistic in-reach options; a phone on a long exposure will pick up color your eyes won't. Worth twenty minutes if you're already out — not worth planning your evening around. It quiets down after tonight, back to level 3 or below for the rest of the week.

Minus 2.6 feet this morning — the last big daytime tides of the summer
TUE 10:19AM (−2.6 FT)  ·  WED 11:07AM (−2.5 FT)  ·  THU 11:52AM (−1.9 FT)  ·  FREE  ·  SUNNY, 76°
Three days of serious exposure at the Seattle tide station, and then it's over — Friday's low is only −0.9 feet and by Saturday the daytime low is above zero. Anything below about −2 feet uncovers the low intertidal that stays wet almost all year: sunflower stars, nudibranchs, moon snail collars, the works. Golden Gardens and Carkeek are the walkable ones; Lincoln Park in West Seattle has the widest bench. Go about an hour before the listed low and work outward with the water. Note the Seattle Aquarium's free naturalist program finished its season at the end of July, so these are self-guided — step on bare rock, put anything you turn over back the way you found it.

"Hell's Highway: The True Story of Highway Safety Films"
THU AUG 13, 7:30PM  ·  THE BEACON, COLUMBIA CITY
Bret Wood's documentary on the gore-soaked driver's-education films of the 1960s and 70s — an industry built in Ohio that fed carnage footage to a generation of American teenagers on the theory that terror makes safer drivers. Underneath the schlock it's a real story about how a country decided road death was an individual failing rather than a design problem, which is the argument every street-safety fight in Seattle is still having. Single showing at the Columbia City repertory house; last week's Beacon one-off sold out.
