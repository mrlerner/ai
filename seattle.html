This Week in Seattle
SUNDAY — THE CLEAREST AFTERNOON OF THE WEEK, AND IT GOT CLEARER
The last free boat ride on Lake Union until spring, then moonrise over Lake Washington
SUN SEP 27  ·  FREE  ·  SIGN-UP FROM 1PM, 1010 VALLEY ST, SOUTH LAKE UNION  ·  MOONRISE ~7:05PM
What moved since yesterday: the hourly cloud forecast for Sunday 2pm–8pm dropped to 6% (from 14–15%), 8 to 11pm is 16%, and rain is 0% all day. Saturday evening went the other way: 69–91% cloud. So the weekend's outdoor plans all go on Sunday.
The afternoon. The Center for Wooden Boats (the South Lake Union boat museum) still lists September 27 as the last public sail of 2026 (checked again this morning). Volunteer skippers take you out for about 45 minutes, boats leaving 1:15 to 5. Sign-up is in person, first come, from 1pm in front of the Wagner Education Center. Everyone in your party has to be there, and they take 80 to 200 people depending on boats and volunteers. If it fills up, you can rent a rowboat instead.
The evening. Sunset 6:58. The moon, 97.5% lit and one day past full, rises at about 7:05 in the east-northeast (these are Thursday's Jet Propulsion Laboratory ephemeris numbers; the moon's path doesn't change with the forecast). Watch from an east-facing Lake Washington shore (Seward Park, Magnuson, Madrona), be there by 6:50, and you're home well before nine.
SATURDAY — FOOD, NEW TO THIS BRIEF; DRY ALL DAY, CLOUDING UP BY EVENING
Northwest Tea Festival, with the free Italian festival next door
SAT SEP 26 10AM–6PM, SUN 10–4  ·  $25 (TWO-DAY PASS, INCLUDES A PORCELAIN CUP)  ·  SEATTLE CENTER EXHIBITION HALL, 301 MERCER ST
The 16th festival since 2008: more than 50 tea vendors, workshops, stage presentations, and guided tastings at a tea bar all weekend. It's a food event built entirely around something other than alcohol. Buy tickets in advance on nwteafestival.org.
Across the lawn, free: Festa Italiana, the 39th year of Seattle's Italian-American festival, in the Armory and Fisher Pavilion. Saturday 10–7, Sunday 10–6. Cooking demonstrations, a grape stomp, the Tutta Bella pizza toss three times a day, and a classic Italian car show (Saturday only).
Weather: 62°, 0% rain, partly sunny. Clouds build through the afternoon (42% late morning, 67% from two to five), so go early. Both events are indoors or under cover anyway. Since Sunday is the outdoor day, this is the one to do Saturday.
TONIGHT — THE STORMS CLEAR BY EVENING
Jason Dove Mark on how each generation forgets what nature used to look like
FRI SEP 25, 7:30PM (OUT BY ~8:45)  ·  $10–35 SLIDING  ·  WYNCOTE NORTHWEST FORUM, TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
The forecast changed: thunderstorms end around five, rain drops to 16% by six and 9% by seven, and it's clear by ten. So getting there tonight isn't a slog. Same pick, better conditions. The former Sierra editor's The Earth Said Remember Me is about "shifting baseline syndrome": each generation takes a thinner salmon run, or a quieter dawn chorus, as normal. His answer is for ordinary people (fishermen, birders) to keep records. With Lynda Mapes.
Music option, same hour: Ganesh Rajagopalan and Marina Albero, South Indian classical violin and piano, $25–50, a full-length concert that runs later.
TUESDAY — A RAINY NIGHT (55%), ALL INDOORS; A NEW FREE MUSIC OPTION
Two Cities, One Industry: how big tech rebuilt Seattle and San Francisco
TUE SEP 29, 6–8PM  ·  $20, $15 MEMBERS  ·  NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, BALLARD
The Tuesday pick, restated because it's the closest fit to your work all week. It's a live taping of two podcasts, Blue City Blues and GeekWire's. Margaret O'Mara (University of Washington historian, author of The Code), GeekWire co-founder John Cook and Jonathan Weber (City on the Edge) talk about what the industry did to the housing, politics and civic life of the two cities. Out by eight. Page checked again this morning.
New, free, and music: Lizzie Steiner's album release, 7–8:30pm (doors 6:30), at the Good Shepherd Center chapel in Wallingford, a restored chapel used as a small concert room. It's a solo harp set from her debut album Reluctant Princess (out today), harp arrangements that lean toward R&B and soul. Free with an Eventbrite reservation, all ages, free parking.
Also that night: Against Tech Oligarchy, a book talk at Elliott Bay Book Company on Capitol Hill, 7pm, free with RSVP. And Town Hall's salmon-run evening with photographer Amy Gulick, 7:30pm, $10–35.
WEDNESDAY — NATURE AND FOOD, $10; PROMOTED FROM YESTERDAY'S CALENDAR
Gyetk: a Ts'msyen kitchen and the seasonal round of the North Coast
WED SEP 30, 6:30PM (DOORS 6)  ·  $10, FREE FOR MEMBERS  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS
Gyetk means "to feed the people." Spencer Greening (Gitga'at First Nation, an anthropologist researching Indigenous harvesting methods and ecological law at the University of Victoria) and Rachel Greening (a dietitian from Lax Kw'alaams whose doctorate is on Indigenous food sovereignty) wrote a cookbook of 60 recipes. It follows the Ts'msyen (Tsimshian) year of harvests along the British Columbia coast, pairing each recipe with the traditional ecological knowledge behind it. It's the rare talk that is about salmon, shellfish and the shoreline and about food. It should wrap by eight, and the Burke is a short drive from most of north Seattle. Wednesday rain chance is 33%.
