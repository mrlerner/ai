TONIGHT · MUSIC, IN YOUR LANE · NEW, FOUND THIS MORNING
Beth Orton, with Kenny Stahl
SAT OCT 3, DOORS 6:30, SHOW 7:30, ENDS BY 10  ·  $35–44 (EVEROUT’S FIGURE; THE TICKET PAGE SHOWS NO PRICE OR SOLD-OUT FLAG)  ·  WASHINGTON HALL, 153 14TH AVE, CENTRAL DISTRICT  ·  ALL AGES, MIXED SEATING  ·  ABBEY ARTS WITH KEXP
Songkick’s Seattle list turned this up; EverOut and the Humanitix ticket page confirm it. Beth Orton is the English singer who made Trailer Park in 1996, the record that put a folk voice over slow electronic beats, and then spent twenty years drifting toward plain acoustic songs: hushed, unhurried, half-whispered, the shelf where your Aldous Harding, Iron & Wine and Nick Drake listening sits. The organizer’s own description of the new album, The Ground Above, calls it “her most direct and unapologetic music to date,” made with Tom Skinner, the drummer of The Smile, which is Thom Yorke and Jonny Greenwood’s band, and Nick Hakim on wordless harmonies (he was yesterday’s free KEXP pick). Washington Hall is a 1908 lodge hall with chairs, a balcony and standing room, so it is a listening room, not a bar. The ticket page gives doors 6:30, show 7:30, and says the show “concludes by 10,” so this is a 10pm night, an hour past your usual, and worth it. Kids 10 and under free. If it is sold out: the star party is the fallback, not the other way around.

Michael Pollack, Sidewalk Nation: a Week Without Driving book talk
MON OCT 5, 6:30–8PM  ·  FREE, ALL AGES  ·  HUSKY UNION BUILDING, 4001 E STEVENS WAY NE, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON CAMPUS  ·  OUT BY 8
Surfaced on EverOut’s Readings & Talks rail this morning. Pollack is a Cardozo law professor whose book is a travelogue through a dozen American cities asking who owns, pays for and polices the sidewalk: “How do we ensure that sidewalks facilitate mobility for disabled people? Whose job is it to clear snow from public space?” and, beyond the prosaic, the First and Fourth Amendment fights that happen on them. The organizer’s description promises the property, tax and zoning law “that shape sidewalk management and land use more broadly,” which is the plumbing under the civic software you build. It is pegged to Week Without Driving (Oct 1–8, the Disability Rights Washington campaign that asks officials to live a week as a non-driver), so the room will be transit-and-sidewalk people. Free, no ticket needed per the listing, 6:30 to 8, on the light rail. Monday evening: 68° by day, dry, clear.
