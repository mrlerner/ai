This week in Seattle
NEW THIS MORNING — AND IT LANDS ON THE SUNNY DAYS
Boats Afloat: a hundred and twenty boats, in the water, at South Lake Union
THU–SUN SEP 17–20  ·  $24 DAY / $40 ALL FOUR  ·  SOUTH LAKE UNION
The autumn edition of the floating boat show ties up at the Lake Union piers, 901 Fairview Avenue North, and this is the version worth going to — the boats are in the water and you walk aboard them rather than looking at hulls on trailers in a hall. More than 120 power and sailing craft, from day boats to long-range cruisers, plus free seminars and live music on the docks.
Hours: Thursday and Friday 11am–6pm, Saturday 10am–6pm, Sunday 10am–5pm. $24 for a single day, $40 for a multi-day pass, teens 13–17 are $7, under-12s free, $15 on a military ID. Online prices are discounted through Sep 20; the numbers above are the at-the-gate ones.
The weather call: go Thursday or Friday. Both are sunny, 73° and zero percent rain, and both are weekdays, so the docks will be walkable instead of shoulder-to-shoulder — the kids' activities and the crowds arrive at the weekend. It is an hour and a half well spent late on a Friday afternoon before the show closes at six. Indoor fallback: not needed this week, but the show runs rain or shine anyway.

NEW — FREE, OUTDOORS, STARTS AT SIX
A power-pop quartet at 6, then Grease on a screen outdoors
FRI SEP 18, 6PM  ·  FREE  ·  UNIVERSITY DISTRICT
Outdoors on the Ave is the neighbourhood group's free music-and-movie series, running the four Fridays after Labor Day in the Square at University District Station — the plaza over the light-rail station at Northeast 43rd Street and Brooklyn Avenue Northeast. Sixth year; this year's theme is Back to School, which is why the films are what they are.
Friday: Hockey Teeth at 6pm, a local quartet playing bright, hooky power pop — the comparisons being made are Phoenix, Elvis Costello and Wilco. Then Grease (1978) at 7:45. Both free, no ticket, sponsored by 4Culture and Amazon.
The honest shape of it: the 6pm set is the part that fits you — a free hour of live music outdoors on a sunny 73° evening, done by 7. The film runs an hour and fifty, so it does not finish until about 9:35, which is past your bedtime; treat the movie as optional. Sunset is 7:16 and it drops to 55° overnight, so take a layer. Rain fallback: not needed — Friday is 0%.

NEW — A 6PM CONCERT, WHICH ALMOST NEVER HAPPENS
Yuja Wang opens the symphony season — at six o'clock
SAT SEP 19, 6PM  ·  BENAROYA HALL, DOWNTOWN
Opening night of the Seattle Symphony's 2026/2027 season, in the S. Mark Taper Foundation Auditorium at Benaroya Hall, 200 University Street. Xian Zhang, in her second season as music director, conducts; the soloist is Yuja Wang, the pianist who is probably the biggest draw the orchestra will book all year.
The programme is short and all Prokofiev: the suite from Lieutenant Kijé, written for the film of the same name, and the Third Piano Concerto, which is the showpiece and the reason to go. It is also the first public look at the hall's rebuilt lobbies and public spaces, finished over the summer.
Why it is in this brief: the start time. Doors at 4:30, music at 6 — an opening night you can attend and still be home at a sensible hour, which is not true of the other 6 or 7 special nights on this calendar. The page does not publish a price; single tickets go through the symphony's box office, and opening nights do sell, so this is a today job rather than a Friday one.

NEW — THE NOVEMBER BALLOT, OUT LOUD
Voter Guide Live, with Seattle CityClub and the public radio politics desk
WED SEP 16, 6:30PM  ·  FROM $17.85  ·  SOUTH LAKE UNION
Seattle CityClub, the civic-forum nonprofit, and Sound Politics, the public radio station's politics programme, take the November 3 general election ballot apart on stage: the big races, the state measures, and the local questions that actually change things. Scott Greenstone and Catharine Smith host, with state government reporter Sarah Mizes-Tan.
Audience questions are part of the format and you can submit yours in advance to shape the conversation — which, given what you build, is the part to use. The Collective Seattle, 400 Dexter Avenue North, walkable from South Lake Union. Tickets from $17.85, and there are genuinely reduced-price tickets for anyone for whom cost is a barrier — they say so on the listing.
6:30 start, indoors — a good use of the one evening this week where nothing outdoor is competing, and it leaves Wednesday daytime free for the first 73° sunny day.

TOMORROW — LAST CALL
An unreleased video about transit and station areas, walked through live
TUE SEP 15, 7PM  ·  $35  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
Third and final flag on this one, because it is tomorrow and it was still on sale when I checked this morning. Ray Delahanty, the planner behind the CityNerd channel, walks the room through a video he has not published yet on transit and station-area development around this region, then sits down with Ryan Packer, who has covered transportation and land use for The Urbanist since 2015.
The unreleased-video format is the whole point — you see the argument before the internet does, and the conversation afterwards is with the person who has been at the actual meetings. The Great Hall at Town Hall Seattle, 1119 Eighth Avenue. $35, or $65 with the 5:45 reception.
The one conflict: 7pm Tuesday is also the best remaining Venus evening. You cannot have both — but Venus is a five-minute look from a west-facing beach and this is a year's worth of regional planning argument, so take the talk. If you would rather have the sky, Wednesday at 4.5° is only marginally worse.

TONIGHT, IF YOU WANT IT
The foraging one — wild food you walk past every day
TONIGHT 7:30PM  ·  FROM $10  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
Alexis Nikole Nelson — the forager with several million people following her plant walks — on her first book, Happy Snacking, Don't Die!, which is half field guide and half cookbook, arranged by season: pickled magnolia flowers, milkweed buds, mushrooms. She is good on the ethics and the safety of it, which is the half of foraging that usually gets skipped.
Doors 6:30, talk at 7:30, Great Hall at Town Hall, tickets from $10 and rising to $129 with the book and better seats. Presented with Seattle Arts & Lectures. There is a livestream if you would rather not go out on a Monday.
It is in here because nature learning is thin this week — no minus tide, no aurora — and this is the one thing on the calendar that sends you back outside knowing more than you did.

MORE PAINT: graffiti, growth, homelessness, and who the city is for
WED SEP 16, 7:30PM  ·  $10–35 SLIDING  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
A photography book and a civic argument about Seattle exactly as it is right now, seen through graffiti, encampments, redevelopment, cleanup and return. It starts on the surface of the city and opens out into growth, public space and displacement — the same questions as the CityNerd night, approached from the street rather than from the plan.
Wyncote Northwest Forum at Town Hall, sliding scale from $10 — the cheapest serious thing on this week's list. It sits an hour after the Voter Guide night on the same evening, so pick one; this is the better pick if you would rather look at the city than at the ballot.

Local Sightings opens Friday — and what else is loose on Saturday
FRI SEP 18 ONWARD  ·  $7–15 A FILM  ·  CAPITOL HILL & ELSEWHERE
Local Sightings is the only festival anywhere devoted to films from this corner of the map — Washington, Oregon, Alaska, Idaho, Montana, British Columbia and the Yukon. It runs Sep 18–27 at the Northwest Film Forum, 1515 12th Avenue on Capitol Hill, in person only this year. Opening night is Friday at 7pm, Again Again (Mia Moore Merchant, 2026, 1h39), $15, or $7 for members. Single tickets across the festival run $7–15, three-film bundles $17–35.
Saturday is crowded, so briefly: the symphony at 6 is the headline above, but Audioasis Live at the radio station's Gathering Space, 472 First Avenue North, runs 5–9pm, $18 adult and $12 under-18, all ages, with TV Star, Plash and glass egg — the cheaper, smaller-room version of the same evening. Earlier in the day, the Burke Museum's bird-collision workshop, 2–4pm, $12, registration required: you sew a fabric bird, and each one stands for a specific bird killed against a Chicago window in 2023, tagged with its species and the date it died.
