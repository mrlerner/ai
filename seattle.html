Then Thursday 73°, Friday softens to 18% light rain, Saturday recovers to 72°. One more clock: the moon goes fully dark Thursday night, and two free star parties sit on the weekend behind it.

This week in Seattle
Water · music · free · last of the season
The last free concert of the year at the Ballard Locks is tomorrow at 2pm
MON SEP 7 (LABOR DAY), 2–3PM  ·  FREE, DONATIONS SUGGESTED  ·  CARL S. ENGLISH JR. BOTANICAL GARDEN AT THE LOCKS, 3015 NW 54TH ST, BALLARD
The Army Corps of Engineers has been running free 2pm concerts on the garden lawn at the locks every Saturday and Sunday since June 6 — community bands, big bands, a pipe band, a ukulele orchestra. The season ends Monday, and the closer is Moondance, a Van Morrison tribute band, on the holiday. There is one before it: Third Harbour plays today at 2pm. After Monday the lawn goes quiet until June.
This is a one-hour show on grass beside a working navigation lock, which is roughly the best possible version of an early evening that isn't an evening at all — you are home by four. Seating is not provided, so bring a chair or a blanket. Today is 72° and mostly sunny at 1%; Monday is 70° and mostly cloudy at 7%, which is fine for sitting outside and not fine for a sunburn. If it rains hard the Visitor Center takes calls at 206-783-7059; the indoor fallback is fifty yards away, because the fish-ladder viewing room is free and open 7am to 8:45pm and the coho are in it.
Quiet note on the salmon: the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife's daily count has not been updated since Thursday's 463-fish day — the running total still reads 972. No new number, but nothing has changed the picture: the run is on and state counters still put the true peak in the last two weeks of September.
Music · free · outdoors · last of the season
Dancing 'til Dusk closes out its sixteenth and final night Tuesday in Occidental Park
TUE SEP 8, 6–9PM (LESSON 6–7PM)  ·  FREE, NO REGISTRATION  ·  OCCIDENTAL PARK, PIONEER SQUARE
Seattle Parks puts a live band in a downtown park on summer Tuesdays and Thursdays, gives away a beginner dance lesson for the first hour, and lets the rest of the night run on the band. The 2026 season has sixteen nights and Tuesday is the last one. The closer is Jo Miller and Her Burly Roughnecks — country and honky-tonk, a long-running Seattle band with a reputation for getting people who did not plan to dance onto the bricks.
No partner needed — the lesson rotates partners through the hour, which is the whole design. It costs nothing, it starts at 6, and you can leave at 7:30 having had a full evening. Tuesday is 72° and mostly sunny at 1% rain, which is the best weather this season finale could have asked for. Occidental Park is the tree-covered brick square behind Pioneer Square Station, so it is a light-rail trip rather than a parking problem. If the sky turns, the honest fallback is that this one has no indoor twin — the fixed-roof alternative that night is the Dylan Hayes Octet album release at the Royal Room, 7:30pm, $20 in advance.
Nature & sky · a window that does not repeat
The moon goes fully dark at 8:27pm Thursday — then two free star parties on the weekend behind it
NEW MOON THU SEP 10, 8:27PM  ·  STAR PARTIES FRI SEP 11 (DUVALL) AND SAT SEP 12 (SNOQUALMIE POINT PARK), BOTH 7:30PM, BOTH FREE
New moon lands at 03:27 Friday Universal Time, which is 8:27pm Thursday here. That makes Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights the darkest of the month — no moonlight at all competing with the Milky Way, which is still high in the south in early September. The Seattle Astronomical Society has parked two free public stargazes right on it: Duvall on Friday at 7:30pm and Snoqualmie Point Park on Saturday at 7:30pm, both with club telescopes set up and members happy to point them at things. Bonney Lake also runs one Saturday at 8pm.
Take Saturday over Friday. Friday carries an 18% chance of light rain and Friday night stays at 18%; Saturday daytime is 72° and mostly sunny at 13%, and Snoqualmie Point Park sits above the valley fog with a genuinely dark eastern sky. Both start at 7:30, which is generous — astronomical dark is later, so the useful hour is 8:30 to 9:30 and you can be driving home before ten.
Two other numbers from the feeds, both modest. Tuesday morning brings the lowest tide in the eight-day file: −1.0 ft at 9:12am at the Seattle gauge — flagged notable, not extreme, so it is a decent poke-around-the-rocks morning rather than a drop-everything one, and it lands on a 72° sunny day. And the planetary Kp index peaks at 4.67 tonight around 11pm, which the space-weather feed calls short of anything visible at this latitude — no aurora expected.

Cities & ideas · water
A two-hour walk through what replaced the viaduct — ending on the Overlook Walk
SAT SEP 12, 10AM–12PM  ·  $27.50 ($31.29 WITH FEES); $24.50 SENIORS 65+ AND STUDENTS  ·  STARTS NE CORNER OF 1ST AVE & MARION ST, DOWNTOWN
The Seattle Architecture Foundation — the volunteer group that runs the city's docent-led building walks — puts this one on maybe once a month, and it is the one that overlaps hardest with what you build software about. It reads the central waterfront as a design problem: a shoreline built for industrial trade, then buried under an elevated highway for sixty years, then handed to Field Operations, the firm that designed the High Line in New York, to turn into public space. The route walks the historic pieces and the new ones against each other and ends up on the Overlook Walk above the aquarium's Ocean Pavilion, which is the single best argument the project has.
Saturday is 72° and mostly sunny at 13% — fine for two hours on foot, and the tour runs rain or shine anyway since it is a street walk. It is a morning commitment, which leaves the whole afternoon and evening free, and two of this week's other picks are that same Saturday afternoon. The same 10am slot also offers Seattle Center: The World's Fair & Beyond at the same price if the 1962 campus interests you more than the shoreline.
Music · partly free · starts at 4:30
Twenty bands on twelve stages down Jackson Street — and the outdoor ones are free
SAT SEP 12, FROM 4:30PM  ·  TWO STREET STAGES FREE; $35 ADVANCE / $40 DOOR FOR THE TEN INDOOR ROOMS  ·  S JACKSON ST, 18TH TO 30TH BLOCKS, CENTRAL DISTRICT
Thirteenth year, and it changed its name from the Jackson Street Jazz Walk this season. Twelve stages along a dozen blocks of Jackson Street — the corridor that was Seattle's Black music district from the 1940s through the 1960s, which the festival is explicitly built to commemorate, with historical displays along the route. Twenty acts across jazz, Afro-Latin, funk, blues and soul, including the Alex Dugdale FADE-Tet, the D'Vonne Lewis Quartet, Eugenie Jones, the Joe Brazil Legacy Band, and SERICA, a world-music ensemble with Chinese, Iranian and American players. New this year is a listening-room conversation with trumpeter Nathan Breedlove, twice Grammy-nominated.
The two outdoor street stages cost nothing; the wristband is what opens the ten indoor rooms and the rooftop sets. Either way it starts at 4:30, which is unusually civilized for a street festival — you can catch three hours and still be home by eight. Co-presented with Pratt Fine Arts Center, whose free Fall Open House runs 5 to 8pm on the same block with live glass-blowing and art demonstrations. Saturday is 72° and mostly sunny at 13%; the indoor venues are the built-in fallback if it turns.
Music · small room · over by 7:30
A jazz show that starts at 5:30 and is finished at 7:30: Ray Skjelbred's trio in Columbia City
SAT SEP 12, DOORS 5PM, SHOW 5:30–7:30PM  ·  $20 ADVANCE ONLINE ONLY / $25 AT THE DOOR  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S  ·  ALL AGES UNTIL 10PM
The Royal Room hangs a hard end time on this one, which almost never happens: 5:30 to 7:30, stated on the ticket page. Skjelbred is a Seattle institution — a Chicago-raised pianist who studied here with the local traditional player Johnny Wittwer, co-founded one of the city's first classic-jazz bands, and accompanied blues singers Victoria Spivey and Barbara Dane. He plays early swing, New Orleans, and blues, and he is doing it here with reed player Jacob Zimmerman and bassist Matt Weiner.
Practical notes the venue is explicit about: advance tickets are online only, they will not sell them at the door in advance, and a ticket does not reserve a seat — seating is first come, first served, so the 5pm doors matter if you want a chair. It is a restaurant as well as a room, and table reservations require an advance ticket. This and the Jackson Street festival are the same Saturday afternoon in neighborhoods four miles apart; the street festival is the free one, this is the seated one.
Music · free · daytime
Bumbershoot's Sunday headliner plays a free half-hour set at KEXP on Monday morning
MON–THU  ·  FREE, OPEN TO THE PUBLIC  ·  KEXP STUDIO, NORTHWEST ROOMS, SEATTLE CENTER
Death Cab for Cutie headlines Bumbershoot tonight across the street; on Monday at 11am they play the KEXP studio for free, open to the public, no ticket. That is the trade the in-studio sessions offer all week — a band you would otherwise pay a festival wristband to see, in a room that holds a few dozen people. Three more follow: Haute & Freddy, Tuesday 3pm; Altın Gün, Wednesday 11am, the Amsterdam band that plays Anatolian folk songs as psychedelic rock; and Shabazz Palaces, Thursday 3pm, Ishmael Butler's long-running Seattle experimental hip-hop project.
Each runs roughly half an hour, and they are broadcast sessions rather than concerts — the video goes up on the station's channel later. KEXP publishes no capacity number and takes no list, so it is arrive-early-and-hope; the daytime slots are the honest catch, since Monday and Wednesday are 11am on working days. Monday is the holiday, which makes the Death Cab one genuinely available.
Ideas & lectures
Two nights at Town Hall, both 75 minutes, both sliding scale from $10
WED SEP 9 AND THU SEP 10  ·  DOORS 6:30, START 7:30, DONE BY 8:45  ·  $10–35 SLIDING SCALE  ·  TOWN HALL SEATTLE, 1119 8TH AVE, FIRST HILL
Wednesday is Casey Michel on United States of Oligarchy in conversation with the longtime local interviewer Steve Scher, in the Mehdi Reading Room — Michel writes about how foreign money moves through American shell companies and lobbying. Thursday is Charles Johnson and David Horsey in the Wyncote Northwest Forum: Johnson won the National Book Award for Middle Passage and also draws; Horsey won two Pulitzer Prizes for editorial cartooning and also writes. The evening is the two of them on working across more than one form.
These are the sturdy indoor option in a week that is mostly outdoors. Wednesday is the 75° cloudless day, so if you only take one, the case for Thursday is that you spend the good afternoon outside and the talk costs you nothing but the evening. Doors 6:30, up at 7:30, out around 8:45 — a 75-minute program that respects a nine o'clock bedtime.
