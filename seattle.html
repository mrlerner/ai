TONIGHT · NATURE & SKY · THE STORM ARRIVED (IN DAYLIGHT) · FREE
Aurora watch, second wave: Kp 5.67 from 8 to 11pm, 3–6% cloud
SUN OCT 11  ·  SUNSET 6:30, FULL DARK 8:11  ·  OBSERVED YESTERDAY: KP 7 (G3) 8–11AM, 6.67 TO 2PM, 4 BY EVENING  ·  TONIGHT’S TABLE: 5.33 5–8PM, 5.67 8–11PM, 4.67 TO 2AM
After three slips, the coronal mass ejections from October 7–8 hit Saturday morning and pushed the planetary index to 7, a strong G3 storm, between 8 and 11am Pacific, still 6.67 at noon: a display that would have been easy from Carkeek, delivered to Europe and Asia instead. By your Saturday evening it had fallen to Kp 4, then 3.67, which is why nothing showed from Snoqualmie Point. NOAA’s 5:30am table today still carries a second wave tonight: Kp 5.33 from 5 to 8pm, 5.67 (moderate, G2) from 8 to 11pm, 4.67 until 2am, from the tail of those same ejections plus a coronal-hole wind stream. The sky is the best of the week for it: 3% cloud at 7, 6% at 8, 4% at 9, a 2%-lit moon that sets at 6:30 with the sun. The honest caveat, and it is bigger than usual: the forecasters’ written discussion, issued the same minute as the table, says the ejection effects are “diminishing” and calls for only “unsettled to isolated active” conditions through Monday. The numbers and the prose disagree. Treat tonight as a check-the-app night, not a drive.
The plan: at 8, look at the 30-minute aurora forecast and the live Kp. If the oval reaches the Washington line, Carkeek Park or the north end of Golden Gardens facing north-northwest, 3-second phone exposures every few minutes until 9; 52° at 8pm. If it is quiet, Saturn is up in the southeast all evening and the stars are as good as they get this week. Second chance: the coronal-hole stream is forecast to bring isolated G1 (Kp 4–5) late Monday into Tuesday; Monday evening is 1% cloud at dusk and 11% after 8.
