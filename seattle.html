This week in Seattle
Nature & sky · free · a window that closes on itself
Venus is as bright as it gets all year and sinking half a degree a night — and Tuesday through Thursday are the clearest evenings of the month
TUE–THU, 7:30–7:50PM  ·  FREE  ·  ANY BEACH WITH AN OPEN WESTERN HORIZON
Two things are moving in opposite directions. Venus is climbing toward its greatest brilliancy — the National Aeronautics and Space Administration marks the peak of this evening appearance on Sep 18; the Jet Propulsion Laboratory ephemeris, computed this morning for 47.61°N, 122.33°W, puts the actual magnitude minimum a week later, −4.804 on Sep 24–25, against −4.77 this Tuesday. The gap is far too small for an eye to see, so treat the whole stretch as peak. Meanwhile Venus is dropping toward the sunset, and that you can see: at 7:30pm it stands 5.0° above the west-southwest horizon Tuesday, 4.5° Wednesday, 4.0° Thursday, 3.4° Friday.
The window, precisely: sunset is 7:23pm Tuesday, 7:21 Wednesday, 7:19 Thursday; Venus sets around 8:03, 8:01 and 7:57. So you have roughly 7:30 to 7:50, after which it is into the haze on the horizon. It will be the brightest thing in that part of the sky by a wide margin — you will not need to hunt for it. Higher up and to the left, the waxing crescent moon goes from a quarter lit Tuesday to half lit Friday, and the space agency has it sliding past Antares, the red heart of Scorpius, and the Teapot of Sagittarius between Sep 14 and 20.
Weather call: hourly cloud cover at 7pm is 6% Tuesday, 0% Wednesday, 1% Thursday, 7% Friday, then 33% Saturday. Tuesday buys you the most altitude, Wednesday the cleanest air; either works. You need an unobstructed west-southwest horizon at almost sea level — Golden Gardens, Carkeek, Alki, or the west bluff at Discovery Park. From inside the city, a hill will not save you: three degrees of altitude is roughly a fist-width at arm's length above the water. Indoor fallback: none needed — if it clouds over, the whole thing costs you a fifteen-minute drive.
Ideas · urbanism · Tuesday
A live walkthrough of an unreleased video about what we have and have not built around the light rail stations
TUE SEP 15, 7PM  ·  $35  ·  GREAT HALL, TOWN HALL SEATTLE, FIRST HILL
Ray Delahanty makes CityNerd, the transport-planning channel, after twenty years doing the work for real; Ryan Packer covers transportation, land use and traffic safety for The Urbanist, the local urban-policy publication. The billed event is not a lecture but Ray walking the room through a video he has not published yet, on transit and station-area development around Puget Sound, then arguing about it with Ryan — housing, transit and whether anyone is planning them as one thing.
Getting in: $35, confirmed still on sale on the Town Hall listing this morning, tickets through the event page. Collision worth knowing: 7pm start puts you out around the time Venus sets, so Tuesday is a choose-one — the sky is just as good Wednesday and Thursday.
Film · five nights · Ballard
A film festival built on your schedule: short at 6, feature at 6:15, out the door by eight
TUE–SAT SEP 15–19  ·  $14, $12 MEMBERS  ·  OSBERG HALL, NATIONAL NORDIC MUSEUM, 2655 NW MARKET ST
The Nordic museum's annual festival, this year built around LGBTQ+ stories from across the Nordic countries, and the nightly shape is unusually civilised: a short film at 6:00, the feature at 6:15, home by eight.
The pick is Wednesday: Sauna (Denmark, 2025), 6:15–8:00, preceded at 6:00 by Skádja, a short from Sápmi about two women meeting in the woods. Tuesday opens with Show Me Love (Sweden, 1998), 6:15–7:45, the small-town teenage classic. Thursday is Heartstone (Iceland), 6:15–8:25, a boy and his best friend in a fishing village — the longest night of the week. Friday has a discussion at 6:00 then And Then We Danced 6:30–8:30. Saturday runs all day: the 1924 silent Michael at 11am, Girls Lost at 2, Tom of Finland at 4:30, Dreams at 7:30.
Getting in: $14 general, $12 members per film; Saturday pass $52 / $44; full festival $100 / $90, which includes museum entry. Ballard, walking distance from the locks if you want to make an evening of it.
Ideas · Thursday is a double · one free
How to change someone's mind without lying to them at 5, and the man who ran the Panama Papers at 7:30
THU SEP 17  ·  FREE AND $10–35  ·  UNIVERSITY DISTRICT, THEN FIRST HILL
5–6pm, free — Colin Marshall, a University of Washington philosopher, launching Just Hear Me Out: How to Change Minds Without Lies, Bullshit, or Moral Compromise, in conversation with his colleague José Jorge Mendoza. At the Barnes & Noble in the University District, 4324 University Way Northeast — the store inside the University Book Store. Registration is free on Eventbrite and takes an hour flat. This is the only University of Washington public lecture on the calendar before Oct 8.
7:30pm, $10–35 sliding — Gerard Ryle in the Wyncote Northwest Forum at Town Hall, with Jas Randhawa. Ryle directs the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists — the outfit behind the Panama Papers, a Pulitzer in 2017, and something like $1.4 billion in taxes recovered by governments since. It is the closest thing to a lesson in how a few hundred reporters in forty countries keep a secret long enough to break it.
You can do both: book to Town Hall between them. If you would rather not, Timothy White Eagle's Has No End is free at 7:30 in the Great Hall the same night — the performance whose earlier date was cancelled, now rescheduled and costing nothing.

Nature · civic · Saturday afternoon
Sew one fabric bird, tagged with the name and death date of a real bird that hit a real window
SAT SEP 19, 2–4PM  ·  $12, REGISTRATION REQUIRED  ·  BURKE MUSEUM, 4303 MEMORIAL WAY NE
A national civic-art project of more than 10,000 bird sculptures, and the reason it works is the bookkeeping: each bird you make is assigned one specific bird that died from a window collision in Chicago in 2023, and gets tagged with its scientific name, its date of death, and your name. The fabric is donated scraps that would otherwise be landfill. You leave with material about bird-safe windows, which is the point — this is a data problem dressed as a craft afternoon.
Run by Nora Lee, a biology doctoral student at the University of Washington who works in the Burke's ornithology collection on hummingbird behaviour and evolution. $12, registration required, tickets online. Bring fabric, scissors and glue if you have them — supplies on site are limited. Weather: irrelevant, it is indoors, and Saturday is the one day the sky turns (33% cloud) anyway.
Music · small room · starts at five
Three Northwest bands playing live on the radio in a room that holds a couple of hundred people, 5 to 9pm
SAT SEP 19, 5–9PM  ·  $18 ADULT, $12 UNDER 18  ·  KEXP GATHERING SPACE, 472 1ST AVE N, UPTOWN
Audioasis is the listener-supported station's long-running show for Northwest music, and a few times a year it comes out of the studio and broadcasts live in front of people. Saturday: TV Star, Plash and glass egg, hosted by DJ Kennady, with an hour-long opening set from Phoenix, an alumnus of the station's teen programme. It is built as a shared listening event rather than a gig — all ages, seated-and-standing, and you are audibly part of a broadcast.
Why this one: a five o'clock start and a nine o'clock end is a rare shape for live music in this town, and it is in the Gathering Space at the foot of Seattle Center rather than up a flight of stairs in a bar. $18 adult, $12 for 18-and-under, tickets on sale now through the station. There is a bar for those who want one; it is nowhere near the point of the evening.
