This Week in Seattle
TONIGHT · NEW · BRANCH-OUT
Christopher Icasiano’s band at Kenyon Hall
FRI NOV 6, 7:30PM  ·  $20  ·  ALL AGES  ·  KENYON HALL, WEST SEATTLE
Seattle drummer and composer who has toured with Fleet Foxes and chaired the Earshot Jazz board, leading a band of Tomo Nakayama (hushed indie folk-pop), pianist Matt Williams and bassist Abbey Blackwell. Sounds like free-jazz looseness folded into indie-rock songs; sits next to Yo La Tengo’s long instrumental side. Opening: OC-EN, an ambient project with Fleet Foxes’ Morgan Henderson. Kenyon Hall is a small 1910s hall with folding chairs, a rainy-night room. Or in your folk lane: Corey Harris & Cedric Watson, West-Africa-rooted acoustic blues with Louisiana fiddle, Town Hall, 7:30, $25–50 sliding scale (a full concert, not a 75-minute talk).
SATURDAY · FREE · DRY UNTIL 4PM
Comics fair in Georgetown, Diwali at the Armory
SAT NOV 7  ·  SHORT RUN 11AM–6PM, SEATTLE DESIGN CENTER  ·  DIWALI 12–6PM, SEATTLE CENTER ARMORY  ·  BOTH FREE
Saturday is the week’s mildest day (63°) and stays dry until about 4pm, so the move is a morning walk outside (Discovery Park bluff or Golden Gardens) and the afternoon indoors. Short Run, the independent comics and zine festival, is 200+ exhibitors plus artist talks, including Stranger co-founder James Sturm on a comic adapted from a World War II Army pamphlet about fascism; bring cash. Across town, Diwali: Lights of India fills the Armory with classical Indian dance (Bharatanatyam, Kuchipudi) on the stage all afternoon, free food, and kolam floor art. Rain after 4pm is the fallback built in: both are indoors.
SUNDAY AFTERNOON · $0–20
Mount St. Helens as a symphony
SUN NOV 8, 2PM (TALK 1:30)  ·  $0–20  ·  TOWN HALL GREAT HALL, FIRST HILL
The volunteer Seattle Festival Orchestra opens its 50th season with Armenian music: Khachaturian’s Sabre Dance and Spartacus, and Alan Hovhaness’s Mount St. Helens symphony, which ends on the 1980 eruption. Pay what you can. If you want a night out instead, Jake Xerxes Fussell plays old ballads on slow fingerpicked guitar at the Tractor, 8pm, 21+ (late, but next to Steve Gunn).
MONDAY · URBANISM
Washington’s best new buildings, judged on stage
MON NOV 9, 7PM  ·  $10–60  ·  TOWN HALL, FIRST HILL
The American Institute of Architects Seattle chapter’s annual Honor Awards: the jury walks through the year’s winning projects around the state, from housing to civic buildings. A good read on what the region is building and why.
VETERANS DAY · FREE TALK, THEN SOLO PIANO
Housing at 5, Brubeck and Cecil Taylor at 7:30
WED NOV 11  ·  HOUSING TOWN HALL 5PM, FREE  ·  PIANO 7:30PM, $20 ADVANCE / $25 DOOR, ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Gregg Colburn, the University of Washington author of Homelessness Is a Housing Problem, makes the case for housing as infrastructure, then a panel on King County’s shortfall (Housing Development Consortium hosts). New this week: the Royal Room’s Piano Starts Here puts Seattle pianists on the house Steinway for an evening pairing Dave Brubeck’s cool-jazz hits with Cecil Taylor’s dense free improvisation. Your Glenn Gould lane, with a jazz twist; doors 6:30, advance tickets online only.
NEXT THURSDAY · NEW · LOCAL BAND
Clouds of the West: cello-and-guitar orchestral rock
THU NOV 12, 7:30PM (DOORS 6:30)  ·  $20 ADVANCE / $25 DOOR  ·  ROYAL ROOM, COLUMBIA CITY
Seattle band built around cellist Rose Bellini and guitarist-singer Quentin Ertel, with viola, Hammond organ, vibraphone and horns; their 2026 debut Glass Radio moves from noir reverb to country-tinged strum. Sounds like The War on Drugs’ wide-highway feel with a chamber section; opener Erin Jorgensen plays marimba. Earlier the same evening: a seismic-retrofit forum on saving old buildings, Town Hall, 6pm, $0–100.
