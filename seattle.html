SUNDAY · BRANCH-OUT OF THE WEEK · 2PM · DRY, 58°
Grupo Falso Baiano: an afternoon of Brazilian choro
SUN OCT 18  ·  2–4PM  ·  $20 ADVANCE / $25 DOOR, ALL AGES  ·  THE ROYAL ROOM, 5000 RAINIER AVE S, COLUMBIA CITY
Choro is Rio’s acoustic instrumental music from around 1900, guitars, mandolin-like cavaquinho and winds playing quick, tangled melodies. This group has played it for two decades and recorded with clarinetist Anat Cohen. Sounds like a string band playing chamber jazz. Sits next to Hermanos Gutiérrez and Steve Gunn’s fingerpicking. Still on the Royal Room calendar this morning. You’re out at 4, with daylight to spare.
Or, free downtown: the Benaroya Hall Open House: doors 9am, National Geographic photographer Steve Winter on wild cats at 11, and the Seattle Symphony with The Head and the Heart at 4. Free tickets for the 4pm show are given out in person 9am–1pm, two per person. (Schedule as read Oct 14; the hall’s site put us in a queue this morning.)

Orionid meteors: set the alarm for 4. The moon is down and the sky is clear
WED OCT 21  ·  BEST ABOUT 2:15–5AM  ·  UP TO ~20 AN HOUR UNDER DARK SKY  ·  FREE, A DARK PARK WITH A WIDE SOUTH-EAST VIEW
These are the Orionids, dust from Halley’s Comet, and they peak the nights of Oct 21–22. This morning’s National Weather Service hourly grid puts 8% cloud from midnight to 5am Wednesday, rising to 34% at 5 and 6am. Thursday morning is 41–76% cloud, so Wednesday is the one shot. The 74%-lit moon sets around 2:15am (JPL Horizons), so the dark window is roughly 2:15 to 5. That’s a little earlier than yesterday’s brief said, because the clouds now arrive at 5. Try 4 to 5am: lie back and look high to the south and east around Orion. Magnuson, Discovery Park or the beach at Golden Gardens beat the backyard. It’ll be 46°, so bring a blanket.
