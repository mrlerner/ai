Old Snowy, then move camp to Goat Lake

Morning: ~6 mi RT · +2,200 ft · Afternoon: ~3 mi · +600 ft with packs

  • Morning, light packs: up to the PCT, north to the saddle, then the old-PCT spine to Old Snowy's summit — steep but straightforward, brief easy scramble at top. Rainier, Adams, St. Helens, the Knife's Edge. Go early; the ridge is exposed if weather builds. No ice axe needed in late August.
  • Afternoon: break camp, traverse Lily Basin Trail to Goat Lake. Carry water — sources on this stretch are dry by late August. Some exposure on the ridge approach.
  • Camp on the knoll above the lake outlet, ≥100 ft from shore. It's windy — pick the most sheltered spot and guy the tent out properly.
  • Sunset option: Hawkeye Point, ~3 mi RT / +900 ft from the lake — turquoise lake below, three volcanoes on the skyline. Or save it for Tuesday morning; the junction is only 0.9 mi from camp.

Driving directions: Packwood → trailhead

US-12 west 2.7 mi → left on FR 21 for ~13 mi (washboard gravel, sedan-OK) → slight left on FR 2150 ~3 mi. Allow 45–60 min. Packwood is your last gas and cell signal.

Fire, food & rules

No campfires — anywhere, at all. Forest-wide Stage 2 fire ban through Oct 31, 2026, wilderness included. Canister/propane stoves with a shutoff valve are fine (clear 3 ft around). Solid-fuel/Esbit stoves are banned too.
Bear canister — your call, evaluated

Skipping the hard canister is reasonable here. It's not required (no food-storage order exists for Goat Rocks), black-bear pressure is low, and bear encounters at these camps are rare. Your usual rule holds.

But don't skip protection entirely: the actual food thieves at Snowgrass and Goat Lake are mice and marmots, and these are two of the busiest camps in the south Cascades — rodents there are habituated pack-raiders. There are no good hang trees at either camp. The right middle ground is an Ursack (or any rodent-resistant bag) with an odor-barrier liner, tied to a stunted tree or boulder. Worst case, sleep with food in an OPSAK inside the tent — common practice here, just never leave food in packs outside.

Separate issue: goats want your salt, not your food. Don't leave sweaty hipbelts, poles, or boots out overnight — they'll chew them. Pee on bare rock 50+ ft from camp and trail.

  • Free self-issue wilderness permit at the trailhead. Group limit 12. No camping within 100 ft of lakes or the PCT; none in Snowgrass Flat proper.
  • Filter all water — heavy goat and human traffic. Carry extra capacity for the traverse and the descent (both dry late season).
  • Keep 50 yards from goats. If one gets pushy: yell, wave a jacket.

Gear beyond the usual

  • Canister stove + fuel (fires banned)
  • Ursack / rodent bag + OPSAK liner
  • 20°F bags — lows 35–45°F, can touch freezing
  • Storm stakes & guylines for Goat Lake wind
  • Trekking poles (scree, long descent)
  • Sun kit: sunscreen, sunglasses, hats — it's all above treeline
  • Extra water capacity (3L+ each for day 2)
  • Rain shell + warm layer, even in a sunny forecast
  • Bug spray (mostly done by late Aug, but cheap insurance)
  • NW Forest Pass for the dash
