How to Help

Put up a flyer at a place you care about, get your neighbors to sign the voter pledge, and get every 2027 council candidate to say in public whether they commit to its three things.

The flyer and the pledge

Print a flyer for a place you care about, a park, a school, or a busy sidewalk, and tape it to a pole nearby or drop it on porches. Its QR code opens the pledge.

The home address turns a signature into a verified voter in a district. A councilmember notices 2,000 voters in their own district more than 20,000 anonymous names.

Organizing timeline to the 2027 election

All seven district council seats are on the ballot in 2027. The goal is a list of verified voters in every district, large enough for candidates to notice, ready before filing week. Signer goals are suggestions.

  • Nov 2026 – Jan 2027
    Start    Set up the organization in the right legal form first (see what to check). Launch at Cal Anderson and one or two public places per district, each with its own sign and page. Publish the first monthly progress report from the city's report data. Recruit a captain for each place: a neighbor who keeps the sign up and brings in signers. Goal: 1,000 signers.
  • Feb – Apr 2027
    Build    Signs go up at schools and parks in all seven districts. Build a coalition across Seattle's usual split: PTSAs and parents near schools, park friends groups, community councils, neighborhood business groups, and at least one shelter or treatment provider who backs rooms first. Pitch reporters a data story every month. Invite sitting councilmembers to sign, especially those running again. Talk to people thinking about running. Goal: 500 verified signers per district by filing week.
  • May 2027
    Ask    As soon as filing week closes, send the pledge to every filed candidate, with a three-week deadline. Announce the dates of the candidate forums (below) at the same time.
  • June 2027
    Publish    Publish the item-by-item grid for every district, with "no answer" shown as plainly as yes or no. Hold a candidate forum on public places in each district, or a few combined forums. Captains door-knock and table near their places with the grid.
  • Mid-July 2027
    Ballots out    Primary ballots are mailed. Every signer gets their own district's grid by email and text, plus a reminder the week before the deadline. Goal: 2,000 verified signers per district.
  • August 3, 2027
    Primary    The top two in each district advance.
  • Aug – Sept 2027
    Narrow    Ask the two finalists again, and hold a second round of forums with just them.
  • Mid-October 2027
    Ballots out    General ballots are mailed. Send the final grid to every signer, with clips from the forums.
  • November 2, 2027
    General    Election day.
  • 2028 on
    Hold them to it    Grade the winners on their votes: the ordinance, the budget each fall, siting rules. The same list and pledge carry over to the 2029 city attorney and mayor races.

Candidate forums on public places

Private citizens can host candidate forums, and many Seattle neighborhood groups already do. A forum puts every candidate on the record, on video, with the same questions.

  • Invite every candidate in the race, on the same terms, and say who was invited. An empty chair with a name card is news. Inviting only friendly candidates makes it a campaign event, which raises legal problems.
  • Co-host across the split, for example a PTSA, a neighborhood business group and a shelter or treatment provider. A local newsroom or KUOW as co-host or moderator makes candidates likelier to come.
  • Use a neutral moderator, and publish the rules and question topics in advance. Equal time, the same questions for each candidate, and a lightning round of yes-or-no questions from the pledge items.
  • Ground the questions in the place. For example: "Your district had 188 places with encampment reports near parks and schools in September. What's that number on election day if you win?" Questions can also come from neighbors through the pledge page.
  • Hold it at a public place on the list (a school gym, a library, a community center near the park), record it, and post the full video and short clips.
  • Check the rules first. If the host is a 501(c)(3), the IRS expects a forum to be nonpartisan and to cover a range of issues, which a single-topic forum may not meet. A 501(c)(4) or an informal neighborhood group has more room. Ask Washington's Public Disclosure Commission whether hosting costs count as contributions.

How candidates are scored

Candidates are scored on each pledge item separately: yes, no or no answer. After the election, winners are graded on their votes.

The legal form

A 501(c)(3) can send every candidate a questionnaire but can't say "vote for the ones who sign." That needs a 501(c)(4) or a registered political committee, and spending on it may need to be reported to Washington's Public Disclosure Commission. Also check the 2027 filing and ballot dates, and which neighborhoods are in which district, before they go on a sign.
