The Fed is untouchable

99.4% no-change at the April meeting — during an active blockade of the world's most important oil chokepoint. The market is not predicting policy. It is predicting Powell's psychology.

Fed: April meeting & succession

24h volume (Fed no-change) $1.13M

This is the single most-priced certainty on the board. There is an active blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, oil has six different binary lines running, a U.S.–Iran ceasefire is mid-negotiation, and the market has priced the next FOMC meeting at 99.4% hold. That is not a forecast about inflation or growth. It is a forecast about institutional posture: in a regime where the executive branch is openly pressuring the Fed, doing nothing is the only move that can't be read as capitulation.

The succession line tells the same story. Warsh — orthodox, hawkish, predictable — is at 94.5%. Shelton — heterodox, gold-standard-curious, the actual political pick — is at 1.3%. The market is saying: even the people who want a different Fed are not betting on getting one. The institutional immune system is the trade.

The CEO read: the press obituary isn't wrong about the destination; it's wrong about when the hearse arrives. If you are writing the "Starmer is finished" column this week, you are on the wrong side of $565K of concentrated opinion saying not yet. Caveat: the May/June/December lines each trade under $20K in daily volume. The April line — where the "not this month" signal lives — is $565K and robust. Treat the year-end 60% number as directional, not precise.

Commodities · Five-line cross-check

Oil refuses to spike

A blockade of the Strait of Hormuz is in active force. The market gives a $120 print less than a 1-in-10 shot, a $200 print less than 1-in-100, and a drop to $70 better odds than either.

WTI crude — April resolution (end of month)
WTI hits $120 (HIGH) 9.7% WTI hits $140 (HIGH) 3.2% WTI hits $200 (HIGH) 0.7% WTI hits $70 (LOW) 10.5% U.S. blockade lifted by Apr 18 (today) 3.9%

Read those five numbers together. A blockade is happening. The blockade will probably not be lifted in the next few hours (3.9%, resolves today). And yet the market thinks oil is more likely to fall to $70 than to spike to $120. That is a coherent worldview — but only one. Translation: the market believes the blockade is cosmetic. Tankers are being inspected, not sunk. The Iranian crude is being re-routed, not destroyed. The "crisis premium" the news cycle keeps pricing into headlines is not making it into the futures pit.

If you're a CFO with hedging exposure: the prediction market is calling the news media a coward. It is also calling OPEC's surplus capacity real. The asymmetric trade is not against $200 oil — it's against the idea that geopolitical chokepoints still move the spot.

If you are a donor, a political consultant, or a journalist covering 2028, your model should currently weight an unnamed governor or two-term mayor meaningfully higher than the named-four consensus. The market already does.

Geopolitics · Fear vs. price

China isn't moving on Taiwan in 2026

8.7% by year-end. 2.4% by June. Every Pentagon hearing, every think-tank panel, every cable segment treats invasion as imminent. The price says it's a 1-in-12 year, not a tail of any normal distribution.

China invasion of Taiwan
Invades by Jun 30, 2026 2.4% Invades by Dec 31, 2026 8.7%

An 8.7% annual probability translates to a roughly 11-year average wait if it stays constant. That is not "imminent." That is the same order of magnitude as a major U.S. recession in any given year. Yet defense-contractor valuations, semiconductor supply-chain memos, and Pacific deployment chatter all price it as a coin flip. One of these markets is wrong.

The CEO read: the prediction market is calling the China–Taiwan story structurally overpriced as a near-term tail event — and on this particular question, the prediction market has more skin in the game than the analysts. If your scenario planning has invasion-this-year as anything above ~10%, you are paying a premium for a fear the participants closest to it have already discounted. (None of which means the long-term risk is small. It means the this-year risk is.)

A 30-point spread between "Iran agrees" (33.5%) and "U.S. obtains" (3.3%). The deal is paper, not metal.

This is the most surgically interesting price on the entire board today. The announcement of an Iranian uranium surrender is given a 1-in-3 chance by month-end. The actual physical transfer of that uranium to the United States gets 3.3%. Even a month later, the transfer line only climbs to 22.5%. The market is forecasting, with extraordinary specificity, the texture of the deal: a signed agreement, a press conference, a third-party verifier (IAEA? Russian? If you wanted to write the story before it happens, the price already wrote it for you. "Iran agrees to suspend enrichment, U.S. lifts sanctions, IAEA inspectors return to Natanz" — that is a 33% outcome by Apr 30. "U.S. flies the HEU to Oak Ridge" — that is a 3% outcome by Apr 30. The first is a deal. The second is a regime change in disguise. The market knows the difference and is pricing it to the basis point.

Cross-market · Internal contradiction

68% peace by June. 32% U.S. invasion by 2027.

Both prices are real. Both have meaningful volume. They nearly partition the probability space — which is itself the story.

Peace deal vs. invasion — sum to 100
Permanent peace deal by Jun 30 68.0% U.S. invades Iran before 2027 32.5%
Naive sum 100.5%

The two numbers add to within a point of 100%. That is either the cleanest "peace or war" partition the prediction markets have ever produced — or it's a sign the same traders are sloshing dollars between adjacent contracts and producing a coincidence. The path-dependent reading: the market believes the next eight months end in either a signed deal or a unilateral escalation, and almost nothing in between. Status-quo grinding-on is being given roughly zero weight.

That is unusual. Most international standoffs trade with a fat "muddle through" middle. Iran does not. Read it as: the participants closest to this story believe Trump's posture forces a binary — either he gets the win, or he uses the failure as the rationale. There is no third year of sanctions and proxy attacks in the price.

Methodology & Caveats

Data source. All prices, volumes, and liquidity figures are pulled from the Polymarket Gamma API as of the morning of April 18, 2026. The set was filtered from approximately 1,500 active markets to roughly 180 newsworthy markets (excluding sports parlays, crypto-price binaries, and markets resolved > 99.5% to one side or already past their end date), then narrowed to the 36 markets cited in this edition.

What these prices represent. Polymarket prices are the marginal trading price of a $1-payoff binary contract, expressed as an implied probability. They reflect the views of the participants who bothered to trade — a self-selected, capital-weighted, and globally distributed group. They are not surveys, not expert panels, and not the wisdom of crowds in any pure sense. Treat them as the price at which a real counterparty was willing to take the other side.

Thin-market caveats. Markets with 24h volume under ~$50K can be noise. We have flagged 24h volume on every callout. The Stewart-2028 line is included precisely because its volume is anomalous, not because its price is.

"Consensus" baselines are editorial. When this paper says a price is "against the consensus," the consensus is our reading of mainstream U.S. and UK press coverage and elite institutional posture. We are not running a sentiment classifier. The disagreement is the interesting object; you may evaluate the consensus claim yourself.

Link policy. Every market cited in this edition links directly to its Polymarket page. Prices are a snapshot at publication — click through to see current state, order book, and full history.

What's missing. This MVP edition uses Polymarket only. Future editions will cross-reference Kalshi, Manifold, and (where data is available) Predictit and PolyBet for cross-platform divergence — a category of story this edition does not yet contain. Volume-of-conversation overlays from news and social are also planned.

The Ledger does not give investment advice. Prediction-market prices are forecasts, not recommendations. Markets are sometimes wrong; that is what makes them interesting to read like a newspaper.
