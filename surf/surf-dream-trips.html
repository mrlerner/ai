The rule for every trip on this board: a genuinely new part of the world, waves inside the band, and a crowd filter — fog, distance, obscurity, or cold — that keeps the lineups honest. Working man's breaks only.

Chile & Peru travel log

A log written in advance — entries from a trip that hasn't happened yet, dated to the window it's waiting for. Jan 7 → Feb 4. A promise, not a memory.

DAY 2 · JAN 9 · AYAMPE, ECUADOR

Landed in Manta yesterday, drove the Ruta del Sol south with the windows down. This morning I walked up the mirador before coffee and there it was: the whole bay laid out, waist-high lines wrapping in over sand, six people out. Surfed until my arms quit. Nobody in the lineup asked what anyone did for work.

DAY 5 · JAN 12 · LOS FRAILES

Small morning, so we did the flat-day thing: the white cove inside Machalilla National Park, water like poured glass, frigatebirds turning overhead. Back in Ayampe by four — the evening tide pushed in chest-high peelers and I had the inside bar to myself.

DAY 8 · JAN 15 · LOS ÓRGANOS, PERU

Night bus over the border at Huaquillas; woke up to desert. The whole world turned brown and dry and the water got even warmer. A mototaxi to the point costs less than a coffee at home. Small clean rights all morning. Boardshorts in January — the inversion works.

DAY 12 · JAN 19 · HUANCHACO

First day back in the 3/2. The caballitos de totora stand along the malecón like sentries — fishermen have ridden waves here for three thousand years and still launch every morning. The left off the pier gave me the longest ride of my life. Then it did it again.

DAY 15 · JAN 22 · PACASMAYO

A working port, not a surf town — faded grand hotels, an iron pier, boats painted like candy. The point is enormous and mostly empty at this size; thick, slow shoulders that let an eight-foot board do all the work. I walked back up the beach instead of paddling. Twice.

DAY 18 · JAN 25 · CHICAMA RECON

Drove over to see the famous one. Gray sky, gray sea, lines to the horizon, one fishing boat, and nobody out. The longest left on Earth at two-to-three foot looks a lot like a Tuesday at home — which is probably why I loved it. Surfed for two hours; saw four other people.

DAY 20 · JAN 27 · TRUJILLO

Rest day in the city. The plaza is painted the color of fruit and the whole town smells like grilled corn. Ceviche twice in one day, no regrets. Shoulders finally stopped aching — which I'm taking as evidence they've adapted rather than surrendered.

DAY 23 · JAN 30 · PICHILEMU, CHILE

Flew south, picked up the van in Santiago, drove to the coast through vineyards. Cold green water again — kelp smell, fog, one surfer out at Punta de Lobos when we pulled up to the cliff. Watched him with the heater running, then drove to La Puntilla and rode the long crumbly inside until dark. Chile at my size. It exists.

DAY 27 · FEB 3 · BUCHUPUREO

End of the road: farm country, sea stacks, a left that peels along a black-sand rivermouth with cows watching from the bluff. Surfed alone under Westport rules — hood optional. Tomorrow the van goes back to Santiago and the board goes back in its bag, carrying sand from two hemispheres.

Wildcards

West Africa

NOV–MAR · 2–3 WEEKS

Dakar & Yoff → Busua, Ghana. Soft warm-water peaks, almost zero surf tourism — the strongest crowd filter on the board and the far end of the Lost Track map.

Fiji, the sandy corner

NOV–MAR · 1 WEEK

Natadola Beach + the Sigatoka rivermouth — the South Pacific without the reef risk. Drivable from Nadi.

Nuquí, Colombia

MAR–SEP · 1 WEEK

Boat-in jungle coast, empty peaks between rivermouths, humpbacks in the lineup Jul–Aug.

Sri Lanka, the quiet side

MAY–SEP · EAST COAST ONLY

The south coast boomed into a beginner-surf boomtown — benched. The sleepier Arugam Bay side stays on the long list.

Tahiti's black-sand beachies

NOV–APR · ADD-ON WEEK

Papenoo and Papara — real, mellow, sand-bottom. Thin value as a flagship; ride along on someone else's Tahiti trip.
