The February Shortlist

The brief: warm water and small, clean waves the kids can actually ride (which, in February, is the same wave Dad wants). A town with a pulse after dinner — livelier than Lo de Marcos, nowhere near Sayulita’s bachelorette circuit. An uncrowded lineup. And no Santa Teresa problem: the town must not be a dusty road you walk down while ATVs buzz your elbows.

Four places pass. Each one is scored against Lo de Marcos — the almost-right baseline — below.

The sleepy–party spectrum

Both vetoes sit at the ends. The amber band is the target zone; all four candidates land inside it — they differ in how they get there.

The baseline · what we’re comparing against

REF

Lo de Marcos

NAYARIT, MEXICO · THE ALMOST

A long, uncrowded crescent of sand, gentle beach-break peaks, golden-hour perfection — and by 8:30 pm, one open taco stand and the sound of your own flip-flops. Everything below keeps what Lo de Marcos gets right (mellow waves, mellow streets, warm water) and fixes the one thing it doesn’t: there’s nothing to walk out to after dinner.

THE VERDICT ON FILE · right waves, right streets, not enough town.

The four candidates

1

Sámara + Playa Carrillo

GUANACASTE, COSTA RICA · THE SAFE GREAT

A reef-sheltered bay on the Nicoya Peninsula — the surf-town equivalent of a heated pool with a view. Carrillo, five minutes south, is the quiet escape valve.

Getting there
SEA→LIR nonstop on Alaska (winter Saturdays, ~7 h), then a 2 h paved drive. Easiest door-to-door on the page.
February surf
Peak dry season: offshore mornings, glassy waist-high rollers over sand. The most forgiving wave on this page.
Water
~83°F. Boardshorts, all day.
Town energy
Beachfront dinner scene, live music some nights, families everywhere. Lively-wholesome, zero party-bus.
The road test · vs Santa Teresa

This is the anti–Santa Teresa layout. In Santa Teresa the town is the road — one dusty artery parallel to the beach, ATVs and dirt bikes threading pedestrians. In Sámara the paved highway comes in perpendicular and dead-ends at the sand; the social spine of town is the beachfront itself, where restaurants open straight onto the beach. You can walk end to end on sand and never negotiate traffic. Back streets are paved, slow, and dust-free. Carrillo is calmer still — a palm-lined crescent.

Pros

  • Nonstop flight from Seattle — huge with kids and boards
  • No travel advisory; easiest place on this list to say yes to
  • Kid-perfect protected wave; surf schools and easy rentals
  • Walkable, beach-facing town that passes the road test outright
  • Howler monkeys, warm nights, Carrillo when you want quiet

Cons

  • Softest wave for Dad — longboard fun, not memorable
  • February is high season: busiest and priciest of the three
  • Least “discovery” feeling — it’s on the gringo trail
  • Book lodging early for mid-February

VS LO DE MARCOS · same warm, small-town DNA — but the town faces its beach and stays awake through dinner, and the wave is friendlier for the kids.

2

La Saladita + Troncones

GUERRERO, MEXICO · THE WAVE

Stay in Troncones (the livelier base, 40 min north of Zihuatanejo’s airport); drive 15 min to Saladita, a left point that peels for 200+ yards at walking speed.

Getting there
SEA→ZIH, 1 stop (LAX or Mexico City), ~9–11 h door-to-door, then a 40 min taxi.
February surf
NW swell wraps in soft and lined-up: knee-to-chest walls, all longboard, almost never closes out.
Water
~79–81°F. Boardshorts.
Town energy
A dozen beachfront kitchens and surf lodges in Troncones; honest margaritas, asleep by 10. The quiet edge of the target band.
The road test · vs Santa Teresa

Passes effortlessly — there is no through-town road because there is barely a town. Troncones is a sandy beach lane with more surfboards than cars; Saladita is a dirt-road hamlet where the traffic is dogs. If anything, it fails in the other direction: evenings here run closer to Lo de Marcos than to Sámara. The bet is that a world-class wave all day makes a quiet night feel earned instead of empty.

Pros

  • The best wave on this page, and it’s a family wave — rare combination
  • Uncrowded by any Sayulita or Nosara standard, especially midweek
  • Warm, small surf-community feel; cheap once you’re there
  • Short airport transfer; no long drives once settled

Cons

  • Guerrero carries a US Level 4 state advisory — the Zihua tourist corridor is heavily visited and fly-in/stay-local is the norm, but it’s a real conversation to have
  • One-stop flights with boards and a kid
  • Evening scene is thin — the “too sleepy” risk lives here
  • Saladita’s point draws a longboard crowd at peak weeks

VS LO DE MARCOS · the nights feel similar — but Lo de Marcos never once handed you a 45-second wave with your kid paddling back out grinning.

3

Playa Venao

AZUERO PENINSULA, PANAMA · THE WILDCARD

A single horseshoe bay near Pedasí, ringed by lodges and restaurants, with a forgiving beach break dead center. Not a town with a beach — a beach that grew exactly enough town.

Getting there
SEA→PTY (1 stop, or via LAX on Copa), then a 4.5–5 h drive or a 35 min charter hop. The long haul of the page.
February surf
Dry-season north winds blow offshore all day; shifty, forgiving peaks that work every tide. Rarely flat, rarely scary.
Water
~77–82°F — February upwelling can add a refreshing edge. Still boardshorts.
Town energy
One curved strip of restaurants, a bar with live music, surf camps. Social every evening, over by midnight.
The road test · vs Santa Teresa

The cleanest pass of all — by architecture. There is no town to walk through: lodges and kitchens ring the bay itself, connected by sand paths, and the coastal road runs behind the properties carrying a handful of cars an hour. Santa Teresa’s problem is structurally impossible here.

Pros

  • Lightest crowds on the page, in and out of the water
  • No travel advisory; Panama is an easy yes
  • Self-contained bay: kids roam free between beach, pool, and dinner
  • All-day offshores in February — clean even at 4 pm

Cons

  • Longest travel day: flight + 5 h drive (or pay for the charter)
  • The scene is one bay deep — by day 8 you’ve met everyone
  • Pricier food and lodging than the value suggests
  • Remote: nearest clinic is in Pedasí, 30 min away

VS LO DE MARCOS · the inverse bet: Lo de Marcos is a town whose wave is an afterthought; Venao is a wave that grew just enough town around it.

4

El Zonte

LA LIBERTAD, EL SALVADOR · THE LATE ENTRY

A cobble-bottom right point with a hamlet of surf lodges and beach cafés grown around it, on the “Surf City” coast 45 minutes from the airport. El Tunco, ten minutes east, supplies the livelier night; El Sunzal’s beginner rollers sit in between.

Getting there
SEA→SAL, 1 stop (LAX or Houston), then a 45 min paved drive — the shortest transfer on the page.
February surf
Dry season, offshore mornings. The small season on these south-facing points: waist-high, clean, empty — kid-right, Dad-modest. The famous size arrives March–October.
Water
~82°F. Boardshorts.
Town energy
A lane of cafés, lodges, and the Bitcoin Beach scene; village-quiet most nights, with El Tunco ten minutes away when someone wants the strip.
The road test · vs Santa Teresa

Passes. El Zonte hangs below the coastal highway — a couple of lanes drop down and dead-end at the sand, and through-traffic never enters the village. El Tunco is the busier strip, but its core is closed to cars at night. You visit the scene; you don’t sleep on it.

Pros

  • US Level 1 advisory as of early 2026 — the cleanest paperwork on the page
  • Cheapest week on the page once you land
  • Kids and Dad share the same forgiving point; El Sunzal’s beginner wave 10 min away
  • 45-minute airport transfer — no long drive with tired kids
  • The lively night is a choice (El Tunco), not the address

Cons

  • February is the off-season for the wave itself — Dad’s surf is at its most modest
  • One-stop flights from Seattle with boards
  • Cobblestone entry — reef booties for the kids
  • Scene leans backpacker-crypto more than family; village-small most nights

VS LO DE MARCOS · the same village quiet — but with a point wave out front and a real night out ten minutes away, quiet becomes a choice instead of a verdict.

The call

BOOK-IT-TODAY · Sámara. The only option with a nonstop flight, no advisory conversation, a wave the kids will love from hour one, and a town that answers the Lo de Marcos complaint without a whiff of Sayulita. The compromise is Dad’s wave — pleasant, not memorable.

IF THE SURF IS THE POINT · Saladita. The best family wave in the Mexican Pacific, honestly bought with two costs: a state-level advisory that deserves a real conversation, and evenings that run as quiet as the place we already vetoed.

IF UNCROWDED WINS · Venao. Emptiest lineup, cleverest layout, zero traffic by design — paid for in travel hours on each end.

IF PAPERWORK AND PRICE WIN · El Zonte. The only Level 1 advisory on the page, the cheapest week, a 45-minute transfer, and the kids on the same point as Dad — bought with February being the small season for the wave itself.

FIELD NOTES · AUG 2026

Flight fact worth anchoring the decision on: Alaska launched a nonstop Seattle–Liberia (LIR) route for winter 2026–27 — Saturdays out, Sundays back, ~7 h — which lines up with a mid-February Saturday-to-Saturday week. Assume it repeats for 2027 but confirm before booking.

On Guerrero: the US State Department lists the state at Level 4 (“do not travel,” reaffirmed May 2026). The Zihuatanejo–Ixtapa–Troncones corridor is a long-standing exception in practice — fly in, stay local — but that judgment belongs to us, not to this page. Costa Rica and Panama carry no equivalent baggage.

On El Salvador: the US State Department downgraded the country to Level 1 (“exercise normal precautions”) in early 2026 — the best advisory rating on this page. The seasonal trade is real, though: the point breaks that made Surf City famous peak March–October, so a February week buys small, clean, and empty rather than the wave the posters are made of.
