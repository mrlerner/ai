Board Shopper — Mid-Length Step-Down

Four candidates to slot in under the Lib Tech Pickup Stick 8'0 — built for softer, less-steep San Diego waves rather than steep North Pacific faces.

Specs & prices checked Aug 6, 2026 · ranks tailored to Matt's surfing

The brief: step down from 8'0 to ~7'4–7'6 without paying a paddle tax. Priorities that drove the ranking: glide and glass over swell power · paddling effort is a first-class cost · 2–4 ft is the happy zone · turtle-roll friendly (no duck-diving) · should feel different from the Pickup Stick, not a smaller clone of it · likely flies to San Diego, so dings matter.

Firewire Seaside & Beyond 7'4

The pick — Machado's glide machine for exactly these waves

49.5 L 7'4 × 21¾ × 2¾ Quad $1,255 new · older-run 7'6 ~$1,025
  • Flattest entry rocker of the four — the direct fix for what the steep-rockered Pickup Stick lacks in soft San Diego walls.
  • Designed by Rob Machado for 1–5 ft lined-up Cardiff-style surf: your 2–4 ft happy zone is its sweet spot.
  • Quad fish DNA means down-the-line speed without pumping — glide and glass, not swell power.
  • 49.5 L keeps the wave count high, and a 7'6 is easier to turtle roll than your 8'0.
  • A genuinely different sensation from the Pickup Stick — this purchase buys you a new feeling, not a smaller copy.
  • Finish caveat: new production is Volcanic (black basalt) only. If you want the classic white-deck/wood-rail Machado look, hunt older-run Helium or white LFT builds — leftover shop stock, B-grades, and San Diego's deep used market.

How the ranking was made

Weighted to how you actually surf, not spec-sheet maximalism:

  1. Wave match first. The target is soft, less-steep San Diego surf in your 2–4 ft comfort band. The two Machado shapes and the Moe are built for exactly that; the TJ Pro Mid prefers a touch more energy.
  2. Paddle effort is a first-class cost. Nothing under 48 L made the list. The Moe (52.9 L) is the paddle king; the Seaside & Beyond gives up little while shedding half a foot of length.
  3. Buy a new feeling, not a smaller clone. The Pickup Stick is already a wide 2+1 funboard. The quad-fish Seaside & Beyond changes how a wave feels; the Moe mostly shrinks what you have. This is the tiebreak that put Machado ahead of Harley.
  4. Forgiveness over performance. Glass and glide beat rail-to-rail shredding — which is why the sharper-railed Xtra Cado sits fourth despite being a superb board.
  5. It has to survive the trip. Boards that fly get dinged. Thunderbolt > Firewire EPS here — the Moe's strongest card, and worth a board bag and travel insurance line-item either way.

Bottom line: order of preference is Seaside & Beyond 7'4 (or an older-run 7'6) → Moe 7'4 → TJ Pro Mid 7'4 → Xtra Cado. If you can demo before buying, try the Seaside & Beyond and the Moe back-to-back on a small day — that head-to-head settles it. Note on looks: every current Firewire in this list ships in a dark Volcanic/black finish only — if that's a dealbreaker, the Moe's colorways or an older-run white S&B are the outs.
