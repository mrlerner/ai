The Five Headline Shifts vs. 2025
The 2026 report is not a continuation of 2025 — it is a regime change. Here is what moved.
New framing

1. "Accelerating," not "improving"

2025 said benchmarks are going up. 2026 says capability is not plateauing: SWE-bench Verified rose from 60% to near 100% of the human baseline in a single year; frontier models gained 30 percentage points on Humanity’s Last Exam.

Regime change

2. U.S.–China gap is closed

2025 said China was “closing.” 2026 says closed: as of March 2026, Anthropic’s top model leads China’s best by just 2.7%. DeepSeek-R1 briefly took #1 in Feb 2025.

New chapter theme

3. Infrastructure is the story

2025 barely touched compute/energy in the top 12. 2026 foregrounds it: 5,427 U.S. data centers, 29.6 GW capacity (NY-state scale), 72,816 tons CO₂e to train Grok 4, one Taiwanese foundry behind nearly every top chip.

New concept

4. The "jagged frontier"

2026 formalizes what practitioners have been saying: models win IMO gold but read analog clocks correctly only 50.1% of the time. Competence is spiky, not a smooth curve.

New labor thesis

5. Entry-level displacement is starting

Not in 2025. In 2026: U.S. software developers aged 22–25 saw employment fall nearly 20% from 2024, even as older-developer headcount kept growing. Productivity gains show up where entry-level jobs are shrinking.

Newly quantified

6. The “chasm” is a category error

Pew asked experts about quality of work (73% positive) and the public heard will I have a job (23% positive). They’re not disagreeing. They’re answering different questions — and both answers are probably right. The real story is the fork underneath.

The talent finding nobody will want to mention

The AI Index reports U.S. inbound AI-researcher migration has fallen 89% since 2017, with an 80% single-year drop in 2025 alone. The U.S. still hosts the most AI talent overall, but it is attracting new talent at the slowest rate in over a decade. This is arguably the single most consequential, least-covered finding in the entire report.

Public Opinion — The Chasm Is a Category Error
The 50-point gap everyone is quoting measures two different questions. Experts were asked about the quality of work. The public heard will I have a job. Both answered honestly. Both were right. The real story is underneath.

The CEO read of the Pew 2025 numbers

When the headline says “73% of experts are positive on AI’s impact on jobs, only 23% of the public is,” that is not a disagreement about the future. It is a disagreement about which future is being forecast. Experts were predicting how the work will change for people still doing it. The public was predicting whether they will be the ones doing it. Both forecasts are defensible. Taken together they describe a fork, not a chasm.

The distributional fork — write this on the wall

The 2027–2030 story is not “who was right.” It is who the forecast was about. Experts are forecasting the experience of knowledge workers who remain employed. Both of their forecasts hold: the work gets better for them. The public is forecasting the experience of people whose jobs are exposed. Both of their forecasts also hold: many of them will not be doing the work. These are compatible predictions about non-overlapping populations.

The product and policy question for 2026 isn’t how to close the gap. It’s how to manage a fork in which the median knowledge worker’s job quality rises 30–50% and the bottom-of-the-ladder cohort contracts 15–30% over five years. Any product, regulation, or narrative that tries to address “AI and jobs” as one thing is going to fail for half the population.

Where experts and public actually agree

On elections and personal relationships, both see net-negative. These are the only two categories where the gap closes. Read that as: the two populations agree where the risk is clearly shared. They disagree where the risk is distributed unevenly.

Who decides the fork narrative

Pew will re-ask this survey in 2026. The gap will narrow from the expert side, not the public side — experts will see agents disappoint in at least one lane (most likely clinical or fully-autonomous coding), and moderate their 80%-of-work-hours forecast toward 55–65%. The public won’t move. Plan product and policy for that asymmetric revision.

Trust in governments to regulate

U.S. lowest of any country surveyed: 31%, vs. Singapore 81%, Indonesia 76%, global avg 54%. The U.S. will regulate AI anyway — just through tort, insurance, and state AGs rather than federal statute. Price this into the compliance roadmap.

Global trust ranking

Pew 2025 across 25 countries: EU most trusted to develop AI responsibly (median 53%), U.S. 37%, China 27%. The EU wins on trust but fields no frontier lab. The trust-provider and the frontier-builder are different continents. That is itself a market.

Responsible AI — The Governance Lag
Capabilities accelerated. Safety disclosures went backwards.
Transparency

FMTI fell 37 → 58 → 40

Foundation Model Transparency Index average rose from 37 (2023) to 58 (2024), then dropped to 40 in 2025. Parameter counts, dataset sizes, and training code are increasingly withheld.

Incidents

362 documented incidents

Up from 233 in 2024 (+55%). Reporting on RAI benchmarks remains sparse compared to capability benchmarks.

Hallucinations

22–94% hallucination range

On a new accuracy benchmark across 26 top models. GPT-4o dropped from 98.2% to 64.4% when the false statement is framed as a user belief. DeepSeek-R1 collapsed from >90% to 14.4%.

Jailbreaks

Safety ratings fall under attack

Frontier models earn “Very Good” or “Good” on AILuminate under normal use. Under adversarial prompting, safety performance drops across all models tested.

Tradeoffs

RAI dimensions fight each other

Empirical studies: training techniques that improve one RAI dimension (e.g. safety) consistently degrade another (e.g. accuracy). There is no free lunch.

Governance

Formal RAI roles growing

AI-specific governance roles +17% in 2025. Share of orgs with no RAI policy fell from 24% to 11%. Top blockers: knowledge (59%), budget (48%), regulatory uncertainty (41%).

Domain Highlights — Science, Medicine, Education
Science

Small beats big in biology

MSAPairformer (111M params) beat leading methods on ProteinGym. GPN-Star (200M) outperformed a model ~200× larger. Virtual cell models (Evo 2, STATE, AlphaGenome) emerged as a new frontier.

Science

AI weather pipeline, end-to-end

Aardvark Weather replaced the full numerical prediction pipeline with one ML system. FourCastNet 3 produces a 60-day global forecast in <4 min, 8–60× faster than prior approaches.

Science

PhD-level research still hard

Best agents score 38.8% on PaperArena vs. 83.5% for PhDs. 17% on real bioinformatics (BixBench). Code fails 58% of the time on Earth observation tasks.

Medicine

Clinical-note AI goes mainstream

Physicians report up to 83% less time writing notes; one system reports 112% ROI. FDA authorized 258 AI medical devices in 2025 — only 2.4% supported by randomized trial data.

Medicine

Diagnostic orchestration

Microsoft’s AI Diagnostic Orchestrator + OpenAI o3: 85.5% on hard cases vs. 20% for unaided physicians. Multi-agent setups show 7–60%+ gains over single-agent baselines.

Education

Students adopted. Schools didn’t.

80%+ of U.S. high school and college students use AI for schoolwork. Only half of middle/high schools have AI policies; just 6% of teachers say their policies are clear.

Education

AI PhDs flow to academia

New AI PhDs in U.S./Canada +22% from 2022–2024 — but all growth went to academia, reversing a decade of flow into industry. CS undergrad enrollment fell 11%.

Education

Fastest skill growth

Outside formal education, AI-engineering skills accelerating fastest in the UAE, Chile, and South Africa. China and UAE mandated AI education starting 2025–26 school year.

Strange Bedfellows — Alliances the Data Already Predicts
Coalitions that wouldn’t exist in normal politics form naturally around AI findings. The ones below are visible in the data today. Some are already organizing. Others are one news cycle away.
Environmentalist × Geopolitics Hawk

Data-center sovereignty

One wants water and grid accountability. The other wants supply-chain control. Same vote on the same siting ordinance. Unifying frame: “AI infrastructure should be accountable to the place it operates in.” Already forming at the state level — watch Virginia and Arizona legislatures in 2026.

Labor Advocate × Parent / Educator × Pro-Natalist Right

“Entry-level must exist”

Unions want apprenticeship jobs. Teachers want their graduates employed. Pro-natalists need the economic base that makes family formation possible. All three converge on: “AI must not eliminate the first rung.” Cross-partisan voting bloc of material size. Whichever party codifies this wins a generational issue.

Safety Researcher × Enterprise CIO × Lloyd’s Underwriter

Auditability as precondition

Alignment researchers want it for safety. CIOs want it for procurement. Insurers want it for pricing. Three buyers, one specification. Unifying frame: “We will not deploy or cover what we cannot audit.” This is the market that makes independent AI auditing real — insurance will force what safety culture couldn’t.

Hollywood Guilds × Truck Driver Unions × Local Newspapers

Training-data royalties

Guilds want residuals. Drivers want data from their routes. Newspapers want payment for scraped archives. Unifying frame: “If our work trained it, we get paid when it earns.” Cross-industry labor IP alliance — first coordinated lawsuit or state statute by late 2026. Low probability per issue, but only one needs to land.

Red-State AG × Blue-State AG

Algorithmic pricing discrimination

Rental screening, insurance pricing, hiring filters. Left AGs frame it as discrimination; right AGs frame it as consumer protection and pricing opacity. Same cases, different filings, same settlements. The first coordinated multi-state AI enforcement action lands in 2026 — before any federal AI law passes.

Disability Rights × Religious Conservatives × Primary-Care MDs

Human-in-the-loop medicine

258 FDA-cleared AI devices, 2.4% with RCTs. Disability rights distrusts algorithmic gatekeeping. Religious conservatives distrust reductive clinical judgment. Primary-care MDs resent being automated. A three-way coalition on “a clinician must sign” — shows up as legislation in at least two states in 2026.

Frontline Worker × Parent × Retiree on Medicare

“What happens to my family?”

Displacement anxiety (worker), pipeline anxiety (parent), care anxiety (retiree). Not a policy coalition — a dinner-table coalition. Larger than the sum of its three constituencies because it is one consistent emotional register. Whichever candidate captures it captures the 2028 primary.

Accelerationist × Enterprise CIO

Agent reliability, now

One wants to ship. One wants to procure. The shared interest is the same number: 99.x% task success. Product alliance, not political. The company that owns the metric owns the 2027 agent market.

Environmentalist × Consumer Surplus Advocate

Efficiency-as-virtue

Efficient inference = more people served per watt. One values the watt saved; the other values the person served. Latent alliance. Needs a single brand to organize it — the AI equivalent of Energy Star would unlock the frame.

Shared Blind Spots — What No Audience Prioritizes
The findings that appear in no audience’s Top 3 are often the most important — because there is no political constituency forming around them yet. These are the openings.

The 50.1% clock-reading

The jagged frontier is the single largest practical deployment risk in the report — cross-cutting across every audience. But no audience in this dashboard made it their #1. It is everyone’s third-priority issue and therefore nobody’s.

Public-private AI capacity gap

Total U.S. public AI investment 2013–2024 was $20.4B. Private investment in 2025 alone was $285.9B. Public institutions are structurally a decade behind in AI capacity. No audience is organized around this.

FDA AI devices without RCTs

258 AI medical devices authorized in 2025. Only 2.4% had randomized-trial evidence. Clinicians know; broader audiences don’t. A future incident makes this a political crisis overnight.

RAI dimensions are a vector, not a scalar

Empirical studies show improving one RAI dimension consistently degrades another. Popular discourse treats safety/fairness/privacy/accuracy as a single “responsibility” score. They aren’t.

“100% to academia” is hiding a hiring freeze

Stanford’s “academia” bucket includes postdocs, adjunct positions, and newly-minted PhDs who haven’t landed a full-time role. The more honest read: industry AI hiring contracted sharply in 2025, postdoc queues lengthened, and some of those PhDs did not get jobs. Enterprise CIOs and Accelerationists depend on that talent funnel — and it is already leaking into parking orbits. No audience is organizing around this, so it will surface as a surprise headline within 18 months.

Median value per user tripled

In one year. Across a tool people often don’t pay for. The distribution question — who captures that surplus — is not raised by any audience. Labor advocates should press it; they haven’t.

Methodology · A note on how this section was built

Audiences are not a dropdown. They are generated structure.

The eight audiences above weren’t retrieved from a canonical list. They were synthesized by asking the report a question it didn’t ask itself: “Who does this affect differently, and where do those differences most disagree?”

That small shift marks a larger one. The first mode of using a language model was “pull the right data out with a clever prompt.” The emerging mode is: the prompt generates structured data, and we reason over that generated structure. The audiences, the gap rankings, the alliance map, the markets-in-the-gaps — these are second-order outputs. None of them exists in the 420 pages of the AI Index. The report is the seed; this audience topology is the growth.

Two practical implications for how to read this dashboard (and any like it):

  1. The findings that feel “obvious” in the composite view are usually the findings that align across multiple audiences. The truly high-leverage insights are the ones that appear only when you pivot to a specific POV — or, conversely, the ones that no single POV surfaces because the report’s authors had their own lens.
  2. Gaps between POVs are where alliances form and markets open. Situational awareness isn’t a single score. It is a topology — a map of who thinks what, and where those maps don’t overlap.

Next iteration of this pattern: let the audience set itself be regenerated per report, so each new document produces its own native audience topology. The generalized report-digest skill would ship with this generator built in.

Operator Lens — Six Claims About the Next 18 Months

A decision memo — opinionated, dated, falsifiable

  1. Benchmarks are already a lagging indicator. Inference economics and task reliability are the next two years of competition. The top-4 Elo cluster is within 25 points and will stay there. Whoever wins 2026–2027 wins on cost-per-useful-task at 99.x% success, not leaderboard position. If your product is priced against capability delta, re-price now.
  2. OSWorld crosses 85% by Q4 2026. Build today as if unattended agents ship in 14 months. The 12→66% curve will not break before it saturates. Assume it. Unattended agent deployment in enterprise functions moves from single digits to 15–25% across customer ops, supply chain, and coding by end of 2027. If you are still designing human-in-the-loop UIs, you are designing for the 2025 market.
  3. The expert–public “gap” is a distributional fork. Stop pitching as if it will close. Quality-of-work gains accrue to remaining knowledge workers; quantity-of-jobs losses hit the bottom of the ladder. Product and policy should target both ends simultaneously — a pro-displacement narrative to knowledge workers reads as triumphalism to the exposed cohort, and vice-versa. We need two voices, not a unified one.
  4. Entry-level displacement is not junior demand collapsing — it’s firms no longer training juniors. The −20% under-25 software dev number isn’t about capability. It’s about senior devs shipping solo with AI assistance, eliminating the apprenticeship line item. The defensible product wedge is “we train your juniors for you” — sold to the firm, priced against the fully-loaded cost of a new hire, and positioned as pipeline insurance for 2028–2030 seniors.
  5. TSMC dependency, talent flight, and FMTI transparency collapse are one story: systems-risk is the next enterprise line item. By 2027 Fortune 500 procurement will require model provenance, supply-chain redundancy, and audit rights the way 2015 procurement required multi-AZ. Ship the certificate now or accept commodity pricing. Independent AI auditing is a category, not a feature.
  6. Schools are a four-year greenfield. Enter at the policy layer, win the tooling layer. 80%+ student use against 6% clear policy is a procurement vacuum at the district level. Whichever vendor owns acceptable-use templates, teacher training, and compliance posture wins the curriculum contract — and the student-facing tool contract follows on a 12–18 month lag. The reverse path (tool first, policy later) is crowded and loses.

Stated agent success continues its doubling curve through at least one more cycle. Enterprise unattended deployment — currently single digits — crosses 15% in customer ops and coding by end of 2027. Judgment-heavy functions stay under 10%.

Aggregate U.S. employment holds. Under-25 cohort drops another 10–15%.

Headline unemployment stays net-positive through 2026 — which is why politicians won’t act. The under-25 knowledge-worker cohort contracts another 10–15% on top of 2025’s −20%. The “we’re fine” macro number will hide a generational displacement.

The “gap” narrows from the expert side, not the public side

Experts revise their 80%-of-work-hours forecast down to 55–65% after at least one high-profile agent failure (most likely clinical or fully-autonomous coding). Public 23%-positive number barely moves. Pew 2026 rereport headlines the expert climbdown.

258 devices cleared, 2.4% with RCTs. The math says a serious incident linked to a cleared AI device with no trial evidence makes national news within 12 months. Triggers a Congressional hearing and a post-market evidence mandate.
