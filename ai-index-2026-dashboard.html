The Five Headline Shifts vs. 2025
The 2026 report is not a continuation of 2025 — it is a regime change. Here is what moved.
New framing

1. "Accelerating," not "improving"

2025 said benchmarks are going up. 2026 says capability is not plateauing: SWE-bench Verified rose from 60% to near 100% of the human baseline in a single year; frontier models gained 30 percentage points on Humanity’s Last Exam.

Regime change

2. U.S.–China gap is closed

2025 said China was “closing.” 2026 says closed: as of March 2026, Anthropic’s top model leads China’s best by just 2.7%. DeepSeek-R1 briefly took #1 in Feb 2025.

New chapter theme

3. Infrastructure is the story

2025 barely touched compute/energy in the top 12. 2026 foregrounds it: 5,427 U.S. data centers, 29.6 GW capacity (NY-state scale), 72,816 tons CO₂e to train Grok 4, one Taiwanese foundry behind nearly every top chip.

New concept

4. The "jagged frontier"

2026 formalizes what practitioners have been saying: models win IMO gold but read analog clocks correctly only 50.1% of the time. Competence is spiky, not a smooth curve.

New labor thesis

5. Entry-level displacement is starting

Not in 2025. In 2026: U.S. software developers aged 22–25 saw employment fall nearly 20% from 2024, even as older-developer headcount kept growing. Productivity gains show up where entry-level jobs are shrinking.

Newly quantified

6. Expert–public perception gap

Not measured in 2025. In 2026: on whether AI will help their jobs, experts say 73% positive vs. public 23% — a 50-point chasm. Similar on economy (69 vs 21) and medical care (84 vs 44).

The talent finding nobody will want to mention

The AI Index reports U.S. inbound AI-researcher migration has fallen 89% since 2017, with an 80% single-year drop in 2025 alone. The U.S. still hosts the most AI talent overall, but it is attracting new talent at the slowest rate in over a decade. This is arguably the single most consequential, least-covered finding in the entire report.

Infrastructure & Environment — The New Bottleneck
Compute grew 3.3×/yr since 2022. Power, water, and a single foundry are the real constraints.
Compute

17.1M H100-equivalents

Global AI compute capacity, up 3.3× per year since 2022. Nvidia >60% of total. Google and Amazon supply most of the remainder; Huawei has a small but growing share.

Power

29.6 GW data-center capacity

Comparable to New York state peak demand. AI data centers are fast becoming a load category competitive with heavy industry.

Emissions

72,816 tons CO₂e

Estimated training emissions for Grok 4 alone — roughly the annual footprint of 15,000 U.S. households.

Water

12M people worth of water

Annual GPT-4o inference water use alone may exceed drinking-water needs of 12 million people. The multiplier is inference, not training.

Supply chain

One foundry, one island

TSMC fabricates nearly every leading AI chip. A TSMC-U.S. expansion began operations in 2025, but the global supply chain remains dependent on Taiwan.

Capex

$150B+ from one hyperscaler

Google alone reported >$150B annual capex in 2025. The "Stargate" joint venture (OpenAI/SoftBank/Oracle/MGX) plans $100–500B by 2029.

Economy & Labor — Productivity Up, Entry-Level Down
The first clean signal of labor displacement, in exactly the roles where productivity gains are clearest.

Where productivity is showing up

  • Customer support: +14–15%
  • Software development: +26%
  • Marketing output: +50%
  • Tasks needing judgment: flat or negative
  • AI agent deployment: single digits across nearly all business functions

Where employment is softening

  • Software devs aged 22–25: −20% from 2024
  • Older devs: still growing
  • Orgs expecting workforce cuts: 1 in 3 in next year
  • Highest anticipated cuts: service ops, supply chain, software engineering
  • Aggregate U.S. employment: not yet falling
Consumer surplus

$172B/yr value to U.S. users

Up from $112B in 2024. Median value per user tripled year-over-year. Most tools remain free or near-free.

Corporate investment

Global corporate AI: 2.1×

Private investment grew +127.5%. Generative AI investment alone grew >200%, capturing nearly half of all private AI funding.

Adoption split

U.S. ranks 24th in GenAI adoption

28.3% of population. Singapore 61%, UAE 54% outperform income levels. U.S. leads in capital and models but lags in consumer penetration.

Public Opinion — The 50-Point Chasm
On almost every question, AI experts and the U.S. public disagree by enormous margins.

Where experts and public agree

Only two areas show alignment: both think AI will hurt elections and hurt personal relationships.

Where they sharply disagree

Jobs, economy, medical care. Public expects GenAI to assist 10% of U.S. work hours by 2030. Experts expect 80%.

Trust in governments to regulate

Global average 54%. U.S. lowest of any country surveyed at 31%. Singapore 81%, Indonesia 76%.

Who does the world trust?

Pew 2025, 25 countries: EU trusted most (median 53%), U.S. 37%, China 27%.

Responsible AI — The Governance Lag
Capabilities accelerated. Safety disclosures went backwards.
Transparency

FMTI fell 37 → 58 → 40

Foundation Model Transparency Index average rose from 37 (2023) to 58 (2024), then dropped to 40 in 2025. Parameter counts, dataset sizes, and training code are increasingly withheld.

Incidents

362 documented incidents

Up from 233 in 2024 (+55%). Reporting on RAI benchmarks remains sparse compared to capability benchmarks.

Hallucinations

22–94% hallucination range

On a new accuracy benchmark across 26 top models. GPT-4o dropped from 98.2% to 64.4% when the false statement is framed as a user belief. DeepSeek-R1 collapsed from >90% to 14.4%.

Jailbreaks

Safety ratings fall under attack

Frontier models earn “Very Good” or “Good” on AILuminate under normal use. Under adversarial prompting, safety performance drops across all models tested.

Tradeoffs

RAI dimensions fight each other

Empirical studies: training techniques that improve one RAI dimension (e.g. safety) consistently degrade another (e.g. accuracy). There is no free lunch.

Governance

Formal RAI roles growing

AI-specific governance roles +17% in 2025. Share of orgs with no RAI policy fell from 24% to 11%. Top blockers: knowledge (59%), budget (48%), regulatory uncertainty (41%).

Domain Highlights — Science, Medicine, Education
Science

Small beats big in biology

MSAPairformer (111M params) beat leading methods on ProteinGym. GPN-Star (200M) outperformed a model ~200× larger. Virtual cell models (Evo 2, STATE, AlphaGenome) emerged as a new frontier.

Science

AI weather pipeline, end-to-end

Aardvark Weather replaced the full numerical prediction pipeline with one ML system. FourCastNet 3 produces a 60-day global forecast in <4 min, 8–60× faster than prior approaches.

Science

PhD-level research still hard

Best agents score 38.8% on PaperArena vs. 83.5% for PhDs. 17% on real bioinformatics (BixBench). Code fails 58% of the time on Earth observation tasks.

Medicine

Clinical-note AI goes mainstream

Physicians report up to 83% less time writing notes; one system reports 112% ROI. FDA authorized 258 AI medical devices in 2025 — only 2.4% supported by randomized trial data.

Medicine

Diagnostic orchestration

Microsoft’s AI Diagnostic Orchestrator + OpenAI o3: 85.5% on hard cases vs. 20% for unaided physicians. Multi-agent setups show 7–60%+ gains over single-agent baselines.

Education

Students adopted. Schools didn’t.

80%+ of U.S. high school and college students use AI for schoolwork. Only half of middle/high schools have AI policies; just 6% of teachers say their policies are clear.

Education

AI PhDs flow to academia

New AI PhDs in U.S./Canada +22% from 2022–2024 — but all growth went to academia, reversing a decade of flow into industry. CS undergrad enrollment fell 11%.

Education

Fastest skill growth

Outside formal education, AI-engineering skills accelerating fastest in the UAE, Chile, and South Africa. China and UAE mandated AI education starting 2025–26 school year.

Strange Bedfellows — Alliances Visible in the Data
Coalitions that wouldn’t form in normal times form naturally around AI findings. Five are already visible.
Environmentalist × Geopolitics Hawk

Data-center sovereignty

Both want local control over where AI infrastructure sits — one for water & grid, the other for supply-chain security. Unifying frame: “AI infrastructure should be accountable to the place it operates in.”

Labor Advocate × Parent / Educator

Pipeline preservation

Both see junior displacement as civic harm. Unifying frame: “AI must augment juniors, not replace them.” Politically potent because it crosses labor and family lines.

Safety Researcher × Enterprise CIO

Transparency mandates

Alignment researchers want auditable systems for safety; CIOs want them for procurement and liability. Unifying frame: “We need to audit what we’re deploying.”

Frontline Worker × Parent

“What will my kids do?”

Consumer-facing political energy around displacement, closer to dinner-table concern than policy analysis. A voting bloc larger than either individually.

Accelerationist × Enterprise CIO

Agent reliability now

One wants shipping, one wants dependable procurement. Unifying frame: “Move agents from single-digit deployment to double-digit reliability.” Product, not policy.

Environmentalist × Consumer Surplus Advocate

Efficiency-as-virtue

Efficient inference = more people served per watt. Both can champion the same trajectory from different values. Latent alliance — not yet organized.

Shared Blind Spots — What No Audience Prioritizes
The findings that appear in no audience’s Top 3 are often the most important — because there is no political constituency forming around them yet. These are the openings.

The 50.1% clock-reading

The jagged frontier is the single largest practical deployment risk in the report — cross-cutting across every audience. But no audience in this dashboard made it their #1. It is everyone’s third-priority issue and therefore nobody’s.

Public-private AI capacity gap

Total U.S. public AI investment 2013–2024 was $20.4B. Private investment in 2025 alone was $285.9B. Public institutions are structurally a decade behind in AI capacity. No audience is organized around this.

FDA AI devices without RCTs

258 AI medical devices authorized in 2025. Only 2.4% had randomized-trial evidence. Clinicians know; broader audiences don’t. A future incident makes this a political crisis overnight.

RAI dimensions are a vector, not a scalar

Empirical studies show improving one RAI dimension consistently degrades another. Popular discourse treats safety/fairness/privacy/accuracy as a single “responsibility” score. They aren’t.

AI PhDs flowing to academia

After a decade of growth into industry, 100% of recent new-PhD growth went to academia. Enterprise CIOs (talent supply) and Accelerationists (innovation pool) should both care. Neither does yet.

Median value per user tripled

In one year. Across a tool people often don’t pay for. The distribution question — who captures that surplus — is not raised by any audience. Labor advocates should press it; they haven’t.

Methodology · A note on how this section was built

Audiences are not a dropdown. They are generated structure.

The eight audiences above weren’t retrieved from a canonical list. They were synthesized by asking the report a question it didn’t ask itself: “Who does this affect differently, and where do those differences most disagree?”

That small shift marks a larger one. The first mode of using a language model was “pull the right data out with a clever prompt.” The emerging mode is: the prompt generates structured data, and we reason over that generated structure. The audiences, the gap rankings, the alliance map, the markets-in-the-gaps — these are second-order outputs. None of them exists in the 420 pages of the AI Index. The report is the seed; this audience topology is the growth.

Two practical implications for how to read this dashboard (and any like it):

  1. The findings that feel “obvious” in the composite view are usually the findings that align across multiple audiences. The truly high-leverage insights are the ones that appear only when you pivot to a specific POV — or, conversely, the ones that no single POV surfaces because the report’s authors had their own lens.
  2. Gaps between POVs are where alliances form and markets open. Situational awareness isn’t a single score. It is a topology — a map of who thinks what, and where those maps don’t overlap.

Next iteration of this pattern: let the audience set itself be regenerated per report, so each new document produces its own native audience topology. The generalized report-digest skill would ship with this generator built in.

Operator Lens — What This Means if You’re Building

A decision memo for someone shipping agentic AI products

  1. The top-4 Elo cluster means capability is no longer the wedge. Anthropic, xAI, Google, OpenAI are within 25 Elo. Compete on cost per useful task, reliability under real conditions, domain coverage, and trust — not benchmark headline numbers.
  2. Agents jumped 5× on OSWorld. Ship agent workflows this year, not next. A 1-in-3 failure rate is acceptable for assistive use and reviewable automation. It is not acceptable for unattended critical-path automation. Design the UX for review and reversibility.
  3. The jagged frontier is a product-design constraint, not a caveat. Any product promising "AI does X" on tasks adjacent to clock-reading, spatial reasoning, or long-horizon planning will produce embarrassing failures. Build evaluation suites on the specific jagged edges of your domain before launch.
  4. Bet on consumer surplus, not per-seat B2B. $172B of U.S. consumer value, value-per-user tripled in a year, and most tools are free. This is where adoption is happening. The AI agent deployment rate in business functions is still single digits.
  5. Entry-level displacement is the thing to manage, not deny. 22–25-year-old software devs −20%. Products that amplify junior practitioners (training wheels, explanation, scaffolding) are more defensible than products that replace them wholesale — because they keep the pipeline alive.
  6. Build for the 50-point perception gap. Don’t pitch products to the public the way you pitch them to experts. The public expects AI to help 10% of work by 2030; experts expect 80%. Your messaging, defaults, and controls need to meet people where they are.
  7. The U.S. is losing inbound AI talent. If you’re hiring, look at Chile, UAE, South Africa, Canada — where AI-engineering skills are growing fastest. South Korea leads AI patents per capita. The geographic frontier is wider than it was a year ago.
  8. Transparency is moving against you as a user. FMTI fell from 58 to 40. Closed frontier systems disclose less about training, data, and compute. Products that wrap frontier models will inherit this opacity. Decide whether your differentiation is better transparency — this is a growing wedge.
  9. Expect the infrastructure conversation to become political. 29.6 GW, a Taiwan dependency, and water usage exceeding the needs of 12 million people will show up in regulation, zoning, and public sentiment. Products with local/efficient inference stories will benefit.
  10. Schools are the biggest under-served market in the report. 80%+ student adoption, 6% clear policies. This is a classic "already happening without infrastructure" opportunity — analogous to Slack before IT bought Slack.
Questions to Watch — What the 2027 Report Will Probably Answer

Does the U.S.–China gap flip?

2.7% is noise. Expect it to invert at least once in 2026. Watch open-weight releases from Alibaba/DeepSeek and the next U.S. frontier cadence.

When do agent failure rates hit 10%?

OSWorld went 12% → 66% in a year. If the next doubling plays out, agents enter the "trustable for unattended work" zone by late 2026.

Does aggregate employment finally move?

Displacement visible in under-25 software devs. 2026 will show whether this generalizes to aggregate U.S. employment or stays contained.

Does the perception gap close or widen?

73% vs 23% on jobs is unsustainable. Either experts moderate (as deployment disappoints) or the public catches up (as value becomes visible).

Does open-weight close the gap again?

Reopened in 2025 to 3.3%. A single DeepSeek-sized release could close it. Track China’s top labs and Meta’s trajectory.

Does the clinical evidence base catch up?
