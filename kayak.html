TWO NIGHTS · PADDLE + COHO · RESEARCHED AUG 1 2026

Kayak Exploration

Five two-night kayak campouts timed to coho season — three in home waters, two across the border. Every route is built the same way: short crossings timed to slack, a camp only boats can reach, and trolling water on the way. Fish while you travel; the fishing is the passage.

Washington · near Seattle & the islands

1 · The Cabin Loop — Guemes → Cypress Island

TOP PICK · AUGUST

The best ratio of adventure to logistics on this list, because it starts from your own beach. Cypress is the wild one of the inner islands — mostly a Natural Resources Conservation Area, no ferry, no town — and it sits one channel west of the cabin.

  • DAY 1
    Launch from the cabin's west side on the morning slack. Cross Bellingham Channel (~1.5 nm of moving water — time it) to Eagle Harbor, then north along the shore to Pelican Beach (~4–5 nm total): 5–10 first-come campsites, composting toilet, fire rings. Afternoon hike up Eagle Cliff for the view over everything you'll paddle tomorrow.
  • DAY 2
    Fish the morning tide change — troll the kelp edges and tide rips off the island's north end and down the east shore. Move camp south to Cypress Head (~3.5 nm), the Cascadia Marine Trail site on the island's prettiest tombolo. Evening bucktail session in the rips off the Head.
  • DAY 3
    Cross back to Guemes at slack, trolling the channel seam on the way. Clean fish at the cabin. Total: ~12–14 nm.
FISHING · MARINE AREA 7 · SAN JUAN ISLANDS Hatchery coho open 7 days/week Aug 1–31 (wild coho must be released); non-selective coho Sept 1–30 — any coho may be kept. Daily limit 2 salmon; release Chinook unless the pamphlet says otherwise for your dates. Single-point barbless hooks for salmon. WA combo licence + catch record card required.
FISHING OPPORTUNITY · STRONG A migration corridor, not a backwater: Nooksack- and Samish-bound coho funnel through Bellingham Channel all August and September, and its tide rips concentrate them within kayak range. The base rate for these waters is real — in Aug–Sep 2024, Area 7 anglers kept roughly 12,500 coho (better than a fish per angler-trip), and the 2026 statewide coho forecast is one of the best in recent memory. August here means hatchery fish with wild releases; the channel seams on your crossing days are the money water. ABSOLUTE PEAK · SEPT 11–20 WDFW creel data (2023–25) from the two ramps serving this exact corridor — Washington Park and Squalicum — puts mid-September far ahead of everything else: ~1.2–1.45 coho per angler Sept 11–20 vs ~0.5 in late August. In the one weak year (2023) the peak slid to Sept 21–30, and the any-coho season closes Sept 30. Go before the first big fall rain — it pulls fish out of the channel and into Bellingham Bay.

Bellingham Channel runs hard mid-cycle — cross at slack, both directions. Watch for the Guemes ferry and give the channel's commercial traffic the right of way.

2 · The One You've Wanted — Matia & Sucia

BEST IN SEPTEMBER

Your instinct that these "might not be best" is half right — in August. Sucia is the most visited marine park in the San Juans and Echo Bay fills with powerboats in high summer. But shift it to a September weekday and everything flips: the boaters thin out, the weather usually holds, and Area 7 goes non-selective on coho — every silver you hook is a keeper. That's the window this trip was waiting for.

  • DAY 1
    Ferry to Orcas with boats on the car; launch North Beach. Cross to Matia — Rolfe Cove (~2.5 nm): a handful of sites on a wildlife-refuge island, no water, magic in the old-growth loop trail. Troll the rips off Parker Reef on the way over.
  • DAY 2
    Short hop to Sucia (~2.5 nm) — camp Shallow Bay for the sunset or Fossil Bay for shelter. Circumnavigate the fingers in the afternoon; fish the tide seams off Ewing Cove and the island's north side where September coho stack up.
  • DAY 3
    Recross to North Beach on the morning calm, trolling Parker Reef again at the tide change. Total: ~9–11 nm.
FISHING · MARINE AREA 7 · SAME RULES AS TRIP 1 September = non-selective coho, 2 salmon daily, barbless. Note the year-round closure zones in Area 7 in the pamphlet before you go; none block the Matia–Sucia water but check your exact troll lines.
FISHING OPPORTUNITY · EXCELLENT IN SEPTEMBER The best fishing-to-paddling match on this page. WDFW's own guidance puts late-summer coho "around the exterior of the San Juan Islands and in the open water of adjacent straits" — Sucia and Matia are the exterior. This is the water behind Area 7's 12,500-coho Aug–Sep in 2024, a run strong enough that WDFW cut the daily limit mid-September because catches were outpacing projections. Parker Reef and the islands' north-side seams are the specific spots, and in September every coho you hook is a keeper. ABSOLUTE PEAK · SEPT 12–19 Area 7 creel data peaks Sept 11–20 in strong years (1.3–1.5 coho per angler, 2024–25) and holds nearly that strong through the Sept 30 close — in 2024 WDFW cut the limit on Sept 23 because catches were still outrunning projections. Hedging against a late run? Sept 15–25 is the safest single window; every recent year's peak falls inside it.

These crossings face the open Strait of Georgia — commit only on a calm morning forecast, and treat the afternoon northwesterly as a wall clock. Matia's sites are few and first-come; have Sucia as the fallback both nights.

3 · The After-Work Shakedown — Blake Island

OPEN NOW → NOV 15

The rehearsal trip: same systems, same fishing, one exit off the highway. Blake is a state park you can only reach by water, twenty minutes from the city, with the Cascadia Marine Trail site on the west shore facing the Olympics.

  • DAY 1
    Launch Manchester (shortest crossing, ~2 nm, and you skip the shipping lanes' worst) or Seacrest in West Seattle if you want the skyline paddle (~4.5 nm, cross the lanes fast and perpendicular). Camp the CMT site; troll the south-end tide rips at dusk.
  • DAY 2
    Dawn troll off Blakely Rock and the Southworth ferry seam — prime Area 10 coho water — then a lazy island lap; the deer own the place.
  • DAY 3
    Morning fish, short crossing home, at your desk by noon smelling faintly of salmon. Total: ~6–10 nm.
FISHING · MARINE AREA 10 · SEATTLE–BREMERTON Coho open now through Nov 15, 7 days/week, non-selective. Daily limit 2 salmon (release wild Chinook per pamphlet). Single-point barbless for salmon.
FISHING OPPORTUNITY · GOOD, NOT MARQUEE Area 10's famous September coho water (Jefferson Head, Richmond Beach) sits a few miles north of Blake, but Blakely Rock and the Southworth ferry seam hold resident coho year-round plus the September push — and the 2026 Puget Sound forecast lifts every corner of the Sound. Right expectation: the fishing is a strong bonus on a great overnight, not the reason to go. That's what a rehearsal trip is for. ABSOLUTE PEAK · SEPT 8–20 Area 10 peaks a touch earlier than Area 7 and fades fast: creel data from the Blake-adjacent ramps (Don Armeni, Manchester) tops out Sept 11–20, then falls off a cliff — ~0.2–0.5 coho per angler by early October even though the season runs to Nov 15. Dawn or overcast on Allen Bank, the WDFW-named coho spot southeast of Blake, is the pattern.

Ferry traffic is the hazard here, not current — cross the lanes perpendicular, in daylight, and assume they can't see you.

British Columbia · two ferries north

4 · The Gentle One — Copeland Islands, Sunshine Coast

PROTECTED WATER

A chain of arbutus-topped islets in Thulin Passage, just north of Lund at the very end of the Sunshine Coast highway — the doorstep of Desolation Sound with none of its commitment. Short hops, warm(ish) swimming, sunsets over the Strait. The drive is the adventure tax: two ferries and most of a day each way.

  • DAY 1
    Launch Lund harbor; paddle ~2.5 nm up Thulin Passage to the Middle Copeland campsite (tent pads, pit toilet). Evening troll along the outside (Strait-facing) kelp edges.
  • DAY 2
    Explore the whole chain to North Copeland; poke toward Sarah Point for the Desolation Sound view. Fish the morning tide change on the outside edges — Area 15 coho are hatchery-marked-only, so expect to release wild fish.
  • DAY 3
    Drift home down the passage. Total: ~7–9 nm, none of it exposed for long.
FISHING · DFO AREA 15 · POWELL RIVER Coho: 2/day, hatchery-marked only (adipose-clipped). You need a BC Tidal Waters licence (non-resident 1/3/5-day options online) plus the Salmon Conservation Stamp to keep any salmon. Rockfish Conservation Areas sit within/near the park — check the RCA map and keep your gear out of them.
FISHING OPPORTUNITY · STRONG — THE SLEEPER The research surprise of this page. Local reports say Lund's coho fishing "has really picked up in the last five years" with good size — and the named hot spots (Mace Point, Iron Mines, Mystery Reef) sit directly on this route's water. Desolation Sound proper fishes mediocre; the outside, Strait-facing edges where you'll troll are the good side. The tempering caveat: hatchery-marked-only retention, and clipped fish are scarcer in BC — expect releases between keepers. ABSOLUTE PEAK · SEPT 1–20 (BULLSEYE: 2ND WEEK OF SEPT) Local and regional sources converge on the first three weeks of September, when the bigger late-run "northern" coho arrive. Better still: in recent years DFO's Sept 1–30 assessment fishery has allowed 1 wild coho/day in Subareas 15-2 to 15-6 — exactly this water — which matters because most coho hooked off Lund are unclipped. Check the current DFO fishery notice in late August; if the wild window is on, September stops being an asterisk.

Thulin Passage carries steady boat traffic bound for Desolation Sound in summer — hug the island side. September quiets dramatically.

5 · The Step-Up — Octopus Islands via Surge Narrows

ADVANCED · CURRENT GATES

The Campbell River option, and the one that scratches the passage-making itch hardest: a wilderness marine park locked behind tidal rapids, reachable only when the ocean says so. This is Torren-in-the-sailing-kayak territory — file it for when the self-rescue class is done and a few Cypress loops are logged, or hire the local guides the first time.

  • DAY 1
    Ferry Campbell River → Quadra; drive to the Surge Narrows Road launch on the island's northeast side. Transit Beazley Pass at slack — non-negotiable — then ~4 nm to a wilderness camp in the Octopus Islands.
  • DAY 2
    Layover: paddle Waiatt Bay's maze, walk to the Small Inlet portage, fish the back-eddies at the mouths of Okisollo Channel on the slack windows — Area 13 coho water with serious current between bites.
  • DAY 3
    Ride the correct slack back through the Narrows. Total: ~9–10 nm plus whatever the maze tempts you into.
FISHING · DFO AREA 13 · CAMPBELL RIVER Coho: 2/day, hatchery-marked only at this time of year. Same BC tidal licence + salmon stamp as Trip 4. Check current DFO fishery notices for Area 13 the week you go — this area changes in-season.
FISHING OPPORTUNITY · VERY GOOD, WITH AN ASTERISK Area 13 is Campbell River — the self-styled Salmon Capital of the World — and September is its coho peak: 8–12 lb fish and "some of the most exciting fishing of the year," per the local guides. The asterisk: the marquee water is the Discovery Passage side, not the Octopus Islands' sheltered maze. Your fish come from the Okisollo Channel mouths at slack windows, improving through September as homing coho move inside. Hatchery-marked-only here too. ABSOLUTE PEAK · SEPT 5–20 Campbell River's guide calendars converge cleanly: coho build through August, peak the first three weeks of September at 12–18 lb, and the biggest fish of the year come late September, with late-run fish into early October. The first half of September adds a residual shot at big Chinook (Tyee season runs to Sept 15), and the same DFO September wild-coho window has covered Area 13 in recent years — same late-August notice-check applies.

Surge Narrows runs to double-digit knots mid-cycle; people have capsized doubles here misjudging slack. Current tables (Ports & Passes), a margin of 30 minutes, and a bailout plan are the entry fee. Guided option: the lodges at Surge Narrows run this exact trip.

From the boat · how the fishing actually works

  • Calibrate expectations to the craft. A charter covers ten times your water — think encounters per tide window, not per day. In a strong year like 2026, one to three coho hookups per dawn or tide-change window in the right rip is realistic from a kayak.
  • Troll while transiting. A small flasher + hoochie or spoon, 15–30 ft down off a light weight or mini-diver, rod leashed and in the holder — your fishing kayak already knows this game. Every crossing between camps is a troll line.
  • Bucktail the rips at first light. Coho feed on the surface at dawn tide seams — a bucktail fly skated 20 ft behind the boat in moving water is the classic kayak coho method and the most fun thing on this page.
  • ID before you keep: hatchery fish = healed scar where the adipose fin was. In August (WA) and all season (BC), unclipped coho go back in the water gently — barbless makes that quick.
  • Kill, bleed, chill. Bleed fish immediately, then an insulated fish bag with a frozen water bottle keeps a September coho perfect until the cabin. In camp, store it hung or in the bag well away from the tent — you're in raccoon (and on Cypress, otter) country.

Safety floor · non-negotiables

  • Dress for the water, not the air — the Sound and the Strait are cold enough to incapacitate in minutes, year-round. Wetsuit or drysuit on crossings, always the PFD, and a VHF on your shoulder.
  • Every crossing happens at slack or with the current, on a morning forecast, with the afternoon wind treated as a hard deadline. You model surf windows for fun — build the paddle window the same way (tide + current + wind, in that order).
  • File a float plan with someone at home; check in from each camp (Garmin inReach earns its keep out here — Matia, the Copelands, and Octopus have no reliable cell).
  • The fishing kayak is right for Trips 1 and 3 close to shore on calm days. For Sucia's open crossings and anything in BC, a sea kayak with sealed bulkheads + a practiced self-rescue is the honest requirement — one weekend class, then this whole page unlocks.
