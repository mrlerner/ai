Cardiovascular fitness

Apple Watch VO2max is noisy (±3–5 points) but directionally useful. Your 38 puts you in "Good" for your age band — not bad, but meaningfully short of what the longevity literature targets. Peter Attia's framework: top quartile for age, which for a 50-year-old male is ~46+ ml/kg/min. The mortality math is striking: Mandsager et al. (2018, JAMA) found going from 50th → 97.5th percentile VO2max was associated with a ~5× reduction in all-cause mortality over 10 years.

Why Zone 2 alone won't get you there

Zone 2 builds mitochondrial density, stroke volume, fat oxidation — all real. But VO2max itself (max cardiac output × max a-v O2 difference) is trained most efficiently at 90–95% of max HR for 3–4 minutes, repeated. Your current cardio (1× Peloton or run at Z2) isn't hitting that band.

The protocol (add once per week)

Norwegian 4×4 — gold standard

Warm up 10 min. Then 4 × 4-minute efforts at 90–95% max HR (RPE 9/10 — hard, sustained), with 3-minute active-recovery jogs between. Cool down 5 min. Total: 38 min. Best on Peloton or rower to avoid impact. Once per week is enough — twice if you feel recovered.

Baseline note: ask Tyson to run a sub-maximal test or get a Cooper 12-min run benchmark. Apple Watch is a starting point, not a source of truth.

Surfing as training load

Surfing one week/month at 2–3 hours/day is ~14–21 hours of paddling + intermittent sprints + core stabilization. That's real work — it's functionally your "mesocycle deload" with different loading. Smart that you're not also lifting that week. The only fitness gap this introduces: you lose the other 6 days of gym work. If longevity is primary, consider one short 20-min bodyweight session (pushups, rows, hollow holds) 2× during surf weeks to keep the neural drive up.
