Met Opera: Macbeth — Opening Night
TONIGHT 6:30PM  ·  METROPOLITAN OPERA HOUSE, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  RUN THROUGH OCT 20  ·  INDOOR
The Metropolitan Opera's season opens tonight with a new production of Verdi's Macbeth — the Norwegian soprano Lise Davidsen stars as Lady Macbeth alongside Quinn Kelsey in the title role, with Yannick Nézet-Séguin conducting. Director Louisa Proske makes her Met debut. Opening night carries real energy: the house is full, the city is paying attention, and tonight's performance is the production at its most charged. The run continues through October 20 if tonight is sold out, but this is the moment. Tickets still show availability at metopera.org.

New York City Ballet: Jewels
OPENING WEEK — SEP 22–27  ·  DAVID H. KOCH THEATER, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  INDOOR
New York City Ballet opens its 2026–27 fall season this week with Jewels, Balanchine's beloved three-part masterwork from 1967 — three gems of ballet vocabulary (Fauré, Stravinsky, Tchaikovsky) danced by the full company. Opening week is when the house has the most energy, the casting is usually strongest, and the audience is genuinely excited to be back. The Koch Theater sits 300 feet from the Met Opera House — two world-class companies on the same plaza, same week. Tickets start around $30.

NY Philharmonic: Dudamel Conducts Mahler's Fifth
THU–TUE, SEP 24–29  ·  DAVID GEFFEN HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  INDOOR
Gustavo Dudamel, in his inaugural season as Music Director of the New York Philharmonic, conducts Mahler's passionate Fifth Symphony paired with a world premiere by Tania León. This is the cultural event of the fall: Dudamel's arrival in New York has been treated as a once-in-a-generation appointment, and Mahler 5 — four movements building to the famous Adagietto — is one of the most emotionally satisfying things a symphony orchestra can do. Geffen Hall is a 10-minute walk from the Upper West Side. Multiple performances; book Thu or Fri.

New York Film Festival — Opening Weekend
OPENS FRI SEP 25 — RUNS THROUGH OCT 12  ·  ALICE TULLY HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  $35 PUBLIC / $30 MEMBERS  ·  INDOOR
The 64th New York Film Festival kicks off Friday night with James Gray's Paper Tiger as the opening film. The festival runs through October 12 at Lincoln Center's Alice Tully Hall and Walter Reade Theater, presenting international premieres and prestige fall releases. Hot ticket: Godzilla Minus Zero — the sequel to the Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One — world premieres as a Spotlight Gala on Saturday, September 26; that's the one to see. General-admission screenings are $35 public / $30 Film at Lincoln Center members; galas run higher. Single tickets went on sale September 15 — some galas may be sold out already, but regular screenings should still have availability at filmlinc.org. Two-ticket limit per screening. A rainy weekend is perfect timing.

Fall for Dance Festival
SEP 22–OCT 3  ·  NEW YORK CITY CENTER, W. 55TH ST  ·  $30 ALL-IN  ·  INDOOR
The annual Fall for Dance Festival at New York City Center is the city's best dance bargain: every ticket is $30 (fees included at the box office), and each program pairs internationally celebrated companies with lesser-known acts. The 23rd edition includes 11 world premieres and seven international companies, plus the Martha Graham Dance Company, Boston Ballet, San Francisco Ballet, and The Royal Ballet. Each evening is a mixed bill — you don't need to know anything going in. Multiple programs through October 3; buy whatever has seats.

Villa Albertine Literary Fall Festival
FRI–SAT, SEP 25–26  ·  VILLA ALBERTINE (UPPER EAST SIDE)  ·  FREE  ·  INDOOR
Villa Albertine is France's cultural center in New York (a rebranded Maison Française), and its annual two-day literary fall festival pairs French and American writers for conversations that don't appear on the usual New York literary circuit. Admission is free; the level of the guests tends to be quietly impressive; and it's held in a handsome mansion-style building on the Upper East Side. Good pairing for a rainy Friday or Saturday if you're already heading up the East Side.
