This week in NYC
Met Opera: Verdi's Macbeth — Opening Night
TOMORROW TUE SEP 22  ·  METROPOLITAN OPERA, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  INDOOR — RAIN PROOF
The Metropolitan Opera's 2026–27 season opens tomorrow night with a new production of Verdi's Macbeth — soprano Lise Davidsen sings Lady Macbeth (she's arguably the greatest dramatic soprano alive right now), baritone Quinn Kelsey is the title role, and Yannick Nézet-Séguin conducts. Director Louisa Proske makes her Met debut. This is the premiere of the new staging — opening-night electricity. Perfect excuse for a rainy Tuesday in the neighborhood. Ten more performances through October 20, but opening night is its own event.

New York Philharmonic: Dudamel Conducts Mahler's Fifth
WED SEP 24 – MON SEP 29  ·  DAVID GEFFEN HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  INDOOR  ·  10 MIN FROM UPPER WEST SIDE
Gustavo Dudamel's inaugural season as Music Director is in full swing, and this week's program is a great one: Mahler's Symphony No. 5 (the big, emotionally overwhelming one) paired with a world premiere by Pulitzer Prize-winning composer Tania León. Geffen Hall is a ten-minute walk from the Upper West Side. Multiple nights this week — the Wednesday, Thursday, or Saturday performance are all available. Dudamel has been the story of classical music in 2026; this is his home court now.

64th New York Film Festival — Opens This Friday
FRI SEP 25 – SUN OCT 12  ·  ALICE TULLY HALL & LINCOLN CENTER  ·  $35 PUBLIC / $15 RUSH
The New York Film Festival, the most prestigious film event in the country, opens Friday with James Gray's crime thriller Paper Tiger — an Adam Driver / Miles Teller / Scarlett Johansson film that premiered at Cannes. The centerpiece is Tony Gilroy's musical drama Behemoth!; the closing film is Ava DuVernay's documentary 14th. Nathan Fielder's Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything is in the lineup. Single tickets ($35 public) went on sale September 15 — you can still get them now. $15 rush tickets are available throughout the festival at the box office, starting two hours before each screening.

Global Citizen Festival — Fugees 30th Anniversary
SAT SEP 26, 4–10PM  ·  GREAT LAWN, CENTRAL PARK  ·  FREE (POINTS-BASED)  ·  OUTDOOR — 40% RAIN RISK
A free outdoor concert on the Great Lawn, timed to the United Nations General Assembly — earn your tickets by completing advocacy actions (signing petitions, contacting reps) at globalcitizen.org. This year's lineup is exceptional: Lauryn Hill and Wyclef Jean performing as the Fugees to mark the 30th anniversary of The Score, plus Lenny Kravitz, Alicia Keys, John Legend, and Victoria Monét. Gates at 2pm, show at 4pm. The catch: Saturday sits at 40% rain — worth watching the forecast. If it's a wash, the New York Philharmonic is the obvious indoor pivot (Saturday is one of the Mahler nights).

Feast of San Gennaro — Final 6 Days (100th Anniversary)
THROUGH SUN SEP 27  ·  MULBERRY ST, LITTLE ITALY  ·  FREE TO WALK
The oldest and largest Italian-American street festival in the United States is in its final six days — and this is the 100th edition, which means expanded programming and bigger crowds than a typical year. Runs 11:30am to 11:30pm daily (midnight on weekends) along Mulberry Street between Canal and Houston. Today's 72°F partly sunny weather makes it the best remaining day to go; Sunday should clear nicely too. The cannoli-eating contest (classic) happens this week.
Brooklyn Book Festival — Free Flagship Day
SUN SEP 27, 10AM–6PM  ·  BOROUGH HALL, DOWNTOWN BROOKLYN  ·  FREE
The largest free literary festival on the East Coast fills eight stages around Brooklyn Borough Hall for a full day: Marlon James, Joyce Carol Oates, Colson Whitehead, Douglas Stuart, Tracy K. Smith, and hundreds of other authors across panels, readings, and the enormous Literary Marketplace (250+ publisher booths). Sunday is forecast to clear — the best outdoor day of the week. Easy subway from the Upper West Side (about 45 minutes on the 3 train).

Cyrus Chestnut Trio at Smalls Jazz Club
FRI SEP 25 – SUN SEP 27  ·  SMALLS JAZZ CLUB, GREENWICH VILLAGE  ·  ~$25 COVER / WALK-IN
Smalls Jazz Club is a 74-person basement in Greenwich Village — one of the great intimate jazz rooms in the city, perfect after an early New York Film Festival screening up the street. Cyrus Chestnut is a deeply swinging pianist and one of the best working in the mainstream jazz tradition. No reservation needed; show up around set time and pay the cover. Sets typically go late. If the New York Film Festival is the main event Friday or Saturday night, this slots in naturally as the second act.
Arthur Jafa: "I Am Tony" — New Museum (Opens Thu)
OPENS THU SEP 24  ·  NEW MUSEUM, BOWERY  ·  THROUGH JAN 4, 2027
The largest retrospective of artist and filmmaker Arthur Jafa ever mounted — two floors of the New Museum's expanded building in the Bowery, covering nearly four decades of his work across film, video installation, sculpture, and photography. Jafa's 2016 video Love Is the Message, the Message Is Death is one of the most discussed works of art of the past decade. Opens Thursday, runs through January. A Thursday afternoon when the weather is gray is the ideal museum excuse.
