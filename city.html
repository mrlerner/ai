64th New York Film Festival — "Once Upon a Time in Harlem" Closes Tomorrow
THRU OCT 12 · ALICE TULLY HALL, LINCOLN CENTER, UPPER WEST SIDE · ~$35
The 64th annual New York Film Festival at Lincoln Center is in its penultimate weekend. The most urgently time-sensitive title: Once Upon a Time in Harlem (director David Greaves), built from 1972 footage of Harlem Renaissance elders — aging writers, musicians, and artists filmed by Greaves's father. Critics have called it breathtakingly beautiful; it plays today and tomorrow only and won't return. Also closing this week: Tony Gilroy's Behemoth! with Pedro Pascal (thru Monday) and Jordan Firstman's debut Club Kid (thru Tuesday). Filmmaker Q&As are the norm; free public talks this weekend include directors Ava DuVernay and Andrew Haigh. Standby lines form 30 minutes before most screenings. Festival runs through Oct 12.

Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous — Opens to the Public Tomorrow
OPENS SUN OCT 4 · THE MET, 1000 FIFTH AVE · $30 ADMISSION (SUGGESTED) · THRU JAN 31, 2027
The Metropolitan Museum of Art's major fall blockbuster opens to the public tomorrow: a dual survey of Lee Krasner and Jackson Pollock, two giants of Abstract Expressionism who were also married to each other. The exhibition reframes the pair — finally giving Krasner equal standing alongside Pollock — and includes major works from museums around the world, including Pollock's Autumn Rhythm: Number 30. Member previews ran Oct 1–3; tomorrow is opening day for everyone. Sunday forecast is rainy (60% after 11am), which makes this the perfect rain-day call: head up to the Met in the morning before the showers arrive, or commit to an indoor afternoon. The exhibition runs through January, but first-week energy is unmatched. Museum closes Tuesdays.
