This week in NYC
64th New York Film Festival — opens Friday
OPENS FRI SEP 25 — OCT 12  ·  ALICE TULLY HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  FROM $15 RUSH / $35 PUBLIC
The biggest prestige-film event in North America kicks off Friday at Lincoln Center, one walk from the Upper West Side. Opening night: Paper Tiger, director James Gray's crime drama. Centerpiece: Tony Gilroy's drama-musical Behemoth! Closing: Ava DuVernay's documentary 14th. Single tickets are on sale now ($35 public, $30 members). Through the run, $15 rush tickets appear at the box office one hour before showtime — first-come, first-served.

NY Philharmonic: Dudamel Conducts Mahler + World Premiere
THU SEP 24–TUE SEP 29  ·  DAVID GEFFEN HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  FROM $38
Gustavo Dudamel's inaugural season continues with Mahler's soaring Fifth Symphony — one of the most emotionally full-throated works in the repertoire — paired with a world premiere by 92-year-old Cuban-American composer Tania León. This is his first season as Music Director; these concerts are historic by definition. Perfect for a rainy night this weekend at a hall that's a ten-minute walk from the Upper West Side.

Met Opera: Verdi's Macbeth
SAT SEP 26, TUE SEP 29 (+ runs THROUGH OCT 20)  ·  MET OPERA HOUSE, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  FROM $33
The Metropolitan Opera opened its new season Monday with this new production of Verdi's Shakespeare adaptation — a role that fits Norwegian soprano Lise Davidsen (as Lady Macbeth) like a glove, opposite baritone Quinn Kelsey. Yannick Nézet-Séguin conducts. One of the most atmospheric operas in the repertoire in one of the great rooms in the world, and tickets are still available for this weekend and Tuesday.

Fall for Dance Festival — 23rd Edition
THROUGH OCT 3  ·  NEW YORK CITY CENTER, W 55TH ST  ·  $30 ALL SEATS
The city's best-value dance ticket: everything $30, featuring 11 world premieres and companies from seven countries including Vienna State Ballet, A.I.M. by Kyle Abraham, Miami City Ballet, and Noche Flamenca. Several programs run this week — each night is a mixed bill, not a single company, which makes it unpredictable and often surprising. Great rainy-weekend plan.

Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony — opens Thursday
OPENS THU SEP 24, THROUGH JAN 4  ·  NEW MUSEUM, 235 BOWERY  ·  INDOOR
The largest survey ever mounted of Arthur Jafa — the filmmaker and visual artist best known for Love Is the Message, the Message Is Death — opens tomorrow at the newly expanded New Museum. Four decades of films, installations, paintings, sculptures, and photographs across two floors. One of the most influential artists working today; Thursday is the right day to see it before the weekend crowds descend (and before the rain arrives). Take the subway to Spring Street and walk.

Feast of San Gennaro — 100th Year, Final Days
THROUGH SUN SEP 27  ·  MULBERRY STREET, LITTLE ITALY  ·  FREE TO ENTER  ·  OUTDOOR
The centennial edition of this Italian-American street festival closes Sunday. Go today or tomorrow (sunny, 66°F) while the weather holds — it's a different experience in the rain. Zeppole, sausage and peppers, a full midway. Classic New York. Indoor fallback this weekend: NYFF screenings at Lincoln Center or the Gramercy Tavern Tavern Room walk-in (arrive before 5:15 or after 8:30 for bar seats, no reservation needed).

New York City Ballet: Jewels — opening week
THIS WEEK  ·  DAVID H. KOCH THEATER, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  UWS HOME TURF
The New York City Ballet fall season opened this week with George Balanchine's Jewels — three pas de deux inspired by gemstones, ranging from Fauré (Emeralds) to Stravinsky (Rubies) to Tchaikovsky (Diamonds). Opening-week energy in the building is always high. The Koch Theater is on the Lincoln Center plaza, so you can combine this with a pre-show dinner at Tatiana's bar or Cafe Luxembourg.
