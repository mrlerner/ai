64th New York Film Festival — Closing Weekend
CLOSES SUNDAY OCT 12 — LAST 6 DAYS  ·  ALICE TULLY HALL, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  INDOOR
The New York Film Festival is the annual curated showcase run by Film at Lincoln Center — not a competition, but the purest selection of the world's best new films each fall. This year's 64th edition runs through Sunday with films from Pedro Almodóvar (Bitter Christmas), Ryûsuke Hamaguchi (All of a Sudden), Paweł Pawlikowski (Fatherland), and more. Closing night Sunday is Ava DuVernay's documentary 14th, examining 150 years of the 14th Amendment's promise of birthright citizenship — expect a packed house with Q&A. Morning press screenings clear by early afternoon; public tickets available with limited slots remaining.

Central Park: Fall Foliage Walk + Ramble
WED–SAT — CENTRAL PARK  ·  FREE  ·  OUTDOOR (RAIN-FREE UNTIL SUNDAY)
Early October is the Ramble's best moment — oaks and maples turning, birding still active, crowds down from peak summer. Five consecutive dry and sunny days (69–73°F) makes this the week to do the Upper West Side loop: Ramble → rowboat rental on the Lake → walk north through the North Woods → dinner nearby. Met Opera: Macbeth
THU OCT 8 @ 7:30PM  ·  SUN OCT 11 @ 3PM  ·  MET OPERA HOUSE, LINCOLN CENTER
Norwegian soprano Lise Davidsen plays Lady Macbeth — she's widely considered the best dramatic soprano working today — opposite American baritone Quinn Kelsey, with Yannick Nézet-Séguin (the Met's music director) on the podium. Verdi's Macbeth is compact, intense, and ideal for a first night at the Met. Two chances this week: Thursday evening or Sunday matinee (plan Sunday before the rain; the matinee ends before the heavy evening showers). Lincoln Center home turf; rush tickets are sometimes available the day of.

Hewa Rwanda — Letter to the Absent
CLOSES SAT OCT 11  ·  BAM HARVEY THEATER, BROOKLYN  ·  NEXT WAVE FESTIVAL
The Brooklyn Academy of Music's annual Next Wave Festival, which brings international avant-garde theater and music to Brooklyn each fall, opens with this Rwandan piece that transforms collective grief into a celebration of memory through poetry, song, and live storytelling. Works like this — emotionally direct, musically alive, non-Western in form — are what Next Wave does better than anywhere else in New York. Five more performances through Saturday. Worth the trip to Fulton Street.
