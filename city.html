64th New York Film Festival
THROUGH OCT 12  ·  FILM AT LINCOLN CENTER  ·  $25–$35/screening
The festival is at full stride this week — the deepest two weeks of a lineup that spans 70+ films. Playing tonight: Mike Leigh's new Tender Loving Care (his first film in years — a Main Slate pick about overwhelmed working-class lives in post-Brexit England); Sandra Wollner's Everytime, which won at Cannes; and Eleanor Coppola's documentary Making Marie Antoinette on the making of her daughter's beloved film. James Gray's Paper Tiger (the opener, starring Scarlett Johansson and Adam Driver) is likely screening again this week. How to get in: Single tickets are available at filmlinc.org — most screenings still have availability mid-week. The Walter Reade Theater is intimate (268 seats); the evening Main Slate screenings at Alice Tully Hall fill faster. Q&As follow many screenings. No rush or lottery — just buy.

Met Opera: Verdi's Macbeth
FRI OCT 2 AT 7:30 PM  ·  METROPOLITAN OPERA HOUSE, LINCOLN CENTER  ·  ~$80–$300
Lise Davidsen — the Norwegian dramatic soprano widely considered the finest in the world today — plays Lady Macbeth opposite baritone Quinn Kelsey, with Metropolitan Opera Music Director Yannick Nézet-Séguin on the podium. The opera runs through October 20, but Friday is prime: the best weather of the weekend, walk to Lincoln Center at 79°F, and the Met on a Friday night. Getting in: Met website has tickets now. Family Circle (top tier) seats start around $30 — the acoustics are excellent. Monday Oct 5 also available if Friday is sold out. Don't wait on Friday; weekend operas fill.

Central Park: The Ramble & North Woods — Peak Fall Day
THU OCT 1  ·  CENTRAL PARK, UPPER WEST SIDE  ·  FREE  ·  OUTDOOR — USE THURSDAY
Thursday October 1 is the week's best outdoor day: 77°F, mostly sunny, light southwest breeze. The Ramble (a 36-acre wooded maze in the center of the park, one of the city's best bird-watching spots) is just starting to turn — early yellows and ochres in the canopy. The North Woods (the park's wild northern section near the Upper West Side) has its own pace. Either route: start at Barney Greengrass (on Amsterdam Ave) for a smoked-fish breakfast before heading in. Picnic-ready by 10am.
Indoor fallback: If Thursday clouds up, the Morgan Library & Museum (East 36th St, 20-min cab) has one of the best reading rooms in the city — quiet, lit by a skylight, J.P. Morgan's private study attached. No advance tickets needed.
Arthur Jafa: I Am Tony — New Museum
JUST OPENED  ·  NEW MUSEUM, BOWERY / LOWER EAST SIDE  ·  SUGGESTED ADMISSION $18
The largest survey to date of Arthur Jafa — filmmaker, artist, and the person behind Love is the Message, The Message is Death (the 7-minute video that changed how people think about images of Black life in America). The New Museum show spans two full floors: films (including The White Album, which won the Golden Lion at Venice), alongside paintings, photographs, and new sculptures. Just opened last week. The New Museum's Bowery building is worth visiting on its own; pair with dinner at Prosciutto (two blocks away — walk-in only, 8 small tables, housemade everything, a true East Village gem).

KRAMER/FAUCI — St. Ann's Warehouse, Brooklyn
JUST OPENED  ·  ST. ANN'S WAREHOUSE, DUMBO, BROOKLYN  ·  OFF-BROADWAY
Daniel Fish — the director who turned Oklahoma! into a genuinely unsettling Broadway experience — opens a new play dramatizing the real, combustible relationship between playwright and AIDS activist Larry Kramer and Dr. Anthony Fauci during the AIDS crisis of the 1980s and '90s. St. Ann's Warehouse in Brooklyn's DUMBO neighborhood is one of the best theater spaces in the city: a converted tobacco warehouse, flexible seating, serious work. Just opened this past Saturday — first week reviews still incoming. Worth watching closely.
Getting there: A, C, or F train to Jay St-MetroTech; 10-minute walk. Or the dumbo ferry from Pier 11. Pair with Lilia or Misi (both 15 min) for pasta before or after.

Buena Vista Social Club — The Musical
THIS WEEK  ·  SCHOENFELD THEATRE, MIDTOWN  ·  FROM $63
The Grammy-winning 1997 album about Cuban musicians finding each other again after decades — brought to Broadway as a theatrical story. The show follows singer Omara Portuondo's return to the studio after years away. Songs in Spanish; dialogue in English. Not a jukebox revue — the music is earned within the story. This week: Tue 7pm, Wed 2pm & 7:30pm, Thu 7pm, Fri 7pm, Sat 2pm & 7:30pm, Sun 3pm. A good pick for a weeknight when you want live music and something with warmth rather than scale.
