Worst on both of your criteria: the most hunters per square mile of the three (easy road access from Yakima and Ellensburg packs it on opening weekend) and the lowest, flattest success rate — it hasn't cracked 10% in a decade. Mule deer only, 3-pt min.

How to read the ranking. On your weighting (crowding first), Wenaha wins — but its solitude is priced in effort: it's a wilderness hunt, and opening-week rifle hunters there average one deer per ~50 hunter-days. If you want the best chance of punching a tag on a short trip over country you already know, Alta is the rational pick; its "crowds" are also concentrated along the Methow valley roads, and thin out fast uphill. Either is defensible. Umtanum isn't — it loses on both axes.

The timberland question

Short answer: there's no timberland permit to buy near any of these three spots — and happily, you don't need one.

None of the three areas has industrial timberland with a hunting-permit program. Washington's pay-to-hunt timberland is almost entirely elsewhere: Weyerhaeuser's permit areas (Vail, Pe Ell, Aberdeen — sold online at recreation.weyerhaeuser.com) are all in western Washington; Manulife/Hancock's new fee program covers Stevens, Spokane and Pend Oreille counties in the far northeast; Green Diamond and Rayonier are west-side; Western Pacific Timber's Klickitat County land has been closed to the public since 2023. These programs sell quota-limited annual permits (Aug 1–Jul 31 year, on sale May–July, ~$200–$400) and popular areas sell out months before rifle season — so "buy online Friday, hunt Sunday" generally isn't how they work even where they exist.

What your three areas actually are:

  • GMU 169 (Wenaha): essentially all public — Umatilla National Forest, mostly inside the Wenaha–Tucannon Wilderness. Nothing to buy; just hunt the forest.
  • GMU 342 (Wenas): your pin sits in the big WDFW-managed block (Wenas Wildlife Area / L.T. Murray country, ~105k acres). Public hunting, no permit — the old Boise Cascade timber rights inside Wenas were bought back by WDFW in 2008. You only need a Discover Pass or Vehicle Access Pass to park — and a Vehicle Access Pass already comes with your WA hunting license. (Discover Pass is $35/yr, sold instantly online at discoverpass.wa.gov, if you need one.)
  • GMU 242 (Alta): mostly Okanogan–Wenatchee National Forest plus DNR/BLM/WDFW tracts; the private land is ranch ground along the Methow bottoms (knock-on-doors, not permits).

Also checked: WDFW's free Private Lands Hunting Access program (Feel Free to Hunt / Register to Hunt / Hunt by Reservation) has no properties enrolled in GMU 169, 242, or 342. The nearest listings are farm and wind-farm ground around Dayton (outside 169) and the Wild Horse Wind Facility in GMU 329.

Before Sunday: nothing to buy except licenses/tags you already have. Make sure the Vehicle Access Pass is in the truck (or grab a Discover Pass online), check fire/road closures for the forest you pick, and remember it's 3-point minimum on mule deer in all three units.

Regulations snapshot — Oct 17–27, 2026

General modern-firearm deer season, from WAC 220-415-020. Verify against the 2026 pamphlet before you shoot.

  • Season: Oct 17–27 in all three GMUs (11 days).
  • GMU 169: mule deer 3-pt min · whitetail 3-pt min.
  • GMU 242: mule deer 3-pt min · whitetail any buck.
  • GMU 342: mule deer 3-pt min (effectively a mule-deer unit).
  • Antlerless: not allowed on the general tag in any of the three.
  • Points: count on one antler; eye guards ≥1" count.
